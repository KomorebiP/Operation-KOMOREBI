# Proof note: finite-dimensional Gaussian Bayesian conditioning

## The statement and its domain

Let X have the Gaussian law N(m,P), where P is a real positive semidefinite
matrix. Let the likelihood at x be N(Hx,R), with R positive definite and H an
arbitrary rectangular real matrix. Equivalently the joint law is generated by
X and an independent noise vector epsilon with law N(0,R), then Y=HX+epsilon.
The state and observation dimensions may differ. The prior may be singular.

Define

    S = H P Hᵀ + R
    K = P Hᵀ S⁻¹
    C = P - K H P
    M(y) = m + K(y-Hm).

The checked conclusions are the evidence law N(Hm,S), the exact joint-measure
identity

    N(Hm,S)(dy) N(M(y),C)(dx) = N(m,P)(dx) N(Hx,R)(dy),

with the coordinate swap made explicit in Lean, and the equality

    posterior(y) = N(M(y),C)

for N(Hm,S)-almost every y. The left side is Mathlib's selected canonical
posterior kernel, not an informal conditional-density symbol.

The explicit kernel y -> N(M(y),C) is a posterior version defined at every y.
This does not imply that every conditional-probability version must agree at
each observation. The Lean statement deliberately keeps the almost-everywhere
qualification for the canonical version.

## 1. Covariance validity and identities

The retained six-module covariance checkpoint proves S positive definite,
hence the actual left and right inverse equations for S⁻¹. It proves

    K S = P Hᵀ
    S Kᵀ = H P
    K S Kᵀ + C = P.

It also proves

    C = (I-KH) P (I-KH)ᵀ + K R Kᵀ.

The right side is a sum of positive semidefinite terms, so C is positive
semidefinite. This justifies the candidate posterior covariance instead of
introducing its validity as an additional assumption.

## 2. Gaussian transport and actual kernel generation

FDGaussianTransport identifies rectangular matrix action as a continuous linear
map on Euclidean coordinate spaces. It proves that the adjoint corresponds to
transpose and proves the exact pushforward law

    A#N(m,P) = N(Am, A P Aᵀ).

FDGaussianSum proves Gaussian convolution and the law of AX+BZ for independent
Gaussian vectors using a product measure. The affine extension adds a mean.

FDGaussianKernel defines a measurable Markov kernel

    gaussianAffineKernel A c R (x) = N(c+Ax,R)

and proves, for the relevant positive semidefinite noise covariance, that the
prior/kernel joint is exactly the pushforward of the prior times independent
centered Gaussian noise. This is the link from kernel semantics to the
independent-noise calculation, not a hypothesis supplied to the main theorem.

## 3. The forward joint law

Order the joint coordinates as (Y,X). The generative maps are

    X -> (HX,X),    epsilon -> (epsilon,0).

Their sum has mean (Hm,m) and covariance

    [ S       H P ]
    [ P Hᵀ    P   ].

FDJointPacking implements an invertible measurable identification of product
Euclidean spaces with Euclidean space indexed by the sum of the two coordinate
types. In particular, pushing measures through this identification is injective.
FDJointLiftAlgebra proves the rectangular block-matrix identities.
FDForwardJointLaw consequently identifies the actual swapped prior/likelihood
joint law with this packed Gaussian.

## 4. The reverse joint law

Generate Y with law N(Hm,S) and an independent Z with law N(0,C). Set

    X' = m + K(Y-Hm) + Z.

FDPosteriorKernel gives the corresponding genuine Gaussian Markov kernel.
FDReverseJointLaw proves that (Y,X') has mean (Hm,m) and covariance

    [ S       S Kᵀ       ]
    [ K S     K S Kᵀ + C ].

The three identities in Section 1 show that this covariance is exactly the
forward covariance. Thus the packed Gaussian measures agree. Injectivity of
packing gives equality of the original joint measures.

This argument avoids inverting P or C and avoids requiring a Lebesgue density
for a possibly degenerate prior or posterior.

## 5. Evidence and disintegration

FDGaussianConditioning contains five concluding theorems:

- fd_joint_disintegration: exact joint-measure equality.
- fd_observation_evidence: equality of the actual likelihood/prior composition
  with the Gaussian evidence, obtained by taking the joint marginal.
- fdPosteriorKernel_eq_canonical: apply Mathlib's
  ae_eq_posterior_of_compProd_eq to the proved joint identity.
- canonicalPosterior_eq_multivariateGaussian: rewrite the explicit kernel in
  innovation form with covariance C.
- canonicalPosterior_eq_josephGaussian: rewrite C in the proved Joseph form.

Neither Gaussian joint-law equality nor the desired posterior equality is
assumed by these concluding statements.

## Verification and limits

The full 18-module finite-dimensional chain was rebuilt after removing all
project .olean outputs, while retaining the pinned mathlib cache. All 51 theorem
and lemma declarations passed an axiom audit. There are 34 newly checked
declarations beyond the 17 in the preceding covariance checkpoint. Counts
include support identities and are not counts of mathematical discoveries.

Two symbolic regression theorems check that zero observation leaves the explicit
posterior equal to the prior, and zero prior covariance keeps the explicit
posterior deterministic. They are universal identities, not finite numerical
tests.

No distributional-distance estimate or finite-dimensional multiscale perturbation
integration is asserted here. That next interface must identify the actual
posterior covariance with the covariance operators used by the earlier bound
and refinement theorems. The present proof does not cover singular innovation
matrices or pseudoinverse conditioning. It establishes no literature priority.
