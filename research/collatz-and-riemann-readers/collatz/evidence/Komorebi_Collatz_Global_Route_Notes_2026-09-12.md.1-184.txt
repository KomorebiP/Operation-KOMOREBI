## The remaining mathematical target

Use T(n)=n/2 for even n and T(n)=(3n+1)/2 for odd n. If a positive counterexample
exists, choose the least one n. Every forward state is at least n. Every positive
ancestor of a forward state is also at least n, because convergence transfers
through a common forward state.

The orbit either eventually repeats a state and enters a nontrivial positive
cycle, or never repeats. In the latter case it eventually leaves every bounded
set: an injective sequence of natural numbers visits a finite interval only
finitely many times. Thus a strategy that relies on infinitely many visits to
a fixed low band cannot silently assume those visits in the injective branch.

A sufficient global induction target, also equivalent to Collatz, is

\[
\forall n>1\quad\exists\,0<m<n,\ a,b\ge0:
T^a(m)=T^b(n).
\]

The smaller source converges by induction, and the common forward state then
transfers convergence to n. Naming this equivalent target is not progress by
itself. The missing work is a universal construction or forcing theorem.

## Route 1: mergers that keep the original source comparison

For an actual prefix n→y, write

\[
My=Pn+C,\qquad M=2^L,\quad P=3^q.
\]

A competing word from a potential smaller source m would satisfy

\[
M'y=P'm+C'.
\]

Eliminating y gives

\[
m=\frac{M'Pn+M'C-MC'}{MP'}.
\]

The precise target is to choose a different word for which this expression is
an integer and lies strictly between 0 and n. Actual realization follows from
the existing positive affine-equation equivalence. The choice of a useful word
for every alleged least-failure orbit remains unproved.

An attractive special case keeps the same length and odd count. If the new
offset is C'>C with P|(C'−C) and C'−C<Pn, then

\[
m=n-\frac{C'-C}{P}\in(0,n)
\]

reaches exactly the same endpoint. This is a concrete offset comparison problem
for different ordered words. There is no claim that every actual prefix has
such a competitor. Allowing different lengths and odd counts may be essential.

The most useful next certificate should compare m directly with n. A local
partner below a high excursion state y need not be below n.

### Failed shortcut: pull a smaller endpoint back along the same prefix

Suppose a smaller state x<y is pulled backward through the same word. Its
candidate source is m=(Mx−C)/P. If that is an integer, then

\[
P(n-m)=M(y-x).
\]

Since gcd(P,M)=1, there is an integer \(\ell\ge1\) with \(y-x=P\ell\) and

\[
m=n-M\ell.
\]

Consequently, if M≥n, no such m is positive. Equivalently, two distinct positive
sources sharing L parity steps differ by at least 2^L. A smaller source cannot
share the entire prefix once 2^L≥n. A late useful partner therefore needs an
earlier difference in its word. This is an elementary written deduction, not
a new formalized theorem in this note.

The previous merger-delay theorem separately rules out a universal fixed pair
of forward and inverse horizons. It does not rule out an adaptive search.

## Route 2: retain the arithmetic when expanding words change

The checked repeated-word identity fixes a word with affine data P,M,C and
D=P−M>0. After k repetitions from a positive integer n it forces

\[
M^k\mid Dn+C,\qquad M^k\le Dn+C.
\]

To handle changing words, let M_i z_{i+1}=P_i z_i+C_i, D_i=P_i−M_i>0, and
A_i=D_i z_i+C_i. Direct substitution gives

\[
M_iD_i A_{i+1}
=P_iD_{i+1} A_i+
M_i(D_iC_{i+1}-D_{i+1}C_i). \tag{S}
\]

For identical consecutive words the last term vanishes. For different words
it must be retained, including its sign and the factors multiplying it.

For example, OOE has (P,M,C,D)=(9,8,5,1), while OOOE has
(27,16,19,11). The expression inside parentheses is −36 for OOE→OOOE and
+36 for the reverse switch. Therefore a proposed argument charging every
switch a positive additive cost fails. Equation (S) was checked by substitution
and ordinary algebra here, not by a new Lean run.

The research target is control of the accumulated, weighted switch terms using
integer divisibility and the original source. Positivity cannot be assumed.
A scalar growth bound or a rational recurrence alone will not settle integer
realization. Start with these two block types to test a proposed invariant, then
retain arbitrary run lengths and long falling blocks before making a global
claim. Success only for a restricted word language is partial progress.

## Route 3: attack general cycle divisibility independently

For a word with q>0 odd steps, length L, and positive offset C, a positive cycle
source must satisfy

\[
(2^L-3^q)n=C,\qquad 2^L>3^q.
\]

Using the existing realization equivalence, divisibility by this positive gap
is also sufficient for the corresponding positive integer cycle realization.
Thus the exact cycle problem concerns the ordered offset C, not only the size
of the gap. To exclude nontrivial cycles it would suffice to prove that every
positive integer solution has n≤2. Trivial cycles and repetitions of their
words must be retained.

The latest family theorem works because its special word forces an exponential
source-height lower bound. A general word has no such bound established here.
Repetition of a complete cycle gives the same equality again: its coefficient
is contracting, so the expanding-word exclusion is inapplicable.

I would investigate whether an exact offset identity or another ordered-word
invariant supplies a source-height contradiction beyond special repeating
families. Tests of such a claim should include words with highly uneven runs
and large excursions. Merely checking more cycle lengths does not supply the
unbounded argument.

## Priority and proof boundary

I would prioritize Route 1 because a forced smaller-source meeting can settle
both branches. Route 2 is a concrete place to look for a new invariant that
might force such a meeting. Route 3 is an independent attack on the cycle branch.

There is currently no proof that every candidate generates a useful competitor
word, pays an unavoidable cumulative switching cost, or violates the general
cycle divisibility equation. These are substantial remaining mathematical gaps.
The last family theorem also keeps its published power-gap theorem as an
explicit formal hypothesis. An entirely self-contained Lean solution would
need that analytic leaf formalized or a proof that avoids it.
