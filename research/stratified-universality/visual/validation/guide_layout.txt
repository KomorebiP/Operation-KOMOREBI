1    Reading this guide
The paper asks when several descriptions of one mathematical object can be compared, transported, or assembled
without losing a distinction that matters. Its answer is relative: first declare the operations, the permitted
realizations, the continuation rules, and the observations. Then prove the relevant comparison.
This guide follows one thread. We begin with exact finite packet coordinates. We then compare complete coordinate
changes with selectors and folds, study chart loops, and turn to task-dependent quotients. The last examples
separate carrier change, observer change, and admissibility change. Each step introduces a different obligation.
Every worked example uses a stated finite model. Exact rational arithmetic checks its displayed identities. A finite
computation supports the example, while the general theorem remains the written argument in the manuscript.
The metric curve near the end is a floating-point illustration of an analytically proved limit.


Question                                 Mathematical object to inspect
Can the proof cross this bridge?         Preservation profile and target hypotheses
Is a coordinate record complete?         Analysis and synthesis inverses
What was discarded?                      Selector residual, fold kernel, or quotient kernel
Do chart paths agree?                    Closed-word holonomy and coherent comparisons
Which distinctions matter?               Future observations and behavioral kernel
Can one carrier replace another?         Semantic fiber containment
Can a missing completion be realized?    Admissible class and attainment


The guide is a companion, not a substitute for the 247-page source volume. The labels printed on each page point
to its exact definitions and results. A label identifies a manuscript result; it is not a Lean declaration or a new
certification claim.

2    A stratum has a passport
An object is represented inside an ambient category. Its allowed models form a smaller category, and its classification
theorem may hold only on a normalized subcategory. A passport keeps these levels separate.

                                         Sα = (Cα , Πα , Mα , Nα , Semα , Iα ).




         Ambient category                 Verified constructors                   Admissible models
                C                                   Π                                     M




        Normalized models                    Native semantics                      Invariant target
                N                                  Sem                              I : Core(N) → D




    The six fields specify where a model lives, which operations are preserved, and exactly what is classified.

For example, a rational generating function and a finite recurrence can represent the same scalar sequence. A
reduced generating function and a minimal reachable-observable state realization may carry a classification theorem.
An arbitrary larger state presentation can contain unused coordinates. Equality of the scalar sequence therefore
does not identify every such larger presentation.
A normalization witness includes a functor and a natural comparison of semantics. It also records its domain,
auxiliary choices, and any restriction. “Normalize” is not permission to delete structure merely because it seems
redundant.
An invariant is complete on its declared target when

                                  I(X) ∼
                                       = I(Y )     ⇐⇒      X∼
                                                            =Y          (X, Y ∈ N ).

This quantifier is the boundary of the classification claim. To transport that claim across an overlap, the bridge
must preserve the normalization and the relevant isomorphisms.

3    A theorem crosses a verified profile
A preservation profile records the constructors a bridge actually preserves. A proof-support certificate records the
constructors its proof actually uses. The transport gate compares these two records.

                                                 Supp(proof) ⊆ ΠF .




           Proof support                      Verified bridge
                                                                                    Target hypotheses
          {sum, compose,                         profile Π
                                                                                      checked locally
               split}                      Declared constructors


             Transport gate: Supp(proof) ⊆ Π, plus target hypotheses


                   Both the constructor inclusion and the target-specific hypotheses must pass.

Suppose an argument uses finite sums, composition, and split packet idempotents. An additive bridge with the
required packet comparisons transports that equational argument. An argument that also uses a kernel, tensor
product, limit, positive metric, or completed sum requires the corresponding additional preservation witness.
For a chain of bridges, a safe common profile is the intersection of their verified profiles. A construction preserved
by only the first bridge does not silently survive the second.
The distinction is practical. Calling a frequency band, a residue class, and a latent channel a “packet” gives a shared
vocabulary. To apply the finite packet theorem, one must still supply idempotents, annihilation, reconstruction, and
the chosen splittings. To claim an energy identity, one must also supply the Hilbert and orthogonality hypotheses.

Exact quotient transport       Required quotient preservation
Norm estimate                  Declared norm and its inequalities
Global gluing                  Coherent comparisons and effective descent

                               2

Here CE acts on maps, rather than on source vectors. This type distinction matters when composing selectors with

The phrase “named residual” means the discarded map remains explicit. A later operation can inspect it, bound it,

If the packets are orthogonal projectors on Hilbert spaces and f is Hilbert–Schmidt, the selector is also an

earlier oblique example does not automatically have those metric properties.

10     A fold has a collision kernel
Consider the additive fold
                                         Φ : Q2 → Q,       Φ(u, v) = u + v.
The inputs (1, 0) and (0, 1) share the same output. Their difference (1, −1) lies in

                                                 ker Φ = Q(1, −1).

The exact sequence
                                                                Φ
                                             0 → Q(1, −1) → Q2 −
                                                               →Q→0
records precisely the distinctions that disappeared. A kernel is more informative than a vague statement that the
record became “smaller.”
If a declared fold comes with a decoder D satisfying DΦ = I on its source, it is split lossless there. Such a decoder
cannot exist for this full two-dimensional source: DΦ(1, −1) = 0 cannot equal (1, −1). It can exist after restricting
to an explicitly chosen normalized core.
This gives a useful three-way distinction:

  A complete coarse corner record changes coordinates.
  A selector drops named positions and keeps a residual.
  A fold merges positions and has a collision kernel.

In an abelian target, image and cokernel add a second question: which target records are actually attainable?
Kernel loss and essential-image failure are independent obligations.

remaining edge contributes a closed-word holonomy He .

13     Compatible support and nonzero action differ
In the swap example, the pair orbits are

                                     ∆ = {(1, 1), (2, 2)},        Ω = {(1, 2), (2, 1)}.

Their support selectors retain the diagonal and off-diagonal parts of h. A singleton such as {(1, 1)} is not compatible:
it sends the invariant identity to a projector that moves under the swap.


   C₂ swap: diagonal / off-diagonal pair orbits              Sign twist: off-diagonal selector has zero action




               a                      b                                       a                  0


               b                      a                                       0                  a

Pair-orbit supports are minimal nonempty compatible supports. Their action also depends on the splitting twist.

Now keep both addresses fixed, but let the loop act on the two lane fibers by

                                                   D = diag(1, −1)

over Q. Equivariance forces Dh = hD, so h = diag(a, b). Every singleton position is a compatible support
because the address action is trivial. Nevertheless, the nonempty off-diagonal support {(1, 2)} selects zero on every
equivariant map.
This example separates an address-level statement from a statement about the section space. A minimal nonempty
support need not define a nonzero endomorphism of that space. The permutation-module case has a stronger
orbit-indicator basis theorem; an arbitrary twisted local system does not inherit it merely from its address orbits.

14     A semantic slice declares the task
The next question is not which coordinates reconstruct the entire object. It is which distinctions matter for a
declared family of future observations.
                                              Σ = (A, G, V, O).
The carrier doctrine A specifies how alternatives combine. The continuation grammar G specifies legal futures. The
observation category V specifies the kind of observed values. The family O specifies the task.
For a continuation monoid M , action ρ, and observations oj , behavior is
                                                                 
                                          Beh(x) = oj (ρ(m)x) m,j .

Two states are behaviorally equivalent when all these values agree. In a module category, the invisible submodule is
                                            \
                                       N=       ker(oj ρ(m)),     Q = X/N.
                                             m,j

The quotient is the coarsest exact representation for that task whenever the required quotient exists. Any surjective
representation retaining all future observations admits a unique map to Q.
The semantic slice and the preservation profile serve different roles. The profile tells us what a bridge preserves.
The slice tells us which distinctions the task asks us to retain. A bridge can preserve the carrier operations while
failing to preserve the declared task distinctions.
Changing the continuation grammar can change the quotient even when the present observation is unchanged. The
next two examples show why present invisibility alone is insufficient.

15     Finite partition refinement discovers future distinctions
Let the state set be {A, B, C, D, E, F }. The observation is zero on A, B, C and one on D, E, F . There is one
continuation generator with transitions

                               A 7→ D, B 7→ A, C 7→ B, D 7→ F, E 7→ D, F 7→ D.

States with the same present observation start in the same class. Split a class whenever its members transition into
different current classes.




     r=0                    ABC                                DEF



     r=1                A                     BC                            DEF



     r=2                A                    B                    C                        DEF




                 After two strict refinements, the partition stabilizes at {A}, {B}, {C}, {D, E, F }.

The first split distinguishes A from B, C: one step reaches observation one from A, but not from B, C. The next
split distinguishes B from C. The three states D, E, F remain indistinguishable because every future stays inside
the observation-one class.
At stabilization, every class has constant observation and transitions to a single class. Induction on continuation
length shows all future observations agree within a class. Conversely, each split records a finite future that
distinguishes the separated states.
This is a finite certificate for this system. The general behavioral-core theorem explains why the stabilized partition
is the greatest continuation-stable invisible congruence.

17     Task strengthening retains more information
Adding observations or continuations can only shrink the invisible kernel. If Ns ⊆ Nw are the strong and weak task
kernels, then
                                          Qs = X/Ns ↠ X/Nw = Qw .
The direction of this forgetting map is easy to reverse accidentally: the strong task retains more, so it can answer
the weak task after forgetting.
For X = Q3 with identity continuations, let the weak task observe only x, and the strong task observe (x, y). Then

                                       Nw = span(e2 , e3 ),    Ns = span(e3 ).

The forgetting map is (x, y) 7→ x. Its exact task-loss sequence is

                                              0 → Qe2 → Q2 → Q → 0.

The lost coordinate is specified by a quotient object Nw /Ns , not by the number of words used to describe the task.
For three nested task kernels, the total loss fits into

                                        0 → N2 /N1 → N3 /N1 → N3 /N2 → 0.

This is extension data. Over a field, a splitting can be chosen, but is generally not canonical. Over other modules,
the extension may fail to split. For example, the quotient map Z → Z/2Z has no additive section: an element of
order two cannot map to a nonzero element of the torsion-free group Z.

19     A relative residual supplies the missing view
In the diamond, the residual needed to augment S with T is

                                         RT |S = ker(J → S) ∼
                                                            = N/(N ∩ M ).

For s(x, y, z) = (x, y) and t(x, y, z) = (y, z), this residual is Qe3 : the source view already knows y, but must add z.
In the reverse direction, the residual is Qe1 . Relative residuals need not be symmetric.
The factorization gate is

                              t factors through s    ⇐⇒     N ⊆M       ⇐⇒      RT |S = 0.

The first example fails the gate. States (0, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 1) have the same S-view but different T -views. This pair
is an exact witness that no map from S can recover the complete T -view.
If we replace t by t′ (x, y, z) = y, the gate passes: t′ is the second-coordinate projection of s. There is no missing
relative residual for this weaker target task.

31     Finite atlas assembly needs effective gluing
Local chart classifications do not automatically classify global objects. A finite profiled atlas supplies chart
categories, typed overlaps, coherent reversible comparisons, and semantic realizations. Its descent category consists
of compatible families of local objects and compatible local morphisms.
The finite pseudolimit construction gives componentwise identities and composition. Invertibility is componentwise,
so the maximal subgroupoid of descent agrees with descent of the local cores. A pseudonatural directional bridge
induces a global functor; it becomes an equivalence only with the corresponding equivalence hypotheses.
For invariant completeness, three obligations must be kept together:

1. Each invariant is complete on its normalized local category.
2. The invariants and chart comparisons are coherent on overlaps.
3. The local invariant-compatible isomorphisms have effective descent.

The last condition is the gluing gate. Equal local invariant values can supply local isomorphisms without ensuring
that those isomorphisms assemble globally. Holonomy, cokernel, Brauer, or higher-coherence defects may obstruct
that assembly in their respective settings.
The manuscript verifies a selected finite certified atlas and separates its reversible connected components from
directional bridges. It does not infer an unrestricted heterogeneous stack or one global coordinate system from the
finite example.
The useful publication claim is exact: finite profiled assembly is proved under its stated coherence, preservation,
and effectivity hypotheses, and the declared finite atlas supplies a concrete realization. Larger atlas classes remain
separate obligations.
