## 1. Finite certificates for bounded local hulls

### Claim card

Let A be a finite product of nonarchimedean local fields K_i, with normalized
integer valuations, and O the product of their valuation rings. A bounded
subset U with a nonzero projection to every factor has smallest full
fractional O-ideal

    H(U)=product_i pi_i^(a_i(U)) O_i,
    a_i(U)=min {v_i(x_i): x in U, x_i!=0}.

This ideal is generated by at most d points of U, where d is the number of
field factors. For a nonempty family of such regions with bounded union,
the union hull is the sum of the individual hulls and is already determined
by at most d family members. A target ideal Q is contained in that hull
exactly when its coordinate valuation inequalities have witnesses in the
same family. Positive weighted log-volumes then have the stated monotonicity.

### Dependency reconstruction

1. Boundedness supplies a lower bound for each nonzero coordinate valuation.
   A nonempty subset of Z bounded below has an attained minimum.
2. Fractional ideals in a product of DVRs are products of principal
   coordinate ideals. The exponents in the displayed formula give exactly
   the smallest such ideal containing U.
3. The central idempotents of O isolate each selected coordinate. A point
   attaining the ith minimum therefore generates that coordinate ideal
   after multiplication by its idempotent and an O_i-unit. Choosing one
   point for each coordinate proves the d-point bound.
4. Taking the minimum over all family members yields the same coordinate
   minima as taking the minimum over their union. Each of these minima is
   attained by one member, so at most d selected members have the full
   union hull.
5. For Q with exponent vector b, Q subset H(U) means b_i>=a_i(U) for every
   i. This is exactly the stated witness criterion, with the same inequality
   orientation.
6. Multiplication of those inequalities by positive w_i log(q_i), followed
   by negation and summation, gives ell_w(Q)<=ell_w(H(U)). This does not
   assert an additive measure for arbitrary weighted regions.

The example with exponents (0,2) and (1,1) correctly shows that equality of
the one weighted degree does not imply componentwise ideal containment.
For the two-field example with exponent vectors (m,4m) and (4m,m), each
region has ordinary measure q^(-5m), their intersection has measure
q^(-8m), their union has measure 2q^(-5m)-q^(-8m), and their hull has
measure q^(-2m). Thus the distinction between individual, union and hull
volume is mathematically substantive.

Strongest safe result: a fixed bounded local union hull has a finite
valuation-witness certificate. The witnesses need not arise from one
family member. They nevertheless must lie in the same fixed family to
which the hull bound applies. This does not construct any admissible IUT
witness, justify mixing different inputs, cover the archimedean places,
or prove a global numerical comparison.

## 2. Homogeneous tensor packets and factor permutations

### Claim card

Let K/Q_p be finite, A=K^(tensor r) with r>=1, and O_A the product of the
valuation rings in its field decomposition. Insert the same nonzero q in
slot i to obtain q_i. For the module hull phi(V)=O_A V and any subgroup
G of the tensor-factor permutation group, with J=phi(U), the claim is

    phi(union_(sigma in G) sigma(q_i U))
       = q_i sum_(sigma in G) sigma(J).

If J is G-invariant, the result is q_i J. For a full fractional ideal J
and a q of nonzero valuation, distinct integer powers q_i^m J and
q_i^n J are distinct. The weighted threshold formula records that same
retained power without substituting ordinary Haar measure for an IUT
normalization.

### Dependency reconstruction

1. Characteristic zero makes K/Q_p separable. Successive factorization of
   its separable minimal polynomial over the field factors gives A as a
   finite product of local fields. This step is explicitly proved in the
   note by the Chinese remainder theorem.
2. A component projection composed with insertion of one tensor slot is
   a unital Q_p-map from K to a component field, hence an embedding.
3. The unique extension of the p-adic absolute value to K means that every
   such component embedding gives q the same absolute value. Consequently
   every component of q_i/q_h has absolute value one. The quotient is
   therefore an O_A-unit, not merely a tuple of abstract isomorphic values.
4. Factor permutations preserve integrality and hence O_A. Module hull
   commutes with those permutations, multiplication by an invertible
   element of the commutative algebra, and arbitrary unions interpreted
   as module sums.
5. Applying the preceding rules gives a sum of q_(sigma(i)) sigma(J).
   Each insertion differs from q_i by an O_A-unit, which fixes the
   O_A-module sigma(J). Factoring out q_i proves the exact orbit formula.
6. If J is invariant, its symmetrization is J. Without invariance, the
   formula retains the symmetrization, so no extra invariance hypothesis
   is being silently discarded.
7. On a full fractional ideal, equality of two insertion powers would
   force (m-n)v_lambda(q_i)=0 in every component. The inserted valuation
   is nonzero when |q|_K!=1, giving m=n. The strict-containment statements
   and the linear weighted-threshold increment follow with the stated
   signs and p-normalized valuations.

The optional weights may be zero individually but have positive total.
Since every insertion has the same p-normalized valuation, the increment
is m v_K(q) times that total, as asserted. Identical hulls have identical
values under a fixed numerical function regardless of whether that function
is invariant under permutations of unequal hulls.
