## Claim card and scope

All triples are positive integers, ordered, satisfy `a+b=c`, and have `gcd(a,b)=1`. The radical is the product of distinct rational prime factors. The parameter `T` is real and at least two; logarithms in the limit are therefore well-defined. The maximum is over actual source triples, not arbitrary local residues. The finite-view index is integers `N≥3`, with reduction from modulus `M!` to `N!` when `M≥N`.

The external theorem concerns a finite-rank subgroup of `(K*)^n`, an equation with nonzero coefficients, and nondegenerate solutions. The note applies it in characteristic zero with `K=Qbar`, `n=2`, coefficients `(1,1)`, and subgroup `U_S×U_S` of rank `2|S|`. This is a number-of-solutions statement, not a bound on the height of an individual solution.

## Decisive derivations

For injectivity, even equality of the first normalized coordinate is enough: if `a/c=a'/c'` with both fractions reduced and all entries positive, uniqueness of the reduced numerator and denominator gives `a=a'` and `c=c'`; the additive equation then gives `b=b'`. Thus the finite normalized-solution set cannot conceal an infinite scaling family, because the stated primitivity removes that scaling.

For the inverse limit, choose `N_0≥3` with `N_0!>B(T)`. Every coordinate of every member of `X_T` lies in `[1,B(T)]`. If two such coordinates are congruent modulo `N_0!`, their difference has magnitude below the modulus and is therefore zero. Let a compatible limit point have component `g_{N_0}`. Exactly one `x∈X_T` maps to it. For every `N≥N_0`, the source triple representing `g_N` reduces to `g_{N_0}`, so injectivity forces that representative to equal `x`. Compatibility then forces all earlier components as well. This proves surjectivity and uniqueness directly.

The coupling's rational recovery leaf is also elementary: a nonintegral real coordinate fails a nearest-integer test because the real distance is at most one half and every integral local norm is at most one. Once the real coordinate is `m∈Z`, testing `m+p^k` gives a positive local product `p^{-k}`. Positivity forces finite nonunit support, and unique factorization gives local valuation `k` at `p`; therefore the local coordinate agrees with `m` modulo every `p^k`, hence equals it. Applying this to the three coordinates justifies the stated source identification when the stated predicates are imposed.

## Scope controls and remaining gap

- The source-recovery tests characterize the intended source; on that source they impose no new height inequality. The coupling does not turn an intrinsic image statement into an algorithm for recognizing those images.
- The source count can bound the number of triples in `X_T`, but this proof gives no location or size of the largest `c`. Neither that count nor inverse-limit stabilization supplies the ABC exponent.
- The lower-scale family establishes only the liminf. Theorem 3 proves an equivalence, not either conjectural upper bound.
