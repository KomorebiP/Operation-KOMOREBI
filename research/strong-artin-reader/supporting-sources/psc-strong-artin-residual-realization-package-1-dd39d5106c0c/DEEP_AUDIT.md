# Deep mathematical audit

## Cyclic torsor and selector layer

The Galois torsor is a direct Clifford theoretic statement. The automorphic torsor is imported from cyclic base change. Determinant selection is exact when the quotient prime is coprime to the rank. The no go theorems prove that this selector is optimal among algebraic central characters and highly ramified epsilon twists.

The total induced object theorem explains why automorphic induction of the base change cannot recover the torsor offset. It is the direct sum of every quotient twist and is invariant under cyclic rotation.

## Extension spectrum layer

Stable subgroup types acquire quotient character multiplicity spaces. Their multiplicity polynomials and Fourier traces are complete invariants for quotient twisting. Moving types are induced blocks and are invariant under quotient twists. Global attachment is precisely the existence of one offset compatible with all places.

## Finite Satake layer

The cyclotomic root bound is asserted only for finite Galois base change, where the residue degree divides the global degree. Integrality and finiteness of the Hecke alphabet follow. The alphabet is used again in the separating prime and residual realization theorems.

## Finite trace image theorem

Let the image algebra generated by a finite semisimple group representation be A. Faithful semisimplicity makes A semisimple. The trace form on A is a direct sum of nonzero multiples of matrix trace because the residual characteristic exceeds the total rank. A basis chosen from group elements makes the trace coordinate map injective. The finite trace alphabet then bounds the number of image elements.

The theorem has been tested on cyclic, diagonal, symmetric, and small matrix group examples. Its historical novelty has not been established.

## One large residual realization

The proof has four stages.

1. The trace alphabet bounds the residual image independently of the residual prime.
2. Taking the prime larger than the bound places the representation in cross characteristic.
3. Nonmodular representation theory lifts the finite image representation to characteristic zero.
4. A common cyclotomic field and an injective reduction on the finite source and target alphabets force exact equality of the lifted Frobenius polynomials.

The common number field and compatible place are essential. The theorem proves existence of an Artin representation after one sufficiently large residual realization. It does not prove that an arbitrary finite Satake cusp form has such a residual realization.

## One prime residual attachment

After restriction to the cyclic extension field, Brauer Nesbitt identifies the semisimplification with the known absolutely irreducible residual representation. Equality of dimensions then gives irreducibility of the restriction itself. The residual extension is therefore one member of the quotient character torsor. Separating reduction lifts the residual polynomial equality to characteristic zero.

## Quotient prime gate

At the prime dividing the cyclic extension degree, every quotient character becomes trivial after reduction. Frobenius on polynomial coefficients is injective over the residue field, so the unramified automorphic and Galois polynomial systems agree residually. This produces residual automorphy automatically. The hard part is a nonregular weight zero lifting theorem.

## l adic finite trace rigidity

Density of the selected unramified Frobenius elements in every finite quotient makes the finite trace condition hold on the compact image. It extends to the Zariski closure. On the connected reductive identity component, the trace is constant. Restriction to a maximal torus and linear independence of characters forces all weights to be trivial. The identity component is therefore trivial and the image finite.

The theorem requires semisimplicity, continuity, finite ramification, and the stated density set. These hypotheses prevent the known infinitely ramified counterexamples.

## Failed completion routes

The manuscript records four failed routes rather than hiding them.

1. Total automorphic induction is torsor blind.
2. Central algebraic invariants and highly ramified epsilon factors are blind at quotient primes dividing the rank.
3. Product functional equations give cyclic norm identities, not factor alignment.
4. General finite Satake descents are singular weight zero objects and are not automatically covered by existing cohomological realization theorems.

## Remaining core

The remaining theorem is existence of one ordinary residual or l adic realization for every finite Satake descent. Once it exists, the new rigidity results make finite image and exact polynomial recovery automatic.
