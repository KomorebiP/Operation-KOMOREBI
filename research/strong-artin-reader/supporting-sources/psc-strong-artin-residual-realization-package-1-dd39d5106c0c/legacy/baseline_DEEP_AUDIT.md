# Deep mathematical audit

## Cyclic torsor layer

The Galois torsor theorem is a direct Clifford theoretic statement. Freeness follows because an intertwiner between an extension and a nontrivial quotient twist restricts to a scalar on the irreducible subgroup representation, forcing the quotient character to be trivial.

The automorphic torsor is imported from cyclic base change and its fibre theorem. The article does not reprove trace formula results.

## Determinant layer

A quotient twist by `chi` changes determinant and central character by `chi^n`. When the quotient has prime order `p` and `p` does not divide `n`, the `n`th power map is a bijection. The base changes of the Galois determinant and automorphic central character agree over the extension field, so their ratio is a quotient character. This proves existence and uniqueness of the determinant matched automorphic descent. Matching determinant is explicitly not promoted to full attachment.

## Local extension spectra

For a stable irreducible subgroup type `tau`, the cyclic obstruction to extension vanishes over complex coefficients. Fixing an extension makes its multiplicity space a representation of the cyclic quotient. The multiplicity polynomial records all quotient character labels. A quotient twist rotates every stable spectrum by the same amount. Moving orbits correspond to induced blocks and are invariant under quotient twists. This gives the exact if and only if extension spectrum criterion.

The Fourier version is equivalent because the discrete Fourier transform on the prime cyclic quotient is invertible. A single nonzero nonconstant Fourier mode uniquely determines a candidate cyclic shift.

## Global alignment

At every place the automorphic descent parameter and the target Galois parameter have isomorphic restrictions after local base change. Both are Artin type locally. Their local cyclic difference is therefore a finite representation theoretic problem. Strong Artin at the cyclic step is exactly the existence of one global quotient index satisfying every local condition. No compactness or finite intersection argument is asserted because the local sets depend on infinitely many places and no theorem currently forces them to stabilize.

## Local Artin descent

The local Langlands parameter is a Weil Deligne pair. Base change keeps monodromy and restricts the Weil representation. If the base changed parameter has zero monodromy, so does the descent parameter. If the restricted Weil image is finite and the subgroup has finite index, the full image is a finite union of cosets of a finite subgroup and is finite. This proof handles ramified as well as unramified places.

## Finite Satake theorem

At an unramified place, base change raises the normalized Satake conjugacy class to the residue degree `f`. If the base changed eigenvalues have exponent dividing `M`, every descended eigenvalue belongs to `mu_(fM)`, hence to `mu_(dM)`. Integrality, finite Hecke field, finite polynomial count, and absolute value one follow directly.

The central character proof uses the fixed order bound outside a finite ramification set together with the finite local images at the remaining places.

## Finite Satake realization reduction

This is a conditional theorem, not a realization theorem. Given the stated realization principle, the realized Galois representation restricts to the known lower field representation by Chebotarev and semisimplicity. Irreducibility of the restriction forces irreducibility upstairs. It is therefore a member of the cyclic extension torsor, and quotient twisting selects the prescribed target.

The complex pseudocharacter gate is a sufficient criterion. The fact that a continuous complex representation of a profinite group has finite image follows because its compact image is a totally disconnected compact Lie group and hence finite.

## Positive saturation

The analytic argument is exact. Artin formalism turns a positive character relation into an identity whose right side is entire. Brauer induction supplies meromorphic continuation of the target Artin function. A pole of the target would produce a pole in every positive power, so no pole exists. This criterion is intentionally positioned within the literature on generalized and almost monomial methods rather than claimed as a wholly new holomorphy theory.
