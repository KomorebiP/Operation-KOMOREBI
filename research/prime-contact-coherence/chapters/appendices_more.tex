\chapter{Extended Derivations and Structural No-Go Theorems}

\section{Why exact synthesis prevents free coordinate splitting}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be normed spaces, and suppose
\[
 J:X\longrightarrow Y,
 \qquad
 S:Y\longrightarrow X
\]
satisfy
\[
 SJ=I_X.
\]
Then
\[
 \norm{x}
 =
 \norm{SJx}
 \le
 \norm{S}\norm{Jx}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \norm{Jx}
 \ge
 \frac{\norm{x}}{\norm{S}}.
 }
\]
An analysis map may make coordinates smaller only by increasing the synthesis norm. This elementary inequality is the common source of several no-free-refinement results.

For a scalar split
\[
 t=y_1+\cdots+y_e,
\]
the child sup norm can fall to
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{|t|}{e}\right\rceil.
\]
The synthesis map has norm $e$, so
\[
 e\left\lceil\frac{|t|}{e}\right\rceil\ge|t|.
\]
This explains why raw shell splitting cannot lower the intrinsic packet height.

\section{Homogeneous higher obstructions}
Let $\mathcal O$ be homogeneous of degree $m$:
\[
 \mathcal O(tx)=t^m\mathcal O(x).
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{\norm{x}}
 {|\mathcal O(x)|^{1/m}}
\]
is scale invariant. Replacing one large coordinate by several coordinates of size roughly the $m$th root does not improve a bound whose obstruction estimate raises those coordinates back to degree $m$.

A higher obstruction can help only if it introduces something genuinely new:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item divisibility growing faster than degree
\item positivity stronger than the triangle inequality
\item all-conjugate cancellation controlled by a product formula
\item a nonhomogeneous regulator
\item a global compatibility law across several equations
\end{enumerate}

This is why second-order phase curvature did not by itself provide an escape from formal smoothness.

\section{Derivation of the stable height}
The real relaxation can be derived directly from aggregate sector variables. Let $A$, $B$, and $C$ denote the aggregate derivative values on the three sectors. Additivity gives
\[
 A+B=C.
\]
The normalized Wronskian is
\[
 -bA+aB=kQ.
\]
For one obstruction unit $k=1$, the real minimization seeks to minimize $H$ subject to
\[
 |A|\le a\sigma_aH,
 \qquad
 |B|\le b\sigma_bH,
 \qquad
 |C|\le c\sigma_cH,
 \qquad
 A+B=C.
\]
The Wronskian has the three equivalent representations
\[
 -bA+aB,
 \qquad
 aC-cA,
 \qquad
 cB-bC.
\]
The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 |W|\le H\min\left\{
 ab(\sigma_a+\sigma_b),
 ac(\sigma_a+\sigma_c),
 bc(\sigma_b+\sigma_c)
 \right\}.
\]
This bound is attained. For example, if the first displayed quantity is minimal, comparison with the other two quantities implies
\[
 a\sigma_a\le b\sigma_b+c\sigma_c,
 \qquad
 b\sigma_b\le a\sigma_a+c\sigma_c.
\]
Hence the vertex
\[
 A=-a\sigma_aH,
 \qquad
 B=b\sigma_bH
\]
is compatible with the third constraint $|A+B|\le c\sigma_cH$ and attains the first bound. The other two cases follow by cyclic permutation.

Since $Q=abc/R$, the exact minimum is
\[
 \overline H
 =
 \frac{c}{R\Delta(a,b,c)},
\]
where
\[
 \Delta
 =
 \min\left\{
 \sigma_a+\sigma_b,
 \frac cb(\sigma_a+\sigma_c),
 \frac ca(\sigma_b+\sigma_c)
 \right\}.
\]
The real problem is therefore completely solved. The unresolved content lies in the integral departure from this stable width.

\section{Formal jet recursion}
Let
\[
 F(\mathbf T)=0
\]
be the shell equation and let
\[
 \mathbf T(t)=x^{(1)}t+x^{(2)}t^2+\cdots.
\]
Suppose the gradient row $\Lambda$ is primitive. Choose an integral vector $v$ satisfying
\[
 \Lambda\cdot v=1.
\]
At order $m$, substitution gives
\[
 \Lambda\cdot x^{(m)}
 +
 G_m(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(m-1)})
 =0.
\]
The integral correction
\[
 \boxed{
 x^{(m)}
 =
 -G_m v
 }
\]
solves the equation at that order. Induction produces an integral formal curve.

The construction is explicit, but coefficient size can grow rapidly. Formal solvability and small archimedean height are logically different problems.

\section{Exact Kummer kernel for a rational packet}
Let
\[
 x=\pm\prod_{q\in S}q^{\nu_q}
\]
and consider its class modulo $M$th powers. For positive $x$, the order is
\[
 \frac{M}{\gcd(M,\{\nu_q\})}.
\]
If $x<0$ and $M$ is even, the sign contributes a separate element of order two. The exact order is therefore the least common multiple of the valuation order and the sign order.

The kernel is bounded by the gcd of $M$ with the valuation vector. Since each nonzero valuation is at most $\log_2|x|$, the kernel is logarithmic in the height of the packet. This is the precise reason Kummer globalization is almost lossless.

\section{The mean-preserving Hecke cascade}
At a Tate curve, an $\ell$-isogeny packet contains one branch multiplying depth by $\ell$ and $\ell$ branches multiplying depth by $1/\ell$. Under uniform averaging,
\[
 \frac{\ell+\ell(1/\ell)}{\ell+1}=1.
\]
After $n$ independent refinements, if the deep branch is chosen $k$ times, the normalized depth is
\[
 \ell^{2k-n}
\]
with multiplicity
\[
 \binom nk\ell^{n-k}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^n
 \frac{\binom nk\ell^{n-k}}{(\ell+1)^n}
 \ell^{2k-n}
 =1.
\]
The first moment is an exact martingale. The mean logarithmic depth is
\[
 -n\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\log\ell.
\]
Most branches become shallow, while rare branches retain the mean. Linear averaging cannot produce a uniform contact contraction.

\section{Arbitrary-index congruence lattices}
Let $v\in\Z^s$ be primitive. Complete $v$ to a unimodular basis
\[
 v,v_2,\ldots,v_s.
\]
In the dual basis, choose the row that vanishes on $v$ and takes value one on $v_2$. Reduce it modulo an arbitrary integer $C$. The resulting row is primitive modulo $C$ and annihilates $v$.

Thus
\[
 \Lambda_C
 =
 \{x:g_C\cdot x\equiv0\pmod C\}
\]
has index $C$ and contains the same fixed vector $v$. This proves that no bound on $C$ can follow from the length or support of $v$ alone.

\section{Direct positive divisibility}
Suppose $A,B>0$ and $c\mid A+B$. Then
\[
 A+B\ge c.
\]
The larger of the two is at least half their sum, so
\[
 \max(A,B)\ge c/2.
\]
This elementary inequality rules out a wide class of direct constructions in which one attempts to build a small positive $S$-unit relation whose sum is divisible by the output.

\chapter{Functorial Gluing of Sparse Relations}

\section{Why gluing matters}
The preceding theory analyzes one sparse relation in isolation. Many arithmetic problems contain chains of relations. Recurrence sequences, addition laws, factorization identities, and arithmetic dynamics naturally produce diagrams rather than single equations.

Consider
\[
 A+B=C
\]
and
\[
 C+D=E.
\]
The term $C$ is an output in the first relation and an input in the second. Its contact packet should not be duplicated independently. A functorial theory should identify the two copies and record how depth moves across the diagram.

\section{Contact pushout}
Let $\Jcal_C^{\mathrm{out}}$ be the contact packet of $C$ in the first equation and $\Jcal_C^{\mathrm{in}}$ its packet in the second. Both are canonically
\[
 \rad(C)\Z/C\Z.
\]
The first gluing operation identifies them through the identity map. The total contact object is the pushout of the two relation diagrams along this common packet.

At the associated-graded level, the operation glues prime shells. At the filtered level, the full carry tower must be identified. Forgetting the extension data before gluing can create spurious rank.

\section{Kummer fiber product}
Balanced Kummer ratios from the two equations define classes
\[
 \beta_1=-B/A
\]
and
\[
 \beta_2=-D/C.
\]
The shared term $C$ creates a multiplicative compatibility. The natural global object is a fiber product of the two Kummer torsors over the Kummer class of $C$.

A complete theory would track:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item common prime valuations
\item sign compatibility
\item shared field extensions
\item induced character quotients
\item cancellation of Kummer kernels
\end{enumerate}

This is a potential source of new global conservation laws. One equation alone cannot detect cancellation between the two Kummer kernels.

\section{Logarithmic matrix composition}
Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be the cross-prime logarithmic matrices of the two relations. The shared term determines a common set of prime channels. The combined system has block form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 L_1&0\\
 *&L_2
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
with a coupling block induced by the shared valuations of $C$.

If the coupling block is nondegenerate, the combined minor can be stronger than the minors of either relation separately. This is one route by which a gluing theorem could escape the one-equation coherence barrier.

\section{Coherence-character composition}
Suppose the two normalized coherence characters have orders $C_1$ and $C_2$. Their product character has order dividing
\[
 \lcm(C_1,C_2).
\]
If the shared term forces a compatibility relation, the actual order can be strictly smaller. Conversely, the combined system can expose a higher-rank determinant even when each individual quotient is cyclic.

The correct operation is therefore not simply multiplication of conductors. It is a derived fiber product of the character maps followed by Smith reduction.

\section{A gluing conjecture}
A general functorial gluing theorem should assign to a finite diagram of sparse relations a filtered adelic complex whose local cohomology is obtained by homotopy pushout and whose global coherence determinant satisfies a submultiplicative law.

A useful first target is:
\[
 \boxed{
 C_{\mathrm{glued}}
 \le
 \frac{C_1C_2}{\rad(C)}
 \times
 \text{subpolynomial correction}.
 }
\]
The displayed formula is schematic. It expresses the hope that the shared term cancels one full support layer. No such theorem is proved.

\section{Applications of gluing}
Potential applications include:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item recurrence sequences with addition formulas
\item chains of perfect-power gaps
\item arithmetic dynamics and orbit collisions
\item decompositions of generalized Fermat equations
\item additive combinatorial identities among $S$-units
\end{enumerate}

The gluing problem is one of the few routes that genuinely changes the unit of analysis from one triple to a network of equations.

\chapter{Applications to Major Diophantine Problems}

\section{Simultaneous Wieferich theorems}
The edge weight
\[
 w_p(q)=v_p(q^{p-1}-1)-1
\]
measures local degeneracy. A first family of theorems could bound the number of pairs $(p,q)$ with $w_p(q)\ge k$ in a declared box. A more relevant target would control simultaneous rows:
\[
 \#\left\{
 (P,Q):
 \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal L_{P,Q}\bmod p^k)
 \text{ is deficient}
 \right\}.
\]
The coherence lattice suggests studying exterior powers rather than entrywise defects alone.

\section{Exceptional \texorpdfstring{$abc$}{abc} by mechanism}
For a quality threshold $1+\delta$, define subclasses:

\begin{align*}
 \mathcal E_K(\delta)&=\{\text{large Kummer preload}\},\\
 \mathcal E_W(\delta)&=\{\text{large local logarithmic defect}\},\\
 \mathcal E_C(\delta)&=\{\text{large collective coherence}\},\\
 \mathcal E_A(\delta)&=\{\text{large automorphic congruence defect}\}.
\end{align*}

The exact factorization makes these definitions disjoint after sequential normalization. A classification theorem could prove separate counting bounds for each mechanism. The coherence class is expected to be the dominant and most difficult one.

\section{Generalized Fermat signatures}
For a signature $(p,q,r)$, local contact depths are multiplied by the corresponding exponents. The first computational filter should test whether one output-prime logarithmic row has a unit minor after exponent normalization. Such a minor would prevent the required deep cancellation.

The residual signatures could then be passed to a Frey curve, Frey abelian variety, or hypergeometric motive. This division of labor avoids attaching an expensive global motive to every local pattern.

\section{Beal under a coherence hypothesis}
Suppose
\[
 A^x+B^y=C^z
\]
is primitive. The input exponent vector contains blocks scaled by $x$ and $y$. At an output prime $p\mid C$, the logarithmic row must annihilate that vector to depth $zv_p(C)-1$.

A nondegenerate $2\times2$ minor involving one prime from $A$ and one from $B$ would bound the possible cancellation depth. This suggests a theorem of the form:

\begin{quote}
For fixed exponent signature, if one normalized cross-prime minor is a unit at a repeated output prime, then the primitive equation has no solution beyond an explicit height.
\end{quote}

This is not a proof of Beal, but it is a concrete family target.

\section{Hall and the dyadic-triadic interface}
For
\[
 x^3-y^2=k,
\]
the prime-contact systems of the square and cube interact through exactly the two exceptional primes of the depth-three Smith form. The dyadic and triadic corrections are not incidental. They are the primary channels in which the weight-three orbit lattice departs from a split $(\Z/M\Z)^3$ form.

A Hall-specific object should combine:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the $2$-adic contact filtration of $y^2$
\item the $3$-adic contact filtration of $x^3$
\item the fixed support of $k$
\item the depth-three polylogarithmic packet
\item the elliptic curve $Y^2=X^3-k$
\end{enumerate}

This is one of the most natural settings for the depth-three theory.

\section{Pillai with fixed residual support}
For fixed $k$, the equation
\[
 x^m-y^n=k
\]
has a fixed residual radical. As $x$ and $y$ grow, the two perfect-power contact packets must cancel against a bounded output support. This makes the problem closer to a regulator inequality than unrestricted $abc$.

An effective theorem under one nondegeneracy condition on the cross-prime logarithmic matrix appears more realistic than a full uniform Pillai theorem.

\section{Bernoulli--Witt direction}
The Bernoulli branch begins with the identity
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}j^k
 =
 \frac{B_{k+1}(m)-B_{k+1}}{k+1}.
\]
The equation with $m^k$ imposes strong congruences on Bernoulli values. A filtered prime-contact treatment should separate:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item denominator primes from the von Staudt--Clausen theorem
\item repeated congruence depth in the numerator
\item finite-difference packets across $m$
\item a separate dyadic branch
\item Witt carries in the Bernoulli polynomial evaluation
\end{enumerate}

The first objective in this direction is to define the object and prove exact local lift laws before formulating an Erd\H{o}s--Moser application.

\chapter{Bibliographic Context and Scope}

\section{Established ingredients}
The theory uses several classical or established ingredients.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Arithmetic derivatives and their relation to $abc$ have an existing literature.
\item Prony-type annihilation and finite-rate signal recovery motivate some signal language but are not part of the number-theory proofs.
\item The Frey curve, modularity, and local Tate uniformization are established arithmetic tools.
\item Atkin--Lehner involutions and Hecke congruence modules are standard automorphic objects.
\item Wiles defects compare cotangent and congruence data in suitable Hecke algebras.
\item Kummer theory gives the global contact class.
\item Fermat character decompositions and Jacobi sums are classical.
\item Polylogarithmic distribution and three-point functional identities are established analytic and motivic relations.
\item Witt vectors provide the canonical carry coordinates of prime-power contact.
\end{enumerate}

The mathematical contribution is the chain of exact recombinations, obstruction theorems, and the resulting coherence object.

\section{Claims that remain conditional}
The following are not proved:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the strong $abc$ conjecture
\item a uniform coherence conductor bound
\item a radical-scale adelic logarithmic determinant theorem
\item a filtered cusp-to-Hecke map with the required kernel bound
\item a sparse motivic quotient with radical-scale integral cost
\item a functorial gluing theorem
\item any full major-conjecture application beyond the exact reductions stated
\end{enumerate}

\section{Finite evidence}
Finite searches and corpora establish exact statements only for the declared input range. Correlations, histograms, maximum observed defects, and apparent template families are hypothesis generators. They are not asymptotic theorems.

\section{Structural obstruction theorems}
Several natural targets are ruled out by exact theorems. These limitations determine the necessary structure of any remaining proof.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The first-order search was solved exactly and shown to be circular as a route to a new $abc$ gain.
\item Pure shell splitting was shown to be synthesis-normalized.
\item The split persistence packet was shown to have the wrong type for a cyclic target.
\item Restoring carry extensions corrected the source to a filtered cyclic object.
\item The full plane Fermat bank carries a line-bundle factor of order $M$, so it does not yield a free intrinsic height saving.
\item A small complex period was shown not to control the integral lattice.
\item Abstract lattice geometry was shown unable to bound the coherence index.
\item Positive divisibility constructions were shown to have height at least $c/2$.
\item Linear Hecke refinement was shown to preserve mean contact depth.
\end{enumerate}

These are necessary constraints on any proof using the present objects.
