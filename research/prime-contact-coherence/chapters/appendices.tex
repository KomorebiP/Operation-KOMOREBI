\section{Arithmetic data}
Consider
\[
 a=3,
 \qquad
 b=125=5^3,
 \qquad
 c=128=2^7.
\]
The radical is
\[
 R=2\cdot3\cdot5=30.
\]
The quality is
\[
 q=\frac{\log128}{\log30}.
\]
The output contact modulus is
\[
 M=\frac{128}{2}=64.
\]
This example is especially useful because each sector consists of one prime, the radical divisor is attained sharply, the output has a long dyadic filtration, and every later object can be written explicitly.

\section{Prime derivative}
The additive row is
\[
 \lambda=(1,75,-448).
\]
The phase row is
\[
 \lambda^{\mathrm{ph}}=(1,25,-64).
\]
The coarse shortest transverse packet
\[
 x=(-2,6,1)
\]
satisfies
\[
 -2+450=448.
\]
The Wronskian is
\[
 W_x=1600=\frac{abc}{R}.
\]
The coarse height is six.

The phase packet
\[
 y=(-8,8,3)
\]
satisfies
\[
 D_y(3)=-8,
 \qquad
 D_y(125)=200,
 \qquad
 D_y(128)=192,
\]
and
\[
 -8+200=192.
\]
Its Wronskian is also $1600$. The phase height is eight. The phase inequality is sharp:
\[
 128
 =
 30\left(\frac13+\frac15\right)8.
\]
This illustrates that the exponent-free phase channel can sharpen the bound even when its packet norm is larger.

\section{Obstruction complex}
The joint exponent-free phase--Wronskian matrix has Smith form
\[
 \operatorname{diag}(1,1600).
\]
The additive lattice maps onto $1600\Z$ through the Wronskian. The primitive obstruction section exists algebraically. Its height remains the metric problem.

The stable real height is computed from
\[
 \sigma_a=\frac13,
 \qquad
 \sigma_b=\frac15,
 \qquad
 \sigma_c=\frac12.
\]
The smallest sector pair is
\[
 \sigma_a+\sigma_b=\frac8{15}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \overline H
 =
 \frac{128}{30(8/15)}
 =8.
\]
The phase packet has height eight, so the integral optimum reaches the real lower barrier exactly.

\section{Moving search}
The primitive obstruction phase is already optimal. The certified stopping bound contains no later coset capable of improving height eight because
\[
 k\overline H\ge8
\]
for every $k\ge1$. This example is therefore a clean calibration of the no-escape identity.

\section{Signal and Frey layers}
The prime-shell energy of $c$ is
\[
 \log128=7\log2,
\]
while the activated output bandwidth is
\[
 \log2.
\]
The output persistence energy is
\[
 6\log2=\log64.
\]

The marked Frey curve is
\[
 y^2=x(x-3)(x+125).
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
 16\cdot3^2\cdot125^2\cdot128^2.
\]
At the odd primes, the marked depths are two at $3$ and six at $5$. The dyadic depth requires the weighted branch.

The rational automorphic channel alone does not determine the marked dyadic depth of the displayed Frey representative. Any passage to an isogenous or twisted representative must therefore carry the differential scaling and the marked local $2$-adic data explicitly.

\section{Filtered contact and Witt packet}
The output contact complex is
\[
 [128\Z\hookrightarrow2\Z]
 \simeq
 [\Z\xrightarrow{64}\Z].
\]
Its cohomology is
\[
 \Jcal_c\cong\Z/64\Z.
\]
The associated graded is
\[
 (\F_2)^6.
\]
The six binary layers are joined by five nontrivial carry extensions. A cyclic target of order $64$ can carry the filtered source exactly, while no cyclic target can contain the split rank-six source injectively.

The local Witt coordinate is
\[
 W_6(\F_2)
 \cong
 \Z/64\Z.
\]
The weighted multiplicative dyadic coordinate is
\[
 (1+4\Z_2)/(1+2^8\Z_2),
\]
which has the same order.

\section{Kummer and Fermat packets}
The global Kummer map is
\[
 \kappa_{128}:\Z/64\Z\to H^1(\Q,\mu_{64}).
\]
Since
\[
 \gcd(64,7)=1,
\]
it is injective.

For the balanced ratio
\[
 \beta=-\frac{125}{3},
\]
the signed exponent vector on the input primes is
\[
 \nu=(-1,3).
\]
Its Kummer order modulo $64$ is full. Choosing $64$th roots gives a point on
\[
 X^{64}+Y^{64}=1.
\]
The coordinate height is
\[
 \frac{\log128}{64}.
\]
With the tensor-power metric induced from $\mathcal O(1)$, the full plane-model canonical scale is
\[
 \frac{61}{64}\log128.
\]
For an arbitrary compatible Weil-height normalization, this formula holds up to an additive $O(1)$ term. It records the line-bundle cost of retaining the complete Fermat curve.

\section{Polylogarithmic path}
Set
\[
 z=\frac3{128},
 \qquad
 1-z=\frac{125}{128},
 \qquad
 \beta=1-\frac1z=-\frac{125}{3}.
\]
The depth-three functional relation closes on these three arguments. At the output prime two,
\[
 v_2(\beta-1)
 =
 v_2\left(-\frac{128}{3}\right)
 =7.
\]
The unscaled principal logarithm has $2$-adic valuation seven. After division by four, the weighted principal coordinate occupies five filtration levels, while the separate sign packet supplies the first dyadic layer.

\section{Coherence lattice}
After removing the sign layer,
\[
 (\epsilon_2,K,W,C)=(2,1,1,32).
\]
The residual row is
\[
 g=(29,31)\pmod{32}.
\]
The lattice is
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{coh}}
 =
 \{(x_3,x_5):29x_3+31x_5\equiv0\pmod{32}\}.
\]
The input vector lies in the kernel because
\[
 29(-1)+31(3)=64.
\]
The full repeated output depth has now been transformed into one arithmetic cyclic character of order $32$, after the bounded sign layer has been separated.

\section{What this example teaches}
The example is simultaneously favorable and cautionary.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The radical divisor is sharp.
\item The phase packet reaches the continuous lower barrier.
\item The Kummer map is injective.
\item The local logarithmic defect is trivial.
\item The remaining depth is pure coherence.
\end{enumerate}

So even the cleanest example does not prove the needed conductor bound. It isolates the final problem in its purest form.

The modular degree of one associated minimal curve is a power of two in the stored example. This disproves the naive claim that the output persistence must divide one modular degree. The local marked depth and global eigenline separation are distinct channels.

\chapter{Collected Theorems and Dependencies}

\section{Purpose}
This chapter records the dependency structure of the exact results. It does not repeat every calculation. It shows which statements rely only on elementary integer algebra, which use local analytic identities, and which invoke external arithmetic theory.

\section{Elementary integer layer}
The following results are elementary consequences of valuations, Smith normal form, the Chinese remainder theorem, and exact lattice algebra.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The universal prime-packet differential obeys Leibniz.
\item The additive condition is one integer row.
\item The arithmetic Wronskian is one additional row.
\item The divisor $abc/R$ divides every additive Wronskian.
\item Sector compression reduces each prime block to one aggregate coordinate plus lift gauges.
\item The joint obstruction matrix has torsion invariant $abc/R$.
\item The additive lattice fits into
\[
 0\to L_0\to L\to\Z\to0.
\]
\item The sharp aggregate solutions form an affine integer line.
\item Each local lift reduces to centered CRT residues and one quotient-sum equation.
\item The first feasible interval intersection gives the exact coset height.
\item The continuous lower bound gives finite stopping in the obstruction multiple.
\item The filtered contact quotient is $R_c\Z/c\Z$.
\item The associated graded is the direct sum of residue-field layers.
\item The maximal image between two cyclic packets has order equal to their gcd.
\item The global Kummer kernel is the gcd of the modulus with the valuation vector.
\item The Fermat character projectors satisfy exact orthogonality and completeness.
\item The coherence lattice has index equal to the coherence quotient.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Finite Fourier and polyphase layer}
The valuation-word Smith theorem uses circulant eigenvalues. The determinant calculation is analytic in appearance but finite and exact. The only nontrivial invariant factor is the aggregate exponent because the nonconstant cyclotomic phases multiply to a unit.

The Atkin--Lehner Walsh bank is a finite product of commuting involutions. Its identities are polynomial consequences of $W_j^2=I$.

The Fermat projector bank and polylogarithmic distribution relation are finite-character orthogonality identities. The latter is first proved in the disk of convergence and then continued analytically.

\section{Formal deformation layer}
Formal smoothness of the shell deformation uses the primitive gradient. Once one coordinate has linear coefficient one, the formal implicit-function recursion is integral at every order. This proves extension existence. It does not bound coefficient growth.

The gauge-collapse statement is an algebraic factorization through aggregate sector products. It explains why internal phase curvature can be corrected formally.

\section{Local logarithmic layer}
For odd $p$, the identity
\[
 v_p(\log_p(1+u))=v_p(u)
\]
holds when $v_p(u)>0$. This yields the exact output notch depth. The coefficient formula
\[
 v_p(q^{p-1}-1)-1
\]
comes from the principal-unit logarithm of the input prime.

At $2$, the logarithm begins on $1+4\Z_2$. The separate sign bit and the shifted principal filtration must be retained. This is the source of the weighted dyadic normalization.

\section{External arithmetic layer}
The following contextual bridges use established external theory rather than proofs given in this paper.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Modularity associates a weight-two newform to a Frey curve.
\item Tate uniformization interprets multiplicative reduction through a local parameter.
\item Hecke cotangent spaces and Wiles defects compare congruence and deformation multiplicity.
\item Fermat Jacobians decompose through character-related factors and Jacobi sums.
\item Motivic, syntomic, and Deligne regulators provide candidate realizations for the depth-three path packet.
\end{enumerate}

No external theorem is treated as providing the missing coherence-conductor estimate.

\section{Conditional implications}
Every conditional $abc$ theorem in this paper has the same logical shape.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Construct a finite source of order comparable to $c/R_c$.
\item Map it into a global target with kernel at most $R^\eps$.
\item Bound the target size by $R_aR_bR^\eps$.
\item Multiply by $R_c$ and absorb logarithmic factors.
\end{enumerate}

The differences among the conditional theorems lie in the source and target objects. The latest endpoint uses the coherence character or an integral adelic regulator attached to it.

\chapter{Exact Computational Procedures}

\section{Exact arithmetic contract}
All factorizations, radicals, contact moduli, obstruction rows, CRT residues, lattice indices, and stopping bounds are computed with arbitrary-precision integers. Floating point is never used to decide feasibility, equality, divisibility, or search termination.

A public arithmetic function must promote integer-like inputs before multiplication. This prevents silent overflow when a NumPy scalar enters an otherwise exact computation.

Every display ratio should also retain an exact rational representation when possible. Decimal output is for plotting and human comparison only.

\section{Factorization and data normalization}
For each triple, the canonical record contains
\[
 (a,b,c),
 \quad
 \{v_p(a)\},
 \quad
 \{v_p(b)\},
 \quad
 \{v_p(c)\},
 \quad
 R,
 \quad
 q(a,b,c).
\]
Derived objects must be computed from this record. No displayed factorization is accepted as input without verification.

The triple is normalized by:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item checking $a+b=c$
\item checking $\gcd(a,b)=1$
\item ordering $a\le b$ when a search convention requires it
\item computing radicals from exact prime sets
\item separating dyadic and odd-adic branches
\end{enumerate}

\section{Exact moving-search pseudocode}
For one triple:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Compute $r_u$, $P_u$, $R$, $Q$, and the exact stable height $\overline H$.
\item Compute the primitive obstruction height $H(1)$ by binary search on the three interval intersections.
\item Set
\[
 K_{\max}
 =
 \left\lfloor\frac{H(1)-1}{\overline H}\right\rfloor.
\]
\item For $2\le k\le K_{\max}$, skip a coset when $k\overline H$ already reaches the current best height.
\item Build centered CRT phase residues for the three sectors.
\item Compute the coarse affine line.
\item Binary-search the smallest $H$ whose three admissible $n$-intervals meet.
\item Construct one witness, verify additivity and obstruction multiple, and update the stopping bound.
\item Store the preceding empty radius as the no-smaller-box certificate.
\end{enumerate}

This is an exact algorithm after factorization. It is much faster than a generic mixed-integer formulation because the geometry has been compressed before search.

\section{Coherence-lattice construction}
The coherence lattice is constructed as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Form the signed input exponent vector $\nu$.
\item Remove the common Kummer preload $K$.
\item For every output prime, construct the finite logarithmic row.
\item Divide each row by its common coefficient depth to obtain the local defect $W_p$.
\item Remove the dyadic sign layer separately.
\item Combine primitive residual rows by CRT.
\item Verify that the normalized exponent vector lies in the kernel.
\item Compute the exact quotient order $C$ and the factorization
\[
 M=\epsilon_2KWC.
\]
\end{enumerate}

The sequential order prevents one depth layer from being counted twice.

\section{Certificate philosophy}
Every finite claim should be accompanied by one of four certificate types.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.68\textwidth}}
\toprule
Certificate & Content\\
\midrule
Algebraic & exact identity, factorization, Smith form, or divisibility check\\
Feasibility & explicit witness satisfying all integer constraints\\
Infeasibility & empty interval, dual bound, or no-smaller-box certificate\\
Numerical & high-precision residual with a declared tolerance and independently computed input\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

A finite data set is evidence about the declared range. It is not an asymptotic theorem.

\chapter{Prime-Theory Applications}

\section{Simultaneous Wieferich networks}
Define a directed prime network by the edge depth
\[
 w_p(q)=v_p(q^{p-1}-1)-1
\]
for odd $p$, with the weighted dyadic analogue. An additive relation selects a finite bipartite subnetwork from input primes to output primes.

Three mechanisms can create deep output contact:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item exceptional individual edges
\item degenerate minors of the logarithmic matrix
\item collective cancellation against the signed exponent vector
\end{enumerate}

A first unconditional objective is to prove density or power-saving bounds for networks exhibiting large simultaneous defects. This is closer to the exact object than a direct treatment of arbitrary prime gaps.

\section{Exceptional \texorpdfstring{$abc$}{abc} classification}
A structural classifier should attach to every high-quality triple the tuple
\[
 (\epsilon_2,K,W,C)
\]
and additional minor data from the cross-prime logarithm matrix. A high-quality exceptional set may then be partitioned into:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Kummer-preloaded families
\item generalized Wieferich families
\item low-rank regulator families
\item collective-coherence families
\item automorphic-congruence families
\end{enumerate}

Even without a uniform $abc$ proof, a theorem showing that one or more mechanisms occur in a density-zero family would be meaningful progress.

\section{Generalized Fermat}
For
\[
 x^p+y^q=z^r,
\]
attach filtered contact packets to the three terms. The exponents multiply the local valuation depths. Balanced Kummer ratios and cross-prime logarithmic filters can eliminate many local patterns before a Frey or hypergeometric motive is attached.

A realistic first target is a family theorem: under a nondegenerate logarithmic-minor condition, prove that no primitive solution exists outside a finite computable set. The modular or hypergeometric method would then handle the exceptional residual signatures.

\section{Beal-type equations}
A primitive counterexample to
\[
 A^x+B^y=C^z,
 \qquad x,y,z>2,
\]
would contain three deep contact packets on disjoint prime supports. This is an especially strong setting for prime-contact filtering.

A concrete intermediate theorem is:

\begin{quote}
If one repeated output prime gives a unit minor in the normalized cross-prime logarithm matrix, then no primitive Beal solution exists in the declared exponent family.
\end{quote}

The exact formulation depends on the rank and local coefficient prime.

\section{Pillai and Hall}
For
\[
 x^m-y^n=k,
\]
the residual support is fixed by $k$. The two perfect-power packets must cancel to high precision while producing a small fixed output. This is a natural two-sector coherence problem.

Hall's equation
\[
 x^3-y^2=k
\]
is particularly attractive because the dyadic and triadic branches are built into the exponents. The polylogarithmic weight-three resonance may be relevant, but a rigorous connection has not yet been established.

\section{Bernoulli--Witt and Erd\H{o}s--Moser}
Faulhaber's formula rewrites
\[
 1^k+\cdots+(m-1)^k=m^k
\]
as a Bernoulli-polynomial identity. The denominator primes of Bernoulli numbers, the finite-difference divisibility, and the separate $2$-adic and odd-adic lift structures suggest a different contact object:
\[
 \boxed{
 \text{Bernoulli contact crystal}
 +
 \text{denominator prime packet}
 +
 \text{finite-difference coherence lattice}.
 }
\]
This branch should be developed independently before it is merged with the logarithmic prime-contact theory.

\section{Why some famous prime problems are later targets}
Twin primes, Goldbach, and the Riemann hypothesis are dominated by prime support rather than repeated prime depth. The calculus is strongest when exponents exceed one and carry filtrations matter. These problems may eventually connect through a broader trace or spectral formalism, but they are not the most credible immediate applications.

\chapter{Unresolved Arithmetic Problems}

\section{Combined logarithmic--coherence conductor}
For the actual rows constructed from the input primes and output-prime logarithms, prove
\[
 WC\le C_\eps R_aR_bR^\eps.
\]
The theorem must control both entrywise logarithmic degeneration and collective coherence and must exploit the addition law. On the subfamily $W=1$, it reduces to a pure bound for $C$. The corresponding statement is false for arbitrary primitive congruence rows.

\section{Adelic minor nondegeneracy}
Find an exterior-power or regulator formulation of the cross-prime logarithm matrix whose primitive nonzero minor has denominator bounded by radical scale. This may be stronger than the one-row coherence formulation and may handle several independent additive relations simultaneously.

\section{Filtered local-global transfer}
Construct the cusp-contact to Selmer to Hecke chain map with Bockstein compatibility. Prove both a small source kernel and a conductor-scale target bound.

\section{Sparse motivic quotient}
Construct a bounded-complexity Jacobi, polylogarithmic, syntomic, or automorphic quotient retaining the balanced Kummer class. Control finite denominator, field degree, conductor, and all archimedean conjugates.

\section{Functorial gluing}
If two sparse relations share a term, construct the fiber product or pushout of their contact, Kummer, and coherence objects. Prove a conservation or cancellation law stronger than applying the two equations separately.

\section{First decisive intermediate results}
Any of the following would materially advance the theory:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a power-saving bound for the exceptional set of large coherence quotients
\item a new density theorem for simultaneous generalized Wieferich defects
\item a family of generalized Fermat signatures eliminated by contact filtering
\item an effective Pillai or Hall theorem under one natural regulator condition
\item a nontrivial kernel bound for a canonical filtered cusp-to-Hecke map
\item a sparse motivic quotient with provable radical-scale conductor
\end{enumerate}
