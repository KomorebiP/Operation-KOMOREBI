\chapter{Exact Computational Methods}

\section{Why exact arithmetic became part of the mathematics}
The exact constructions depend on integer indices, Smith factors, radicals, valuation depths, and exact congruence classes. A fixed-width overflow does not merely perturb a decimal display. It can change the lattice, the obstruction modulus, and the stopping certificate.

Exact arithmetic requires the rule:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Every public arithmetic input is promoted to an arbitrary-precision integer before multiplication.}
}
\]
Display values may be converted to floating point only after the exact rational or integer quantity has been stored.

Ratios such as
\[
\frac{\Phi}{\overline H},
\qquad
\frac{\Phi}{k\overline H},
\qquad
\frac{H(1)}{\Phi}
\]
are not probabilities. They are constrained to be at least one by their definitions. Values above one are meaningful integrality overheads or improvement factors.

\section{The finite-certificate pattern}
The exact optimization routines return two outputs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a witness realizing the claimed optimum
\item a certificate that no smaller witness exists
\end{enumerate}
Examples include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item exact mixed-integer and CRT interval solutions for transverse packets
\item Smith normal forms and determinantal divisors
\item finite stopping bounds for the obstruction multiple $k$
\item exact Kummer kernel formulas checked against brute force on small cases
\item exact reconstruction of contact orders and filtered defects
\item symbolic verification of polynomial and Witt carry identities
\end{itemize}

This witness-plus-exclusion pattern is the required standard for related searches.

\section{Exact moving search}
The first-order search became exact after three reductions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The prime coordinates in each sector were compressed to one aggregate integer.
\item A fixed obstruction multiple selected a finite polyphase residue state.
\item The remaining search became a one-dimensional balance parameter constrained by exact integer intervals.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting algorithm did not rely on a guessed cutoff. The exact real lower bound ruled out all later obstruction cosets once they could no longer beat the incumbent.

The same principle applies more broadly:

\begin{quote}
Whenever a large integer search is generated by local congruence packets and one global balance relation, search first for an exact phase/coarse factorization and a proof-producing stopping rule.
\end{quote}

\section{The 361-triple finite data set}
The calculations use a finite set of 361 distinct primitive triples assembled from:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item all selected $abc$ hits through $10^4$
\item a broader quality-filtered scan through $10^5$
\item a high-quality reference set
\end{itemize}
The data set was used to verify exact identities, not to infer an asymptotic distribution.

Representative finite findings include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the Kummer kernel was one in most rows
\item local generalized Wieferich defects were rare
\item the coherence factor was nontrivial in most difficult rows
\item the relation vector was often much shorter than a random relation at the same lattice index
\item abstract lattice geometry still could not explain the index
\end{itemize}

\section{Limits of finite evidence}
A high finite correlation between quality and a coherence statistic does not prove a density theorem. A small maximum kernel in the finite data set does not prove a uniform bound better than the exact theorem. A successful sampled Hecke projector is not automatically the complete congruence exponent. Every table should be read with its declared scope.

\section{Standards for computational evidence}
A computational arithmetic argument should contain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item an exact object definition
\item a precise statement of the theorem being tested
\item arbitrary-precision arithmetic at the core
\item negative controls and out-of-class examples
\item a complete description of the input data
\item witness and no-smaller-witness certificates where optimization is involved
\item a separation between exact identities, finite evidence, and asymptotic conjectures
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Applications in Prime Theory}

\section{The cross-prime resonance network}
The most direct generalization is a directed network on the prime numbers. For distinct primes $q$ and $p$, attach the depth
\[
\boxed{
w_p(q)=v_p(q^{p-1}-1)-1
}
\]
for odd $p$, together with the weighted dyadic analogue. The edge
\[
q\longrightarrow p
\]
measures how deeply the input prime $q$ lies in the principal-unit filtration at the output prime $p$.

A sparse additive relation selects a finite bipartite subnetwork
\[
\{q:q\mid\text{input terms}\}
\longrightarrow
\{p:p\mid\text{repeated output terms}\}.
\]
If $p^e$ divides an output term exactly, the signed input-exponent signal must cancel through the $p$-row to the required depth after the Kummer and local-image layers have been removed.

Deep output can arise through three distinct mechanisms.

\subsection{Individual resonance}
One or more edges have exceptional depth. This is generalized Wieferich behavior.

\subsection{Minor degeneration}
A determinant of several logarithmic rows or columns has exceptional divisibility. This is a regulator-type failure of transversality.

\subsection{Collective coherence}
The entries are individually ordinary, but the specific signed exponent packet creates a deep simultaneous cancellation. This is the genuinely new coherence mechanism.

These mechanisms should not be merged prematurely. Their frequencies, local signatures, and global consequences are different.

\section{Simultaneous Wieferich and regulator theory}
This is the closest immediate field because the cross-prime logarithmic matrix is already the main object. For finite sets of primes $P$ and $Q$, define
\[
\Lcal_{P,Q}
=
\left(
\ell_p(q)
\right)_{p\in P,q\in Q}.
\]
Natural theorem targets include:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a rank criterion for the matrix modulo prescribed prime powers
\item an exact decomposition of depth into entry defects, minor defects, and relation-specific cancellation
\item a density-zero or power-saving theorem for simultaneous deep degeneration
\item a regulator lower bound that is uniform over sparse prime supports
\item an effective classification of low-rank cross-prime networks
\end{enumerate}

A useful first theorem would state that every depth above a threshold belongs to a union of finitely many explicitly described entry and minor degenerations, together with a residual collective-coherence class. That would turn the new network into a classification tool rather than only a visualization.

\section{Exceptional abc classification}
The full uniform inequality may be approached through the exceptional set. A structural target is:
\[
q(a,b,c)>1+\delta
\]
implies at least one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a large generalized Wieferich defect $W$
\item a highly divisible logarithmic minor
\item an unusually short relation in the normalized coherence lattice
\item an exceptional automorphic congruence or regulator degeneration
\end{enumerate}

This would classify high-quality triples by mechanism. A counting theorem could then estimate each mechanism separately. The finite data already distinguish Kummer preload, local logarithmic degeneration, and collective coherence exactly. It does not yet prove that the collective class is sparse.

\section{Generalized Fermat and Beal}
Consider
\[
x^p+y^q=z^r.
\]
Every term carries repeated depth by construction. The prime-contact calculus attaches:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item filtered contact crystals for the three powered terms
\item balanced Kummer ratios
\item cross-prime logarithmic filter matrices
\item one or more coherence lattices
\item an equation-specific Frey curve, higher Frey variety, or hypergeometric motive when available
\end{itemize}

The calculus can serve as a pre-modular filter. It may eliminate valuation patterns, identify forced local signatures, or isolate a finite exceptional family before an automorphic object is attached.

For the Beal equation
\[
A^x+B^y=C^z,
\qquad
x,y,z>2,
\]
a primitive counterexample would require three deep contact systems with disjoint support. A concrete intermediate theorem is:

\begin{quote}
Prove the expected common-prime conclusion for every family in which one repeated output prime has a unit maximal logarithmic minor after Kummer normalization.
\end{quote}

The condition is natural in the calculus and strong enough to admit exact finite testing.

\section{Pillai and fixed perfect-power gaps}
For
\[
x^m-y^n=k,
\qquad
k\ne0\text{ fixed},
\]
two deep contact packets collide through a residual channel whose prime support is fixed. This is more rigid than unrestricted $abc$ because the output bandwidth is prescribed by $\rad(k)$.

The coherence matrix has input columns from the prime factors of $x$ and $y$, while the output rows are controlled by the prime factors of the perfect-power terms and of $k$. A first effective theorem should impose a nondegenerate logarithmic-minor condition and derive explicit bounds for $x$, $y$, $m$, or $n$ in terms of $k$.

The calculus suggests a separation into:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item local preloaded depth
\item individual Wieferich depth
\item collective cancellation depth
\item fixed residual bandwidth from $k$
\end{itemize}
This decomposition is available before invoking a general linear-form theorem.

\section{Hall's square-cube gap}
Hall's problem concerns
\[
x^3-y^2=k.
\]
The exponents two and three make the dyadic and triadic branches intrinsic. The phase/coarse Smith calculations and the later depth-three polylogarithmic resonance suggest a natural two-three contact object.

A serious Hall branch should begin by defining:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{the square contact crystal of }y^2,\\
&\text{the cube contact crystal of }x^3,\\
&\text{their joint dyadic-triadic coherence lattice},\\
&\text{the elliptic curve and its marked integral packet}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The weight-three resonance of the path construction is suggestive but not yet a theorem about Hall's conjecture. The first goal is a precise correspondence between the cube-side contact determinant and the elliptic regulator.

\section{A Bernoulli--Witt branch for Erd\H{o}s--Moser}
The equation
\[
1^k+2^k+\cdots+(m-1)^k=m^k
\]
is controlled by Faulhaber and Bernoulli polynomials. The broader seed calculus already contains Euler and Bernoulli families, residue packets, finite-difference divisibility, and dyadic/odd-adic lifting.

A dedicated object should combine:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Bernoulli denominator support}
+
\text{Witt depth}
+
\text{finite-difference coherence}.
}
\]
This branch should remain separate from the logarithmic prime network until an exact transfer between the Bernoulli and contact packets is proved.

\section{Why prime gaps, Goldbach, and the Riemann hypothesis are not first targets}
The present machinery is strongest when repeated prime depth is substantial. In prime gaps, twin primes, Goldbach representations, and most direct formulations of the Riemann hypothesis, the central data are squarefree or spectral rather than repeated-depth contact packets.

This does not make those topics inaccessible. It means a new bridge is required.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Prime gaps need a translation from additive distribution to a filtered coherence statistic that does not vanish when every exponent is one.
\item Goldbach needs a density or transfer theorem across many representations rather than one deep output packet.
\item The Riemann hypothesis needs a rigorously connected trace formula, positive form, or self-adjoint operator. The present coherence lattice does not yet supply one.
\end{itemize}

These should be later programs rather than immediate extrapolations.

\chapter{Central Open Theorems}

\section{The abc endpoint}
For the output contact modulus
\[
M=\frac{c}{R_c},
\]
the final exact factorization is
\[
\boxed{
M=\epsilon_2KWC.
}
\]
The dyadic sign factor $\epsilon_2$ is bounded. The Kummer factor $K$ is at most logarithmic. The local logarithmic defect $W$ and the coherence quotient $C$ contain the remaining arithmetic depth.

Strong $abc$ would follow from
\[
\boxed{
WC
\le
C_\eps R_aR_bR^\eps.
}
\]
The equation is exact as a target because
\[
c=R_c\epsilon_2KWC.
\]
No estimate of this strength is proved.

\section{Open theorem A: combined logarithmic--coherence conductor}
Let
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{coh}}:\Z^{S_{ab}}\longrightarrow\Z/C\Z
\]
be the normalized coherence character constructed from the actual input primes and output-prime logarithmic filters.

\begin{conjecture}[Combined logarithmic--coherence bound]
For every $\eps>0$,
\[
\boxed{
WC
\le
C_\eps R_aR_bR^\eps.
}
\]
\end{conjecture}

A proof must control both entrywise logarithmic degeneration and collective coherence, using the arithmetic origin of the rows and the relation $a+b=c$. Abstract lattice geometry cannot imply it. On the subfamily $W=1$, this reduces to the pure coherence-conductor estimate
\[
 C\le C_\eps R_aR_bR^\eps.
\]
A counterexample to the combined bound would require a family in which the local defect, the collective coherence, or their product exceeds every radical-scale estimate.

\section{Open theorem B: simultaneous logarithmic determinant}
A stronger matrix formulation seeks a lower bound for an appropriate nonzero minor of the cross-prime logarithm matrix after normalizing entrywise defects.

\begin{conjecture}[Adelic minor nondegeneracy]
For every $\eps>0$, a suitable primitive minor or exterior-power regulator of the normalized matrix satisfies an adelic lower bound whose denominator is at most
\[
C_\eps R_aR_bR^\eps.
\]
\end{conjecture}

This would force the coherence quotient to remain at radical scale after entrywise normalization. To imply the combined endpoint, it must be paired with a bound for the removed local factor $W$. The correct minor size may depend on the input support and on the rank of the signed exponent relations.

\section{Open theorem C: filtered local-global transfer}
The filtered contact construction proposes a map
\[
\mathbf J_c
\longrightarrow
\mathbf H_f
\]
from the cusp-contact complex into a Hecke, Selmer, or deformation complex.

The required theorem has two parts:
\[
\abs{\Ker H^0}\le R^\eps
\]
and
\[
\abs{H^0(\mathbf H_f)}\le R_aR_bR^\eps.
\]
The first is depth occupancy. The second is integral automorphic size. Existing modularity and $R=\mathbb T$ theorems do not by themselves construct this filtered map or give these bounds.

\section{Open theorem D: sparse motivic or Jacobi quotient}
The Kummer--Fermat and polylogarithmic constructions produce a full character bank and then a fixed-depth path quotient. The full bank has a line-bundle and synthesis cost of order $M$. The fixed complex packet is small but has an uncontrolled integral lattice.

The target is a sparse quotient that simultaneously retains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the Kummer contact class
\item the additive three-point relation
\item a bounded number of character or motivic channels
\item radical-scale conductor and field degree
\item radical-scale integral denominator and period norm
\end{itemize}

The object may be Jacobi, polylogarithmic, syntomic, or automorphic. No candidate currently satisfies all five conditions.

\section{Open theorem E: functorial gluing}
A general prime-contact calculus should assign objects not only to individual relations but also to diagrams of relations. If
\[
A+B=C
\]
and
\[
C+D=E,
\]
one should understand how the contact, Kummer, and coherence objects glue across the shared term $C$.

A functorial gluing theorem could reveal conservation or cancellation laws invisible in one isolated equation. It is also necessary for applications to recurrence sequences, additive chains, and arithmetic dynamics.

A decisive next result need not prove a famous conjecture immediately. Any one of the following would establish a substantial new theorem.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]

The most direct next problems are:

\section{Final synthesis}
The initial derivative formulation and the final coherence formulation isolate different aspects of the same arithmetic relation. The decisive remaining question is an arithmetic bound on the coherence character generated by simultaneous logarithms of the actual input primes.

The change is substantial.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{first-order formulation: }&\text{find a short point in a large lattice},\\
\text{arithmetic formulation: }&\text{bound the conductor of a specific arithmetic character}\\
&\text{whose kernel contains the additive exponent packet}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The first problem was generic enough to be circular. The second contains the prime identities, local depth, dyadic and odd-adic lifts, Kummer normalization, and addition law simultaneously.

The conductor bound remains open. It is the central arithmetic object for the prime-theory applications developed here.
