# Source crosswalk

This manuscript is a focused synthesis and extension of several earlier Komorebi branches. It does not attempt to reproduce every result from those sources.

## 1. Nonlinear Transfer Operad

### Inherited foundation

1. Packet resolved multilinear tensors
2. Directed interactions with several inputs
3. Exact multilinear reconstruction
4. Rooted tree and Faà di Bruno composition
5. Recursive refinement
6. Named residuals for omitted hyperedges
7. Hilbert Schmidt interaction energy and pruning

### Role in the current paper

1. Interaction tensors are placed under a surjective coarse map.
2. An exact quotient criterion is stated for every interaction order.
3. Constancy of coefficients across packet fibers is proved equivalent to descent.
4. Interaction defects are connected to certificates for posterior error.

## 2. Projector Resolved Seed Calculus VI

### Inherited foundation

1. Transfer semigroups and refinement intertwining
2. Markov lumpability
3. Conditional expectation as packet projection
4. Bayesian normalization
5. Hidden Markov filtering
6. Evidence factorization
7. Finite approximation estimates

### Role in the current paper

1. Unary lumpability becomes the first order case of a theory for interactions of every arity.
2. Bayesian refinement is combined with nonlinear quotient flows.
3. Complete filtering cocycles are treated in one commutative ladder.
4. Dynamical and observational defects are propagated together.

## 3. Combined Stochastic, Bayesian, and Fractional Transfer Block Calculus

### Inherited conceptual bridge

1. Uncertainty and memory as transfer laws compatible with packets
2. Positive evidence routing
3. Filtering as alternating prediction and normalization

### Current boundary

Stochastic Itô contractions and fractional memory are discussed but are not developed as the main theorem system. They remain natural subjects for Papers 2 and 3.

## 4. Proof Path Theory

### Inherited methodology

1. Typed theorem and evidence records
2. Separation of proof, exact finite computation, numerical evidence, and conjecture
3. Machine readable certificate ledgers
4. Explicit claim boundaries

### Use in the current paper

Theorem identifiers and computational checks are linked through a reproducible ledger. The proof hypergraph is kept distinct from the dynamical interaction hypergraph.

## 5. RRCT and Bayesian projector models

### Possible application role

1. Uncertain activation of packet edges
2. Adaptive refinement
3. Dense fallback triggered by posterior uncertainty
4. State aggregation and reconstruction of a self model

These implementations are not used as evidence for the finite quotient theorems. They remain candidates for a later applications paper.
