# Principal-seven norm transport and a second-action rigidity theorem

**Komorebi H12, v0.12.0. Base: Fourier Flattening v0.11.0.**

## Status and target

The original equality U_Gamma = U_alg is NOT proved. No relative gamma/model
scalar on the real three-torus has yet been constructed, and its required
principal-norm compatibility is not asserted here.

This block makes three concrete advances. It constructs the seven-point
refinement of the ACTUAL gamma lattice, evaluates its product by an exact
three-factor gamma identity, and proves that a second, expanding principal-norm
relation would force every smooth Fourier-orbit obstruction to vanish. The
relative phase only needs that relation for its conjugation-asymmetric part.

A tempting stronger claim is refuted: the raw seven-point gamma product is NOT
F(beta*z). Their orders at z=tau are four and zero. The precise correction is
retained rather than suppressed as a putative root of unity. The abstract
compact comparison model pulls back to a trivial gerbe under the same principal
map, but this does not identify its section with the gamma section.

Thirty-four new Std-only Lean theorem constants are freshly compiled and
axiom-audited. They certify actual integer matrices, a cubic norm invariant,
finite weighted inversion, Fourier trace residues, and model pullback carries.
The analytic gamma identity, smooth/global-holomorphic convergence, and bundle
interpretation are ordinary proofs with explicitly stated inputs, NOT Lean
axioms. No gamma-arithmetic power identity or new interval certificate is claimed.

## 1. The principal map is not an auxiliary guessed symmetry

Retain beta^3=7 with the fixed lower complex embedding, epsilon=2-beta, and

    tau=(beta^2+2beta)/15, t=-tau, s=-(beta+2)/15,
    p_row=(tau,s,1),
    M=[0,1,0; -4,2,15; -1,0,4].

The inherited relation is p_row*M=epsilon*p_row. Consequently

    N=2I-M=[2,-1,0; 4,0,-15; 1,0,-2]

satisfies p_row*N=beta*p_row. Direct integer identities give

    det N=7, N^3=7I, NM=MN.                              (1)

On X=C^3/Z^3 and on its real torus T^3, N is a degree-seven covering. On the
compact CM threefold C^3/Z[omega]^3 its degree is 49. These two degrees refer
to different lattices; they must not be interchanged.

Put v=(1,2,4)^T. The integer identities

    Nv=7(0,-8,-1)^T,
    Mv=2v+7(0,8,1)^T

show that the kernel of N on X is generated by v/7, with seven elements.
There are exactly seven because det N=7 and the displayed point has order seven.
Thus the same vector used in the previous CM model occurs in the actual gamma
lattice refinement, not just in its finite model.

Define

    delta7=(tau+2s+4)/7=(-t+2s+4)/7.                       (2)

Then

    beta*delta7=-8s-1,
    beta/3=1/3-5s-1.                                     (3)

In lattice terms L=Z tau+Z s+Z and

    beta^(-1)L=L+Z delta7.

Indeed beta delta7 is in L, the coset has exact order seven, and both
superlattices have index seven over L. The conductor point e2/3 is fixed modulo
Z^3 by both M and N, with carries (0,5,1) and (0,-5,-1), respectively.

For reference, rho=M^(-1) at the level of the period row acts on the seven
labels by k -> 4k modulo seven. Writing 4k=r+7m, 0<=r<7,

    rho(1/3+k delta7)
      =1/3+r delta7+(5+8k+m)tau+(-k+2m)s+(1+4m).          (4)

This is an actual representative identity; it is not permission to omit the
theta factors in transporting gamma values.

## 2. Exact gamma norm on these seven points

Use the BCG additive elliptic gamma convention [1]. For Im(a),Im(b)>0 set

    S_5(z;a,b)=Gamma(z;a,b)^5/Gamma(5z;5a,5b),
    F_{t,s}(z)=1/S_5(z+t;t,s).

The second expression is precisely Gamma(z;-t,s)^5 divided by its fifth-scale
denominator in the inherited continuation convention. No new gamma normalization
is introduced. At our cubic parameters delta7 also has positive imaginary part.

### Theorem 2.1 (actual seven-point product)

If t,s,d are in the upper half-plane and t+7d=2s+4, then, as a meromorphic
identity in z,

    product_(k=0)^6 F_{t,s}(z+kd)
      = S_5(z;s,d) / [S_5(z;t,d) S_5(z+s;t,d)].            (5)

In particular (5) applies with d=delta7 from (2).

**Proof.** Write P=exp(2pi i t), Q=exp(2pi i s), D=exp(2pi i d), and
Z=exp(2pi i z). The parameter equation is P D^7=Q^2. In a common convergence
region the logarithms are

    log S_5(z;a,b)=5 sum_(n>=1,5∤n)
          (Z^n-(AB/Z)^n)/[n(1-A^n)(1-B^n)],

    log F_{t,s}(z)=-5 sum_(n>=1,5∤n)
          (P^n Z^n-Q^n Z^(-n))/[n(1-P^n)(1-Q^n)].

At each surviving frequency suppress n in the notation. Summing the seven
shifts multiplies the positive and negative coefficients by sum D^k and
sum D^(-k), respectively. The two rational identities are

    P sum_(k=0)^6 D^k /[(1-P)(1-Q)]
      =(1+Q)/[(1-P)(1-D)]-1/[(1-Q)(1-D)],

    Q sum_(k=0)^6 D^(-k)/[(1-P)(1-Q)]
      =D[-Q/(1-Q)+P(1+1/Q)/(1-P)]/(1-D).                  (6)

They follow from P D^7=Q^2 and

    P-Q^2=(1+Q)(1-Q)-(1-P)
         =P(1+Q)(1-Q)-Q^2(1-P).

These match the logarithm of the right side of (5) exactly. A common convergence
region is nonempty: take Im(s)>6 Im(d) and 0<Im(z)<Im(s)-6 Im(d). Then
Im(t)=2Im(s)-7Im(d)>0 and every series used above converges absolutely. Exponentiate
there. The positive-parameter cone Im(d)>0, 2Im(s)>7Im(d) is connected; meromorphic
continuation in parameters and z gives the identity throughout it. The integral
real shift 4 creates no extra exponential because exp(2pi i*4)=1. QED.

### The raw principal-norm guess is false

Define the exact correction

    R_7(z)=S_5(z;s,delta7)/
      [S_5(z;t,delta7) S_5(z+s;t,delta7) F_{t,s}(beta z)].   (7)

It is not constant. At z=tau=-t, F(z) has a zero of order four. The six other
factors F(z+k delta7), 1<=k<=6, are finite and nonzero there: the t coefficient
of k delta7 is -k/7, which is not an integer, and the corresponding fifth-scale
coefficients also cannot be integral. Rational independence of 1,t,s excludes
any alternative representation in a gamma zero/pole cone.

On the other hand

    beta*tau=2tau+4s+1=-2t+4s+1.

The positive-period gamma argument in F(beta*tau) is -t+4s+1. Its period
coefficients (-1,4) have mixed signs, so it belongs neither to the zero cone
(j+1,k+1) nor to the pole cone (-j,-k), with j,k>=0. The same is true in the
fifth-scale denominator. Thus F(beta*tau) is regular and nonzero. Therefore

    ord_(z=tau) R_7=4.                                   (8)

The naive equation product F(z+k delta7)=F(beta z) is exactly refuted, not
merely numerically disfavored. A scalar root of unity cannot repair it.

Numerically, at z=1/3 the ratio R_7 is approximately

    642695604.46328845143 - 890320148.2754176285 i.

This is only an illustration; (8) is the mathematical obstruction. It does not
refute principal-norm compatibility AFTER the genuine relative comparison and
its correction terms are constructed.

## 3. The dual lattice and its norm invariant

Put A=M^T and B=N^T=2I-A. The degree-seven character functional is

    ell(k)=k0+2k1+4k2.

Exactly,

    k-ell(k)e0=B(-k1,k2,-8k2),
    B(0,-2,15)=7e0.

Hence

    k in B Z^3 iff ell(k)=0 mod7.                         (9)

If ell(k)=7r, the explicit inverse image is

    B^(-1)k=(-k1,k2-2r,15r-8k2).

Also

    ell(Ak)=2ell(k)+7(8k1+k2),
    (Ak)2=k2 mod3.                                       (10)

A useful exact orbit separator is the integral cubic

    V(k)=det[k,Ak,A^2k]
       =15x^3+90x^2y+12x^2z+180xy^2+69xyz+6xz^2
         +15y^3+48y^2z+12yz^2+z^3.                       (11)

It satisfies

    V(Ak)=V(k), V(Bk)=7V(k), V(e2)=1.                     (12)

In fact V(k) is nonzero for any nonzero rational k: otherwise a polynomial of
degree at most two would annihilate k under A. Its characteristic polynomial
(X-2)^3+7 is irreducible by translating the Eisenstein polynomial X^3+7;
Bezout with that polynomial would then give k=0. Consequently B sends each
nonzero A-orbit to an orbit with a distinct cubic invariant. This is not a
claim that V alone classifies all orbits.

## 4. A second-action norm identity kills all smooth orbit obstructions

For functions on T^3, define

    (S_N h)(v)=sum_(kappa in ker N) h(v+kappa),
    (C_N h)(v)=h(Nv),
    (L_N h)(v)=sum_(Nw=v) h(w),
    delta_M h=h-h composed with M.

The operator L_N is the UNNORMALIZED transfer (sum over seven preimages).
The kernel in S_N has the seven real representatives j(1,2,4)/7.
For h=sum c_k exp(2pi i k dot v),

    S_N h=sum_(k in BZ^3) 7c_k e_k,
    L_N h=sum_k 7c_(Bk)e_k,
    S_N h=(L_N h) composed with N,
    L_N C_N h=7h.                                        (13)

Since M and N commute and M permutes ker N, these operators commute with
composition by M in the required identities.

### Theorem 4.1 (principal-norm rigidity)

Let h,r be smooth mean-zero complex-valued functions on T^3. Suppose the exact
functional relation

    S_N h-h composed with N=delta_M r                      (14)

holds. Then h=delta_M g for a smooth mean-zero function g. An explicit formula is

    g=-(1/7) sum_(j>=1) L_N^j r.                          (15)

If h,r extend holomorphically to all X, so does g.

**Proof.** Apply L_N/7 to (14). By (13),

    L_N h-h=(1/7)delta_M L_N r.

Thus for every n,

    L_N^n h-h=(1/7)delta_M sum_(j=1)^n L_N^j r.            (16)

It remains to justify the limit, not merely manipulate a formal infinite sum.
Because B^3=7I,

    L_N^(3n)f=sum_k 343^n c_(7^n k)e_k.                   (17)

There is no constant term. For a smooth f its Fourier coefficients have finite
weighted l1 norms of every order. In any C^q norm, choose s>max(q,3). The
right side of (17) is bounded, up to the derivative convention constant, by

    7^[n(3-s)] sum_(k!=0) ||k||^s |c_k|.

This tends to zero geometrically and is summable in n. The other two residue
classes of iterations are obtained by the two fixed continuous transfer
operators L_N and L_N^2. Hence the series (15) converges in every C^q norm.
Let n tend to infinity in (16); this gives h=delta_M g.

For functions holomorphic on all X, the Laurent coefficients decay faster
than every fixed exponential. Applying (17) on a fixed compact product annulus
shows normal convergence there: the coefficient decay at 7^n k dominates both
343^n and the annular factor exp(R||k||). The same argument for the other two
iteration classes gives a global holomorphic g. QED.

### Fourier-orbit interpretation

Let Q_O(h) be the absolutely convergent coefficient sum on a nonzero A-orbit,
as in v0.11. Taking its charge in (14), at the orbit B O, gives

    7 Q_(B O)(h)=Q_O(h).                                 (18)

Consequently Q_(7^n O)=7^(-3n)Q_O. Smooth coefficient decay, with any exponent
s>3, forces Q_O=0. This recovers the earlier orbit criterion through ONE
principal-cover relation, rather than a separate proof for every orbit.

### Finite certificates

When r is a finite Laurent polynomial of mean zero, (15) terminates. If every
frequency has coordinate height at most H, choose n with 7^n>H. Then
L_N^(3n)r=0, by (17). The checker implements exactly this derived stopping
bound, not an arbitrary orbit search cap.

Coefficientwise, the recurrence is c_j=7c_(j+1)-d_(j+1). Its finite identity is

    c_0=7^n c_n-sum_(j=1)^n 7^(j-1)d_j.                  (19)

This and the finite Fourier trace coefficients are Lean-checked. The global
smooth/holomorphic theorem is the ordinary analytic proof above.

## 5. Phase-only compatibility is enough

Write h#(v)=conjugate(h(conjugate(v))). On T^3 it is actual conjugation, with
Fourier coefficients conjugate(c_(-k)). The real matrix N and symmetric kernel
make S_N and C_N commute with this operation.

Suppose a genuine nonzero smooth comparison multiplier has been constructed:

    a_Delta(v)=alpha e^(2pi i m dot v) exp(h(v)),
    m in Z^3, h mean zero,

and suppose its based value is ACTUALLY identified with U_Gamma/U_alg.
It suffices to prove (14) for h-h#, rather than h itself. Theorem 4.1 then gives
h-h#=g-g(Mv). The gauge exp(-g/2) replaces h by (h+h#)/2, which is real on T^3.
At p3=e2/3 the periodic gauge has equal values at Mp3 and p3, so the based
multiplier becomes

    a_Delta(p3)/a_Delta(0)
       =exp(2pi i m2/3) exp(real number).

The inherited modulus equality, AFTER the based identification, forces that
real number to be zero. Thus the ratio has cube one, and the inherited branch
enclosure selects ratio one.

Neither the comparison multiplier nor its principal-norm relation is assumed
to exist for gamma in this release.

Winding is not a new obstruction to (14). For an integer frequency m,

    (7I-B)m=(I-A)(B^2+B)m.                                (20)

Thus the winding part of the multiplicative principal-norm defect is itself a
unit boundary. Its free constant alpha contributes alpha^6 and must be separated
before applying the mean-zero logarithmic theorem. It cancels in the based
ratio, not automatically in an unbased multiplier.

## 6. Why regularity cannot be skipped

Take k=e2 (not in BZ^3) and define on T^3

    f(v)=sum_(j>=0) 7^(-j)e_(B^j k)(v).                   (21)

The series converges with its first two derivatives: B^3=7I makes the
second-derivative majorant a constant times sum 7^(-j/3). It satisfies exactly

    S_N f=f composed with N.

Yet Q_(A^Z k)(f)=1. Distinct B^j k lie in distinct A-orbits by (12). Thus f is
not a smooth unit coboundary. In fact f is not C^3: along the subsequence
B^(3n)k=7^n k the third derivative's Fourier coefficients would be the same
nonzero constant, contradicting the Riemann-Lebesgue property of a continuous
third derivative.

Replacing f by theta(f-f#), theta a nonzero real number, gives a purely
imaginary C^2 logarithm. Its exponential is nowhere zero, has modulus one on
the real torus, and satisfies the principal norm relation, while its based
phase at p3 is exp[theta*(7/6)*(omega-omega^2)], which varies continuously.
Thus the smoothness/decay assumption in the phase criterion is substantive.
This example does not challenge the smooth theorem; it explains why the
actual gamma comparison must first have its relevant divisors cancelled.
