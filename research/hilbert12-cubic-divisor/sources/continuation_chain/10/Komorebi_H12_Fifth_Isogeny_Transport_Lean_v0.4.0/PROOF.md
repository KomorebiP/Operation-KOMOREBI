# The normalized theta quotient as a fifth-isogeny differential

**Komorebi H12 continuation, version 0.4.0.**

## Status

This block derives the normalized degree-five quotient map from the previously proved theta formula. It gives the complete rational map on an order-five Tate curve, its source and target discriminants, a generator-change transformation, and the rational involution exchanging the source and target j-invariants. The actual cubic chart change becomes group inversion in the normalized model and commutes with the quotient. Two logarithmic derivatives of the gamma function satisfy explicit inhomogeneous difference equations whose right sides are these isogeny expressions.

There are 31 new compiled Lean theorem constants, and 140 including the unchanged inherited 109. The formal core proves field and polynomial identities, not the analytic uniformization theorem or the main gamma-arithmetic comparison. The constructions are explicit specializations of classical elliptic-function/isogeny theory; no historical novelty is asserted.

The outstanding independent evaluation of the cubic return limit remains unproved. No membership of the actual discrepancy in a root-of-unity group follows here.

## 1. Input and conventions

For any t in the upper half-plane, let

    Lambda_t = Z + Z t,
    q_t(z) = theta0(z;t)^5 / theta0(5z;5t).

The predecessor proves

    q_t(z) = (a-b)^2 / [(wp_t(z)-a)(wp_t(z)-b)],
    a=wp_t(1/5), b=wp_t(2/5).                           (1.1)

The theta product is the BCG convention [1]. The Weierstrass ordinate is the full derivative y=wp', so y^2=4x^3-g2*x-g3. All identities at exceptional points are interpreted meromorphically. Pointwise field computations retain their nonzero-denominator hypotheses.

Let P=1/5, d=a-b, ya=wp_t'(P), yb=wp_t'(2P). The predecessor also proves

    ya*yb=d^3,
    u=-yb/ya, lambda=ya/d,
    a=lambda^2*(u^2-6u+1)/12, b=a+u*lambda^2,
    ya=-u*lambda^3, yb=u^2*lambda^3.

Thus u and lambda are nonzero. Coordinates

    X=(wp_t(z)-a)/lambda^2,
    Y=[wp_t'(z)-ya-(1-u)*lambda*(wp_t(z)-a)]/(2lambda^3)

satisfy

    C_u: Y^2+(1-u)XY-uY=X^3-uX^2,                       (1.2)
    P=(0,0), 2P=(u,u^2),
    q_t(z)=u^2/[X(X-u)].                                (1.3)

The parameter u is period-dependent. Rational expressions in u are not thereby algebraic numbers at the cubic period.

## 2. A logarithmic derivative is the fifth-isogeny map

Set

    Lambda_t^+ = (1/5)Z + Zt,
    wp_+(z) = wp_(Lambda_t^+)(z) = 25 wp_(5t)(5z).

The identity map of C induces the quotient E_t -> C/Lambda_t^+ with kernel generated by P and degree five.

### Theorem 2.1

Define the single-valued meromorphic logarithmic second derivative

    L_t(z) = (q_t'(z)/q_t(z))'.

Then

    L_t(z)=wp_+(z)-5wp_t(z)+2(a+b).                     (2.1)

Equivalently,

    wp_+(z)=5wp_t(z)+L_t(z)-2(a+b).                     (2.2)

**Proof.** At zero q_t has order four, so L_t has principal part -4/z^2. At each of the four nonzero kernel points q_t has a simple pole, so L_t has principal part +1/(z-Pj)^2. There are no simple-pole terms in a derivative of a logarithmic derivative. These principal parts agree with wp_+-5wp_t. Their difference is an elliptic function without poles and hence constant. At zero, (1.1) gives

    q_t(z)=d^2 z^4 [1+(a+b)z^2+O(z^4)].

Consequently L_t(z)=-4/z^2+2(a+b)+O(z^2), fixing the constant. QED.

Independently, the quotient has the paired-sum expression

    wp_+(z)=wp_t(z)+sum_(j=1)^4 [wp_t(z+j/5)-wp_t(j/5)].  (2.3)

It is invariant under the enlarged lattice, has the required pole, and has zero constant Laurent coefficient. Applying the addition formula [2] to the pairs +/-P and +/-2P yields the same rational map as (2.2). This is the normalized quotient construction underlying Velu's formulas [3].

The constant 2(a+b) is necessary; dropping it fails both symbolic and numerical controls.

## 3. Complete rational quotient map

Write

    b2=u^2-6u+1, b4=u^2-u,
    Z=2Y+(1-u)X-u,
    D_u(X)=4X^3+b2 X^2+2b4 X+u^2.

Then Z^2=D_u(X). Define

    A4=-5u(u^2+2u-1),
    A6=-u(u^4+10u^3-5u^2+15u-1).

The quotient curve is

    C'_u: Y'^2+(1-u)X'Y'-uY'
          =X'^3-uX'^2+A4 X'+A6.                        (3.1)

Define

    Phi_u(X)=X+u(u-1)/X+u^2/X^2
                +u^2(u+1)/(X-u)+u^4/(X-u)^2.          (3.2)

The full quotient map is

    X'=Phi_u(X),
    Z'=Phi_u'(X) Z,
    Y'=[Z'-(1-u)X'+u]/2.                               (3.3)

There are no radical choices. Its invariant differential satisfies dX'/Z'=dX/Z wherever computed pointwise and hence as meromorphic differentials.

### Exact polynomial certificate

Put

    V=X^2(X-u)^2,
    N=X^5-2uX^4+(u^3+3u^2-u)X^3
        +(-3u^3+3u^2)X^2+(u^4-3u^3)X+u^4,
    H=N'V-NV'.

Then Phi=N/V and Phi'=H/V^2. The polynomial identity

    H^2 D_u(X)
      =4N^3V+b2 N^2V^2+(2b4+4A4)NV^3+(u^2+4A6)V^4    (3.4)

is proved in Lean. Clearing denominators in (3.4) proves that (3.3) maps source points to the target curve. The explicit derivative polynomials are independently checked in SymPy; the Lean derivative numerator is an explicitly defined polynomial, not a formalized analytic derivative.

Also

    N(0)=u^4, N(u)=u^6, Resultant_X(N,V)=u^20.           (3.5)

Thus for u!=0 there is no cancellation at the kernel poles. The x-map has rational degree five. The analytic lattice quotient proves the geometric degree and group-morphism interpretation; these are not inferred merely from a finite sample or from the field identity in Lean.

One further polynomial form of (2.2) is

    N=(5X-2u)V + [X^2+(X-u)^2]D_u(X)
       -(2X-u)X(X-u)[6X^2+b2 X+b4].                    (3.6)

This is the cleared algebraic formula for the logarithmic-second-derivative construction.

The exact checker additionally verifies invariance of both quotient coordinates under addition by P, reducing numerators modulo the source curve equation. This check is separate from (3.4).

## 4. Generator changes and their exact signs

Changing the marked generator from P to 2P induces

    u -> u*=-1/u,
    X -> X*=(X-u)/u^2,
    Y -> Y*=(Y-uX)/u^3.                                (4.1)

Direct substitution gives

    u^6 C_(u*)(X*,Y*)=C_u(X,Y),

where C denotes the curve polynomial with all terms on the left. This is formalized. The normalized theta function is invariant:

    u_star^2/[X_star*(X_star-u_star)]=u^2/[X(X-u)].                    (4.2)

Here u_star and X_star are the transformed parameter and coordinate in (4.1).

Applying (4.1) twice fixes u and X and sends

    Y -> -Y-(1-u)X+u.                                  (4.3)

That is elliptic group inversion, consistent with 4P=-P. Four generator changes are the identity. This change of marked generator is NOT identified with an Artin action or with doubling the gamma argument.

## 5. Source, target, and a reversible unmarked parameter

The source and quotient discriminants are

    Delta_u=u^5(u^2-11u-1),
    Delta'_u=u(u^2-11u-1)^5.                            (5.1)

For actual elliptic periods they are nonzero. In abstract characteristic zero, the displayed models are nonsingular when u(u^2-11u-1)!=0.

The c4 invariants are

    c4=u^4-12u^3+14u^2+12u+1,
    c4'=u^4+228u^3+494u^2-228u+1.

The associated c6 invariants and c4^3-c6^2=1728 Delta identities are checked symbolically and in Lean.

Set

    h=u-11-1/u=(u^2-11u-1)/u.

It is invariant under u->-1/u, so it forgets the choice between the two nonzero kernel x-coordinates. The j-invariants become

    j_source(h)=(h^2+10h+5)^3/h,
    j_quotient(h)=(h^2+250h+3125)^3/h^5.                 (5.2)

The rational involution

    h -> 125/h                                         (5.3)

exchanges the two expressions:

    j_source(125/h)=j_quotient(h),
    j_quotient(125/h)=j_source(h).

These are exact rational identities formalized under the indicated nonzero hypotheses. They describe the duality at the level of unmarked cyclic-isogeny moduli. They do not specify an unchosen marked order-five generator on the dual curve or identify the source and target pointwise.

### Optional analytic identification of h

In the specified marking P=1/5,

    h(t)=125 [eta(5t)/eta(t)]^6.                         (5.4)

Here is a normalization proof. From (5.1), Delta'/Delta=h^4. The quotient lattice is (1/5)Lambda_(5t). The classical identity

    g2(t)^3-27g3(t)^2=(2pi)^12 eta(t)^24

and homogeneity [2,4] therefore give

    h(t)^4 = [125 eta(5t)^6/eta(t)^6]^4.

Both sides before taking the fourth power are nonvanishing holomorphic functions on the upper half-plane. Their ratio is a constant fourth root of unity. For t purely imaginary, u and lambda are real, lambda!=0, and the source discriminant divided by lambda^12 is positive. Since Delta_u=u^6 h, h is positive there. The eta quotient is also positive there. Hence the ratio is one throughout the connected upper half-plane. This fixes the phase without numerical fitting.

Equation (5.4) is an ordinary analytic proof, not a Lean-formalized eta theorem. The numerical checks compare the Tate expression with direct eta products independently.

## 6. The actual cubic chart change is inversion on one normalized model

Specialize

    beta^3=7, beta=7^(1/3)exp(-2pi i/3),
    epsilon=2-beta, rho=epsilon^(-1),
    t=-(beta^2+2beta)/15, s=-(beta+2)/15.

The exact relations

    rho=4-15t, rho*s=-1+4t

give rho Lambda_s=Lambda_t. Moreover rho/5 is congruent to -1/5 in Lambda_t. Weierstrass homogeneity gives

    a_s=rho^2 a_t, b_s=rho^2 b_t,
    ya_s=-rho^3 ya_t, yb_s=-rho^3 yb_t.

Consequently

    u_s=u_t, lambda_s=-rho lambda_t.                    (6.1)

The full Tate point coordinates obey

    X_t(rho z)=X_s(z),
    Y_t(rho z)=-Y_s(z)-(1-u)X_s(z)+u.                   (6.2)

Thus the normalized inter-chart map is group inversion. Its ordinate sign must be retained even though q depends only on X.

The quotient also commutes with that inversion:

    Phi_X(-P)=Phi_X(P), Phi_Z(-P)=-Phi_Z(P).

In addition rho Lambda_s^+=Lambda_t^+, so the analytic lattice quotient and the cubic inter-chart map form a commuting square. The field negation identity is formalized; the homothetic analytic identification and derivation of (6.2) are ordinary arguments, with independent symbolic and numerical checks.

This is NOT a new endomorphism of E_t. Multiplication by rho maps E_s to E_t; it does not preserve Lambda_t. Retaining this distinction prevents a false CM identification.

## 7. Differential form of the gamma transport and its remaining phase

Retain the actual smoothed gamma function

    F(z)=Gamma_ell(z;-t,s)^5/Gamma_ell(5z;-5t,5s).

Its inherited difference laws are

    F(z+s)/F(z)=1/q_t(z),
    F(z-t)/F(z)=q_s(z).

Define G=(F'/F)', a single-valued meromorphic function. By (2.1),

    G(z+s)-G(z)=5wp_t(z)-25wp_(5t)(5z)-2(a_t+b_t),       (7.1)
    G(z-t)-G(z)=25wp_(5s)(5z)-5wp_s(z)+2(a_s+b_s).       (7.2)

These are explicit rational isogeny expressions in their respective elliptic coordinates. The new numerical checker differentiates the convergent gamma logarithmic series termwise on overlapping strips and compares against independent Weierstrass evaluations. It does not obtain the left sides by defining them from (7.1)-(7.2).

Differentiation is not a phase proof. Even exact equality (f'/f)'=(g'/g)' only forces f/g=exp(Az+B) locally, not f=g. The integration constants must remain in any arithmetic comparison.

### A sufficient normalized uniqueness statement

Suppose two nonzero meromorphic functions f,g on C:

1. have the same inhomogeneous equations (7.1)-(7.2) for their G functions;
2. are period-one functions;
3. satisfy the same exactly normalized eight-factor multiplication law

       f(2z)=product_(a,b,c in {0,1}) f(z+(a-bt+cs)/2),

   and likewise for g;
4. are holomorphic and nonzero at zero with f(0)=g(0).

Then f=g.

Indeed the difference of their G functions has periods 1,t,s. The subgroup generated by these periods is nondiscrete because they are Q-linearly independent in C. A meromorphic function with arbitrarily small nonzero periods is constant: near a regular point the difference quotients show its derivative is zero. Thus (f'/f)'-(g'/g)'=C. Integrating the meromorphic logarithmic derivative of f/g gives f/g=B exp(Cz^2/2+Az), with B!=0; a zero or pole would contradict its entire logarithmic derivative. Period one forces C=0. Applying the eight-factor law to the ratio forces A=0 by the coefficient of z, and then B^7=1. The common zero anchor gives B=1.

This is an ordinary conditional uniqueness theorem. It is not a claim that the arithmetic candidate has already been extended to such a function g. Constructing and independently identifying that comparison function is the missing step. No finite closed-cycle consistency condition is substituted for these global functional hypotheses.

## 8. Research boundary

Closed here: the explicit fifth-isogeny coordinate map, its source/target invariants and dual parameter, the generator re-marking, and the sign-correct cubic inter-chart compatibility. The theta-to-isogeny differential identity is proved analytically and certified algebraically after substituting the Tate model.

Still missing:

    lim D_m=epsilon^6/U_alg^3

as an independent arithmetic evaluation. The inherited analytic proof supplies epsilon^6/U_Gamma^3. Neither the rationality of Phi nor that of the moduli formulas makes their period-dependent coefficients algebraic, and no U_alg appears in the new numerical checks.

The differential reformulation gives a more concrete functional comparison target, but its integration constants and normalized multiplicative law cannot be dropped. No claim of an original gamma equality, an arithmetic power equality, or a general Hilbert-12 solution is made.
