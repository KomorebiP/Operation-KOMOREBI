## S1. Complete-intersection cycle class and period

R. Villaflor Loyola, *Periods of complete intersection algebraic cycles*, arXiv:1812.03964v5, 30 March 2021.

URL: https://arxiv.org/html/1812.03964v5

Verified locators: Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, and the paragraph immediately after Theorem 1.2 describing perturbation to a smooth cycle.

**Theorem contract.** X is a smooth complex projective hypersurface of even dimension n and degree d. Z is a middle-dimensional complete intersection in the ambient projective space, its ideal is generated by the specified f_i, and F=sum f_i g_i. Set H=(f_1,g_1,...,f_(n/2+1),g_(n/2+1)). The primitive cycle class is -(n/2)!/d times the (n/2,n/2) residue component of det Jac(H). For a homogeneous numerator P of degree (d-2)(n/2+1), if P det Jac(H)=c det Hess(F) modulo the Jacobian ideal, its period over Z is (2pi i)^(n/2)/(n/2)! times c(d-1)^(n+2).

**Translation.** n=4, d=5q, Z=Z_kappa, P=Q_b. The smoothness of X follows immediately from the Fermat gradient. The regular-sequence, saturated ideal, and integral complete-intersection conditions on Z are proved in Section 4 of this release. Equation (5.1) supplies F=sum f_i g_i. The row ordering and Omega normalization match the paper. The numerator degree is 3d-6. The Hessian coefficient is [d(d-1)]^6.

**Important boundary.** Z itself need not be smooth in the stated theorem; the source's proof explicitly uses a perturbation to reduce to that case. We have not inserted a missing smoothness hypothesis or used an arbitrary singular subscheme without checking it is the stated complete intersection.

**Result of verification:** exact theorem and its application checked at manuscript level. The source is not formalized or independently re-proved by this bundle. No quotation longer than necessary is reproduced.

## S2. Known standard-family identification

R. Jumagulov, *The Hodge conjecture for Fermat fourfolds of odd degree at most 199*, arXiv:2608.18134v1.

URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18134v1

Verified locators: Proposition 1.9, the discussion of even-degree midpoint exclusions following it, and the bibliography entries for N. Aoki's 1987 and 2000 papers.

**Claim actually used.** For an odd prime p dividing m, Aoki's standard characters have the padded arithmetic-progression form (x,x+m/p,...,x+(p-1)m/p,-px), subject to the stated nonzero and order conditions. At p=5 these are grade-three characters. Setting m=5q and x=b gives (3.1) in this release. The excluded order-two midpoint at even q is decomposable and is also covered by our direct construction.

**Attribution boundary.** This verifies that the algebraicity family is already known, not that our precise equations or determinant coefficient were absent from prior work. No novelty claim is made about those. We have not rerun, authenticated, or adopted the paper's full odd-degree census or other new theorems.

**Original-source retrieval failure.** Attempts to fetch Aoki's 1987 paper from J-STAGE and Project Euclid did not yield readable full text; one route returned a security interstitial. Thus we did not compare the exact original cycle equations. We rely on the checked later source for the family attribution and on our direct construction plus S1 for the positive proof.

Historical reference: N. Aoki, *Some new algebraic cycles on Fermat varieties*, J. Math. Soc. Japan 39 (1987), no. 3, 385–396, DOI 10.2969/jmsj/03930385.

It was not a systematic global novelty survey. There was no independent second researcher.
