# Universal Unknown-Edge Recovery

This release contains one complete mathematical and computational study of unknown-edge recovery in a declared finite-rate signal class.

## Main result

The unrestricted problem is proved impossible without a model class. The positive theorem covers every real periodic signal of the form

\[
f=s+j,
\]

where `s` is a trigonometric polynomial of degree at most `K` and `j` is a zero-mean piecewise-constant packet with at most `L` distinct off-grid jumps.

Given the low Fourier modes and `2L` consecutive modes above the background band, the implementation recovers:

- the active jump count
- every jump location
- every jump amplitude
- the edge packet
- the smooth packet
- the complete signal

The final reconstruction restores the edge in its own packet. It does not approximate the jump by a truncated global Fourier series, so the reconstructed object has no Gibbs ringing within the declared model.

## Boundary

This is not a theorem for arbitrary unknown functions, arbitrary measurements, colliding noisy edges, or arbitrary continuous two-dimensional curves. The exact theorem is universal over the stated one-dimensional finite-rate class. The finite dictionary theorem applies in any finite dimension once its residualized secant condition holds.
