## Editorial purpose

Build a single continuous, theorem-led mathematical book about evidence-bearing computational realizations that acquire information, support predictive and executable methods, preserve correctability, discover improvements, and maintain guarantees when their representations and institutions change. Keep a separately navigable source/Lean/verification archive; do not merely concatenate the old source-reader PDFs.

**Target main-text length:** approximately 500–700 pages, subject to genuine admitted proof length rather than a quota. Retain all mathematically important hypotheses and proof details. The 1,919-page October 9 archive is a historical source corpus, not the intended main manuscript.

## Book architecture — 30 chapters

The organizing proof route is **evidence → representation → prediction/correction → decision/execution → proof/discovery → revision/organization**. A source title indicates where a chapter's material comes from, **not** that every source theorem is imported or has been independently rechecked for this new book.

### Part I — Realization, evidence, and representation (Chs. 1–5)

1. **The mathematical object of machine reasoning.** Define tasks, admissible observations, protected queries, operators, evidence, resources and authority. Introduce Realization Calculus and Komorebi Space as a family of coherent local views, not a universal claim that all mathematics is one set or topology. Sources: *Komorebi Space*, *Stratified Universality*, connected synthesis. **Missing:** a precise, bounded translation between ambient view systems and the concrete finite models used later.
2. **What evidence determines.** Fibre-factorization criterion, actual accessibility, non-identifiability, model classes and attainable initial states. Sources: *Grounded Abstraction*, *Machine Understanding*, reviewed core 1.1. **Existing:** selected-core Lean checked; **missing:** reader-level distinction between semantic existence and algorithmic acquisition.
3. **Reusable state and behavioral quotients.** Operation words, continuation equivalence, minimum initialized predictor, evidence-access conditions and scope changes. Sources: *Sufficient Resolution*, reviewed core 1.2, Large Signal Model. **Existing:** selected-core Lean checked; **missing:** careful bridge from fully known operators to learned models.
4. **Packets, residuals, and probability interpretation.** Projector decomposition, hidden return, exact reconstruction, invariant/positive cones, zero-weight components. Sources: core 2.1/2.5, Large Signal Model, Realization Calculus. **Open formal target:** core 2.5; explicitly distinguish algebraic packets from probability measures.
5. **Transport and common observable quotients.** Carrier changes, typed commuting maps, switching between compatible candidate systems, invariant discrepancy spaces, method transport. Sources: core 2.3/2.4, *Grounded Abstraction*, *Stratified Universality*. **Open formal target:** core 2.4; do not infer executable translators from abstract existence.

### Part VI — Synthesis, precise limits, and future theory (Chs. 28–30)

28. **One complete finite reasoning realization.** Expand the calibrated-bit example into a single typed system with partial actions, stochastic updates, correction memory, source/artifact certificates, revision, and an optional plural extension. Preserve *one declared model*; every new operation must be specified and checked. **New editorial construction** and exact negative controls required.
29. **Interface composition and its obstructions.** Revisit all 16 source-synthesis bridge entries. Provide a theorem/assumption ledger for evidence access, operational transport, positive filtering, resource cost, dependencies, and authority. Derive only **restricted** composition theorems from explicit matching types and hypotheses. **Major new synthesis task.** No unrestricted composition theorem is currently supported.
30. **What has been established and what remains open.** Separate finite theorem validity, scoped Lean checks, source/program verification, empirical support, and proposed integrations. State impossibility, non-identifiability, gauge ambiguity and causal/operational boundaries. End with precise research questions rather than broad claims about general intelligence.
