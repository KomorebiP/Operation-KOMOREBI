\section{Correcting the global target before raising contact}\label{sec:correction}
\subsection{A missing input factor changes the theorem}
The September continuation begins with a correction, rather than a larger contact claim. Set
\[
 R=\rad(abc),\qquad Q=abc/R.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:foundations} proved the exact identity $Q/(ab)=c/R$. A uniform subpolynomial bound on $Q$ alone is not equivalent to $abc$. It is false unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}[Counterfamily for the unbalanced target]\label{thm:false-target}
For every fixed $0\le\eta<1$ and $A\ge0$, no constant $K$ makes
\[
 Q\le K R^\eta(\log(3c))^A
\]
valid for all primitive positive triples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $a=1$, $b=2^k$, $c=2^k+1$ for integers $k\ge1$. The triple is primitive and $c$ is odd. Thus
\[
 R=2\rad(c)\le2c\le3\cdot2^k,
 \qquad Q=2^{k-1}\frac c{\rad(c)}\ge2^{k-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{Q}{R^\eta(\log(3c))^A}
 \ge\frac{2^{(1-\eta)k-1}}{3^\eta(\log(3(2^k+1)))^A}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
An exponential with positive exponent eventually exceeds every fixed power of $k$.
\end{proof}

The correction retracts the discarded global target. It does not retract coefficient identities or local valuations proved independently of that target. Those valid statements are developed next, with their exact scope retained.

\subsection{A necessary leading cost for powers of Q}
A stronger elementary obstruction measures the exponent cost of any all-triple bound on the unbalanced quantity.
\begin{theorem}[Necessary cost lower bound]\label{thm:necessary-cost}
Fix $s>0$. Suppose $B,A\ge0$ and $C>0$ satisfy
\[
 Q^s\le C R^B(\log(3c))^A
\]
for every primitive positive triple. Then $B\ge2s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and take $a=t^d$, $b=(t+1)^d$, $c=t^d+(t+1)^d$ for $t\ge1$. The inputs are coprime. As $\rad(t^d)\le t$ and $\rad((t+1)^d)\le t+1$,
\[
 R\le t(t+1)c,\qquad
 Q=\frac{abc}{R}\ge[t(t+1)]^{d-1}.
\]
The assumed inequality implies
\[
 [t(t+1)]^{s(d-1)}\le C[t(t+1)c]^B(\log(3c))^A.
\]
Take logarithms, divide by $\log t$ and send $t$ to infinity. Since $\log c/\log t\to d$ and $\log\log(3c)/\log t\to0$, this yields
\[
 2s(d-1)\le B(d+2).
\]
It holds for every $d$. Sending $d$ to infinity proves $B\ge2s$.
\end{proof}
No contact multiplier can make the ratio $B/s$ tend to zero for this false target. A valid certificate must instead include $(ab)^s$ in its global size bound, or directly control $M_c$ relative to $R_aR_b$.

\subsection{Two obligations that a new determinant must meet}
The proposed determinant program therefore has two separate obligations. It needs a nonzero contact expression, with an actual first nonzero coefficient and local divisibility. It also needs an arithmetic realization whose global size has the correct $(ab)^s$ normalization. The following local theorems solve parts of the first obligation. They leave the second as an explicit hypothesis.
