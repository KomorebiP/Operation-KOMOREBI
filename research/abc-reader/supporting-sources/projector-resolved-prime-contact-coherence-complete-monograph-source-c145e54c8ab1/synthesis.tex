\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
The Projector-Resolved ABC program began with one prime-coordinate derivative lattice and ended, in its current form, with a filtered adelic coherence object attached to sparse additive relations. Along the way, several apparently promising routes were solved exactly and then shown to be insufficient. The prime-packet derivative produced a valid radical divisibility inequality but reduced the conjecture to a small transverse lattice point. Polyphase refinement separated dyadic and odd-adic lift trees but left a global synchronization problem. The exact obstruction complex and moving-coset search then proved that deeper first-order search cannot create an $abc$ gain because the master inequality becomes the exact real floor multiplied by integrality overhead.

The program next moved through signal theory, elliptic and automorphic packets, divisor-polyphase persistence, Atkin--Lehner filters, congruence quotients, cotangent complexes, filtered cusp contact, Witt vectors, global Kummer occupancy, Fermat character packets, polylogarithmic path quotients, and finally simultaneous $p$-adic logarithm filters. Each stage supplied an exact object and exposed a more precise roadblock. The current reusable core is a calculus of
\[
\boxed{
\text{sparse additive relations}
+
\text{prime-power contact depth}
+
\text{adelic coherence}.
}
\]
For a primitive relation, the calculus separates activated prime support from repeated depth, preserves dyadic and odd-adic carries, globalizes most of the contact packet through Kummer theory, resolves cross-prime logarithmic notches, and constructs a cyclic coherence quotient with an explicit kernel lattice. What remains is an arithmetic global estimate on the conductor or determinant of the specific coherence character generated by the actual input primes and the additive relation.

The monograph also develops the resulting prime-theory research program. The closest applications are simultaneous Wieferich and regulator phenomena, classification of exceptional $abc$ triples, generalized Fermat and Beal-type equations, Hall and Pillai equations, and a separate Bernoulli--Witt branch for Erd\H{o}s--Moser-type congruence problems. Prime gaps, Goldbach, and the Riemann hypothesis are treated as lower-priority targets because the present machinery acts most strongly when repeated prime depth is central.

\section{The correction chain}
The most important conceptual transitions are not simply from a weaker theorem to a stronger theorem. They are changes in the object being measured.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item A short derivative vector was replaced by a joint obstruction complex.
\item A local lift tree was replaced by a global branch with an archimedean height.
\item An incomplete search was replaced by an exact moving-coset search and then ruled out as a route to a new gain.
\item A shell-energy picture was replaced by an integral automorphic signal with marked local depth.
\item A cyclic congruence quotient was replaced by a cotangent complex when multiplicity was needed.
\item A split residue packet was replaced by a filtered contact module when carries were shown to matter.
\item A filtered local packet was globalized through a Kummer class.
\item A full Fermat character bank was replaced by a nonlinear polylogarithmic path quotient.
\item An unspecified integral lattice was replaced by an explicit simultaneous logarithmic coherence lattice.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Common notation and the master object}

\section{Primitive sparse additive relations}
Let
\[
\epsilon_1n_1+\cdots+\epsilon_rn_r=0,
\qquad
\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\},
\]
be primitive in the sense that the integers have no common prime divisor. The $abc$ relation is the case
\[
a+b-c=0.
\]
For each term define
\[
R_i=\rad(n_i),
\qquad
M_i=\frac{n_i}{R_i}.
\]
The radical $R_i$ records activated prime support. The powerful part $M_i$ records repeated valuation depth.

\section{Filtered contact packets}
For a prime power
\[
p^e\parallel n_i,
\]
define the local filtered contact packet
\[
\Jcal_{i,p}=p\Z_p/p^e\Z_p.
\]
Its order is
\[
\abs{\Jcal_{i,p}}=p^{e-1}.
\]
Its associated graded is
\[
\gr\Jcal_{i,p}\cong(\F_p)^{e-1}.
\]
The distinction is essential. The filtered packet is cyclic. The associated graded has $p$-rank $e-1$. The non-split extensions between successive grades are the arithmetic carries.

Globally, the contact packet of one term is
\[
\Jcal_{n_i}
=
R_i\Z/n_i\Z
\cong
\Z/M_i\Z.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact contact quotient]
For every positive integer $n$,
\[
\boxed{
\rad(n)\Z/n\Z
\cong
\Z/(n/\rad(n))\Z.
}
\]
Its associated graded is
\[
\boxed{
\gr\left(\rad(n)\Z/n\Z\right)
\cong
\bigoplus_{p^e\parallel n}(\F_p)^{e-1}.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $\rad(n)$ identifies $\Z/(n/\rad(n))\Z$ with $\rad(n)\Z/n\Z$. The primary filtration is the product of the filtrations $p^j\Z_p/p^e\Z_p$. Each consecutive quotient is one-dimensional over $\F_p$.
\end{proof}

\section{Witt coordinates}
The local packet has a natural $p$-typical coordinate description
\[
\Jcal_{i,p}
\cong
W_{e-1}(\F_p)
\]
as an additive filtered object. Frobenius and Verschiebung make the prime type explicit. The dyadic branch uses the same additive depth but a shifted multiplicative logarithm coordinate on principal units.

\section{Kummer globalization}
Let
\[
x=\prod_{q\in S}q^{\nu_q}\in\Q^\times
\]
and fix a modulus $M$. The Kummer class of $x$ lies in
\[
H^1(\Q,\mu_M)
\cong
\Q^\times/(\Q^\times)^M.
\]
Its order is controlled exactly by the valuation vector.

\begin{theorem}[Kummer order from the valuation vector]
Assume $x>0$. Then
\[
\boxed{
\ord([x])
=
\frac{M}{\gcd(M,\{\nu_q\}_{q\in S})}.
}
\]
For a negative rational number and even $M$, one additional dyadic sign layer may occur.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The class $[x]^k$ is trivial exactly when every $k\nu_q$ is divisible by $M$. The least positive such $k$ is the least common multiple of $M/\gcd(M,\nu_q)$, which is $M/\gcd(M,\{\nu_q\})$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cross-prime logarithmic filters}
Let $Q$ be a set of input primes and $P$ a set of output primes. At odd $p\in P$, define
\[
L_{p,q}
=
\frac1p\log_p\langle q\rangle_p
\]
with the appropriate finite-depth truncation. At $p=2$, use the weighted principal-unit coordinate together with a separate sign bit.

The matrix
\[
\Lcal_{P,Q}=(L_{p,q})
\]
is a cross-prime signal operator. A sparse additive relation supplies a short signed valuation vector $\nu$ satisfying deep congruences in every output row.

\section{The coherence lattice}
After removing common Kummer powers and local logarithmic image defects, the surviving rows combine by the Chinese remainder theorem into a primitive cyclic character

The coherence lattice is

=

When the row is primitive,
\[
[\Z^Q:\Lambda_{\mathrm{coh}}]=C.

The normalized exponent vector of the relation lies in this kernel.

\chapter{Consolidated exact results and no-go theorems}

\section{Prime-packet derivative and radical divisibility}
For a primitive triple
\[
a+b=c,
\qquad
R=\rad(abc),
\]
the first attack attaches one coordinate to every prime dividing $abc$. An arithmetic derivative produces an additivity row, while an arithmetic Wronskian produces a nondegeneracy row. The key local divisibility theorem is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{abc}{R}
\mid
W_x(a,b)
}
\]
for every additive packet $x$. A nonzero Wronskian therefore satisfies
\[
\abs{W_x(a,b)}\ge\frac{abc}{R}.
\]
Combining this with the derivative-size bound yields a valid master inequality involving the shortest transverse packet. The theorem is exact. The missing estimate on the packet size was not proved.

\section{Valuation-word Smith form}
For the valuation word
\[
V_e(T)=1+T+\cdots+T^{e-1}
\]
acting on
\[
\Z[T]/(T^N-1),
\]
the phase/coarse block has Smith form
\[
\boxed{
\SNF(C_{e,N})
=
\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,e)
}
\]
when $\gcd(e,N)=1$. Thus all nonconstant phase channels are integrally invertible and the entire multiplicity is concentrated in one aggregate channel.

The aggregate obstruction then factors into a weighted dyadic lift and the odd-adic lifts. This proves exact local solvability. It does not prove a small global archimedean representative.

\section{Exact obstruction complex}
The phase-refined additivity and Wronskian rows define an integer matrix whose Smith form is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{diag}\left(1,\frac{abc}{R}\right).
}
\]
On the additive lattice $L$, the normalized Wronskian gives an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow L_0
\longrightarrow L
\longrightarrow\Z
\longrightarrow0.
\]
The sequence splits algebraically and locally. The full shell deformation is formally smooth over $\Z$. Every compatible first-order tangent extends to all formal orders.

This proves that the obstruction is not a failure of local lifting or higher formal continuation.

\section{Exact first-order moving search}
The moving-coset search solves
\[
H(k)
=
\min\{\norm{x}_\infty:x\in L,\ \omega(x)=k\}
\]
exactly for every supplied factored triple. Sector compression and the Chinese remainder theorem reduce each fixed-$k$ problem to the overlap of three integer intervals. A real lower bound supplies a finite stopping rule in $k$.

The decisive identity is
\[
\boxed{
\overline H
=
\frac{c}{R\Delta}
}
\]
for the exact real relaxation. The master inequality
\[
c\le R\Delta\Phi
\]
therefore becomes
\[
\boxed{
c
\le
c\frac{\Phi}{\overline H},
\qquad
\frac{\Phi}{\overline H}\ge1.
}
\]
The first-order search is complete and cannot create a new $abc$ gain.

\section{Prime-shell energy identities}
For
\[
n=\prod_pp^{e_p},
\]
define the shell signal
\[
\Psi_n(p,r)=\sqrt{\log p}\,\mathbf 1_{0\le r<e_p}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\norm{\Psi_n}_2^2=\log n.
}
\]
If $P_{\mathrm{act}}$ retains only the first shell at every active prime, then
\[
\boxed{
\norm{P_{\mathrm{act}}\Psi_n}_2^2
=
\log\rad(n).
}
\]
For the direct-sum signal of an $abc$ triple, the standard quality is the output-sector energy divided by activated bandwidth.

Every unitary transform in the depth coordinate preserves this energy ratio. Fourier, wavelet, and unitary polyphase analysis can reorganize the shell signal but cannot by themselves create the radical saving.

\section{Divisor-polyphase perfect reconstruction}
For
\[
n=\prod_pp^{e_p},
\]
define
\[
B_d(n)=\prod_{p\mid n,\ d\mid e_p}p.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
n=\prod_{d\ge1}B_d(n)^{\varphi(d)}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\frac{n}{\rad(n)}
=
\prod_{d\ge2}B_d(n)^{\varphi(d)}.
}
\]
This is an exact multiplicative polyphase decomposition of prime-power persistence.

\section{Filtered contact correction}
The split module
\[
\bigoplus_{p^e\parallel c}(\F_p)^{e-1}
\]
is the associated graded of the cyclic contact module
\[
\boxed{
R_c\Z/c\Z
\cong
\Z/(c/R_c)\Z.
}
\]
The direct split-to-cyclic rank obstruction is therefore a theorem about the graded approximation, not the filtered source.

For cyclic packets of orders $m$ and $\eta$, the largest possible image has order
\[
\gcd(m,\eta).
\]
The exact source loss is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{m}{\gcd(m,\eta)}.
}
\]
Thus the correct cyclic obstruction is prime-power depth shortfall.

\section{Witt--Kummer globalization}
The local contact packet is naturally a truncated Witt packet
\[
W_{e-1}(\F_p).
\]
Globally, the Kummer map
\[
\kappa_c:\Z/M\Z\longrightarrow H^1(\Q,\mu_M),
\qquad
k\longmapsto[c^k]
\]
has exact kernel
\[
\boxed{
\gcd(M,e_1,\ldots,e_r)
}
\]
when
\[
c=\prod_ip_i^{e_i}.
\]
The kernel is at most $\log_2c$. Therefore most of the contact packet is already a global Galois class.

\section{Fermat lift and geometric payback}
Choose
\[
u^M=\frac ac,
\qquad
v^M=\frac bc.
\]
Then
\[
u^M+v^M=1.
\]
The point $[u:v:1]$ on the Fermat curve has exact projective height
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log c}{M}.
}
\]
However
\[
K_{F_M}\cong\mathcal O_{F_M}(M-3),
\]
so the canonical plane-curve scale is
\[
\frac{M-3}{M}\log c.
\]
The full Fermat carrier pays back nearly the entire coordinate compression.

\section{Polylogarithmic path compression}
The root-of-unity distribution identity
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\operatorname{Li}_n(\zeta_M^ju)
=
M^{1-n}\operatorname{Li}_n(u^M)
}
\]
compresses an $M$-branch orbit to one weight-$n$ coordinate. Through weights one, two, and three, the synthesis gains multiply to
\[
M^3.
\]
The complex depth-three path packet has at most cubic logarithmic growth. A fixed-rank integral presentation carrying order $M$ must nevertheless have a large coefficient, denominator, degree, or covolume. Small complex periods are not enough.

\section{Adelic coherence factorization}
For the balanced input ratio, the output contact modulus factors exactly as
\[
\boxed{
M=\epsilon_2KWC.
}
\]
Here $\epsilon_2$ is the weighted dyadic sign layer, $K$ is the common Kummer power, $W$ is the residual local logarithmic image defect, and $C$ is the genuine cross-prime coherence quotient.

The coherence quotient is the index of an explicit lattice
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{coh}}
\subset
\Z^{S_{ab}}.
\]
The normalized input exponent vector lies in this lattice. The corresponding dual character has order $C$.

\section{No-go theorem: abstract lattice geometry}
Let $v\in\Z^s$ be primitive with $s\ge2$. For every prescribed modulus $C$, one can choose a primitive row modulo $C$ whose kernel contains $v$. Therefore a fixed short primitive relation can lie in a primitive congruence lattice of arbitrarily large index.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Short vector plus large lattice index is possible abstractly at every scale.}
}
\]
The arithmetic origin of the logarithmic row is essential.

\section{No-go theorem: direct positive contact}
If positive integers $A$ and $B$ satisfy
\[
c\mid A+B,
\]
then
\[
A+B\ge c
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\max(A,B)\ge\frac c2.
}
\]
A direct positive $S$-unit relation divisible by $c$ is already at the scale one is trying to bound.

\section{No-go theorem: linear Hecke averaging}
In the local Tate model, the $\ell+1$ isogeny branches multiply contact depth by
\[
\ell,
\quad
\underbrace{\ell^{-1},\ldots,\ell^{-1}}_{\ell\text{ branches}}.
\]
Uniform averaging preserves the linear mean exactly. Typical logarithmic depth falls, but rare deep branches retain the mean. Linear Hecke averaging is redistribution rather than contraction.

\chapter{Reconstructed Attack V: signal theory and integral automorphic aliasing}

\section{Why the signal stage mattered}
The sequence has no separately preserved Attack V PDF. Its mathematical content is reconstructed here because it was the bridge between the exact collapse of the first-order lattice route and the later automorphic persistence program.

The signal stage asked a different question from the derivative attacks. Rather than searching immediately for another inequality, it asked which transforms retain the information that $abc$ needs and which transforms necessarily discard it.

The answer was sharp.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The prime-shell representation retains both radical support and valuation depth exactly.
\item Unitary transforms of shell depth preserve the decisive energy ratio and therefore cannot create a saving.
\item The Frey curve is a nonlinear encoder that separates conductor support from discriminant depth.
\item The ordinary rational automorphic eigen-signal is still insufficient because integral representatives inside one isogeny or twist family can have different marked heights.
\item The missing datum is an integral automorphic packet containing the eigen-signal, its lattice, local depth, differential normalization, marking, and synthesis map.
\end{itemize}

\section{Prime-shell signals}
For a positive integer
\[
n=\prod_pp^{e_p},
\]
the shell signal
\[
\Psi_n(p,r)=\sqrt{\log p}\,\mathbf1_{0\le r<e_p}
\]
is a rectangular pulse of length $e_p$ in each prime channel.

Its depth Fourier transform is a Dirichlet packet:
\[
\widehat\Psi_{n,p}(\theta)
=
\sqrt{\log p}\,
\exp\left(-\frac{i(e_p-1)\theta}{2}\right)
\frac{\sin(e_p\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}.
\]
The zero phase records the exponent. Nonzero phases record the endpoint and residue geometry of the valuation run.

This explains why polyphase refinement was natural. It also proves why a unitary depth transform is not enough. Parseval preserves the total shell energy
\[
\log n
\]
and the activated first-shell energy
\[
\log\rad(n).
\]
The transform changes coordinates, not the underlying imbalance.

\section{The Frey curve as a nonlinear signal transform}
Attach the marked Frey curve
\[
E_{a,b,c}:y^2=x(x-a)(x+b).
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta_E=16a^2b^2c^2.

\section{Canonical operations}
The following operations are exact.

\subsection{Permutation}
Permuting the terms or the prime coordinates permutes the corresponding packet components and leaves all invariant factors unchanged.

\subsection{Sign reversal}
Multiplying the full relation by $-1$ changes the signed exponent vector but not the contact depths, Kummer orders, or coherence indices.

\subsection{Primitive reduction}
Dividing a nonprimitive relation by the common gcd removes a support layer shared by every term. The primitive relation is the correct input to the calculus because pairwise support separation is then visible.

\subsection{Primary decomposition}
Every filtered contact object decomposes exactly into its $p$-primary components. The dyadic branch may require a shifted multiplicative coordinate, but the additive filtered length remains $e-1$.

\subsection{Polyphase refinement}
A valuation word can be refined into residue phases. Exact synthesis must be retained when comparing norms. Phase-coordinate shrinkage without synthesis normalization is not an intrinsic gain.

\subsection{Kummer quotient}
Common divisibility in the valuation vector produces an exact cyclic quotient of the contact modulus. The quotient kernel is computed by a gcd.

\subsection{Logarithmic normalization}
Local image defects of the logarithmic row can be factored before measuring cross-channel coherence. This separates individual Wieferich-type degeneration from collective cancellation.

\chapter{Computational architecture and reproducibility}

\section{Why exact arithmetic became part of the mathematics}
Several stages of the program depend on integer indices, Smith factors, radicals, valuation depths, and exact congruence classes. A fixed-width overflow does not merely perturb a decimal display. It can change the lattice, the obstruction modulus, and the stopping certificate.

The Attack IV correction established a permanent rule:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Every public arithmetic input is promoted to an arbitrary-precision integer before multiplication.}
}
\]
Display values may be converted to floating point only after the exact rational or integer quantity has been stored.

Ratios such as
\[
\frac{\Phi}{\overline H},
\qquad
\frac{\Phi}{k\overline H},
\qquad
\frac{H(1)}{\Phi}
\]
are not probabilities. They are constrained to be at least one by their definitions. Values above one are meaningful integrality overheads or improvement factors.

\section{The finite-certificate pattern}
The strongest computations in the series return two outputs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a witness realizing the claimed optimum
\item a certificate that no smaller witness exists
\end{enumerate}
Examples include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item exact mixed-integer and CRT interval solutions for transverse packets
\item Smith normal forms and determinantal divisors
\item finite stopping bounds for the obstruction multiple $k$
\item exact Kummer kernel formulas checked against brute force on small cases
\item exact reconstruction of contact orders and filtered defects
\item symbolic verification of polynomial and Witt carry identities
\end{itemize}

This witness-plus-exclusion pattern is the right standard for future searches.

\section{The moving-search architecture}
The first-order search became exact after three reductions.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The prime coordinates in each sector were compressed to one aggregate integer.
\item A fixed obstruction multiple selected a finite polyphase residue state.
\item The remaining search became a one-dimensional balance parameter constrained by exact integer intervals.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting algorithm did not rely on a guessed cutoff. The exact real lower bound ruled out all later obstruction cosets once they could no longer beat the incumbent.

The lesson for future prime theory is broader:

\begin{quote}
Whenever a large integer search is generated by local congruence packets and one global balance relation, search first for an exact phase/coarse factorization and a proof-producing stopping rule.
\end{quote}

\section{The 361-triple coherence corpus}
The later attacks used a common corpus of 361 distinct primitive triples assembled from:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item all selected $abc$ hits through $10^4$
\item a broader quality-filtered scan through $10^5$
\item a historical high-quality stress set
\end{itemize}
The corpus was used to verify exact identities, not to infer an asymptotic distribution.

Representative finite findings include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item the Kummer kernel was one in most rows
\item local generalized Wieferich defects were rare
\item the coherence factor was nontrivial in most difficult rows
\item the relation vector was often much shorter than a random relation at the same lattice index
\item abstract lattice geometry still could not explain the index
\end{itemize}

\section{Corpus values that must not be overinterpreted}
A high finite correlation between quality and a coherence statistic does not prove a density theorem. A small maximum kernel in the tested corpus does not prove a uniform bound better than the exact theorem. A successful sampled Hecke projector is not automatically the complete congruence exponent. Every table in the collected papers should be read with its declared scope.

\section{Future benchmark standard}
Every future branch should contain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item an exact object definition
\item a theorem and claim-status ledger
\item arbitrary-precision arithmetic at the core
\item negative controls and model-mismatch examples
\item a complete data manifest
\item witness and no-smaller-witness certificates where optimization is involved
\item a separation between exact identities, finite evidence, and asymptotic conjectures
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Prime theory beyond the ABC sequence}

\section{The cross-prime resonance network}
The most direct generalization is a directed network on the prime numbers. For distinct primes $q$ and $p$, attach the depth
\[
\boxed{
w_p(q)=v_p(q^{p-1}-1)-1
}
\]
for odd $p$, together with the weighted dyadic analogue. The edge
\[
q\longrightarrow p
\]
measures how deeply the input prime $q$ lies in the principal-unit filtration at the output prime $p$.

A sparse additive relation selects a finite bipartite subnetwork
\[
\{q:q\mid\text{input terms}\}
\longrightarrow
\{p:p\mid\text{repeated output terms}\}.
\]
If $p^e$ divides an output term exactly, the signed input-exponent signal must cancel through the $p$-row to the required depth after the Kummer and local-image layers have been removed.

Deep output can arise through three distinct mechanisms.

\subsection{Individual resonance}
One or more edges have exceptional depth. This is generalized Wieferich behavior.

\subsection{Minor degeneration}
A determinant of several logarithmic rows or columns has exceptional divisibility. This is a regulator-type failure of transversality.

\subsection{Collective coherence}
The entries are individually ordinary, but the specific signed exponent packet creates a deep simultaneous cancellation. This is the genuinely new coherence mechanism.

These mechanisms should not be merged prematurely. Their frequencies, local signatures, and global consequences are different.

\section{Simultaneous Wieferich and regulator theory}
This is the closest immediate field because the cross-prime logarithmic matrix is already the main object. For finite sets of primes $P$ and $Q$, define
\[
\Lcal_{P,Q}
=
\left(
\ell_p(q)
\right)_{p\in P,q\in Q}.
\]
Natural theorem targets include:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a rank criterion for the matrix modulo prescribed prime powers
\item an exact decomposition of depth into entry defects, minor defects, and relation-specific cancellation
\item a density-zero or power-saving theorem for simultaneous deep degeneration
\item a regulator lower bound that is uniform over sparse prime supports
\item an effective classification of low-rank cross-prime networks
\end{enumerate}

A useful first theorem would state that every depth above a threshold belongs to a union of finitely many explicitly described entry and minor degenerations, together with a residual collective-coherence class. That would turn the new network into a classification tool rather than only a visualization.

\section{Exceptional abc classification}
The full uniform inequality may be approached through the exceptional set. A structural target is:
\[
q(a,b,c)>1+\delta
\]
implies at least one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a large generalized Wieferich defect $W$
\item a highly divisible logarithmic minor
\item an unusually short relation in the normalized coherence lattice
\item an exceptional automorphic congruence or regulator degeneration
\end{enumerate}

This would classify high-quality triples by mechanism. A counting theorem could then estimate each mechanism separately. The present corpus already distinguishes Kummer preload, local logarithmic degeneration, and collective coherence exactly. It does not yet prove that the collective class is sparse.

\section{Generalized Fermat and Beal}
Consider
\[
x^p+y^q=z^r.
\]
Every term carries repeated depth by construction. The prime-contact calculus attaches:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item filtered contact crystals for the three powered terms
\item balanced Kummer ratios
\item cross-prime logarithmic filter matrices
\item one or more coherence lattices
\item an equation-specific Frey curve, higher Frey variety, or hypergeometric motive when available
\end{itemize}

The calculus can serve as a pre-modular filter. It may eliminate valuation patterns, identify forced local signatures, or isolate a finite exceptional family before an automorphic object is attached.

For the Beal equation

A^x+B^y=C^z,
\qquad
x,y,z>2,

\begin{quote}
Prove the expected common-prime conclusion for every family in which one repeated output prime has a unit maximal logarithmic minor after Kummer normalization.

The condition is natural in the calculus and strong enough to admit exact finite testing.

\section{Pillai and fixed perfect-power gaps}
For
\[
x^m-y^n=k,
\qquad
k\ne0\text{ fixed},
\]
two deep contact packets collide through a residual channel whose prime support is fixed. This is more rigid than unrestricted $abc$ because the output bandwidth is prescribed by $\rad(k)$.

The coherence matrix has input columns from the prime factors of $x$ and $y$, while the output rows are controlled by the prime factors of the perfect-power terms and of $k$. A first effective theorem should impose a nondegenerate logarithmic-minor condition and derive explicit bounds for $x$, $y$, $m$, or $n$ in terms of $k$.

The calculus suggests a separation into:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item local preloaded depth
\item individual Wieferich depth
\item collective cancellation depth
\item fixed residual bandwidth from $k$
\end{itemize}
This decomposition is available before invoking a general linear-form theorem.

\[

The weight-three resonance found in the path program is suggestive but not yet a theorem about Hall's conjecture. The first goal is a precise correspondence between the cube-side contact determinant and the elliptic regulator.

The equation

\item a canonical filtered cusp-to-Hecke map with a nontrivial source-kernel bound

\end{enumerate}

\section{Final synthesis}
The sequence began by asking for a short derivative vector. It now asks for an arithmetic bound on one coherence character generated by simultaneous logarithms of the actual input primes.

The change is substantial.

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{old endpoint: }&\text{find a short point in a large lattice},\\
\text{current endpoint: }&\text{bound the conductor of a specific arithmetic character}\\
&\text{whose kernel contains the additive exponent packet}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The first problem was generic enough to be circular. The second contains the prime identities, local depth, dyadic and odd-adic lifts, Kummer normalization, and addition law simultaneously.

That endpoint is still open. It is also a substantially better mathematical object on which to build prime theory.
