# Block 49: an alternate elliptic representation

Base snapshot: bbf78879d4b5714c818dd1521053dcd6b80c0694799b120b440fa850efec091a.
Status: direct proposition with exact invariant check; parent audit pending.

The second elliptic representation is absolutely irreducible modulo 7 throughout the current \(3990\mid C\) branch. It has an uncontrolled residual conductor supported on \(B\). Thus it removes one obstruction but does not close the branch.

## The curve and a precise irreducibility proposition

Suppose \(A,B,C\) are positive, pairwise coprime integers satisfying
\[
A^3+B^5=C^7,\qquad 5\mid C.
\]
Define
\[
E:\quad y^2+3Axy-B^5y=x^3.
\]
Then \(E[7]\) is absolutely irreducible over \(\mathbf Q\).

**Proof.** The point \(P=(0,0)\) has order three: its tangent is \(y=0\), whose intersection with the nonsingular cubic is \(3P\). Direct Weierstrass formulas give
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_4&=9A(9A^3+8B^5),\\
c_6&=-27(27A^6+36A^3B^5+8B^{10}),\\
\Delta&=-27B^{15}(A^3+B^5)=-27B^{15}C^7.
\end{aligned}
\]
Primitivity gives \(5\nmid AB\). Reducing the equation modulo five gives \(B^5=-A^3\), so \(c_4\equiv9A^4\not\equiv0\). Consequently
\[
v_5(j(E))=-7v_5(C)<0.
\]
In particular \(E\) is not a CM elliptic curve: CM \(j\)-invariants are algebraic integers.

If \(E[7]\) were reducible over \(\mathbf F_7\), its invariant line would give a rational cyclic subgroup of order seven. Adding the rational subgroup generated by \(P\) gives a Galois-stable cyclic subgroup of order 21. But the non-CM rational points on \(X_0(21)\) give only
\[
-\frac{3^2 5^6}{2^3},\quad
\frac{3^3 5^3}{2},\quad
-\frac{3^2 5^3 101^3}{2^{21}},\quad
-\frac{3^3 5^3 383^3}{2^7}
\]
as \(j\)-invariants. All are integral at five, a contradiction.

Finally, complex conjugation has determinant \(-1\) on \(E[7]\) and order two, so its two eigenspaces have distinct eigenvalues \(1,-1\) and are defined over \(\mathbf F_7\). Any line invariant under the entire Galois group over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_7\) would be one of these eigenspaces, and hence would already be defined over \(\mathbf F_7\). The irreducibility just proved is therefore absolute. \(\square\)

**Source scope.** The exact four-value list is Remark 5 of Bourdon–Gill–Rouse–Watson, *Odd Degree Isolated Points on \(X_1(N)\) with Rational \(j\)-invariant*, [arXiv:2006.14966v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14966v1). Its non-CM qualifier is respected above; the negative valuation first excludes CM. The classical integrality fact is explicitly recalled in the introduction of Habegger, *Singular Moduli that are Algebraic Units*, [arXiv:1402.1632v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.1632). No assumption about irreducibility of the genus-two RM representation enters this proposition.

## What survives in the residual conductor

Let \(q\ne3,7\) divide \(BC\). At a prime dividing \(B\), primitivity gives
\[
c_4\equiv81A^4\not\equiv0\pmod q,\qquad
v_q(\Delta_{\min})=15v_q(B).
\]
At a prime dividing \(C\), it gives
\[
c_4\equiv9A^4\not\equiv0\pmod q,\qquad
v_q(\Delta_{\min})=7v_q(C).
\]
The integral displayed model is minimal at these primes because \(c_4\) is a unit; the reduction is multiplicative.

For a multiplicative elliptic curve at \(q\ne7\), pass to the at-most-unramified quadratic extension making it a Tate curve. Its mod-7 inertia matrix has off-diagonal tame character multiplied by \(v_q(\Delta_{\min})\). Hence inertia is trivial exactly when that integer is divisible by seven. An unramified quadratic twist does not change this criterion. This yields the exact local result
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{prime} & \text{exponent in the conductor of }E[7]\\ \hline
q\mid C,\ q\ne3,7 & 0\\
q\mid B,\ q\ne3,7,\ 7\mid v_q(B) & 0\\
q\mid B,\ q\ne3,7,\ 7\nmid v_q(B) & 1.
\end{array}
\]
The inertia assertion also follows directly from the Tate Kummer extension: over a maximal unramified extension the units are 7-divisible for \(q\ne7\), so only the valuation class remains. This supplies the needed implication without assuming any level-lowering theorem.

On the current branch \(3990\mid C\), the surviving \(B\)-primes avoid \(2,3,5,7,19\), but their number and sizes are not bounded by these arguments. Put
\[
R_7(B)=\prod_{\substack{q\mid B\\7\nmid v_q(B)}}q.
\]
The part of the residual conductor away \(3,7\) is exactly \(R_7(B)\). Primes outside \(3BC\) have good reduction. Thus a modular argument based on this curve would have to control the varying levels containing \(R_7(B)\). The fixed-level Hilbert newform enumeration for the genus-two representation cannot be applied to this elliptic curve.

## Relation to the previous lanes and a bounded checkpoint

This curve is the specialization of \(E_3^+(t):y^2+3xy+ty=x^3\) with \(t=-B^5/A^3\), after clearing denominators. Equation (19) of Pacetti–Villagra Torcomian, [arXiv:2512.17845v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17845v1), supplies this same family. Theorems A and B in that paper explicitly retain \(p=7\) among the excluded primes; they do not already prove the target case.

The work47 failure of unrestricted residue refinements and the nonintegral hypergeometric coefficient is not reopened. The new input here is the rational 21-isogeny obstruction, a global classification that is unavailable from a finite residue sieve.

The file work49/computations/alt_elliptic_invariants.py checks four polynomial identities exactly over \(\mathbf Z[A,V]\), with \(V=B^5\), including \(c_4^3-c_6^2=1728\Delta\). It separately checks all relevant unit pairs modulo \(5,7,11,19,29,41\), and evaluates the four rational \(j\)-values at five. These computations support the formulas and finite residue checks; the displayed proposition uses the written argument and cited classification, not an extrapolation from bounded tests.

The next missing implication is concrete: obtain a uniform constraint on the seventh-power-free part of \(B\), or relate this curve's varying conductor to a separate representation without presuming such a relation. Irreducibility of this curve alone cannot discharge the reducible RM branch. No complete proof of the \((3,5,7)\) case or of Beal's conjecture is claimed.
