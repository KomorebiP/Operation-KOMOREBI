# Independent audit of the opposite-extension route

Disposition: ACCEPT. The additional-prime theorem and the separately
identified abstract-extension dimension statement pass fresh review.
Only this audit file is edited by the reviewer; frozen Block48 is unchanged.

## Claim being reviewed

Under the inherited Block48 arithmetic contract, let positive pairwise-
coprime integers satisfy A^3+B^5=C^7 with 3990 dividing C. The new claim is
that some rational prime divisor q of C belongs to one of the classes

    1, 8, 13, 22, 27, 29 modulo 35.

Equivalently, some prime v of K=Q(sqrt(5)) above C has Nv=1 modulo 7.
Such q is outside {2,3,5,7,19}. This is a new necessary condition on
the solution branch, not an exclusion of that branch or a fixed extra
divisor of C. The proof imports the separately audited Block48 implication
15|C => absolute residual reducibility, with its declared modular and
database inputs. This audit does not recompute those inputs.

## Opposite Ribet lattice: ACCEPT

The retained Ophir--Weiss PDF is arXiv:2111.01559v3, dated 1 February
2024. I directly read Theorem 1.3 on printed p.3. Its conclusion is an
extension of chi_1 by chi_2; its notation must be read from the theorem
itself because the preceding introductory sentence uses the names in
the other order. With n=1, chi_1=chi, chi_2=1, the two distinct residual
characters have m=0 and s=1. The theorem therefore gives exactly

    0 -> k -> W -> k(chi) -> 0,

with W indecomposable, hence nonsplit. Here k=F49 and the coefficient
DVR is the ring of integers in E=Q7(sqrt(5)). All these are permitted
by the theorem; G_K is compact and the Tate representation continuous.
The corresponding nonzero extension class lies in H^1(K,k(chi^-1)).
The inverse twist is essential to the subsequent local cohomology.

The characteristic-zero irreducibility input is logically separate
from residual reducibility. Potentially total multiplicative reduction
at three gives nonzero monodromy of rank two on the full Q7 Tate
module. The monodromy commutes with the degree-two RM coefficient
field, so it has rank one on the rank-two E representation, also after
twisting by nu. After a finite local extension an inertia element is
a nonidentity unipotent matrix.

Faltings semisimplicity, with its primary locator checked below, is
sufficient to turn this into absolute irreducibility. For clarity,
scalar extension of semisimplicity here is justified by the finite-
dimensional image algebra: the Q7 algebra generated by the Galois
matrices is semisimple because it acts faithfully on a semisimple
module. Over the perfect characteristic-zero field Q7 it is separable,
so its scalar extension remains semisimple. The RM idempotent factors
over an algebraic coefficient extension are consequently semisimple.
A reducible rank-two such factor would be a direct sum of characters;
every element would then be diagonal, contradicting the exhibited
nonidentity unipotent element. No assumption of residual
irreducibility is used in this argument.

## Lattice-independent local splitting: ACCEPT

Let U be a characteristic-zero G_F-stable line with character alpha
reducing to chi. For any stable O_E lattice Lambda, U intersect Lambda
is rank one and saturated: Lambda/(U intersect Lambda) injects into
the characteristic-zero vector space V/U and is torsion free.
Reduction thus embeds a chi-line in W. Whenever chi is nontrivial on
G_F, this line cannot equal the globally supplied trivial line of W.
Two distinct lines span W and give a local equivariant splitting.

The statement uses the actual characteristic-zero subline. It does not
transport the residual ramification of the original Jacobian lattice
through Ribet's lattice change.

At seven, canonical toric uniformization yields, for every power 7^n,
the torsion sequence of the torus and period lattice. Passing to the
inverse limit and then tensoring Q7 gives a subspace with character
chi_7 psi and quotient with character psi. Both spaces have Q7
dimension two. The constructions are functorial for endomorphisms, so
each is a one-dimensional E subspace or quotient. Twisting by nu,
whose local character equals psi, gives chi_7 below and 1 above.
The residual chi has inertia order six because K_7/Q7 is unramified.
Thus the splitting argument applies to every selected stable lattice
at seven. No finite-flatness claim for the new lattice is required.

At v above q|C with q outside {2,3,5,7}, the normalized nodal fiber
has two K_v-rational nodes and common branch character nu. The
normalizing substitution is defined over K_v and involves scaling the
square coordinate by a cube, so it introduces no extra quadratic
twist. The toric Tate subspace after twisting again has character
chi_7 and the quotient character 1. Both are E-lines by functoriality
and dimension. Thus e is locally zero whenever Nv is not 1 modulo 7.
The even-C calculation at two gives the same lines after twisting;
N(2)=4 is not 1 modulo 7, so it splits there too.

When Nv=1 modulo 7 the two residual characters coincide. The two lines
can reduce to the same line; splitting cannot be inferred. The route
correctly retains those places as its exceptional set.

## Local cohomology at three and five: ACCEPT

For residue characteristic ell different from seven, put
M=k(chi^-1). This module is unramified, with arithmetic Frobenius
acting by a=Q^-1, where Q is the residue cardinality modulo seven.
Wild inertia has no homomorphism to the additive seven-group M.
In tame inertia only Z7(1) contributes. Writing F tau F^-1=tau^Q,
the invariants in H^1(I_F,M) can be nonzero only when a=Q, or
Q^2=1 modulo seven. This verifies the Frobenius convention and the
inverse coefficient character in the claimed calculation.

Inflation--restriction gives an injection of H^1(G_F,M) modulo its
unramified part into those invariant inertia homomorphisms. The
unramified part is M/(a-1)M and vanishes if Q is not 1. Consequently

    Q not in {1,-1} modulo 7 => H^1(G_F,k(chi^-1))=0.

For K_(3), Q=9=2 modulo seven; for K_(sqrt(5)), Q=5 modulo seven.
Neither is plus or minus one. Thus the extension splits locally at
both primes, independently of its chosen lattice or its potentially
multiplicative local extension parameters.

## Global restriction and class-group implication: ACCEPT

Assume the exceptional set is empty. The preceding arguments make e
locally zero at every prime dividing 2*3*5*7*C. At every other prime,
J has good reduction by the sextic discriminant and nu is unramified.
The characteristic-zero representation is unramified there, so the
representation on every stable lattice is unramified as well.

Let L=K(zeta_7). The quadratic fields K and Q(sqrt(-7)) differ, so
K intersects Q(zeta_7) in Q and [L:K]=6. The coefficient character
chi^-1 becomes trivial on G_L. Since six is invertible in k,
H^1(Gal(L/K),k(chi^-1))=0; restriction of e to G_L is injective.
Thus it yields a nonzero continuous homomorphism G_L -> (k,+).
It is unramified at every finite prime by the previous paragraph.
The field L is totally imaginary, so ordinary unramified class field
theory applies without a narrow-class or real-place qualification.
A nonzero additive k-valued homomorphism gives a nonzero quotient of
the seven-primary part of Cl(L).

Set M0=Q(zeta_35). It contains both K and Q(zeta_7), has degree 24,
and [M0:L]=2. If h(M0)=1, extension of any ideal class of L followed
by norm is both trivial and its square. A class of seven-power order
with trivial square is trivial. Hence Cl(L)[7^infinity]=0, a
contradiction. This descent does not assert that the entire class
group of an arbitrary subfield injects into that of M0; only the
prime-to-two subgroup is needed and the norm argument proves it.

## Residue-class translation: ACCEPT

The local norm condition is determined as follows for q outside {5,7}.

| q modulo 5 | residue degree in K | condition modulo 7 | q modulo 35 |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 or 4 | 1 | 1 | 1,29 |
| 2 or 3 | 2 | 1 | 8,22 |
| 2 or 3 | 2 | -1 | 13,27 |

These are exhaustive CRT cases. The ramified prime five has norm five,
the prime seven has norm 49, and the norms at two, three and nineteen
are 4,9,19. None is 1 modulo seven. Thus the new q is distinct from
every prime already forced by 3990|C. Thirteen and twenty-nine are
eligible examples, not forced divisors. No finite computation is
being promoted to an all-prime statement: the table is the full CRT
classification of the two splitting possibilities.

## Primary-source checks: ACCEPT

The [Ophir--Weiss v3 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01559v3)
was read directly as described above, including the exact orientation
and the n=1, m=0 specialization of Theorem 1.3.

I independently viewed the table and theorem on printed p.179 of John
Myron Masley, *Solution of small class number problems for cyclotomic
fields*, Compositio Mathematica 33(2) (1976), 179--186, from the
[primary journal scan](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1976__33_2_179_0.pdf).
The theorem gives class number one for the admissible conductors of
degree at most 24 outside its displayed exception table. Its restrictions
m>2 and m not congruent to 2 modulo 4 hold for 35, and phi(35)=24.
Thirty-five is absent from the table; hence h(Q(zeta_35))=1. The image
was checked because the extracted PDF text omits that table. This is
a theorem application, not an unverified cyclotomic database value.

For semisimplicity I independently viewed Faltings's own exposition,
*Die Vermutungen von Tate und Mordell*, Jahresbericht DMV 86 (1984),
1--13, in the [archived journal issue](https://www.mathematik.de/images/DMV/Jahresberichte/Jahresberichte_Archiv/JB_DMV_086_1.pdf).
Printed p.5 states the semisimplicity of the rational Tate module;
printed p.8 explains that the number-field argument, presented with
K=Q for convenience, applies to arbitrary number fields; printed p.10
explicitly uses the semisimplicity in the number-field setting. The
review read the corresponding images, not merely OCR matches. This
author exposition is the verified primary locator; the proof does not
depend on an unlocated theorem number in the 1983 citation.

## Companion abstract-extension dimension: ACCEPT

The separately reviewed `extension_selmer_sharpness.md` claims

    dim_F7 H_S = |S|,

where S consists of K-primes away from seven with Nv=1 modulo seven,
and H_S consists of opposite extension classes unramified outside S
and seven and split at K_7. This companion is not needed to prove
the additional-prime theorem. Its proof also checks out, for the
following reasons.

Each v in S splits completely in L/K, so the residue multiplicative
groups above v form an induced Delta-module. Their quotients modulo
seventh powers are the regular F7[Delta] module: each character,
including chi^-1, occurs once. No action on a chosen residue field
remains in its stabilizer, because the decomposition group is trivial.

The units cannot remove a chi^-1 component. The field L is CM, and
for a unit u every complex absolute value of u/c(u) is one. It is
an algebraic integer and a unit, hence a root of unity by Kronecker's
theorem. Thus the units modulo roots of unity are even under complex
conjugation. In the quotient modulo seventh powers their only
possible odd component comes from the seven-primary roots of unity.
It has character chi, distinct from chi^-1 since chi has order six.
The chi^-1 component is zero. Semisimplicity of the Delta action,
whose order six is prime to seven, justifies isolating this component
in the ray-class exact sequence. Together with Cl(L)[7^infinity]=0,
each member of S therefore contributes exactly one dimension.

The local splitting condition at seven subtracts no dimension. Seven
is inert in K and becomes totally ramified in L/K, so L has a unique
prime P7 above seven. Delta fixes P7, hence its class in the ray
group of modulus supported above S. An equivariant homomorphism to
the nontrivial character chi^-1 kills this fixed class. Since the
ray extension is unramified at P7, this kills its local Frobenius
character and makes the local class zero. Local restriction from
K_7 to L_P7 is injective, again because the extension degree is six.

Conversely, a class in H_S restricts to a character unramified at
seven, with possible ramification only above S. At those residue
characteristics an abelian exponent-seven extension is tame and
has conductor exponent at most one, so the displayed ray modulus
captures every class. Inflation--restriction gives the exact rule
h(delta g delta^-1)=chi^-1(delta)h(g), confirming the orientation
of the Hom_Delta space. Thus both inclusion directions are proved.

Extending coefficients from F7 to F49 preserves the dimension over
the coefficient field, and doubles it over F7. These are abstract
Galois extension classes; the proof neither realizes them in a
Jacobian's Tate module nor produces an integer solution. Their
existence explains precisely why the ordinary class-number argument
stops once eligible primes are allowed.
