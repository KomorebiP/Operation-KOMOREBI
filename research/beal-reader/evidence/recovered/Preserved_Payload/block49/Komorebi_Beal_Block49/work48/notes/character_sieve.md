# Exact tame-character classification and a prime-19 irreducibility extension

Status: written ray-class calculation plus exhaustive finite-field calculations.
The conclusion is conditional on the arithmetic character contract already
proved in Blocks 46–47. It is not an exclusion of integer solutions.

## Claim and dependencies

Let positive pairwise-coprime integers satisfy A^3+B^5=C^7, let C be even,
and let J be the Jacobian of

    y^2+(x^3+B)y=2Bx^3+3Ax+B^2.

Put K=Q(sqrt(5)), O=Z[phi], phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2. Hypothetical absolute
reducibility of the residual rank-two representation at seven supplies a
finite character nu:G_K -> Fbar_7^*, unramified at every finite prime other
than (3) and (sqrt(5)), tame at those two primes, with

    nu^8=1,       nu(Frob_(2))=-1.

Its realizability as a constituent of the underlying J[7] semisimplification
is part of the contract. Arithmetic Frobenius is used throughout.
The exact dependencies are `work47/notes/mod7_irreducibility_extension.md`,
`work47/notes/evenC_torus_frobenius.md`, and the class-number argument in
`work46/mod7_oddC_written_proof.md`. For the final combined scope we use
the stronger canonical Block-47 theorem in
`work47/notes/mod7_good_reduction_irreducibility.md`. Both real places are
retained below.

The result is:

1. There are exactly two possible characters under this contract. Both are
   quadratic, one odd at both real places and one even at both real places.
2. Frobenius at 11 excludes the odd character on every primitive residue
   pair, including the bad-reduction lane.
3. Frobenius at 19 excludes the remaining character whenever 19 does not
   divide C. Consequently the residual representation is absolutely
   irreducible whenever 19 does not divide C.
4. The surviving character is the graph character of the universal C=0
   stable reduction. Thus that lane survives at every auxiliary prime.

The third assertion includes odd C by the predecessor theorem. The new
content here is the even-C branch. No conclusion at C divisible by 19 is
claimed from this sieve.

## The exact ray quotient, including infinity

The field has class number one by the predecessor's norm-Euclidean proof.
Its unit group is generated by -1 and phi. For completeness, multiply a
unit by a sign and a power of phi so that its first real embedding x lies
in [1,phi). Its other embedding is plus or minus 1/x. In the norm-one case
the integral trace lies in [2,sqrt(5)), so it is 2 and the unit is 1. In
the norm-minus-one case the trace lies in [0,1), so it is zero; an element
with trace zero and norm -1 would be a root of T^2-1, which is incompatible
with norm -1 in this quadratic field. This proves the unit assertion.

Tameness makes the finite conductor divide (3)(sqrt(5)). Include both real
places in the modulus. Class field theory gives the quotient

    G = (F_9^* x F_5^* x {+,-}^2) / image(O^*).

Take generators phi modulo 3, 3 modulo sqrt(5), and the negative sign at
each real embedding, the first embedding having sqrt(5)>0. The reduction
of phi has order eight modulo 3, because phi^4=3phi+2=-1. Modulo sqrt(5),
phi=3 has order four. In additive coordinates the ambient group is

    Z/8 x Z/4 x Z/2 x Z/2,

and the two unit relations are

    -1 : (4,2,1,1),       phi : (1,1,0,1).

Write g for the second generator and h for the fourth. The phi relation
eliminates the first generator as -g-h; the -1 relation gives the third
generator as 2g+h. The remaining relations are exactly 4g=0 and 2h=0.
Conversely these substitutions satisfy every original relation, proving

    G = (Z/4)g x (Z/2)h.

The totally positive generator 2 has residue exponents (4,3,0,0), whose
image is -g. Depending on the reciprocity convention its Frobenius class
is g or -g. The condition nu(Frob_(2))=-1 therefore forces nu(g)=-1.
There are precisely two choices for nu(h), namely plus or minus one.
In particular nu is quadratic. The two real values agree because
nu(2g+h)=nu(h). Assuming evenness beforehand would have discarded one of
the two candidates without justification.

Equivalently, write an ambient character as

    (e3,e5,s1,s2) -> zeta8^(k e3+2j e5+4u s1+4v s2).

The complete solution of the two unit constraints and the Frobenius-two
constraint is

| character | k mod 8 | j mod 4 | (u,v) | real parity |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| nu_CM | 0 | 2 | (1,1) | odd, odd |
| nu_real | 4 | 2 | (0,0) | even, even |

These equations are also exhaustively checked by `character_sieve.py`.

## Identification and Frobenius values

The two quadratic extensions are

    L_CM = Q(zeta_5) = K(sqrt(-(5+sqrt(5))/2)),
    L_real = Q(zeta_15)^+ = K(sqrt(3(5-sqrt(5))/2)).

The second character is the product of the first with the restriction of
the quadratic character of Q(sqrt(-3))/Q. The radicands with plus and minus
sqrt(5) define the same K-extension, since their ratio is phi^2.
The cyclotomic extensions ramify only at 3 and 5, and these relative
quadratic extensions are tame there. Both have value -1 at (2): its
residue degree over Q is two, and 2^2=-1 modulo 5 while 2^2=1 modulo 3.
Their real parities distinguish them, so the classification identifies
them without an uncomputed ray-class table. The displayed real field
identification also follows directly from the trigonometric identity

    4 cos(2pi/15) - phi = sqrt(3(5-sqrt(5))/2).

For a rational prime q outside {3,5}, let f=1 if q splits in K and f=2
if q is inert. The CM character value at every K-prime above q is q^f
modulo 5, interpreted as plus or minus one. It belongs to {1,-1} because
f is the residue degree in the quadratic subfield K of Q(zeta_5). The
real character value is this value times the f-th power of (-3/q).
The two split-prime values agree, since both extensions are abelian over Q.
In particular:

| q | f | nu_CM(Frob) | nu_real(Frob) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | -1 | -1 |
| 7 | 2 | -1 | -1 |
| 11 | 1 | +1 | -1 |
| 19 | 1 | -1 | -1 |

Thus the separate Block-47 relation between two and seven is respected by
both actual global characters. It cannot exclude either on its own.

## The necessary full Frobenius test

At q outside {2,3,5,7} with q not dividing C the monic sextic

    Z^2 = X^6+10BX^3+12AX+5B^2

is smooth. Exact counts N1 and N2 over F_q and F_(q^2) give

    s1=q+1-N1,       s2=(s1^2-q^2-1+N2)/2,
    Pq(T)=T^4-s1 T^3+s2 T^2-q s1 T+q^2.

For split q, the arithmetic Frobenius on J at either K-prime has this
same full four-dimensional polynomial. A constituent with value v must
satisfy Pq(v)=0 modulo 7. At inert q one instead squares the four roots;
the code uses the exact polynomial Pq(sqrt(T))Pq(-sqrt(T)). It never
compares rational Frobenius directly with Frobenius at norm q^2.

Because both candidate characters are F7-valued, coefficient conjugation
of the rank-two F49 representation supplies the same character again in
the underlying four-dimensional semisimplification. Their eigenvalues
must therefore be double roots of the full polynomial modulo 7. The code
also tests this stronger necessary condition. It yields no extra exclusion
in the fourteen-prime range below. The prime-11 and prime-19 conclusions
already follow from the simple-root condition and do not require this
additional multiplicity argument.

There is a useful stronger global consequence. The Weil pairing makes
the multiset of four semisimple characters invariant under
eta -> chi/eta. Since nu is quadratic, the two copies of nu force two
copies of chi*nu. These are distinct global characters because the
cyclotomic character is nontrivial. Thus hypothetical reducibility forces

    J[7]^ss = nu direct-sum nu direct-sum chi*nu direct-sum chi*nu,
    rho_RM^ss = nu direct-sum chi*nu.

In particular the rank-two determinant is chi in this hypothetical case.
After the prime-11 exclusion, nu=nu_real has nontrivial quadratic tame
inertia at both (3) and (sqrt(5)), as seen directly from k=4 and j=2 in
the ray table. The rank-two residual semisimplification therefore has
Artin conductor exponent exactly two and Swan conductor zero at both
primes. Preservation of Swan conductor under reduction, already justified
in the predecessor, would exclude any specialization with characteristic-
zero conductor exponent three: Swan zero leaves tame conductor at most
the rank, namely two. Determining which specializations actually have
exponent three is a separate local input, not settled by this note.

## Prime 11 eliminates nu_CM

There are 110 smooth coefficient pairs (A,B) modulo 11. Every one admits
a unique C modulo 11, since the seventh-power map is a permutation. Exact
point counts give P11(1) in {1,2,3,4,5,6} modulo 7 on this entire set.
This excludes nu_CM whenever 11 does not divide C. The result covers the
A=0 and B=0 lanes as well as A and B both nonzero.

If 11 divides C, A and B are units and the standard normalization gives

    F(uX)/u^6 = (X^2+X-1)^2 (X^2-2X+5),
    u=A^2/B^3.

The nodes are r=3,7 modulo 11. Their branch values 6-3r are 8 and 7,
both nonsquares. Frobenius is therefore -I on the two-dimensional graph
homology, giving full Tate polynomial

    (T+1)^2(T+11)^2.

Its residual roots are 6 and 3, so it also excludes the value +1 required
by nu_CM. This bad-prime calculation uses the actual nonsplit torus; a
relaxed choice of either torus sign would lose the exclusion.

Consequently nu=nu_real in every hypothetical reducible even-C case.

## Prime 19 gives a new sufficient condition for irreducibility

There are 342 smooth coefficient pairs modulo 19. The seventh-power map
is again a permutation, and every pair is admissible for the equation.
Exact point counts give P19(-1) in {1,2,3,4,5,6} modulo 7 for every pair.
Since nu_real(Frob)=nu_CM(Frob)=-1 at either prime above 19, this excludes
hypothetical reducibility whenever 19 does not divide C.

At 19 dividing C the normalized nodes have nonsquare branches and the
torus polynomial is (T+1)^2(T+19)^2, which has -1 as a residual root.
That branch remains compatible. Combining the result with the canonical
Block-47 theorem, whose scope is 14 not dividing C, gives the enlarged
sufficient condition

    266 does not divide C

for absolute irreducibility. Equivalently, at least one of 2,7,19 does not
divide C. The remaining branch is 266 dividing C. This does not assert
that a solution exists on that branch, or exclude a solution on the
irreducible branch.

## Why this character sieve has a structural surviving lane

The normalized C=0 model gives nodes

    r=(-1 plus or minus sqrt(5))/2.

The branch values are

    6-3r = 3(5 minus or plus sqrt(5))/2.

Their ratio is phi^2, so both branches split in the same quadratic
extension, exactly L_real/K. Hence at every prime away from {2,3,5,7}
dividing C the graph Frobenius character is nu_real itself, with full
eigenvalues

    nu_real(Frob), nu_real(Frob),
    Norm(q) nu_real(Frob), Norm(q) nu_real(Frob).

For an inert rational q the branch norm is 45, whose quadratic character
in F_q is -1, agreeing with nu_real(Frob)=-1. For a split q this identity
is the usual quadratic-character evaluation of the displayed radicand.

This is an all-prime written identity, not an inference from the finite
run. It explains why the reducible semisimplification
nu_real plus chi*nu_real is compatible with every such C=0 local model.
It does not prove the Frey representation globally reducible.

For any finite set of auxiliary primes, the simultaneous local residues
A=-1, B=1, C=0 are primitive and satisfy the Beal equation modulo each
prime, and they pass these torus-character tests. Such residues lift to
positive integers in their congruence classes by the Chinese remainder
theorem, though not necessarily to an exact integer solution. They can
also satisfy the even-C parity lane and the condition 7 divides C.
Therefore no finite independent auxiliary-prime character sieve of this
type can rule out the remaining real character. A global argument or a
new local constraint beyond these torus-character tests is needed.
