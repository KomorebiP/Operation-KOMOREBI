## Critical checks

1. Fifth powering is bijective on \(\mathbf Z_2^\times\), so the scaling \(u^5=-A\) is valid. Dividing the original integral Weierstrass equation by \(u^6\) gives precisely the displayed \(G\). The factorization of \(\beta^5-1\) has an odd cofactor, hence \(v_2(\beta-1)=7v_2(C)\) without an extra factor of two.

2. With \(r^2=1-r\), one has \(r^3=2r-1\) and \(r^4=2-3r\). Substitution independently gives \(G=G_X=G_Y=0\), \(\det H=15(r-2)\), and \(G_\beta=-5r\). Both last quantities are units. The two roots \(r\) are integral and distinct over the unramified quadratic base. Thus the integral node calculation, rather than the completed-square discriminant, controls the thickness.

3. The formal implicit-function theorem applies to the gradient because its Jacobian is the unit Hessian. After translation to the critical section, the constant term has first derivative \(-5r\) in \(\epsilon\), so it is \(\epsilon\) times a unit. Over the unramified branch-splitting extension, the two distinct tangent directions lift to transverse linear factors over \(\mathcal O_L[[\epsilon]]\). An invertible linear substitution puts the quadratic part in the form \(uv\). At degree \(n\ge3\), a correction \(u\mapsto u+a,\ v\mapsto v+b\) changes that degree by \(av+bu\); every homogeneous term of degree \(n\) can be eliminated in this way, and products of corrections have higher degree. The formal iteration uses no division by two and preserves the constant term. Specializing \(\epsilon\in2\mathcal O_L\) is convergent coefficientwise. Both nodes therefore have exact thickness \(7v_2(C)\).

4. The inherited special fiber has one rational component and two self-nodes. Its graph has two independent loops; their branches and period lattice split over an unramified extension. The length pairing in this loop basis is diagonal with the two thicknesses. Under the graph/analytic comparison, compatible character and period bases therefore give \(v_2(q_{ij})=7v_2(C)\delta_{ij}\).

5. For the actual toric uniformization \(J=T/Y\), the seven-torsion extension is generated by \(\mu_7^2\) and seventh roots of these periods. Removing the seventh-power uniformizer contributions leaves units. Their seventh roots lie in the maximal unramified extension by residue-field lifting and Hensel, because seven is a unit at two. The same holds for \(\mu_7\). Thus all the generators are inertia-fixed, including the extension data; semisimplification is not used. Inertia is unchanged by the unramified splitting extension, proving the claim already over \(F\).
