## C54.1: Brauer localization and trivial-coefficient H^2

Milne VIII.4.2, printed241 (PDF index249): the Brauer localization map is
injective. Milne II.1.30, printed70 (index78): corestriction after restriction
multiplies by the finite index. The continuous version is covered in II.4,
printed87-88 (indices95-96). Hilbert90 is II.1.22, printed67 (index75), and
the cohomological Brauer identification is IV.3.14, printed137 (index145),
with passage to the separable closure via direct limits.

Application dictionary: take L=K(zeta7), whose relative degree divides6;
identify the TRIVIAL F7 module with mu7 only over L, and split F49 into two
F7 components. The Kummer long exact sequence supplies Br(L)[7]. Restriction
is injective because6 is invertible on the seven-torsion coefficient group.
Thus local vanishing everywhere implies global vanishing in H^2(K,k).
The new proof writes this reduction explicitly; Milne is not being cited as
having proved the particular Frey diagonal statement.

## C54.2: Ray presentation and local characters

Milne V.1.7, printed150 (index158), gives the unit/residue/ray/class-group
sequence. V.1, printed151 (index159), supplies the residue-unit filtration.
V.3.5-3.6 and Remark3.8, printed158 (index166), give reciprocity, existence,
and conductor interpretation. In the application Cl(K)=1, odd-order signs
vanish, and fixed local splitting imposes the four principal-ideal rows.
The fifth row is the fundamental unit. The additional loss of one power of7
in each local inertia factor is B54.2, not a claim imported from these notes.

Local next-precision lifting uses the standard tame presentation or,
equivalently, local reciprocity's uniformizer/residue-unit decomposition.
The concrete lifting calculation is supplied in GLOBAL_CUP_VANISHING.md.
It does not impose a global conductor condition on the lift.
