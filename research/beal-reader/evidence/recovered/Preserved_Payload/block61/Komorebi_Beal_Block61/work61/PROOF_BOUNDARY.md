# Proof boundary and preservation record

## Proved in this block, with exact scope

B61.1 is a universal local matrix implication assuming the exact toric trace,
determinant, trivial actual eta, and inherited natural-period obstruction.
B61.2 is cohomological descent of beta for the stated inert support and split7
condition, using the inherited no-seven class-group/inertia-injectivity input.
B61.3 is a correctly twisted ray-Frobenius presentation and exact rank-three
kernel. B61.4 excludes actual depth-two beta with all ramification in S.
B61.5 gives n=1 for COMPLETE H(C) subset U, and proves outside-U beta
ramification necessary at depth2.

The first new proof is not a converse to Block60's local torsion theorem.
It uses information at other forced bad primes in one global representation.

## Not proved

No individual survivor prime is excluded with unrestricted additional beta
ramification. No global class or curve is constructed by the local families.
No proof of depth-one impossibility, general depth-two impossibility, an
unrestricted conductor bound, the full signature, or Beal is claimed.
No actual Tate-module semilinear comparison or coefficient-field descent is
proved. No ordinary eta ray rows are imposed directly on beta.

## Evidence levels

- Written mathematical proofs: conditional on the named inherited leaves and
  checked classical cohomology/reciprocity statements.
- Exact finite computations: integer cyclotomic arithmetic, finite fields,
  exhaustive finite kernels and common-coefficient pairs, and toric matrix tests.
- Separate implementation: different field basis, norms, power symbols, and
  determinant method, with no primary arithmetic imports.
- Self-review: explicit normalization, quantifier, and counterexample checks.
- No new Lean compilation, proof-assistant certificate, fresh-agent review,
  or independent mathematical acceptance.

The original Block60 archive is retained unchanged. Its local positive
families, diagonal smoothing formulas, power-precision controls, and every
nested historical failure remain intact. The new result is a global support
exclusion, not a revision of the local period theorem.
