## B54.1. Strict support and full-precision norm congruence

If w(N_21)=n, then for an integer s>=0,

    n=t+2s,  h=w(Q-1)>=m,  s<h.                            (1)

Moreover the natural local extension alpha is zero at this place.

Proof. Choose a primitive integral vector z=(x,y)^T on the common image/kernel
line of N. The argument in Block51, repeated here, gives

    N=mu [[-xy,x^2],[-y^2,xy]],  w(mu)=t,
    F=I+z(p,r),                 px+ry=Q-1.                 (2)

For N, factor each column through z using a unit coordinate; the row killing z
is an integral multiple of (-y,x). For F, N(F-I)=0 gives the second factorization
by the same primitivity argument. Finally Fz=Qz gives the scalar identity.
This establishes the integral row (p,r), not merely a row over E.

Put s=w(y). Exact N_21 depth gives n=t+2s. Since F_21=yp,

    w(p)>=n-s=t+s.

Since t>=1, t+s>=floor((t+2s)/2)+1=m. The two terms in

    Q-1=px+ry

are therefore both divisible by 7^m: px by the preceding bound and ry by
F_22-1 in (H). Thus h>=m. Also s<m, so s<h. No division by x has occurred.

For the natural residual class, inertia is zero because N is divisible by7.
If s=0, y is a unit and ry in7^m implies r in7O. If s>0, x is a unit and
w(r)>=m-s=floor(t/2)+1>=1. In either case F_12=xr is zero modulo7. Also
Q=1 mod7, so the local residual coefficient action is trivial, and the
unramified extension is determined by its Frobenius value. Hence alpha|G_v=0.
QED.

This is strictly stronger than the older s<=h. The old upper endpoint s=h is
now excluded for the ACTUAL representation. Block51's abstract sharpness
family remains a valid family under its original, weaker hypotheses.

## B54.2. A nontrivial diagonal inertia value loses one tame power

Write j=w(N_11). Suppose j<n, so the actual diagonal correction on a unit tame
inertia generator T=I+N is nontrivial modulo7^n. Its exact order is 7^k, where
k=n-j, and

    k+1 <= h.                                             (3)

Thus, at every variable prime, ramified or not in beta,

    ord(eta_n(I_v)) divides 7^max(h-1,0).                   (4)

Proof. In (2), y cannot be a unit: otherwise w(N_21)=t>=n would give j>=n.
Consequently x is a unit and s=w(y)>0. We have

    j=t+s, w(N_21)=t+2s>=n, w(p)>=n-s=k+t.

Block53's inertia argument, or 2j=t+(t+2s)>=n+1, gives j>=m. Thus
k=n-j<=n-m and 2m>=n+1 implies m>=k+1. Combining

    Q-1=px+ry, w(px)>=k+t, w(ry)>=m

gives h>=min(k+t,m)>=k+1.

For completeness, a principal unit 1+z with w(z)=j>=1 has exact order
7^(n-j) modulo7^n when j<n. The binomial expansion gives
w((1+z)^7-1)=j+1: all terms other than 7z have strictly larger valuation.
Induction proves the order assertion. The whole seven-primary tame inertia
image is generated by a unit tame generator, so (4) follows. If j>=n the
image is trivial and (4) is automatic. QED.

This does NOT assert that eta ramification implies beta ramification. There
are checked local examples with w(N_21)>n but j<n. Such places must be included
in the global character's ramification set.

## B54.3. The exact inertia order at beta support

At a beta-ramified place, ord(eta_n(I_v))=7^s, with n=t+2s.
For s>0, x is a unit, j=t+s=n-s, so B54.2's unit-order formula applies.
For s=0, t=n and j>=n, so the order is1. This includes the case x=0.

In particular, a global exponent restriction on eta gives a bound on the
actual local slope, not merely on a freely chosen extension parameter.
