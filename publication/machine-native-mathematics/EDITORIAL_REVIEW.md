### Residual composition law

**Source:** v10, page 7. **Original verdict: ill-typed under an unrestricted set-map reading.**

The normalized revised claim concerns composable linear maps between vector
spaces over a common field, with linear endomorphisms on the spaces. The original
display subtracts maps without stating an additive setting. The revised prose
restricts the discussion to linear maps and describes the corresponding additive
setting with bilinear composition.

The dependency chain is: defined Hom-space subtraction, bilinear composition,
residual definition, and cancellation. The local algebra is
`(T_Z g - g T_Y) f + g (T_Y f - f T_X) = T_Z g f - g f T_X`.
Typing and that expansion pass under the stated restriction. The chain formula
retains the identity convention for empty products. There is no external theorem
or computational dependency in this check.

### Joint abstractions

**Source:** v10, page 6. **Verdict: correct only after a stated codomain restriction.**

Pairing two surjections need not reach the whole Cartesian product. Pairing the
identity on a two-element set with itself, for example, reaches only the
diagonal. The revised text takes the codomain to be the image of the pair map.
This restores surjectivity and retains the intersection-of-kernels identity.
The displayed formula is unchanged.

### Equivalence-relation pushforward

**Source:** v10, pages 6 and 7. **Verdict: conditional on the pushforward convention.**

The revised text defines pushforward as the equivalence relation generated by the
direct image. The direct image alone need not be reflexive on the full target.
Under this convention, a target equivalence contains the pushforward exactly when
its inverse image contains the source relation. This checks the stated adjunction
and its direction. For a bijection the operations are inverse.

### Value and decision sufficiency

**Source:** v10, page 26. **Verdict: refuted as an unconditional comparison.**

With one available action and different rewards in two states, a constant
abstraction preserves the decision but not the optimal value. Conversely, two
states can have the same optimal value and disjoint sets of optimal actions.
A constant abstraction then preserves value without supplying a common optimal
decision. These are two-state counterexamples to a general ordering.

The revised edition retains the separate policy and value factorization criteria.
It removes the unqualified "stronger" comparison and the schematic inclusion
display. Any hierarchy now requires appropriately nested task families and action
languages. No new inclusion theorem is asserted.
