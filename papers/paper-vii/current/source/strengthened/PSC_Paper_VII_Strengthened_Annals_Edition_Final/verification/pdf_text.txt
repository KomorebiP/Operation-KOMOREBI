Quantum Packet Calculus, Symmetry,
Effective Dynamics, and Continuum Towers
By Komorebi P.

Abstract
A finite quantum system becomes packet resolved once a complete family
of orthogonal projectors is chosen. We develop the resulting calculus for
states, gates, Hamiltonians, channels, code spaces, and geometric transfer.
Finite group characters give isotypic packets, while cyclic charges give
selection rules and tensor fusion. Circuits admit exact path expansions
and block conservation laws. Moving packets are transported by the Kato
connection, and a time dependent estimate identifies the off packet part of the
geometric Hamiltonian. Two sided operator packets resolve density matrices
and channels. Covariant channels preserve coherence order, and their Choi
matrices inherit the same symmetry. The Knill Laflamme condition is
expressed as vanishing of a compression obstruction, yielding orthogonal
syndromes and exact recovery. Schur Feshbach elimination gives spectral
equivalence, a dissipative self energy in the upper half plane, and a positive
definite memory kernel. Positive transfer matrices give finite reflection
positivity. Discrete tori converge at fixed diffusion time to the continuum
heat trace and spectral dimension. Constraint closure is measured in the
quotient by the constraint span. Exact and approximate continuum tower
theorems are proved under bounded hypotheses. Unbounded field theoretic
and gravitational conclusions are stated as separate requirements.

1. Introduction
Quantum mechanics repeatedly uses the same operation. A Hilbert space
is split into sectors, an operator is resolved between those sectors, and the pieces
are propagated or recombined. Symmetry sectors, eigenspaces, syndrome spaces,
adiabatic subspaces, and retained model spaces are familiar examples. This
article treats them as instances of one finite packet calculus.

The first part begins with quantum seeds. A state is cyclic only relative to a
declared observable algebra. For an algebra in multiplicity form, the local orbit
dimension is controlled by the rank of a coefficient matrix. This recovers the
relation between Schmidt rank and the dimension reached by local operations.
The finite group section then constructs central isotypic projectors. Cyclic
symmetry gives a sharper charge selection rule. An operator with charge q can
move a state only from packet j to packet j + q.
A circuit is resolved by inserting the identity between successive gates. This
produces a sum over packet paths. The target probability contains diagonal
path terms and interference between distinct paths. Symmetry can remove
entire path families before any matrix multiplication is performed. Unitarity
gives exact row and column relations among the transfer blocks.

Density matrices require two packet labels. The superoperators

form a complete orthogonal packet system on Hilbert Schmidt operator space.
Dephasing is the diagonal projection. Every channel has input and output
superblocks, with the same composition law as ordinary matrix blocks. A
covariant channel preserves the isotypic packets of the conjugation action. In
the cyclic case these are coherence orders. The Choi matrix carries the equivalent
symmetry under U ⊗ U .
Error correction is expressed through compression to a code. Let P be
the code projector. The class of P AP modulo scalar multiples of P is the
obstruction to making A invisible inside the code. The Knill Laflamme criterion
says that this obstruction vanishes for every product Ea∗ Eb from the error family.
Diagonalizing the resulting positive coefficient matrix produces orthogonal
syndrome packets and an explicit recovery on the error span.
The effective dynamics section returns to ordinary Hilbert space packets.
Schur complementation gives the exact retained resolvent and a frequency
dependent self energy. The same hidden block gives a causal memory kernel in
time. For a self adjoint hidden Hamiltonian, the self energy has a fixed sign in
the upper half plane, and the time kernel is positive definite. Uniform spectral
separation gives direct bounds that can be carried through a refinement tower.
The geometric part has two finite components. A positive transfer
matrix gives a reflection positive Hankel kernel and reconstructs a nonnegative
Hamiltonian through its logarithm. A finite Laplacian gives a heat trace and a
scale dependent spectral dimension. The article proves fixed time convergence
from periodic lattices to the continuum torus. Poisson summation then gives
the continuum small time dimension.
Constraint closure needs a different obstruction. If Ca are self adjoint
constraints and Pphys projects onto their common kernel, then
Pphys [Ca , Cb ]Pphys = 0
holds automatically. This compression cannot test closure. The correct finite
obstruction is the class of [Ca , Cb ] in the quotient of End(H) by the span of
the constraints. Vanishing means that the constraint span is closed under
commutators.
The last part gives two bounded continuum tower theorems. Exact
compatible finite Hamiltonians define a bounded self adjoint operator on
the Hilbert direct limit. A norm summable sequence in a common Hilbert
space converges to a bounded self adjoint limit, and its unitary and Euclidean
evolutions converge uniformly on bounded time intervals. Reflection positivity
and fixed algebraic closure pass to limits under the stated convergence
assumptions.
