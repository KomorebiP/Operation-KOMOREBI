Editorial reading note
This edition preserves the original author’s attribution and mathematical scope while bringing the
recovered Paper VII sources into one reading order. Part I uses the corrected integrated finite manuscript
and the latest finite completion. Part II retains the strengthened manuscript in full, including all 79
theorem, proposition, lemma, and corollary proofs. Part III retains every substantive mathematical
section of the six formal proof supplements, followed by valid earlier derivations that were otherwise
absent from the integrated paper.
The original finite item numbers are preserved inside Part I with the prefix I. The strengthened
development has independent numbering with prefix II. The supplementary constructions have prefix
III. Repeated results are retained because the later versions supply different scope, proof detail, or
connections; the combined count is not a count of distinct new discoveries.
The selected saved finite formal checkpoint records 19 covered manuscript items, 323 theorem
declarations, and 25 active Lean modules. These figures describe the archived formal development.
This editorial build does not constitute a new Lean compilation, and the strengthened Part II is not
added to that formal count. Compiler records, exact hypotheses, supporting declarations, numerical
evidence, and reproduction instructions are presented in the separate Lean/evidence guide.
The earlier Feshbach statement is read with both eliminated-block and full-resolvent invertibility.
Exact common-kernel compression is treated as automatic and is distinguished from operator closure.
Normalized channels and unnormalized error families are treated with their correct recovery scales. The
projected connection uses the actual range bundle and the Lie-bracket term. Part II also makes explicit
the nonzero-space, common-space, self-adjointness, finite-address, and trace-class hypotheses needed in
its limit statements.
The five physical continuum targets remain open in this corpus. The bounded direct and norm limits
in Part II do not provide an interacting continuum field theory, unbounded gravitational constraint
closure, Lorentzian reconstruction, an Einstein infrared action, or a physical massless spin-two sector.

Definition I.1.1 (Finite quantum seed system). A finite quantum seed system is a tuple
Q = (H, ψ0, O, R, {Pℓ,α}, H, τ) ,
where H is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, ψ0 ∈H is a nonzero seed, O is the declared operator

packets; circuit products become sums over packet paths; Hamiltonian symmetry and its controlled
breaking are visible as diagonal blocks and leakage; moving packets carry a canonical connection and
curvature; channels, measurements, and error-correction conditions admit exact packet formulations;

seed reachability + symmetry resolution + circuit paths
+effective dynamics + geometric transport + constraint diagnostics

tested and too substantial to be replaced by finite numerical resemblance.

Quantum mechanics repeatedly uses the same operation. A Hilbert space is split into sectors, an
operator is resolved between those sectors, and the pieces are propagated or recombined. Symmetry
sectors, eigenspaces, syndrome spaces, adiabatic subspaces, and retained model spaces are familiar
examples. This article treats them as instances of one finite packet calculus.
The elementary coordinate object is a complete family of orthogonal projectors

These identities are simple. Their value appears when the same coordinates are used across representation
theory, circuits, channels, error correction, effective dynamics, and geometric limits.
Finite dimensional quantum information and operator theory supply the standard background
[18, 27, 3]. Finite group projectors come from character theory [23, 9]. Moving spectral subspaces and
geometric phase originate in the work of Kato, Berry, Wilczek, and Zee [12, 13, 2, 28]. Completely
positive maps and quantum dynamical semigroups are treated in [24, 5, 15, 16]. Exact error correction
is governed by the Knill Laflamme condition [14, 22, 1]. Projection methods and hidden state memory
are linked by the Feshbach, Nakajima, and Zwanzig constructions [8, 17, 29, 4].
The first part begins with quantum seeds. A state is cyclic only relative to a declared observable
algebra. For an algebra in multiplicity form, the local orbit dimension is controlled by the rank of a
coefficient matrix. This recovers the relation between Schmidt rank and the dimension reached by local
operations. The finite group section then constructs central isotypic projectors. Cyclic symmetry gives
a sharper charge selection rule. An operator with charge q can move a state only from packet j to
packet j + q.
A circuit is resolved by inserting the identity between successive gates. This produces a sum over
packet paths. The target probability contains diagonal path terms and interference between distinct
paths. Symmetry can remove entire path families before any matrix multiplication is performed.
