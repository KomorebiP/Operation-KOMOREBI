# Claim 9.1: scope after Block 25

**Decision: keep the full source claim partial.** Block 25 proves the missing system-to-member implication under the appropriate arithmetic sampling input and proves that input for a fixed cyclotomic extension of $\mathbb Q$. It does not prove general arithmetic Frobenius coverage.

The authoritative source is the theorem “Finite Frobenius separation” in Section 9 of the Block 24 amended manuscript. It starts with finitely many pairwise nonisomorphic strictly compatible systems, absolutely irreducible members and common algebraic traces $t_i(v)\in E$. It concludes that distinct permitted places give a matrix $C=(t_i(v_j))_{j,i}$ with nonzero determinant. Every coefficient embedding preserves this determinant condition. Identification with ordinary member traces is conditional on the selected place being good for that coefficient prime.

The source does not supply arithmetic density as an extra hypothesis. Its proof invokes density of unramified Frobenius conjugacy classes. Keeping that ingredient as a generic premise proves a valid intermediate theorem, but does not close the entire arithmetic source claim. This differs from 8.2 and 11.2, whose relevant closure and existence requirements are explicit in their statements.

## The representation-theoretic implication is now proved

`MemberwiseEquiv ρ i j` means actual representation equivalence at every coefficient index. It does not mean equality of matrix bases, integral lattices, optional labels or spine records. For a selected index $\lambda_0$, the new theorem proves

\[
\operatorname{MemberwiseEquiv}(\rho_i,\rho_j)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Nonempty}(\rho_{\lambda_0,i}\simeq\rho_{\lambda_0,j}).
\]

The data use one fixed embedding $E\hookrightarrow K_\lambda$ shared by the systems at each coefficient index. Common traces are required only on `good λ`. The interface uses a good-place domain common to the finite family at each index; that domain may vary with the index. Density is required on the appropriate pairwise overlaps, not on an asserted universal all-prime domain.

An isomorphism at $\lambda_0$ reflects equality of the common traces using injectivity of that embedding. Compatibility transports this equality to any other member on the overlap. Continuity and conjugacy-saturated density give equality of its actual characters. The proved character-rigidity theorem then supplies an actual representation equivalence. Character rigidity needs irreducibility in characteristic zero; the source's absolute irreducibility is stronger than necessary.

`finite_system_separator` uses this implication to derive nonisomorphism at the selected coefficient prime. It does not assume both system nonisomorphism and member nonisomorphism separately. The selected samples avoid an arbitrary fixed finite set, and the common determinant remains nonzero after every coefficient embedding.

## A complete restricted arithmetic sampling case

Fix a cyclotomic number field $L/\mathbb Q$ generated by a primitive $n$-th root of unity, where $n\ne0$. Mathlib's `Nat.forall_exists_prime_gt_and_eq_mod` supplies primes in every unit residue class modulo $n$ above any bound. The actual equivalence `IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.galEquivZMod` transports this to every automorphism of $L/\mathbb Q$. Taking the bound above a finite forbidden set proves the required finite avoidance.

The new source proves the attachment, not only the exponent on roots of unity. The sampled automorphism stabilizes a maximal ideal above $p$ and acts on every residue of an algebraic integer by $p$-th power. The prime ideal has ramification index one. Lying-over produces such an ideal; `exists_arithmetic_frobenius_prime` combines it with the prescribed automorphism, finite avoidance and lower bound. The convention is arithmetic Frobenius.

The specialized system separator therefore needs no coverage or character-continuity hypothesis. Coverage follows from Dirichlet, and continuity follows from discreteness of this finite Galois group with its existing Krull topology. Compatibility, cofinite good-place domains and pairwise system nonisomorphism remain explicit inputs.

These are systems of representations of the fixed finite group $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)$. The theorem does not establish the corresponding attachment for arbitrary representations of the full absolute Galois group.

## The remaining boundary

Ordinary quotient coverage is insufficient for the overlap argument. The checked $C_2$ example has a surjective sample family with the nonidentity element only at index zero. Deleting that index destroys coverage. Its rational sign and trivial characters agree on every remaining sample despite differing globally.

For full Claim 9.1, the remaining arithmetic leaf is coverage of every relevant finite Galois quotient by permitted unramified Frobenius classes after finite exclusions, together with the actual arithmetic sampling/restriction attachment. Neither the pinned inventory nor Komorebi's finite-table separator supplies this general result. Komorebi explicitly marks density beyond its supplied table as unchecked.

The scope decision is therefore unchanged: **17 checked / 3 partial**. The new restricted case is substantive, but does not promote 9.1, 9.2 or 11.1.
