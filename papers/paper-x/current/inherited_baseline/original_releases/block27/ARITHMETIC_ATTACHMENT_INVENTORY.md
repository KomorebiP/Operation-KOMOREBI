## Exact scope of Claim 9.2

The manuscript supplies a semisimple strictly compatible carrier together with
its decomposition into strictly compatible constituent systems,

\[
\mathscr W\cong\bigoplus_i\mathscr W_i^{\oplus c_i},
\qquad
V_{\lambda_0}\cong\bigoplus_i W_{i,\lambda_0}^{\oplus m_i},
\qquad 0\le m_i\le c_i.
\]

It constructs the system using the same multiplicities at every coefficient
prime and claims finite Frobenius-trace recovery. The manuscript explicitly says
that finding an unknown compatible carrier is not an additional obligation of
this conditional theorem. Its remaining formal obligations include preservation
of the intended actual local arithmetic structures and the arithmetic separator
input. Replacing those structures with arbitrary labels or predicates would not
complete Claim 9.2.

### The period rings are present, but their representation theory is not supplied by these files

`BDeRham.lean` works with

```lean
(R : Type u) [CommRing R] (p : ℕ) [Fact p.Prime]
[Fact ¬IsUnit (p : R)] [IsAdicComplete (Ideal.span {(p : R)}) R]
```

It defines `fontaineThetaInvertP` by inverting `p`, then

```lean
BDeRhamPlus R p :=
  AdicCompletion (RingHom.ker (fontaineThetaInvertP R p))
    (Localization.Away (p : WittVector p (PreTilt R p)))
```

and defines `BDeRham R p` by localizing this ring at the multiplicative closure
of the images of generators of the theta kernel. The source explains the
classical specialization when `R` is the ring of integers of `ℂ_[p]`.

The file itself lists these unfinished steps: extending theta to `BDeRhamPlus`,
proving the discrete valuation-ring property, and proving the theta kernel is
principal for an integral perfectoid base. No Galois action, filtered comparison
map, de Rham representation predicate, or Hodge–Tate comparison theorem appears
in the opened period-ring file. Instantiating its hypotheses for the required
arithmetic base and constructing the necessary actions would require further
work; existence of the type definition alone does not complete that work.

## Exact project bridge already checked in predecessors

`PaperX.GaloisComparisonCompletion.localRestriction p` has the actual type

```lean
Field.absoluteGaloisGroup ℚ_[p] →ₜ* Field.absoluteGaloisGroup ℚ
```

and is defined by `Field.absoluteGaloisGroup.map (algebraMap ℚ ℚ_[p])`.

`PaperX.GaloisComparisonCompletion.rational_galois_comparison_completion`
specializes certificate completion to these local/global groups. It assumes:

- An adically complete coefficient ring `R`, a finitely generated ideal `I`,
  and finite `R ⧸ I`.
- Finite positive-depth allowed class sets and occupied certificate nodes at
  every positive depth.
- Supplied integral local templates `τ`, a fixed detecting depth for each
  comparison, and continuous representatives for the allowed finite classes.
- An injective continuous coefficient homomorphism from `R` to a topological
  field `K`.

It produces a continuous field-valued global representation and actual matrix
units conjugating its local restrictions to the supplied local templates.
Those are whole-action isomorphisms. The theorem does not define Hodge–Tate
weights or attach Weil–Deligne data.

`PaperX.ArithmeticCertificateExample.absoluteQuadraticCharacter` is an actual
continuous character of `Field.absoluteGaloisGroup ℚ` onto the supplied order-two
group, defined through the action on a square root of `-1` in `AlgebraicClosure
ℚ`. Its nontriviality and continuity are proved. This is genuine arithmetic
input, but the predecessor module does not attach a period comparison to it.

`PaperX.WeilData.Datum` takes an arbitrary group `G`, field `K`, norm character
`ν : G →* Kˣ`, actual representation `r`, nilpotent endomorphism `N`, and the
relation `r(g) N = ν(g) N r(g)`. `fixedKernel` is the actual intersection of
`ker N` with subgroup invariants. `PaperX.WeilSmooth.InertialDatum` additionally
requires finite inertia image and an open inertia kernel; finite-sum
preservation is checked. These modules intentionally leave identification of
the abstract group and subgroup with arithmetic Weil and inertia groups
separate.

`Field.absoluteGaloisGroup ℚ_[p]` from `PadicAlgCl p` to its completion, then
constructing an explicit semilinear period comparison for a finite-image
representation. The scalar Hilbert 90 and normal-basis results are relevant
ingredients, but the action and descent comparison are not supplied by the
opened files. General finite-dimensional descent is a separate step from the
scalar Hilbert 90 theorem.

For Block 20, general finite logarithmic extraction is the clearer bounded
target. It directly removes the `N^3 = 0` limitation in the already checked
explicit exponential reconstruction route. It still does not establish
arithmetic attachment or finish the Hodge–Tate/Weil–Deligne clauses of Claim
9.2. The full arithmetic ledger should remain 15 checked and 5 partial unless
additional proofs establish those clauses.
