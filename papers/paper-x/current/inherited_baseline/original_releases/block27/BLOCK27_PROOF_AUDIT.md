## Claim card

This block therefore checks the rational-base arithmetic interface. - A `Witness p` retains an actual maximal ideal Q of B, its lying-over property above the integer ideal (p), an absolute automorphism γ stabilizing Q, and the equality `mk_Q (γ • x) = (mk_Q x)^p` for every x in B. Existence is proved for natural primes p. No vanishing of absolute inertia is built into this record.
- L is any number field normal over ℚ. Normality and characteristic zero make the extension finite Galois. Its chosen embedding is the same one used to define `AbsoluteGaloisRestriction.restriction`. The ring map, contracted ideal, residue map, and action identities all use that embedding.
- Maximality of the contracted prime is proved for p prime and Q prime above (p). The integral and residue injectivity claims are separately proved. The residue injection is not asserted to be surjective.
- The finite unramifiedness theorem uses the actual local ramification index, or the commutative-algebra predicate `Algebra.IsUnramifiedAt ℤ P`. Discriminant nondivisibility supplies that predicate. The exceptional finite set depends on L.
- Two absolute witnesses may have different ideals above p and different Frobenius lifts. Their comparison is conjugacy modulo the actual ideal inertia at the second witness. A representation need kill that inertia only; the finite-factor application proves that condition outside the discriminant.
- The trace equalities are valid at their stated module generality. For finite-dimensional modules they are the usual representation character equalities. No irreducibility, coefficient embedding, or cross-coefficient comparison is inferred from these equalities alone.
- `FiniteFieldCoverage s` quantifies every actual finite Galois subfield of the fixed algebraic closure, every finite forbidden set of sample indices, and every finite automorphism. `ExistentialArithmeticCoverage` additionally uses rational-prime indices, discriminant avoidance, and actual witnesses. The equivalence with any chosen witness family derives choice independence; the prime-occurrence assertion itself remains unproved.

## Dependency graph and argument check

1. **Prime and residue restriction.** `RingOfIntegers.mapRingHom` restricts the actual chosen field embedding to integral elements. Its underlying value is the field embedding, proving injectivity. `restriction_commutes` gives equivariance. The prime downstairs is literally the comap of Q along this map, so primality and injectivity of its induced quotient map are direct ideal-theoretic statements. Integer casts commute with the ring map, giving the same prime below. The nonzero integer p lies in the contracted ideal and remains nonzero in the characteristic-zero ring of integers. That prime is therefore nonzero and hence maximal in the Dedekind ring of integers. No integral lifting or residue surjectivity premise is inserted.

2. **Simultaneous arithmetic witnesses.** Equivariance transports stabilizer membership by the pointwise ideal action and transports inertia by its exact definition `γ • x - x ∈ Q`. Reducing the same identity transports the forward p-power residue action. The existence theorems use the preceding absolute integral Frobenius construction, then contract its actual ideal and restrict that same absolute element. They do not combine unrelated finite and infinite witnesses, and do not claim a prescribed finite automorphism occurs.

3. **Absolute choice comparison.** In a continuous profinite action on a discrete invariant integral algebra, the pinned transitivity theorem supplies one group element carrying any prime above a base prime to any other. Here the actual integral algebra and absolute Galois action satisfy these hypotheses by the predecessor construction. Conjugating γ and its prime transports the p-power residue equation in the correct order `a * γ * a⁻¹`. At the second prime the two residue actions agree; applying the inverse of its stabilizing Frobenius to their difference puts `γ_v⁻¹ * a * γ_u * a⁻¹` in its inertia. A representation killing that subgroup identifies the conjugated operators, and trace cyclicity gives equality of characters. Absolute inertia itself need not be trivial.

4. **Ramification and the finite exceptional set.** The actual finite Galois action on the ring of integers is obtained from its fraction-field action. The exact inertia-cardinality theorem applies to a finite flat integral extension with perfect base residue field. Those properties are supplied by the number-field ring-of-integers setting, rather than assumed arbitrarily. It identifies the cardinality of actual inertia with the actual ramification index. Index one gives a subgroup of cardinality one, hence the identity subgroup. The kernel of the decomposition action on the actual residue extension is precisely inertia, making this residue action faithful. Equality on all integral representatives therefore gives equality of the stabilizing field automorphisms. The nonzero integral discriminant has finitely many prime divisors; outside them the exact discriminant theorem proves `IsUnramifiedAt`, hence index one at every prime above p. This proves finiteness of ramified primes, not distribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes.

5. **Finite witness and trace independence.** Absolute inertia restricts into the inertia at the contracted prime by step 2. Step 4 kills this finite inertia outside the discriminant. Applying restriction to the absolute comparison of step 3 therefore gives conjugacy of the two finite automorphisms. Every factored representation kills the actual absolute inertia there and has witness-independent trace. The final proof binds the restriction as an explicit homomorphism to normalize Lean's definitionally equal absolute-group aliases; this changes neither the object nor the mathematical argument.

6. **Embedding independence.** For an actual normal subfield L of the fixed algebraic closure, any ℚ-embedding has image L. Restricting the chosen embedding gives one automorphism b of L with inclusion composed with b equal to that embedding. Applying the actual action commutation identities gives `b * q_chosen(σ) = q_canonical(σ) * b`, hence conjugacy by this same b for every σ. This closes the otherwise real mismatch between the arbitrary chosen embedding and the canonical subfield embedding used by the infinite Galois correspondence.

7. **Every finite quotient is represented by a field.** An open normal subgroup H of the actual absolute Galois group is closed. Its fixed field has fixing subgroup exactly H by the infinite Galois correspondence. Openness makes this field finite over ℚ; normality makes it Galois. Canonical restriction to that field has kernel H. Conversely, canonical restriction to each finite Galois subfield has open normal kernel. Restriction is surjective by extension of automorphisms to the normal algebraic closure.

8. **Exact conditional coverage equivalences.** Given finite-field coverage and an open normal H, use its actual fixed field from step 7. Lift a conjugating automorphism of that finite field to the absolute group. Its restriction identity shows the conjugated sample and the target lie in the same H-coset. Conversely, apply quotient coverage to the open kernel of canonical restriction and a lift of the prescribed finite automorphism. Thus neither direction silently strengthens coverage. Adding the field's finite discriminant primes to the forbidden set gives the unramified formulation. Steps 5 and 6 show an existential actual witness can be replaced by any chosen witness at the same p without changing the finite conjugacy class. Finally the predecessor cofinite-coverage theorem gives conjugacy density on each pair of cofinite good sets, retaining arbitrary further finite exclusions.
