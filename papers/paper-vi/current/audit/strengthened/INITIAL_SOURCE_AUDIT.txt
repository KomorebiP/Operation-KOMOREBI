DECISIVE FINDINGS

F1 — Holomorphic contours (strengthened thm:holomorphic_defect).
Raw wording permits a finite union of positively oriented enclosing Jordan curves without stating common-resolvent membership or winding number one. Take scalar A_f=1, A_c=0, C=1, g(z)=z and two concentric circles of radii 2 and 3. Each circle encloses both spectra. The stated left side is 1, while the two contour integrals sum to 2. Use an admissible common-resolvent contour of winding one around the union of spectra; for example, the positive boundary of finitely many pairwise disjoint bounded Jordan domains containing the spectra, inside the holomorphy region. The formula and norm estimate then follow by the stated resolvent identity and Dunford calculus. The exact-naturality corollary inherits this restriction.

F2 — Positive decay rate (strengthened thm:memory_self_energy).
The L1 estimate M/gamma requires gamma>0 (and a nonnegative bound M). The raw statement does not say this. For scalar B=0, coupling blocks 1, M=1, gamma=-1, its exponential assumption holds for t≥0 but K(t)=1 is not integrable and the proposed right side is negative. Explicit gamma>0 repairs the estimate.

F3 — Bounded spectral half-plane (strengthened thm:memory_self_energy).
The conclusion is valid for a bounded hidden operator on a complex Banach space, but the proof must bridge “right of the spectrum” to an exponential norm bound; the earlier Laplace result only assumes Re z>||B||. If the hidden space is zero, both kernel and self-energy are zero and the identity is immediate. Otherwise set s(B)=max Re Spec(B). For s(B)<beta<Re z, spectral mapping and the spectral-radius formula give ||exp(nB)||≤M_beta exp(beta n). Writing t=n+r, 0≤r<1, gives ||exp(tB)||≤M_beta exp(||B||+max(0,-beta)) exp(beta t). The Laplace integrand is integrable and decays to zero; integration of its derivative yields both inverse identities. This preserves the original bounded Banach scope. The zero-space exception was identified in root review of the proposed addition and relayed before final implementation. The spectral/growth implication was checked in Engel–Nagel, Chapter IV, Corollaries 3.11–3.12, especially 3.12(iv), printed p.281, author-hosted source listed below. It does not extend automatically to arbitrary strongly continuous semigroups with unbounded generators.

F4 — Finite-moment closure (strengthened thm:local_closure).
Equivalence of zero memory, zero self-energy, and autonomous retained propagation is valid for bounded blocks. Equivalence to the first d moment identities uses Cayley–Hamilton and requires dim X_Q=d<infinity. State this explicitly. For d=0, the moment list is empty and the hidden space contributes zero memory; this boundary is consistent. Interpret P(zI-A)^(-1)P=F_P(z)^(-1) after restriction to X_P, rather than equating operators on different spaces.

F5 — Killing versus transience (strengthened thm:killing_survival).
For Q Markov and V≥0, Q-D_V is a substochastic/killed generator, but absorption need not occur almost surely. V=0 leaves the original conservative chain. Replace “transient killed chain” by “killed/sub-Markov chain”; assert eventual absorption only with the spectral stability condition used in the moments theorem. The expectation identity and derivative of survival are correct without eventual absorption.

F6 — Eventual cause masses (strengthened thm:competing_risks).
Finite-time identities f=sum f_r and h=sum h_r need no new stability assumption. The inverse formula a_r^T(-A)^(-1)p0 requires strict spectral stability. The section's explicit entry/sign conditions admit A=0, for which the displayed inverse is undefined. Repeat Spec(A) contained in the open left half-plane for the eventual-mass formula. If “transient” was intended to encode this, write the condition explicitly to distinguish it from F5.

F7 — Product-topology inverse limit (earlier thm:projective_limit_semigroup and cor:projective_generators).
The earlier definition says continuous connecting maps, although the generator statement uses a vector-space limit. Use bounded linear connecting maps. Coordinate continuity then proves strong continuity in the product topology, and compatible coordinate derivatives prove the generator assertion. This is a different topology from the bounded supremum-norm inverse limit; it must remain a separately scoped retained result.

F8 — Rational memory realization (earlier thm:rational_memory_realization).
Partial-fraction Jordan blocks realize every strictly proper rational complex matrix function. The equivalence with a given time-domain kernel requires a usual uniqueness class: continuous matrix kernels of exponential order give pointwise equality; locally integrable kernels whose suitable exponential weighting is integrable give equality almost everywhere. Changing a kernel at one isolated time preserves its Laplace transform and refutes unrestricted pointwise equivalence. Ordinary kernels also exclude impulsive delta terms, whose transforms need not be strictly proper.

F9 — Auxiliary block dimensions (earlier cor:exponential_sum_memory).
If B_j and C_j factor through a d_j-dimensional space, the direct-sum state matrix uses lambda_j I_(d_j), not a literal one-dimensional scalar for every matrix summand. Repeated poles are realized by Jordan blocks. The theorem itself is sound with this dimension-correct proof.

F10 — Mild-solution class and conventions.
The Volterra proof uses continuous mild solutions and well-defined Bochner integrals; make strong continuity of S, continuity of x and xtilde, and integrability of g explicit. The estimate M_T B T epsilon exp(M_T L T) is correct. Minimum-transfer-order paths should include length-zero paths so diagonal blocks are covered. The scalar one-phase example inherits lambda>0 from the stable subgenerator setup; retain that setup in the standalone supplement.
