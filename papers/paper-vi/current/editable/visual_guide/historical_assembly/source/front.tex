\head{Three distinctions to keep visible}
A coordinate decomposition retains the whole vector if all components are kept. An aggregation map usually loses information. Elimination recovers exact retained dynamics only after adding a memory term and any hidden-state initial forcing.

An exact example checks its stated finite model. A theorem provides an argument under quantified assumptions. A Lean record certifies its declared formal statement under the recorded environment. None of these automatically certifies the other two.
\head{Preservation and revision}
The original 26 TikZ plates and the mathematical text of the first 28 visual pages are retained. Only editorial references, edition wording, and historical evidence descriptions have been updated. Fourteen new vector plates support the extended examples. All recovered source snapshots are included separately in the final source package.

The inherited formal baseline records 33 scoped results, 341 supporting theorem declarations, and 47 proof modules. The strengthened traditional branch is a separate written-proof collection. Its broader content is not added to the formal denominator.

Traditional Part III retains complementary results with an R prefix: reversibility and Dirichlet forms (R2), prior disintegrations and filtering (R3), potential perturbation and coarse Feynman--Kac transfer (R4), and finite realizations (R5). Related models appear on V--5--8, V--11, V--13, V--20, V--26, and V--39--40. The bounded-operator reciprocal result migrated from Paper IV is R7.2; V--25 and V--41 explain related finite matrix calculus. These models do not replace the supplementary hypotheses.
