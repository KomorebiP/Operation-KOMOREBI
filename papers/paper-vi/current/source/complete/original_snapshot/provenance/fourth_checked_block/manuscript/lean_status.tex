\subsection*{The three completed fractional connections}
\paragraph{Exact normalization and literal resolvents (10.3).}
For $0<\alpha<1$, the change of variables $x=t/(1+t)$ and Euler's reflection
formula give
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{1+t}\,dt
=B(\alpha,1-\alpha)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi\alpha)}.
\]
The weighted beta integral also gives
\[
\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}
\int_0^\infty\frac{t^\alpha}{(t+m)^2}\,dt
=\alpha m^{\alpha-1},\qquad m>0.
\]
Both scalar evaluations are proved in Lean. They are not normalization hypotheses.
The CFC representation uses the explicit positive measure
$(\sin(\pi\alpha)/\pi)\,dt$; its nonzero density transfers integrability to
Lebesgue measure. The positive resolvent is identified with the actual inverse
of $tI+A$, with both inverse laws and bounds $(t+m)^{-1}$ under an operator-order gap.

For $C:H_f\to H_c$, continuous bilinear composition passes through the Bochner
integrals. The rectangular resolvent identity gives the literal integrand
$t^\alpha(tI+B)^{-1}D(tI+A)^{-1}$, where $D=CA-BC$.
Its low- and high-parameter bounds are
\[
2\norm C\,t^{\alpha-1},\qquad \norm D\,t^{\alpha-2}.
\]
Splitting at $r=\norm D/(2\norm C)$ when both norms are positive gives exactly
$K_\alpha=2^{1-\alpha}\sin(\pi\alpha)/(\pi\alpha(1-\alpha))$.
The cases $C=0$ and $D=0$ are included. The weighted integral gives the exact
common-gap constant, and $D=0$ implies $D_\alpha=0$.
The integral, estimates and intertwining statements use actual nonnegative CFC powers.

\paragraph{Semigroup refinement (10.4).}
The real-time derivative of rectangular complex operator composition is proved
using the continuous bilinear composition map restricted to real scalars.
The fundamental theorem of calculus yields the stated negative Duhamel integral.
CFC proves contraction of $e^{-tA^\alpha}$ and the gap bound $e^{-tm^\alpha}$.
Substituting the checked estimate from 10.3 therefore gives
\[
\norm{Ce^{-tA^\alpha}-e^{-tB^\alpha}C}
\le t e^{-tm^\alpha}\alpha m^{\alpha-1}\norm D.
\]
No bound on the fractional defect is assumed in place of the generator bound.

\paragraph{Varying-space towers (10.5).}
The Hilbert space may depend on the level. Uniformly bounded coarsening maps and
$\sum_\ell\norm{D_\ell}^{\alpha}<\infty$ imply
$\sum_\ell\norm{D_{\alpha,\ell}}<\infty$ by the checked H\"older estimate.
Under a uniform positive gap, $\sum_\ell\norm{D_\ell}<\infty$ suffices instead.
For $t\in[0,T]$,
\[
\norm{C_\ell e^{-tA_{\ell+1}^\alpha}-e^{-tA_\ell^\alpha}C_\ell}
\le T\norm{D_{\alpha,\ell}}.
\]
This is a summable majorant independent of $t$, and Lean proves uniform convergence
of the real norm series. In a declared common operator space, the inherited
fixed-space theorem additionally proves convergence of the operator-valued series.
An operator sum across unrelated spaces is not asserted.

\subsection*{The two remaining partial results}
\paragraph{Memory and self-energy (13.2).}
The new complex proof establishes
\[
e^{-zt}e^{tA}=e^{t(A-zI)}
\]
and, if $\norm{e^{tA}}\le M e^{\omega t}$ with $\Re z>\omega$, proves Bochner
integrability, both inverse identities, and
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}Ue^{tA}V\,dt=U(zI-A)^{-1}V.
\]
For a normal operator, CFC derives the growth estimate from
$\Re\lambda\le\omega$ on the spectrum. For arbitrary finite \emph{nonnormal}
matrices, deriving growth from the spectral-abscissa condition remains unproved,
as does the final retained/hidden rectangular specialization. The stronger supplied
growth hypothesis is explicitly recorded; it does not complete the printed general theorem.

\paragraph{Holomorphic naturality (15.1).}
Continuity of the literal finite matrix resolvent and holomorphicity on the declared
neighborhood now supply integrability on each chosen common resolvent circle.
The actual contour integrals satisfy rectangular naturality. What remains is to
construct an admissible common contour in an arbitrary neighborhood of both spectra,
and prove contour independence and identification with the canonical holomorphic
functional calculus. The general statement therefore remains partial.
