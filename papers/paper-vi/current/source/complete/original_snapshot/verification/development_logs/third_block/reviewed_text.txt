Abstract
This paper develops the transfer-semigroup realization of the projector-resolved seed calculus. A
seed is propagated by a one-parameter semigroup, a Markov kernel, a normalized Bayesian transfer,
a fractional spectral power, a Green operator, or a potential-weighted Feynman–Kac operator.
Exact packet projectors resolve every transfer into input-output blocks, and the semigroup law
becomes block convolution over intermediate packets. Coarse and fine generators are compared by a
refinement defect. A Duhamel identity proves that this generator-level defect is exactly the source
of the corresponding semigroup defect and gives a quantitative stability bound. In the stochastic
realization, point-mass seeds generate random walks and continuous-time Markov evolution; exact
lumpability is the vanishing of the same refinement defect. Conditional expectation is treated as a
weighted orthogonal packet projector, with nested partitions producing martingale-difference packets.
Bayesian conditioning is a normalized nonlinear operator. Its independent-data composition law,
packet-evidence decomposition, stability bound, conjugate updates, and continuous likelihood flow
are proved explicitly. The Bayesian refinement conjecture is resolved in finite dimensions: for strictly
positive likelihoods and at least two coarse packets, exact commutation for every prior is equivalent
to blockwise-constant likelihoods up to global scale, and every failure factors through an explicit
information-loss packet. Fractional powers are constructed packetwise through the spectral projectors
of a positive operator, and the square-root semigroup is recovered by subordination. The fractional-
naturality conjecture is also resolved for bounded nonnegative self-adjoint refinement systems: the
fractional intertwining defect has an exact resolvent integral, a gap-free Hölder certificate, a sharper
common-gap estimate, and a Duhamel semigroup bound. Green kernels, finite-difference Poisson
problems, and second-order low-mode convergence provide the PDE realization. A finite-state
Feynman–Kac formula is proved by path enumeration. Exact Schur–Feshbach elimination shows
that discarded latent packets return as a frequency-dependent self-energy and, in time coordinates,
as a memory kernel. Survival and hazard functions are generated by transient semigroups. Nineteen
code-generated figures, machine-readable results, the original thirty-seven automated tests, and
seventeen added boundary-case checks accompany this reviewed edition. Three checked Lean blocks
verify 28 of the 33 theorem-like results in Appendix A. Finite-dimensional theorems, classical
infinite-dimensional extensions, numerical certificates, and open continuum claims are kept separate.

Scope and claim boundary. The paper proves the stated finite-dimensional semigroup,
packet, Markov, Bayesian, conditional-expectation, fractional spectral, Green-kernel, Feynman–
Kac, Schur–Feshbach, and survival identities. It also proves quantitative refinement-defect
formulas under explicit norm hypotheses, including exact transfer of a bounded generator defect
to every fractional power 0 < α < 1 for bounded nonnegative self-adjoint generators. It does
not claim that an arbitrary infinite-dimensional operator generates a semigroup, that every
stochastic process admits a finite packet representation, that every posterior family is conjugate,
that finite Bayesian sufficiency automatically extends to infinite disintegrations, or that every
PDE continuum limit follows from a finite test. Domain, closability, sectoriality, measurability,
tightness, and compactness hypotheses remain necessary in their respective infinite-dimensional
settings. Classical sources are retained in the separate end-of-series reference ledger rather than
reproduced in this paper.

Conclusion
The transfer-semigroup realization gives one common operator language to several subjects that are
often presented separately. Markov propagation is linear transfer of a probability seed. Conditional
expectation is stochastic projection. Bayesian conditioning is normalized nonlinear transfer, and its
exact finite coarsening law is now characterized by packetwise likelihood sufficiency. Fractional calculus
is spectral packet reweighting, and bounded positive refinement defects now transfer to fractional
powers and fractional semigroups by explicit resolvent and Duhamel estimates. A Green operator
reconstructs a field from source packets. Feynman–Kac propagation adds path weights to stochastic
transfer. Schur–Feshbach elimination shows that hidden packets are not destroyed; they return through
self-energy and memory. Survival and hazard are scalar reconstructions of a transient semigroup.
The central structural identity is
generator defect −→Duhamel semigroup defect −→controlled refinement error.
Together with exact packet reconstruction, this makes the semigroup paper the natural bridge from
the foundational calculus to the quantum, computational, and arithmetic realizations that follow.
