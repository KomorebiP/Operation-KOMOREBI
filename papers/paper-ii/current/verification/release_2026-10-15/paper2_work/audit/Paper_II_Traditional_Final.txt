Overview
A complete family of idempotents gives exact coordinates, but a single family does not record
scale or integral visibility. We study towers in which every coarse idempotent is the sum of its fine
descendants. The coordinate changes are invertible and coherent, and every operator block is the
sum of its refined blocks. Residue selectors give a model over arbitrary rings. When roots of unity
are available they agree with character averages, and coprime moduli factor through the Chinese re-
mainder theorem. In a complete filtered algebra, a diagonal operator plus positive weight transfers
has an inverse obtained by summing admissible weighted paths. The formula truncates exactly at
finite precision, while finite transfer graphs have rational path series. For a rational spectral decom-
position over a discrete valuation ring, resultants give denominator bounds. The exact denominator
is the largest Smith exponent of the lattice gluing module. This yields a necessary and sufficient pre-
cision delay, monotonicity under refinement, and a full congruence solving formula. We close with
exact formulas for the propagation of a coarsening defect through powers, resolvents, holomorphic
functions, exponentials, and multilevel towers.
This edition integrates the expanded mathematical narrative with the canonical v1.2.1 certificate level
formulations. It preserves the distinction between general written proofs, formal specifications, and
finite computational examples. The visual atlas and the separate Lean guide provide worked models
and the complete formal contract crosswalk.

1 Introduction
A complete family of pairwise annihilating idempotents decomposes a module into exact coordinate
pieces. Many problems require more than one such decomposition. A signal may be split first into
two residue classes and then into four. A filtered operator may be resolved into transfers that appear at
different valuation depths. A rational spectral splitting may exist over the fraction field while remaining
invisible on the original lattice. These are all refinement problems, but they involve different notions
of scale.
The first part of the article concerns exact refinement. If a fine packet family refines a coarse family,
then each coarse idempotent is the sum of its fine descendants. The coordinate spaces at the two levels
are canonically isomorphic because both represent the whole module. The refinement relation adds
more information. It tells us which fine coordinates belong to each coarse coordinate, and it gives a
compatible decomposition of every operator block. This is the algebra behind polyphase coordinates
and nested filter banks. Standard treatments of those subjects may be found in [9, 1].
The second part studies inversion in a complete filtered algebra. Suppose an operator has the form
𝑇= 𝐷+ 𝑁,
where 𝐷is invertible and every packet transfer in 𝑁raises the filtration by a positive amount. The
ordinary Neumann series can then be expanded into weighted paths. The weight is not the number
of edges. It is the total filtration gain along the path. At precision 𝐿, every path of weight at least 𝐿
vanishes. A finite transfer graph also gives a rational generating function for the number of paths of
each total weight. The relevant generating function facts are classical and are discussed in [8].
The third part concerns integral separation. Let 𝑉be a vector space over the fraction field of a discrete
valuation ring 𝒪, and let Λ be an 𝒪lattice in 𝑉. A rational projector 𝐸need not preserve Λ. Its separation
weight is the least 𝑠such that

A Bézout construction gives an upper bound through a polynomial resultant. That bound need not
be exact because it does not see the position of the lattice. The exact answer is controlled by the finite
module

where 𝑉= ⨁𝛼𝑉𝛼is the rational packet decomposition. The largest Smith exponent of this gluing
module is exactly the common denominator needed to make every packet projector integral. This in-
terpretation also proves that separation weight cannot decrease under refinement. Background on local
fields, Smith normal form, and matrix polynomials may be found in [7, 5, 2].
The last part records how a defect between fine and coarse operators propagates. If
Δ = 𝐶𝑇𝑓−𝑇𝑐𝐶,
then exact formulas describe the corresponding defect for powers, resolvents, holomorphic functions,
exponentials, and a chain of several refinement levels. These formulas are elementary, but collecting
them in one place makes the later analytic uses transparent. The spectral and resolvent background
follows the usual operator theory in [3].
Every theorem in this paper concerns linear operators. An exact refinement retains the full object and
changes its coordinates. Questions about nonlinear operation trees, nonlinear correction, and obstruc-
tion complexes require a separate theory.

2 Four kinds of depth
Several quantities below are measured by nonnegative integers. They do not measure the same thing.
Keeping them separate makes the later formulas easier to read.
The first is packet depth. A finer packet family has more idempotents and a more detailed address
set. The passage from residues modulo two to residues modulo four is an example. Packet depth is
combinatorial. It says how many distinctions have been made in the coordinate system.
The second is filtration depth. A transfer in 𝐹𝑟ℬis invisible modulo 𝐹𝑟ℬand becomes visible at greater
precision. Two paths with the same number of edges can have different filtration depths. A longer path
can appear earlier when its edges have smaller weights.
The third is separation depth. A rational packet projector may require multiplication by 𝜋𝑠before it
preserves the chosen lattice. This number depends on both the rational decomposition and the lattice.
It can be zero even when the corresponding eigenvalues are close, and it can be positive even when the
rational projector is simple.
The fourth is compatibility depth. A fine operator and a separately chosen coarse operator need not
commute with coarsening. Their discrepancy is
Δ = 𝐶𝑇𝑓−𝑇𝑐𝐶.
This is not a packet depth or a denominator. It measures the failure of one operator model to represent
another exactly.
The four notions meet in applications, but no one of them determines the others. An exact refinement
tower may have zero coordinate error while its fine spectral packets have positive lattice weight. A
filtered inverse may truncate exactly at a given depth while the coarse operator still has a nonzero
compatibility defect. The paper treats each quantity with its own theorem before combining them.
The central idea
Packet depth changes the address of a component. Filtration depth controls when a transfer sur-
vives a precision cutoff. Separation depth clears a rational denominator on a chosen lattice. Com-
patibility depth measures a discrepancy between two operator models. These quantities can in-
teract, but each needs its own hypothesis and its own calculation.

14 Scope and limits
The exact statements above have several deliberate boundaries.
First, refinement changes coordinates while preserving reconstruction. If a numerical method discards
fine packets, the resulting error must be estimated separately.
Second, the weighted inverse is linear. A nonlinear map creates higher order interactions among packet
coordinates. Those interactions are not contained in the path formula of theorem 5.2.
Third, a resultant is only a denominator bound. The exact weight depends on the lattice and is given
by theorem 8.2. The two examples in examples 7.4 and 8.10 show that the same eigenvalue separation
can give different integral packet weights.
Fourth, the dyadic sign example is a model rather than a classification of all phenomena at the prime
two. Character averages, trace splittings, quadratic refinements, and deformation projectors must each
be checked on their own lattices.
Finally, the defect formulas do not prove that a chosen coarse operator is a good approximation. They
say exactly how a known one step defect propagates. A useful approximation theorem must also bound
that defect in the problem under study.
