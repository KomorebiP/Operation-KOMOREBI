Abstract
A packet operator is a linear map written between the members of a finite direct sum. We study
the resulting computational representation before choosing a processor or kernel. Exact formulas are
given for block sparse application, block diagonal plus low rank algebra, Woodbury inversion, support
propagation, block preconditioning, and packetwise perturbation. Products of low rank corrections admit
optimal recompression through a small singular value problem, with explicit error propagation through
a product chain. A rooted candidate decomposition supports an exact compiler when representation
cost and error are additive. Dynamic programming gives the optimal Lagrangian pruning, an exact
quantized error budget algorithm, and a local update rule. Discrete precision assignment becomes a
multiple choice budget problem. Arithmetic, storage, data movement, launch count, discovery, transfer,
and materialization are kept as separate costs. A preserved corpus of 154 recorded CPU and GPU
measurements illustrates regular packet gains, precision frontiers, crossover behavior, and retained
negative controls. Its largest reported structured ratio is 14.89 against a device resident dense GPU
calculation, with archived matching-metadata limitations stated explicitly. The paper proves finite
algebraic and optimization statements. Hardware measurements remain specific to their recorded
environment, and no generic dense matrix exponent or universal discovery algorithm is claimed.

couplings, tensor factors, and transform coordinates can remove most of the arithmetic and storage
before a kernel is launched. The gain is mathematical first. A device can exploit only the structure
that the representation exposes.

The underlying linear algebra is classical. Direct and iterative methods are treated in [14, 25, 9].

attainable performance are shaped by data movement and arithmetic intensity [19, 27, 2, 12].
The first task is semantic. A compressed representation must specify the operator that it denotes. It
must also specify what an algorithm returns. A product represented by block and low rank factors is
not the same output as an explicit matrix. A timing comparison is valid only after the output contract
and timing scope agree.

The third task is combinatorial. Sparse transfer graphs predict possible support under powers,

The fourth task is numerical. Packet perturbations can be certified by their block sensitivities. A
continuous model assigns tolerances through a convex calculation. Upper bounds on available tolerances
produce a clipped allocation. A finite set of data types gives a multiple choice budget problem that can
be solved exactly after the errors are quantized.
The central computational result concerns compilation. Suppose a finite rooted tree records candidate
refinements. Each node may be kept as one representation or split into its children. If cost and error
are additive across the selected leaves, the optimal pruning is found exactly by dynamic programming.
A quantized error budget has a second exact dynamic program. The decision is stable when the score
gap exceeds the perturbation in the local models. A change inside one subtree requires recomputation
only along the ancestor chain. These results prove a restricted packet compiler. They do not solve
unrestricted discovery or arbitrary device scheduling.
The measured part uses a sanitized corpus collected on one recorded CPU and one NVIDIA GeForce
RTX 3070 Ti Laptop GPU. The preserved corpus contains 154 recorded rows from sixteen suites.
The timings are inherited observations; no GPU timing is rerun for this edition. Regular equal block
operators and block diagonal plus low rank operators show large gains. Recursive HODLR, Strassen
multiplication, iterative refinement, a small Walsh Hadamard transform, and an attempted two stream
overlap provide negative controls. These rows are retained because structure does not guarantee speed.

2.2
Output contracts
Definition 2.7 (Output contract). An output contract specifies the mathematical object returned by
an algorithm. The contract may require an explicit vector, an explicit matrix, block factors, low rank
factors, spectral factors, or an implicit operator with a declared application rule.
Definition 2.8 (Timing scope). A timing scope states which costs are included. Possible costs include
structure discovery, compilation, host transfer, device transfer, kernel execution, graph capture, graph
replay, factor reconstruction, and explicit materialization.
Proposition 2.9 (Contract conversion). Suppose one algorithm returns a compressed representation R
and another returns an explicit object Y . A direct timing comparison is valid only if
decode(R) = Y
and the time required by decode is included, or if the downstream task consumes R directly.
Proof. Without decoding, the two procedures return different mathematical objects. A timing ratio
between different outputs does not compare implementations of the same map. Including the decoding
cost gives two procedures with the same output. If the next operation consumes R, decoding is not
part of the declared computation.
Remark 2.10. A compressed product and an explicit matrix product may both be exact. They still
have different contracts. This distinction becomes decisive when materialization costs n2 entries.

Arithmetic, storage, and data movement
We count one scalar multiplication and one scalar addition as two modeled floating point operations.
These counts describe a declared event model for a representation. They do not predict wall time
without a memory and launch model. A multiply–accumulate charges two scalar operations even for
the first coefficient. Thin corrections accumulate into an already computed diagonal or sparse result.
Index metadata, temporary workspace, launch overhead, discovery, transfer, and materialization are
accounted for separately.

9.4

Definition 9.10 (Decision gap). At an internal node v, let
gv = |Kλ(v) −Sλ(v)| .
This is the gap between the keep and split scores after the child subproblems have been solved.
Theorem 9.11 (Compiler stability). Suppose the solved keep score and split score at every internal
node change in absolute value by less than gv/2. These are scores after child subproblems have been
solved, rather than unconstrained changes in raw local costs. Then every strict keep or split decision in
the original optimal pruning remains unchanged.
Proof. If the original keep score is lower by gv, then after perturbation it can increase by less than gv/2,
while the split score can decrease by less than gv/2. The order remains strict. The split case is the
same. Apply the argument from the leaves upward.
Theorem 9.12 (Local recompilation). Suppose local costs change only inside the subtree rooted at u,
while ancestor and sibling local models are unchanged. Use correct cached sibling values from the old
solve. Then every dynamic programming value outside that subtree and outside the ancestor chain from
u to the root remains unchanged. Recomputing the subtree at u and that ancestor chain gives the exact
new global optimum.
Proof. A node value depends only on its own local data and the values of its children. Nodes outside
the changed subtree and outside the ancestor chain have the same local data and the same child values.
Their values remain unchanged. Every affected dependency lies inside the changed subtree or on the
unique path from u to the root.
Remark 9.13 (Boundary of the compiler theorem). The exact compiler requires a finite candidate tree
and additive local models. Arbitrary overlapping decompositions, nonlocal cache interactions, global
rank coupling, and unrestricted structure discovery can destroy optimal substructure. No universal
approximation factor is proved for those settings.

10.1
Shape classes
Definition 10.1 (Shape class). Two active packet blocks lie in the same shape class when their input
dimension, output dimension, right hand side count, scalar type, accumulation type, and kernel contract
agree.
Proposition 10.2 (Launch reduction by shape). Suppose B independent active blocks fall into g shape
classes, and suppose the backend accepts one batched array or pointer list for each class. Grouped
execution then requires at most g launches instead of B, without changing the arithmetic or the output.
Proof. Place all blocks in one class along a batch dimension and invoke one batched kernel for that
class. Every block receives the same local computation. One launch per class is sufficient.
CUDA provides graph capture and replay for repeated dependency graphs [23]. These mechanisms
reduce repeated launch work, but they do not change the operator or its arithmetic count.

Benchmark contracts and measurement protocol
Performance data are meaningful only after the mathematical and timing contracts are fixed. The
protocol follows standard concerns from numerical linear algebra, floating point analysis, and machine
performance modeling [17, 14, 27].
Definition 11.1 (Case signature). A case signature records the dimensions, packet layout, rank, right
hand side count, random seed, source values, source type, reference computation, and output contract.
Definition 11.2 (Fair match). Two measured rows are a fair match when they share one case signature,
return the same declared output, use the same correctness reference, and have identical timing scopes.
If one row includes an extra conversion or setup cost, it is a different scope and must be reported
separately or added to both rows before comparison.
Proposition 11.3 (Fair matching is an equivalence relation). On rows with complete metadata, equality
of the case signature, output contract, correctness reference, and timing scope is an equivalence relation.
Proof. Equality of each metadata field is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. Their conjunction has
the same properties.
Definition 11.4 (Representation gain). Representation gain compares dense and structured work or
storage for the same operator, independently of the processor.
Definition 11.5 (Device gain). Device gain compares processors for the same representation, output,
and timing scope.
Definition 11.6 (Kernel gain). Kernel gain compares two implementations on the same device for the
same representation, output, and timing scope.
Definition 11.7 (Amortization gain). Amortization gain records the effect of one time discovery,
compilation, transfer, capture, or materialization at a declared reuse count.
A measured total speedup can mix these four effects. A multiplicative decomposition is only exact
when matched rows isolate the factors. Otherwise the decomposition is interpretive.
Remark 11.8 (Logical work rate). A structured method may report a dense equivalent logical work rate
greater than the physical dense throughput because it does not execute the dense operations. This
statistic describes avoided work. It is not a claim that the device performed those operations.

Implementation and verification
The release contains a small reference implementation of the exact operators and optimization algorithms
used in the paper. The mathematical interface is deliberately narrow. This Python implementation is a
numerical prototype, not extracted Lean code. Its stored-key support can retain zero-valued blocks,
whereas the exact support graph uses nonzero blocks. Its reduced numerical QR is not the exact
rank-compact range construction proved in Appendix F. Prototype tests and theorem proofs therefore
have different targets.
1. A packet layout records dimensions and coordinate slices.
2. A packet operator stores active blocks and evaluates them without materializing inactive blocks.
3. BDLR factors support exact application, product, solve, and small core recompression.
4. Graph utilities compute support products, path closure, Schur fill inclusion, and topological levels.
5. Tree dynamic programs solve the Lagrangian and quantized budget models.
6. A multiple choice dynamic program assigns discrete precision options.
7. Benchmark utilities audit case identity, output contracts, timing scopes, and retained negative
controls.
The strengthened release records a deterministic verifier with 37392 checks in nine groups. It covers
packet evaluation, adjoints, composition, structured primitives, recompression optimality, product chain
error, graph support, Schur fill, tree compilation, precision assignment, and the benchmark corpus. The
strengthened release records eleven passing unit tests and twenty three passing original-source tests for
BDLR operations, Krylov methods, mixed precision, measured data schema, and figure generation.

Hardware implications and exact boundaries
The finite results suggest a device interface with grouped block kernels, low rank contractions, packet
metadata, a transfer graph scheduler, and per edge precision tags. Such an interface may reduce
materialization and repeated launch work when the operator family remains closed under the chosen
representation.
The exact results stop at a finite candidate tree and a declared device model. Three broader problems
remain open.
1. A universal discovery algorithm would need to find a profitable packet structure from an arbitrary
operator and data distribution. The simultaneous spectral theorem and randomized low rank
approximation cover special cases only.
2. A universal compiler would need a controlled approximation factor when costs are nonlocal and
candidate decompositions overlap. The dynamic programs in theorems 9.4 and 9.8 do not cover
that setting.

Conclusion
A packet operator representation records which block maps exist, how they are stored, and what output
they denote. Exact block evaluation and composition turn the support graph into algebra. Arithmetic,
storage, and one pass data movement follow from the active block dimensions. Block diagonal plus low
rank operators remain closed under multiplication before recompression. Their inverse is reduced by
Woodbury, and their correction can be recompressed optimally through a small singular value problem.
Sparse transfer graphs predict path support, triangular schedule depth, and possible fill under
elimination. Packet diagonal structure gives local Krylov spaces and a certified block Jacobi condition
bound. Packetwise perturbations give a direct mixed precision certificate. Continuous tolerances follow
from a convex allocation, while finite data type choices lead to an exact multiple choice budget program.
A finite candidate refinement tree supports a genuine packet compiler. The Lagrangian optimum,
the quantized error budget optimum, and local recompilation are exact under additive hypotheses. This
is narrower than unrestricted automatic discovery, but it turns one broad compiler conjecture into a
proved algorithmic statement.
