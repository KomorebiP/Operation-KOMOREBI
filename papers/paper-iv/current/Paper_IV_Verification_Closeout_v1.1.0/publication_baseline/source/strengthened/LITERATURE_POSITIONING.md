# Literature Positioning

## 1. Classical foundations

The root filter and multisection formulas belong to classical generating function and special function theory.

Appell sequences, binomial type sequences, and Bell polynomial expansions belong to classical umbral and combinatorial analysis.

The relation between the convex hull of the frequencies of an exponential sum and its zero strips begins with work of Pólya and was developed by Langer and later authors. Moreno gives a detailed account of zero locations for exponential polynomials under additional frequency conditions. Levin's monograph supplies the broader entire function setting.

Hadamard factorization, zero exponents, canonical products, and indicator functions are standard entire function tools.

## 2. Position taken by the article

The manuscript does not claim that cyclic multisections, exponential sum zero strips, Appell sequences, or canonical products are new.

Its contribution is the exact organization of one symmetric family and the deductions proved from that organization. The most distinctive claims are the explicit large zero strings on the symmetry rays, their first exponentially small displacement, the resulting exact zero count, the trace class zero model, and the entire correction in the Hurwitz zeta comparison.

## 3. Historical boundary

A complete novelty audit has not been finished. In particular, the exact ray statement and first correction should be compared against the detailed exponential sum literature beyond the sources already cited.

The manuscript therefore uses restrained language. It says that symmetry permits an explicit specialization. It does not claim priority over the general zero distribution theory.
