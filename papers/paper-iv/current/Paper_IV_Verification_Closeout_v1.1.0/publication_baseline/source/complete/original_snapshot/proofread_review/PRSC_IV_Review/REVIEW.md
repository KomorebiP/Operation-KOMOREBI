# Paper IV: proofreading and manuscript–Lean review

**Source reviewed:** `PRSC_IV_v1.0.0(2).zip`  
**Manuscript:** *Projector-Resolved Seed Calculus IV: Cyclic Analytic Functions, Reciprocal Families, and Spectral Zero Data*, Komorebi P., version 1.0.0, September 5, 2026.  
**Review date:** September 5, 2026.

## Verdict

The manuscript is substantially up to date with the Lean development **bundled in this archive**. Its version, formalization ledger, mathematical register and final closure modules describe the same v1.0.0 development. I did not find evidence that the PDF is an older, partly formalized manuscript left behind after the latest Lean changes.

Several corrections should be made before treating the prose as final. The most important are the logarithmic-derivative wording, normalization of the analytic complex-power germ, a wrong set of indices in the written determinant proof, and the ledger’s use of “limit” where the result is a limsup. These are manuscript corrections or scope clarifications, not newly discovered failed Lean proofs.

**A fresh Lean verification did not run.** The release command stopped because `lake` is absent in this environment. The supplied successful build and kernel-audit records are evidence in the archive, not an independently reproduced build from this review. Consequently, this report is not a new kernel-verification certificate or an unconditional release-ready sign-off.

The accompanying PDF is clearly marked **proofread draft**. It contains the editorial corrections described below. No Lean source has been changed.

## Corrections applied to the draft

Locations below refer to the **original** PDF and original `paper/paper4.tex`. Page breaks can move in the draft. The source excerpts file preserves the relevant original passages and Lean declarations.

### P01. Logarithmic derivative: distinguish the denominator from its reciprocal

**Location:** page 4, section 2; source line 268.  
**Lean correspondence:** `AnalyticReciprocalLaws.lean`, theorem `reciprocal_fixed_log_derivative`, lines 56–64; the definition of the tangent in manuscript section 8.

Immediately after writing `S_N^(r) = D_N^(-r)`, the text says “Its logarithmic derivative is T_N.” This is ambiguous, and is incorrect for a general reciprocal order if “its” refers to `S_N^(r)`.

The identities are

\[
\mathcal T_N=-\frac{\mathcal D_N'}{\mathcal D_N},
\qquad
\frac{(\mathcal S_N^{(r)})'}{\mathcal S_N^{(r)}}=r\mathcal T_N.
\]

The draft now defines `T_N` explicitly and identifies it as the logarithmic derivative of the order-one reciprocal. No theorem or Lean proof needs to change for this correction.

### P02. Normalize the logarithm when identifying an analytic power with the formal germ

**Locations:** page 5, section 2, source line 290; page 17, definition 9.3, source line 1356.  
**Lean correspondence:** `NormalizedAnalyticPowers.lean`, `normalizedAnalyticPower` and `normalizedAnalyticPower_taylor`, lines 61–79.

A fixed logarithm is enough to define a branch-dependent analytic power. It is not enough, by itself, to identify that power with the particular formal series normalized at zero.

For that identification, the domain should contain zero and the logarithm of the unit should satisfy `L(0)=0`. Replacing a logarithm by `L+2πik` multiplies the power of `C` by `exp(2πikr)`. For example, with `r=1/2`, a logarithm taking the value `2πi` at zero gives constant value `−1` rather than the required `1`.

The Lean Taylor theorem uses the normalized analytic germ. The draft makes that normalization explicit in the introductory convention and in definition 9.3. The fixed-logarithm addition law itself remains valid with a consistently chosen logarithm; the correction concerns identification with the named normalized coefficient family.

### P03. Correct the indices in the determinant proof

**Location:** page 14, proof of theorem 7.6; source lines 1088–1097.  
**Lean correspondence:** `NewtonDeterminant.lean`, `newtonHessenberg` at lines 15–17, `newtonHessenberg_det` at lines 59–61, and `reciprocalOrdCoeff_newton_determinant` beginning at line 112.

The displayed determinant and recurrence agree with the corresponding Lean result. The prose explanation names the wrong superdiagonal entries: `−1,−2,…,−(k−1)`.

In a last-row expansion, the entry `p_k` is in column `n−k+1`. Its complementary triangular block contributes

\[
\prod_{\ell=n-k+1}^{n-1}(-\ell)
=(-1)^{k-1}\frac{(n-1)!}{(n-k)!},
\]

with empty product 1 when `k=1`. This sign cancels the cofactor sign. The draft specifies the last-row expansion and corrects the entries to `−(n−k+1),…,−(n−1)`.

This is a correction to the written proof explanation, not to the determinant formula. The Lean determinant argument is unaffected.

### P04. State the tangent expansion locally

**Location:** page 15, definition 8.1; source line 1177.  
**Lean correspondence:** `TangentBoundary.lean`, `analyticTransferTangent_hasSum_all_orders`, beginning at line 58.

The original sentence “Its block expansion has the form” does not state a convergence region. The draft says “Near the origin, its convergent block expansion has the form.” This matches the analytic result, which carries a zero-free disk condition. It avoids suggesting that a Taylor series automatically converges throughout the full meromorphic domain.

### P05. Introduce the indicator proof’s notation before use

**Location:** page 22, proof of theorem 11.4; source line 1746.

The proof uses `c_N` and `s_N` just before their definitions in the next paragraph. The draft states `c_N=cos(π/N)` and `s_N=sin(π/N)` at their first use. This is an expository correction only.

### P06. Repeat the inherited order restriction in the zero-zeta section

**Location:** page 33, opening of section 14; source line 2636.  
**Lean correspondence:** `ZeroZetaConvergence.lean`, `canonicalSignedDenominator_zeta_absolute_convergence` and `entireReducedDenominator_zeta_absolute_convergence`.

Section 11 already assumes `N≥2`, so this is not a newly discovered false theorem. However, the zero-zeta section is much easier to read independently if that assumption is repeated. Its precise convergence thresholds and pole statements are not order-one statements: at `N=1`, the signed denominator is `exp(−z)` and has no zeros.

The draft explicitly retains `N≥2` throughout section 14, matching the hypotheses of the corresponding Lean theorems.

### P07. Replace “indicator limit” with “indicator limsup formula”

**Location:** page 37, formalization ledger, row 11.2, 11.4–11.11; source line 2949.  
**Lean correspondence:** `IndicatorLimit.lean`, especially `canonicalSignedDenominator_indicator_nonzero_radii`, lines 113–116.

Theorem 11.4 correctly states a limsup. The ledger calls it an “indicator limit,” which can be read as asserting an ordinary limit even on tie directions. The draft uses “indicator limsup formula.” This preserves the precise statement actually proved.

### P08–P09. Small notation and source cleanup

On page 37, source line 2965, plain `D_N` in the product ledger is changed to the manuscript’s `\Dfun_N` notation. The unused `\statusarticle` macro, beginning at source line 115, is removed because it contains an obsolete “Lean formalization pending” message. **That macro was not invoked in the original manuscript and did not produce a pending label in the supplied PDF.** Its presence was not evidence that any result remained partial.

The version line is also marked “proofread draft” so this editorial artifact cannot be mistaken for a newly verified release.

## PDF currentness and reproducibility

The source successfully rebuilt twice, and both local clean builds produced the same hash. The shipped PDF nevertheless has a different binary hash:

```
Shipped: 079d9f517f10b6f30b752f4516f454c3ba4213e9d00732042677403e92374144
Rebuilt: a83664bf8a7aa2b565431d4d3c46396a9d9097f7cece7e5d7cd7805d32677b09
```

The release checker reports this as a stale PDF. The further comparison does **not** support interpreting that message as stale manuscript content: all 39 pages have exactly matching extracted text and exactly matching rendered pixels at 96 dpi. The shipped PDF identifies pdfTeX 1.40.25 and PDF 1.5, while the local rebuild identifies pdfTeX 1.40.26 and PDF 1.7. These are genuine environment/output-format differences. Binary equivalence across those environments is a separate requirement from content equivalence.

The reproduction notes pin Lean and Mathlib, but I did not find a pinned TeX distribution/engine specification in the release’s Markdown, TOML, JSON or shell metadata. For a strict canonical binary-PDF gate, pin the full relevant TeX environment and keep the canonical-build hash test. Do not silently relabel every hash mismatch as harmless: the content comparison was performed in this particular case and is recorded in `evidence/pdf_comparison.json`.

The original PDF and the draft were visually inspected. The final draft has 39 pages, preserves every one of the 95 mathematical label numbers, and compiles without LaTeX warnings or box warnings. It deliberately does not share the original PDF hash.

## What remains before a new release sign-off

The corrections are ready as an editorial draft, but acceptance into a release should be followed by regeneration of the paper, register and checksums, then execution of the complete release gate in the pinned Lean/Mathlib and canonical TeX environment. The saved v1.0.0 audit should not be represented as a fresh audit of a newly packaged release.

Imported standard fact 11.1 remains explicitly marked as imported. The checked growth results have their own formalized arguments. The paper’s further questions about all zeros, a complete displacement expansion and a functional equation have not been promoted to theorems. No conclusions about historical priority or unprovided newer Lean revisions are made here.
