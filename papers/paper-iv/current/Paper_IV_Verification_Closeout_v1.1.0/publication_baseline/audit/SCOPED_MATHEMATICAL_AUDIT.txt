OBLIGATION MATRIX AND DECISIVE CHECKS

A. Root filters, signed sections and companion model — PASSED.
Locators: thm:root_filter, prop:covariance, thm:cyclic_product, thm:exponential_cycle, thm:hypergeometric_form, thm:signed_cycle, thm:finite_exponential_representation, thm:companion_matrix_realization, cor:fundamental_matrix_entries, cor:companion_addition_inversion.
The character sum gives coefficient selection; finite projector resolution also works pointwise on a rotation-invariant domain. The hypergeometric factorial identity can be read directly as grouping the factors j+1,...,j+Nn into N residue classes, with (1)_n omitted. The signed shift has J^N=-I and cyclic vector e_0, so its minimal and characteristic polynomials are X^N+1. The wrap sign in each entry agrees with the path crossing. The N=1 exceptional determinant/Wronskian behavior is excluded by the stated N>=2 hypotheses, while the cycle itself is valid at N=1. The basis claim properly requires nonzero lambda.

B. Reciprocal, logarithmic, residue-section, Appell and theta branch — PASSED in the algebra subreview.
See algebra_review.txt and algebra_review.json for the exact claims and derivations. The order-one case gives D_1=e^(-z), P_(1,n)(r)=r^n, tangent=1 and theta=(-2u)^n. Normalized analytic powers require the logarithm to vanish at zero to recover the named formal series. All formal powers have unit constant term. The determinant proof's corrected last-row cofactor indices are preserved. Zero-moment interpretations depend on the product branch, not on finite regressions.

C. Growth, indicator and two-term confinement — PASSED.
Locators: prop:orders, thm:indicator_polygon, lem:two_term_dominance, lem:near_cancellation.
The exact order of Delta is 1/N and the finite exponential sum has type one. The indicator is a limsup, particularly on tie rays containing infinitely many zeros. Away from ties, compactness gives a uniform positive projection gap. For N>=3, sector zeros satisfy |Im z| <= log(N-1)/(2 sin(pi/N)). The nondominant exponents give a remainder bounded by C exp(-gamma Re z), with the same type of bound through two derivatives. These bounds work on any enlarged fixed strip after changing C. The near-cancellation logarithm estimate at |u|<=1/2 is sufficient and uniquely identifies the model center.

D. Large-zero localization, reality, uniqueness and signs — PASSED.
Locators: thm:large_zero_strings, thm:first_zero_correction, cor:N3_zero_correction, cor:case_two_zeros, cor:eventual_simplicity, cor:reduced_zeros, thm:zero_counting.
On a fixed disk around t_m the principal-log map t_m+log(1+R(z))/(2 i s_N) is a contraction for large m. The disk radius ensures the exponential/log equivalence without a hidden branch wrap. The derivative lower bound ensures simplicity. Conjugation of the unique zero forces reality; rotational symmetry and sector exhaustion give the full tail with unique ray/index. Differentiation produces the signed factor (-1)^(m+1), and the N=3 correction has the stated (-1)^m/sqrt(3) sign. The N=2 cosine case is separate, as required because cos(pi/2)=0. The z->z^N map is locally invertible at nonzero zeros, preserving multiplicity. Finite exceptions contribute only O(1) to the counts.

E. Genus-zero products, multiplicities and diagonal Fredholm specialization — PASSED under the stated classical factorization input.
Locators: thm:genus_zero_product, thm:trace_class_model, prop:normalized_section_product, thm:newton_sums, cor:mittag_leffler_tangent.
The subunit order excludes a nonconstant exponential factor; value one at zero fixes the constant. The full zero multiset includes finite exceptional zeros and repeats multiplicity. Reciprocal zero summability makes the diagonal operator bounded and trace class. Its finite-compression determinants are the finite scalar products, converging locally uniformly. The trace of the mth power is the absolutely convergent moment. This is the specified diagonal encoding, not a general resolvent theorem. The partial-fraction series converges normally away from poles by a reciprocal-zero majorant. The normalized section statement's N>=2 restriction governs its product and moment clauses; its order assertion also allows N=1. Source-level review also inspected the project's actual quotient-rigidity construction in SublinearHadamard, EntireQuotientGrowth and HadamardIdentification; the final identity does not assume an unproved growth certificate.

F. Nearest-pole coefficient asymptotics — PASSED with stated conditions.
Locators: thm:nearest_circle_expansion, cor:leading_dominant_zero, cor:orbit_reduced_asymptotic, cor:secant_asymptotic.
Subtracting all principal parts at the least modulus gives a holomorphic remainder up to the next zero modulus. Cauchy's estimate is used only at an intermediate radius. Positive integral r is essential for meromorphicity. The leading Laurent coefficient is (-rho D'(rho))^(-r). Orbit factors are identical at exponent m=Nn because rho D'(rho) is rotation-invariant. The lower-Laurent error is absent at r=1. The statement does not falsely turn a possibly cancelling leading sum into a ratio asymptotic. The separate secant specialization has nonzero equal contributions and the correct normalization.

G. Zero zeta, branches and derivative weighting — PASSED.
Locators: thm:zeta_convergence, thm:zeta_continuation, cor:exact_zeta_models, cor:branch_grouped_continuation, thm:derivative_weighted_convergence.
The counting constants establish the exact abscissae, including divergence of the absolute series at the boundary. On every compact s-set the tail difference is an exponential error times at most a polynomial in m, so it is normally summable. Finite exceptional terms and omitted lattice terms are entire. The reduced residue is 1/(N A_N); the absolute residue is N/A_N. The identity Z_abs(s)=N Z_red(s/N) respects multiplicities. Arbitrary branch choices occur only in the finite exceptional reduced set. The derivative-weighted series uses actual simple zeros, positive integral q, and arguments bounded in one fixed interval, giving a uniform polynomial numerator against exponential derivative decay for N>=3. N=2 is only asserted on Re(s)>1. No claim about a zeta functional equation is made.
