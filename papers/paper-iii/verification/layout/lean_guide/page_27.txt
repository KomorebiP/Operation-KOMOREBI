The output is Nonempty (ObstructionPacket residual candidate). This is a proposition asserting
that the certificate type has an inhabitant. It is not a computable function that enumerates packets
or finds one within a time bound.
Neither Packet nor Candidate needs a finite enumeration for this logical equivalence. The adjective
“finite” in the overall contract describes the walk sums, paths, packet resolutions, and operation-
tree syntax where finiteness is imposed. It should not be misread as an extra Fintype assumption
on this candidate theorem.
6.5 The quantifiers determine the conclusion
For one proposed candidate 𝑐, the theorem says
¬(∀𝑝, 𝑟(𝑐, 𝑝) = 0)
⟺
a certificate of 𝑟(𝑐, 𝑝) ≠0 exists.
To show that a global solution does not exist, one needs the additional assertion that every can-
didate fails, or an argument producing an obstruction for each candidate. To prove existence of
a global solution, one must produce a candidate with every residual zero. Neither conclusion
follows merely from the certificate interface.
The interface is nevertheless useful: it makes local failure portable and checkable once a witness
is supplied. The formal type records the residual, the candidate, the packet, and the exact nonzero
assertion. A later theorem can build on these data without confusing rejection of one candidate
with a universal impossibility result.
