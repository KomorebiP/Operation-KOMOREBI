theorem wordOp_append [Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] [Semiring R]
(first second : List E) :
wordOp G S (first ++ second) = wordOp G S second * wordOp G S first := by
induction first with
| nil => simp
| cons e rest ih =>
simp [wordOp, ih, Matrix.mul_assoc]
The operator for first ++ second is the operator for second multiplied by the operator for first.
The proof is list induction plus associativity. Its order is essential in a noncommutative coefficient
system.
The next theorem removes every word with a broken interface, not just a break at the start.

theorem wordOp_eq_zero_of_not_composable
[Fintype V] [DecidableEq V] [Semiring R]
(word : List E) (h : ¬ Composable G word) :
wordOp G S word = 0 := by
induction word with
| nil => simp [Composable] at h
| cons e rest ih =>
cases rest with
| nil => simp [Composable] at h
| cons f tail =>
simp only [Composable, not_and_or] at h
rcases h with hbreak | htail
· exact wordOp_eq_zero_of_first_break G S e f tail hbreak
· change wordOp G S (f :: tail) * edgeOp G S e = 0
rw [ih htail]
simp
The induction distinguishes a first-pair mismatch from a mismatch in the tail. The former invokes
the two-edge support result. The latter uses the induction hypothesis to make the tail operator zero.
Empty and singleton words cannot satisfy the noncomposability hypothesis, so those branches
eliminate a contradiction.
