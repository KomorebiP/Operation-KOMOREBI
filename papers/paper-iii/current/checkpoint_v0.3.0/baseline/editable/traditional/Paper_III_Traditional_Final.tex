\KomorebiOpeningNote{Begin with typed edge words and exact coordinate transport. A chosen connection introduces defects, holonomy, and packet obstruction witnesses. Part II develops nonlinear packet jets, tree expansions, correction complexes, filtered lifting, elimination, and smooth transport.

Part I retains the finite v1.0.1 formal contract. Part II gives the strengthened nonlinear and analytic development with ordinary proofs. Formal coverage stays attached to the released declarations. The visual atlas at the end of this volume provides twenty-four worked plates. The separate Lean guide explains the formal contract and its evidence.}
\begin{abstract}
Finite projector packets turn linear transfer into typed walks and arbitrary maps into exact coordinate representations. Canonical coordinate transport satisfies an exact cocycle law. A chosen connection can fail to compose, producing holonomy and packet obstruction witnesses. Nonlinear packet jets lead to higher composition, convergent rooted tree expansions, graded operations, and nonlinear refinement defects. A specified correction map produces a canonical cokernel obstruction. Relative exact sequences, mapping cones, Hilbert splitting, nonlinear correction, and filtered lifting describe how these obstructions behave. Exact elimination produces Schur formulas, and finite transport reduces gluing to fundamental cycle holonomy. For smooth orthogonal packet families, direct packet rotations converge in operator norm to canonical unitary transport. The finite formal contract and the broader ordinary proof development retain their separate scopes throughout.
\end{abstract}
This edition preserves the existing mathematical development and all ninety-five proof bodies. Worked examples and reading boxes explain how to use the constructions. The included Lean evidence comes from the preserved release. Current checks establish source consistency and replay finite computations.

\section{Visual atlas}\label{app:visual-atlas}
The plates give exact finite models for the algebraic constructions in this volume. Their stable labels run from V1 to V24. Each plate names the related statements in the paper. These models make the hypotheses and map directions visible. They do not extend the finite Lean contract to the analytic results of Part II.
\begin{readerroadmap}[title={Three routes through the atlas}]
For typed transfer, read V3 to V8. For coordinates, connections, and gauge, read V9 to V18, followed by V23. For candidate witnesses and trees, read V19 to V22, followed by V24. V1 and V2 introduce the objects and the questions they answer.
\end{readerroadmap}
Products act in traversal order. The first edge traversed is the rightmost operator. Rows represent targets and columns represent sources.
