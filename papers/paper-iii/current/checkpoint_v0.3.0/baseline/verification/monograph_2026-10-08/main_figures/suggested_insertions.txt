NEW FIGURE 1: PACKET JETS
Suggested placement: chapter 03, after the proof of cor:packet_taylor, before
the paragraph interpreting the nonzero jets as a directed hypergraph.

For a finite illustration, use the standard coordinate packets on
\(\mathbb R^2\) in both source and target, and take
\(F(x,y)=(x+xy,y+x^2)\). At the origin the first derivative is the identity.
The two Hessians are
\(H_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and
\(H_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
The mixed interaction in the first output uses both ordered input slots;
the repeated first-packet interaction in the second output has derivative
response \(2\). The Taylor factor \(1/2\) recovers the polynomial
coefficients exactly.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{packet_jet_worked_example.pdf}
\caption{An exact finite packet-jet model at the origin. The first derivative
is the identity. The two displayed Hessians give every second-order packet
response, including both ordered mixed inputs. Summation with the Taylor
factor \(1/2\) recovers \(xy\) and \(x^2\), and all higher jets vanish.}
\label{fig:packet-jet-worked-example}
\end{figure}

NEW FIGURE 2: CORRECTION PLANE
Suggested placement: chapter 04, after the proof of
thm:orthogonal_obstruction (the Euclidean splitting is a finite instance).
It also illustrates the earlier criterion thm:cokernel_obstruction.

For \(L:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2\), \(L(a,b)=(a,0)\), a defect
\(d=(2,3)\) has range component \((2,0)\) and orthogonal residual
\((0,3)\). The correction \(u=(-2,0)\) removes the range component but
leaves \(d+Lu=(0,3)\). The detector \(\ell(x,y)=y\) annihilates
\(\im L\), so \(\ell(d)=3\) proves that the cokernel class is nonzero.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{correction_cokernel_plane.pdf}
\caption{A finite correction datum in the Euclidean plane. For
\(L(a,b)=(a,0)\), the defect \(d=(2,3)\) splits into a correctable
horizontal component and the orthogonal representative \((0,3)\) of
\([d]\in\coker L\). The chosen scalar detector \(\ell(x,y)=y\)
has \(\ell L=0\) and \(\ell(d)=3\ne0\). The scalar representative
uses the displayed identification of the quotient with \(\mathbb R\).}
\label{fig:correction-cokernel-plane}
\end{figure}

NEW FIGURE 3: ROTATING PROJECTOR
Suggested placement: chapter 04, immediately after ex:rotating_line.

In \cref{ex:rotating_line}, the generator for the complete pair
\((P(\theta),I-P(\theta))\) is
\([P'(\theta),P(\theta)]
=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
For a step \(\delta\) with \(|\delta|<\pi/2\), the operator in
\cref{def:packet_direct_rotation} is
\(S=\cos\delta\,R(\delta)\); its positive polar normalization is
exactly \(R(\delta)\). Thus every displayed uniform mesh already
produces \(R(\pi/3)\), a special exact identity for this rotating-line
model within the general convergence theorem.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{rotating_projector_transport.pdf}
\caption{Exact transport for a rotating line and its orthogonal complement
from \(0\) to \(\pi/3\). The canonical generator is constant. For
\(|\delta|<\pi/2\), the direct polar rotation equals \(R(\delta)\).
The ordered products on the uniform meshes with \(2\), \(4\), and
\(8\) steps therefore all equal \(R(\pi/3)\) exactly. This finite
identity illustrates, but does not prove, the general convergence theorem.}
\label{fig:rotating-projector-transport}
\end{figure}
