READER’S MAP
Three meanings of more digits
Traditional companion: Section 1, p. 1;
Section 3, p. 12;
Section 4, p. 28
This guide follows two kinds of objects through the same arithmetic discipline: coefficient sequences
and polynomial equations. In the first, a valuation tells us which terms can be discarded at a chosen
precision. In the second, a residual digit tells us whether an approximate root can be corrected. The
common word “depth” does not make their indices interchangeable.

An address is not a modulus. A one-step lift is not yet an infinite branch.
One sustained route through the paper
Start with the Euler family defined by an actual reciprocal series. Keep track of the normalization,
learn to read a finite difference table, and then use valuation bounds to obtain periods and truncations.
The four dyadic packets provide an unusually sharp case: both their least periods and their completed
images are known.
Next, replace a coefficient difference with the residual of a polynomial. A Jacobian image decides one
lifting step. Its kernel describes the choices of digit. Compatibility and a nonsingularity hypothesis
then connect finite steps to an exact root and a local parameter chart.

Two easy confusions to prevent
The family address j is a denominator parameter. It is not the residue class of the nonzero output
degrees: the reciprocal has support at d = Nn. Also, the notation PN,j for a selector is distinct from
any similarly named source series in the arithmetic proof.
Whenever a formula moves by T packet rows, write the corresponding degree shift NT beside it.
Whenever a normalized coefficient is compared with an original coefficient, write U = j!C before taking
valuations.
Read this as: Read the ambient ring before the divisibility symbol. In the field Qp, every
nonzero scalar divides every scalar. Precision means ideal membership in Zp, or an explicitly
stated valuation bound.

Read the first disagreement
The binary digits of 1 and 9 first disagree at the 23 position. Their difference therefore has order three.

Each ball modulo pL splits into p residue balls modulo pL+1. A compatible lifting path chooses one
child at each stage. A theorem guaranteeing a child at the current stage need not guarantee a child at
every future stage.
Zero has unlimited precision
The value vp(0) is not zero. The mathematical convention is infinity. In the example checker, infinity
is stored by a separate value rather than a large cutoff. Thus an empty omitted tail is exactly zero,
not merely “very divisible.”
Read this as: More matching low-order digits means smaller p-adic distance. Every precision
claim in the appendix can be read as a claim about a shared residue ball.

Refinement keeps all children
An odd coefficient lies in either address one or address three modulo four. Keeping both children

additional restriction.

synchronization, not a theorem that arbitrary data at infinitely many primes come from one integer.
Selection is not compression of the index
The compressed subsequence bq = aNq+j is useful, but its index is q, not Nq + j. A shift by one in
the compressed subsequence advances by N in the original sequence. The recurrence-packet theorem
explicitly uses the jump matrix CN for precisely this reason.
Read this as: Exact reconstruction requires a complete address family. Finite CRT solves a
finite congruence problem; global descent is a different question, revisited on V – 28.

The residues are compatible
Reducing nr+1 modulo 6r gives nr. The two prime-adic limits are therefore zero in Z2 and one in Z3.

The topology explains the distinction
The diagonal image of Z in the product of its prime-adic completions is dense because every finite
collection of congruences can be solved. It is not the whole product. Finite synchronization naturally
constructs a completed object; identifying which tuples descend to a global object requires additional
data or a separate theorem.
