# Factorial-strengthened differences for all actual N=4 addresses

## Proven conclusion

For the normalized address-zero coefficients

    F_0(t)=[sum_(n>=0) t^(4n)/(4n)!]^(-1),
    C_0(n)=(4n)! [t^(4n)]F_0(t),

one has the mixed-step higher-difference estimate

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_0(n))
       >= k+v_2(k!)+sum_(r=1)^k v_2(u_r)

for every n,k>=0 and every positive integer step u_r. If a step is zero and k>0, the mixed difference vanishes. Empty mixed differences are integral.

In particular,

    v_2(Delta_u^k C_0(n)) >= k+v_2(k!)+k v_2(u).

The full positive-order conclusion is

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_j(n))
       >= k+v_2(k!)+sum_(r=1)^k v_2(u_r)+b_j,
    (b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)=(0,0,3,1),

for every j in {0,1,2,3}, n>=0, k>=1 and positive steps. The proof uses the classical Frobenius Euler congruence as an explicit external input. That input has been checked by the parent against the primary text listed below. Address one transfers from address zero; an elementary Bernoulli central-factorial argument proves address two; address three transfers from address two. The estimates hold from the first row and are uniform in the number and sizes of the steps.

## Classical input

Let E denote the ordinary Euler umbra, defined by

    L_E(E^d)=Euler_d,
    sum_d Euler_d t^d/d! = sech(t).

Its odd moments vanish. Frobenius's congruence gives

    v_2(Delta_1^m Euler_(2n))=m+v_2(m!)

for all n,m>=0, with the interpretation m=0 saying that the even Euler numbers are odd. Only the inequality at n=0 is needed for the main bound. It says that the linear functional on P=(E^2-1)/2 has integral divided-power moments:

    L_E(P^m/m!) in Z_2.

Use a second, independent ordinary Euler umbra F, and define Q=(F^2-1)/2. Then

    L_(E,F)(P^m Q^r/(m! r!)) in Z_2.

An extra odd power of either E or F makes the moment zero.

## Hyperbolic factorization and exact algebra

In K=Q_2(i), let a=(1+i)/2 and b=(1-i)/2. Although a and b each have valuation -1/2, the following exact identities remove their denominators:

    F_0(t)=2/(cosh(t)+cos(t))=sech(a t)sech(b t).

Thus the umbra U=aE+bF has moments

    L(U^(4n))=C_0(n).

Direct expansion gives

    U^2=EF+i(P-Q),

and consequently

    H=(U^4-1)/2
      = P+Q-P^2/2-Q^2/2+3PQ+iEF(P-Q).

These are identities in the polynomial ring over K with the definitions of P and Q substituted. In particular, the negative valuations of a and b do not remain in H.

## Integral divided-power algebra

Let R=Z_2[i], and let Gamma_R(P,Q) be the R-subalgebra of K[P,Q] generated by the divided powers

    gamma_m(P)=P^m/m!, gamma_r(Q)=Q^r/r!.

Adjoin symbols E,F with relations

    E^2=1+2P, F^2=1+2Q.

The resulting algebra A is an integral R-algebra, embedded in the corresponding polynomial umbral algebra. As a Gamma_R(P,Q)-module it is generated by 1,E,F,EF. The umbral functional takes all four kinds of generators to R:

    L(E^epsilon F^zeta gamma_m(P)gamma_r(Q)) in R,
    epsilon,zeta in {0,1}.

If either epsilon or zeta is one, the moment is zero because each polynomial in P is even in E and each polynomial in Q is even in F. If both are zero, integrality follows from the classical input and independence. Therefore L maps every element of A into R.

Every summand of H has all its divided powers in A:

1. P and Q are divided-power variables by definition.
2. For P^2/2,

       (P^2/2)^m/m! = [(2m)!/(2^m m!)] gamma_(2m)(P).

   The bracketed coefficient is an integer, counting pair partitions. The same holds for Q^2/2.
3. For 3PQ,

       (3PQ)^m/m! = 3^m m! gamma_m(P)gamma_m(Q).

4. P-Q has integral divided powers by the binomial expansion

       (P-Q)^m/m! = sum_(r=0)^m (-1)^r gamma_(m-r)(P)gamma_r(Q).

   Multiplication by iEF preserves that assertion: its m-th divided power is i^m(EF)^m gamma_m(P-Q), an element of A. Reduction of powers of E and F uses only the integral defining relations.

Finally, sums of elements with integral divided powers also have integral divided powers, since

    gamma_k(X+Y)=sum_(r=0)^k gamma_r(X)gamma_(k-r)(Y).

It follows that

    gamma_k(H)=H^k/k! belongs to A

for every k>=0. Applying L gives

    L((U^4-1)^k) in 2^k k! R.

That moment is rational, so membership in 2^k k!R implies the same valuation bound in Q_2.

## Starting index and mixed steps

Write W=U^4-1=2H. For ordinary forward differences,

    Delta_1^k C_0(n)=L(U^(4n) W^k)
                    =sum_(s=0)^n binom(n,s)L(W^(k+s)).

Every summand is divisible by 2^(k+s)(k+s)!, hence by 2^k k!. This proves all starting indices rather than just n=0.

For positive u_r, expand

    U^(4u_r)-1=(1+W)^u_r-1
              =sum_(m_r=1)^(u_r) binom(u_r,m_r)W^m_r.

Put M=sum m_r. A term in the product, with an extra base-index factor W^s from U^(4n), has valuation at least

    M+s+v_2((M+s)!)+sum_r v_2(binom(u_r,m_r)).

Using

    v_2(binom(u_r,m_r)) >= v_2(u_r)-v_2(m_r),
    m_r-1 >= v_2(m_r),
    M+s >= k,

the displayed lower bound is at least

    k+v_2(k!)+sum_r v_2(u_r).

All expansions are finite. This proves the stated mixed-step bound without a limiting argument.

## Address one through an exact tangent factor

Put

    A_1(t)=(sinh(t)+sin(t))/(2t),
    F_1(t)=A_1(t)^(-1),
    C_1(n)=(4n)! [t^(4n)]F_1(t).

The same constants a,b give the exact identity

    D(t)=A_1(t)F_0(t)
        = [tanh(a t)/(a t)+tanh(b t)/(b t)]/2.

Indeed, writing A=at and B=bt, one has

    sinh(t)+sin(t)=2b sinh(A)cosh(B)+2a cosh(A)sinh(B),
    cosh(t)+cos(t)=2cosh(A)cosh(B),
    ab=1/2.

Only degrees divisible by four survive in D, and its Hurwitz packet coefficients are

    d_i=(-1/4)^i 2^(4i+2)(2^(4i+2)-1)B_(4i+2)
             /[(4i+2)(4i+1)].

Here B_m are the ordinary Bernoulli numbers. Their elementary dyadic bound 2B_m in Z_2 gives

    v_2(d_i)>=2i.

For completeness, substitute u=exp(t)-1 in t/(exp(t)-1)=log(1+u)/u. The Stirling expansion gives

    B_m=sum_(r=0)^m (-1)^r r! S(m,r)/(r+1).

Every 2r!/(r+1) is 2-adically integral. If s=v_2(r+1)>0, then r>=2^s-1 and v_2(r!)>=2^s-1-s>=s-1, using 2^s>=2s; for s=0 the assertion is immediate. Thus 2B_m is integral directly, without invoking von Staudt-Clausen.

The i=0 coefficient is one. Write

    D(t)^(-1)=sum_(i>=0) h_i t^(4i)/(4i)!.

The Hurwitz inverse recurrence proves h_0=1 and v_2(h_i)>=2i by induction: every convolution term for index i has product valuation at least 2i, and the binomial multiplier is integral.

Since F_1=F_0 D^(-1), one obtains the exact sequence identity

    C_1(n)-C_0(n)=sum_(i>=1) h_i binom(4n,4i) C_0(n-i).

For a natural index n, terms i>n vanish. To use discrete products without a cutoff, extend C_0 continuously to Z_2 by the threshold-free Mahler theorem. Its factorial-strengthened differences also hold on Z_2 by continuity: approximate any 2-adic starting index by nonnegative integers. In this sense every term in the displayed infinite sum is defined on Z_2. The sum converges uniformly, because h_i is divisible by 4^i while both the binomial polynomial and the interpolated C_0 are Z_2-valued.

We now prove a stronger factorial difference bound for the polynomial

    P_i(n)=4^i binom(4n,4i), i>=1.

Its constant coefficient is zero. Its degree-d coefficient is

    a_(i,d)=4^i 4^d s(4i,d)/(4i)!,

where s(m,d) is a signed Stirling number of the first kind. In the coefficient formula for the falling factorial, every product contributing to s(4i,d) selects 4i-d factors among 1,...,4i-1. Of the 2i-1 even numbers in this list, at most d-1 can be omitted. Thus each product contains at least 2i-d even factors when d<=2i. Therefore

    v_2(s(4i,d)) >= max(0,2i-d).

Since v_2((4i)!)=4i-s_2(i), it follows that

    v_2(a_(i,d)) >= 2d-2i+s_2(i)+max(0,2i-d)
                    >= d+s_2(i)
                    >= d+1.

For k>=1, Delta_1^k n^d is an integer polynomial taking values divisible by k!, and it vanishes if d<k. One way to see this is to use the Stirling expansion n^d=sum_r S(d,r) r! binom(n,r), together with Delta_1^k binom(n,r)=binom(n,r-k). Consequently

    Delta_1^k P_i(n) belongs to 2^(k+1)k! Z_2.

The k=0 estimate P_i(n) in 2Z_2 also holds. Hence the same bound with the extra bit applies at every order k>=0.

The pointwise product of a sequence with differences in 2^(k+1)k! and a sequence with differences in 2^k k! retains the extra-bit estimate. The discrete product rule gives factors

    binom(k,r) [2^(r+1) r!] [2^(k-r)(k-r)!]
        =2^(k+1)k!.

Apply this to P_i(n) C_0(n-i), and then multiply by the integral scalar h_i/4^i. Uniform convergence permits summing the bounds. Thus

    v_2(Delta_1^k(C_1-C_0)(n)) >= k+v_2(k!)+1

for every k,n>=0 (and indeed for every starting point in Z_2).

Combining this with the address-zero result proves the factorial-strengthened bound at address one. The standard finite expansion of arbitrary positive step operators gives

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_1(n))
      >= k+v_2(k!)+sum_r v_2(u_r).

The difference C_1-C_0 has one additional bit in the same mixed-step estimate. Therefore any proved exact leading-unit result for C_0 would transfer automatically to C_1.

## An elementary Bernoulli divided-power lemma

Let Z be the ordinary Bernoulli umbra, L(Z^r)=B_r with B_1=-1/2. Its exponential transform is t/(exp(t)-1). Set X=Z(Z+1). Then

    L(X^m/m!) belongs to Z_2 for every m>=0.

Here is a finite algebraic proof. Define

    P_r(X)=product_(a=0)^(r-1) [X-a(a+1)].

Substituting X=Z(Z+1) gives

    P_r(X)=product_(a=0)^(r-1) (Z-a)(Z+a+1)
          =(2r)! binom(Z+r,2r).

The Bernoulli generating transform gives the formal identity

    L((1+T)^Z)=log(1+T)/T,

so L(binom(Z,j))=(-1)^j/(j+1). Vandermonde's identity therefore yields

    L(binom(Z+r,2r))
      =(-1)^r sum_(q=0)^r (-1)^q binom(r,q)/(r+q+1)
      =(-1)^r (r!)^2/(2r+1)!.

The last equality follows by expanding the finite beta integral integral_0^1 t^r(1-t)^r dt; it is an ordinary rational identity and makes no claim about positivity of a p-adic measure. Consequently,

    L(P_r(X))=(-1)^r (r!)^2/(2r+1).

Expand X^m=sum_(r=0)^m T(m,r)P_r(X). Since

    X P_r(X)=P_(r+1)(X)+r(r+1)P_r(X),

the integer coefficients satisfy

    T(m+1,r)=T(m,r-1)+r(r+1)T(m,r).

Induction, using that every r(r+1) is even, proves

    2^(m-r) divides T(m,r) for 0<=r<=m.

For m>0, the r=0 term vanishes. Each remaining evaluated term in L(X^m/m!) has valuation at least

    m-r+2v_2(r!)-v_2(m!)=r+s_2(m)-2s_2(r)>=0.

Indeed s_2(m)>=1, and r>=2s_2(r)-1 for r>=1. The latter bound follows from r>=2^(s_2(r))-1 and 2^a>=2a for a>=1. The case m=0 is L(1)=1. This proves the lemma without importing a generalized Bernoulli congruence.

## Address two from the Bernoulli lemma

The umbra B=2Z+1 has exponential transform

    exp(t) [2t/(exp(2t)-1)] = t/sinh(t).

Its odd moments vanish and, setting P=(B^2-1)/4, one has P=Z(Z+1). The lemma shows that all divided-power moments L(P^m/m!) are integral. Introduce an independent second copy D and Q=(D^2-1)/4.

For the actual normalized address-two series,

    F_2(t)=t^2/[cosh(t)-cos(t)]
          =[at/sinh(at)] [bt/sinh(bt)],

the umbra U=aB+bD has moments L(U^(4n))=C_2(n). Direct algebra gives

    (U^4-1)/4
       =P+Q-P^2-Q^2+6PQ+iBD(P-Q).

Work in the divided-power algebra generated by P,Q, adjoining B,D with B^2=1+4P and D^2=1+4Q. The umbral functional is integral on its module basis 1,B,D,BD, because odd B or D moments vanish. Every summand of the displayed expression has integral divided powers, by the same arguments used at address zero (P^2 here is even easier than P^2/2). Consequently,

    L((U^4-1)^m) belongs to 4^m m! Z_2.

Expanding the base index U^(4n) gives the universal stronger estimate

    v_2(Delta_1^k C_2(n))>=2k+v_2(k!)

for all k,n>=0. The k=0 statement is ordinary integrality.

To obtain the requested three extra bits at every positive order, only the first two exact Newton coefficients are additionally needed. From the actual source recurrence,

    C_2(0)=1, C_2(1)=-1/15, C_2(2)=13/45,
    a_1=Delta_1 C_2(0)=-16/15,
    a_2=Delta_1^2 C_2(0)=64/45.

Thus v_2(a_1)=4 and v_2(a_2)=6.

For m>=3, the divided-power estimate gives v_2(a_m)>=2m+v_2(m!). The Newton formula

    Delta_1^k C_2(n)=sum_(m>=k) a_m binom(n,m-k)

is finite at every natural n. It therefore supplies the positive-order estimate

    v_2(Delta_1^k C_2(n))>=k+v_2(k!)+3

For k>=3 this follows directly from 2k>=k+3; for k=1,2 the exact low coefficients and the tail bound settle it. Arbitrary positive mixed steps then obey

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_2(n))
      >=k+v_2(k!)+sum_r v_2(u_r)+3,

for k>=1, by the same finite step-operator expansion as before. No extra-bit statement is intended for order zero.

## Address three through an exact cotangent factor

The normalized address-three source and reciprocal are

    A_3(t)=3[sinh(t)-sin(t)]/t^3,
    F_3(t)=A_3(t)^(-1).

Using the same constants a,b, exact addition identities yield

    sinh(t)-sin(t)=2a sinh(at)cosh(bt)+2b cosh(at)sinh(bt),
    cosh(t)-cos(t)=2sinh(at)sinh(bt).

Consequently,

    D_3(t)=A_3(t)F_2(t)
           =(3/2)[coth(at)/(at)+coth(bt)/(bt)].

This apparent Laurent expression is a power series: the polar terms cancel because a^(-2)+b^(-2)=0. Its constant coefficient is one, and every degree not divisible by four cancels. Its actual Hurwitz packet coefficients are

    d_i=3(-1/4)^i 2^(4i+2) B_(4i+2)/[(4i+2)(4i+1)].

Again v_2(d_i)>=2i, by the elementary Bernoulli bound just proved. Thus the inverse Hurwitz packets h_i of D_3^(-1) satisfy v_2(h_i)>=2i. The exact coefficient identity F_3=F_2 D_3^(-1) gives

    C_3(n)-C_2(n)=sum_(i>=1) h_i binom(4n,4i) C_2(n-i).

The scaled-binomial argument proved for address one applies unchanged: P_i(n)=4^i binom(4n,4i) has differences divisible by 2^(k+1)k! at every order including zero; C_2 has the basic factorial bound 2^k k! at every order including zero; their pointwise product retains the extra bit. The continuous extension of C_2 exists by the threshold-free theorem, and uniform convergence is provided by 4^i. It follows that

    v_2(Delta_1^k(C_3-C_2)(n))>=k+v_2(k!)+1

for all k,n>=0. Since C_2 itself has three additional bits at every positive difference order, one concludes

    v_2(Delta_1^k C_3(n))>=k+v_2(k!)+1

for every n>=0 and k>=1. The finite mixed-step expansion adds sum v_2(u_r), proving the remaining address of the full theorem.

The cotangent expansion and tangent expansion used in the transfer arguments are formal Bernoulli identities obtainable directly from t/(exp(t)-1). Their required valuation input is only 2B_m in Z_2, proved above from the Stirling formula. Thus the sole external arithmetic theorem used by this file is the classical Frobenius Euler congruence.

## Exact leading units for addresses zero and one

The parity of the address-zero divided-power moment can also be proved. Use the Euler variables of that proof, and put

    X=P+Q, tau=iEF, epsilon=1+tau.

Recall H=(U^4-1)/2. One has the exact decomposition

    H=gamma_2(X)+epsilon X+2G,
    G=-gamma_2(P)-gamma_2(Q)+PQ-tau Q.

Every summand of G has integral divided powers. Thus gamma_k(H) is congruent modulo 2A to gamma_k(K), where K=gamma_2(X)+epsilon X. In addition,

    epsilon^2=2(tau-P-Q-2PQ),

so epsilon^j belongs to 2A for j>=2. For every r>=0,

    gamma_r(gamma_2(X))
      =[(2r)!/(2^r r!)] gamma_(2r)(X),

and the bracketed integer is odd. The divided-power addition identity for K therefore gives, for k>=1,

    gamma_k(H) congruent to
       gamma_(2k)(X)+epsilon gamma_(2k-1)(X) modulo 2A.

In the second term the multiplication coefficient is binom(2k-1,1)=2k-1, again odd. Frobenius's exact valuation says that L(gamma_m(P)) and L(gamma_m(Q)) are odd for every m>=0. Independence then yields

    L(gamma_m(P+Q)) congruent to m+1 modulo 2.

The tau-weighted part has zero moment because it has an odd E and an odd F factor. Consequently,

    L(gamma_(2k)(X)) congruent to 1,
    L(epsilon gamma_(2k-1)(X)) congruent to 0,

and L(gamma_k(H)) is odd for every k>=0. Hence

    v_2(Delta_1^k C_0(0))=k+v_2(k!).

At a general starting index, the s>0 terms in the base-index expansion are divisible by one extra bit after division by 2^k k!, so the same equality holds for every n>=0. The already-proved extra-bit estimate for C_1-C_0 transfers the equality to address one:

    v_2(Delta_1^k C_j(n))=k+v_2(k!), j=0,1.

### Homogeneous steps and the mixed leading parity

For positive steps u_1,...,u_k, expand each Delta_(u_r) as a polynomial in D=Delta_1. A multi-index (m_1,...,m_k), with every m_r>=1, has total M=sum m_r. The valuation bound for the corresponding term exceeds or equals the base precision

    L=k+v_2(k!)+sum_r v_2(u_r)

by at least

    [M-k-sum_r v_2(m_r)]+[v_2(M!)-v_2(k!)].

The first bracket is nonnegative, and it vanishes only if every m_r is one or two. If there are at least two occurrences of two, the second bracket is positive because the factorial interval contains an even integer. If there is exactly one two, the second bracket vanishes only when k is even. In that sole exceptional case, the binomial coefficient has the minimally allowed valuation only if the associated u_r is even. Its normalized residue is one.

Thus for j=0,1, the mixed difference divided by 2^L has parity

    1, if k is odd;
    1+e modulo 2, if k is even,

where e is the number of even steps. This establishes an exact valuation L whenever k is odd or e is even. If k is even and e is odd, the leading terms cancel and the valuation is at least L+1; no exact further valuation is claimed.

For homogeneous steps u_1=...=u_k=u, e is either zero or k. It is therefore always even when k is even. The universal exact formula is

    v_2(Delta_u^k C_j(n))
      =k+v_2(k!)+k v_2(u), j=0,1,

for all positive u,k and all n>=0.

## A stronger address-two filtration

In the Bernoulli address-two proof, put X=P+Q, tau=iBD and epsilon=1+tau. For H=(U^4-1)/4 one has

    H=epsilon X+2G,
    G=-gamma_2(P)-gamma_2(Q)+3PQ-tau Q,
    epsilon^2=2(tau-2P-2Q-8PQ).

Again G has integral divided powers. The expansion

    gamma_k(H)=sum_(r=0)^k 2^r gamma_r(G)
                       epsilon^(k-r) gamma_(k-r)(X)

shows membership in 2^(floor(k/2))A, since every summand has valuation at least r+floor((k-r)/2)>=floor(k/2). For k=1, L(epsilon X)=L(P)+L(Q) is even because the two terms are equal and the tau part vanishes. Thus the moment has at least one extra bit at every positive order, and at least floor(k/2) extra bits at order k.

In particular, the universal mixed-step estimate improves to

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_2(n))
       >=2k+v_2(k!)+floor(k/2)+sum_r v_2(u_r)

for k>=1. The earlier three-extra-bit bound remains useful at the lowest orders, so one may take the maximum of the two bounds. This estimate alone is a lower bound; the appendices below prove exact valuations at power-of-two orders.

## Analytic interpolation and explicit Taylor coefficient bounds

Let a_m=Delta_1^m C_j(0). The proved factorial bounds imply

    v_2(a_m/m!)>=m+b_j, m>=1,
    (b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)=(0,0,3,1).

Expanding the falling factorial in the Newton interpolation gives

    C_j(x)=1+sum_(ell>=1) d_(j,ell) x^ell,
    d_(j,ell)=sum_(m>=ell) a_m s(m,ell)/m!,

where s(m,ell) are integral signed Stirling coefficients. The coefficient sums converge because every summand has valuation at least m+b_j. Thus

    v_2(d_(j,ell))>=ell+b_j.

The interchange of sums is justified on Z_2 by these uniform geometric coefficient bounds. This power series agrees with the continuous interpolation and converges throughout |x|_2<2. It gives a genuine analytic interpolation, rather than inferring analyticity from the earlier threshold-free bound alone.

At address two, the stronger filtration gives

    v_2(d_(2,ell))>=max(2ell+floor(ell/2),ell+3).

Therefore its Taylor series converges throughout |x|_2<2^(5/2). These estimates give guaranteed domains; optimality for addresses zero, one and two is proved below.

### Exact Taylor radius at addresses zero and one

For j=0,1, the proved exact Newton valuation is v_2(a_m)=m+v_2(m!). In the Taylor coefficient sum, the m=ell summand has valuation exactly ell because s(ell,ell)=1, while every summand with m>ell has valuation at least ell+1. Hence

    v_2(d_(j,ell))=ell for every ell>=1, j=0,1.

The ordinary Taylor radius is therefore exactly 2. At any x_0 in Z_2, the translated coefficient of y^ell is

    sum_(m>=ell) d_(j,m) binom(m,ell) x_0^(m-ell).

Its m=ell term again has valuation ell and all later terms at least ell+1, so its valuation is also exactly ell. The Taylor radius is exactly 2 at every integral center. At any argument with |y|_2=2, each positive-degree term has valuation zero, so the Taylor series diverges there; this boundary assertion concerns that ordinary Taylor expansion, not every possible analytic continuation.

### Exact address-two radius from an infinite subsequence

The stronger address-two radius is also optimal. Let mu_m=L(gamma_m(P)) for the Bernoulli variable P=Z(Z+1). The central-factorial proof above gives the stronger moment estimate

    v_2(mu_m)>=s_2(m)-1, m>=1.

Indeed every summand there has valuation at least s_2(m)+r-2s_2(r), and r-2s_2(r)>=-1. It follows that mu_m is even unless m is a power of two. At a power of two, only the r=1 and r=3 terms can contribute an odd residue; all other r have r-2s_2(r)>=0.

The r=1 term is odd, because T(m,1)=2^(m-1) and v_2(m!)=m-1 when m is a power of two. For m>=4 even, the r=3 term is even: the central-factorial recurrence gives

    T(m,3)/2^(m-3)=h_(m-3)(1,3,6),

where h_d denotes a complete homogeneous polynomial. Its generating series modulo two is

    1/[(1-t)(1-3t)(1-6t)] congruent to 1/(1-t)^2,

so the indicated coefficient has parity m-2=0. For m=1,2 the r=3 term is absent and the moments are directly odd. Including mu_0=1, one obtains

    mu_m congruent to 1 modulo 2
      iff m=0 or m is a power of two.

Now take k=2^r>=4, and put q=k/2, which is even. In the address-two notation epsilon^2=2S, with

    S=tau-2X-8PQ congruent to tau modulo 2.

In the expansion of gamma_k(H)/2^q, only the terms with auxiliary index zero or one can contribute modulo two. Their sum is

    S^q gamma_k(X)+epsilon S^(q-1) G gamma_(k-1)(X).

Since tau^2 is one modulo two, q is even, and q-1 is odd, this reduces to

    gamma_k(X)+epsilon G gamma_(k-1)(X).

Modulo two, G=gamma_2(X)+tau Q. Terms with a single tau factor vanish under the umbral functional, so the second evaluated term reduces to

    L([gamma_2(X)+Q] gamma_(k-1)(X)).

The gamma_2(X) product has coefficient binom(k+1,2), which is even because k is divisible by four. The Q product has expansion

    L(Q gamma_(k-1)(P+Q))
      =sum_(a=0)^(k-1) (k-a) mu_a mu_(k-a).

Its possible odd terms require k-a=1, because all other odd positive indices have even mu. Then a=k-1 has even mu for every k>=4 divisible by four. Hence this contribution is zero modulo two.

Finally,

    L(gamma_k(P+Q))=sum_(a=0)^k mu_a mu_(k-a).

The generating series for its parities is

    [1+t+t^2+t^4+t^8+...]^2
      =1+t^2+t^4+t^8+... in F_2[[t]],

and its coefficient is one for every k=2^r>=4. This proves

    v_2(L(gamma_k(H)))=k/2,
    v_2(Delta_1^k C_2(0))=2k+v_2(k!)+k/2

on that infinite subsequence.

At the corresponding Taylor degrees k, the m=k term is the unique term with valuation 2k+k/2: every m>k term has strictly greater lower bound 2m+floor(m/2). Therefore

    v_2(d_(2,k))=5k/2 for k=2^r>=4.

Together with the universal lower coefficient bound, this establishes an ordinary Taylor radius exactly 2^(5/2). At any integral center, translation retains these unique leading coefficients at the same degrees, so the radius is exactly 2^(5/2) there as well. Over extensions containing an argument of that absolute value, the terms on this subsequence do not tend to zero, so the Taylor series diverges on that boundary. No complete exact valuation law at address two is claimed.

### Exact mixed differences at power-of-two orders, address two

Inserting an arbitrary starting index in the divided-power expression contributes only corrections with at least one additional bit relative to the displayed leading term. Thus the unit-step equality at k=2^r>=4 holds for every starting index.

For arbitrary positive mixed steps, the leading term in the step-operator expansion is the product of the steps times Delta_1^k. A higher multi-index of total M>k has extra valuation at least

    2(M-k)+floor(M/2)-floor(k/2)
      +v_2(M!)-v_2(k!)-sum_r v_2(m_r)
      >=M-k>=1.

Hence the leading term cannot cancel, giving

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)...Delta_(u_k) C_2(n))
      =2k+v_2(k!)+k/2+sum_r v_2(u_r)

for every n>=0, every k=2^r>=4 and all positive mixed steps.

The low orders also have exact formulas. At k=1 the Newton coefficient a_1 has valuation four and the tail begins at valuation six, giving exact mixed precision 4+v_2(u_1). At k=2 the coefficient a_2 has valuation six and the next coefficient has lower bound eight. A higher step-operator term of total order three can lose at most one bit through its binomial coefficient, so it remains strictly above six; all later terms have still greater lower bound. Thus

    v_2(Delta_(u_1)Delta_(u_2) C_2(n))
      =6+v_2(u_1)+v_2(u_2).

These exact statements cover every positive power-of-two order. They do not assert exact valuations at the remaining orders.
