## Inputs do not become conclusions

The central arithmetic route starts with a represented residual problem, a supplied applicable patching package and full patched support. It descends that support to an actual finite-level module or complex. A comparison of support does not alone supply a Hecke point over the original coefficient field: the compatible point y with y composed with f equal to x remains explicit. A surviving eigensystem is not automatically classical. The selected realization input must apply to that eigensystem, supply the occurring object and its attached representation, and identify the same Frobenius polynomials as the original specialization. Continuity, Hausdorff coefficient topology, density, semisimplification and Brauer–Nesbitt inputs remain explicit.

Theorem 10.5 constructs compatible continuous representatives from admissible finite conjugacy classes only under its coefficient completeness, separatedness, finite-quotient, continuity, nonempty-level, integral-spine and every-frame polynomial-relation hypotheses. It neither supplies arbitrary missing finite classes nor proves that every arithmetic local condition is a polynomial ideal.

Propagation compares two already existing compatible families attached to the same automorphic object and common algebraic spine. It does not construct missing companions. Augmentation at coefficient prime two does not remove residual-image, local-geometry or classicality hypotheses. No proof of Paper XI's general Conjecture 66 is assumed.
