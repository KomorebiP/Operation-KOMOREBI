Seeing the objects
Patching Support, Hecke Packets, and Conditional Automorphic Completion

The paper repeatedly asks three different questions: does an object exist, where is a supplied object
nonzero, and when does that object have the desired arithmetic meaning? This guide makes those
distinctions tangible through examples that can be followed by hand.
Existence
Is there a represented deformation problem, a nonzero carrier, or a compatible
family to start with?
Support
At which prime ideals does the supplied module or complex survive?
Realization
Does the surviving eigensystem come from a classical automorphic object with
the required attached representation?
Three models, rather than one overloaded analogy
Model
What it lets you see
Pages

Conditions on power series, infinitesimal points, obstructed lifts,
and compatible precision levels.
V – 3–V – 5
A presented module
Matrix rank, support, Fitting ideals, changes of coordinates,
and nonreduced structure.
V – 6–V – 8
A finite Artin algebra
Generalized eigenspaces, nilpotent directions, canonical packets,
and empty packet modules.
V – 12–V – 14
The models are connected by the paper’s theorems, but they are not the same ring. A polynomial
coordinate plane is not a complete local spectrum, and a local branch is not an Artin product factor.
Explicitly changing models prevents a picture from silently changing the hypotheses.
A route through the guide
Use the object atlas on V – 2 when a symbol becomes unfamiliar. The local model starts on V – 3; the
matrix model on V – 6. Pages V – 9–V – 11 explain what quotients, patching, and derived fibers preserve.
Pages V – 15–V – 16 separate finite occupancy from genuine infinite compatibility. The exact arithmetic
boundary is on V – 17.
How to read a visual claim
Each page gives an explicit object, a calculation, and a source reference. Coordinate sketches display only
the stated real points. Coefficient arrays and finite specialization diagrams describe the stated algebraic
data exactly. Line thickness never stands for nilpotent multiplicity.
Read this as: These are explanations of the formal release, not additional assumptions or new Lean
certificates. The accompanying scripts check the explicit finite calculations. They do not establish
universal arithmetic existence.

Why arrows reverse when points appear
A ring map f : R →T sends a prime of T to its inverse image in R:
f∗: Spec T −→Spec R,
q 7−→f−1(q).
Similarly, a point y : T →E′ gives the point y ◦f : R →E′. The equality y ◦f = x is an actual
requirement in the final theorem, not a consequence of drawing a connecting arrow.
Two kinds of point
Local quotient factorization is stated for continuous local coefficient-algebra maps to deformation test
rings. The later field-valued point x : R →E′ uses its kernel and scalar fiber. A characteristic-zero field
point is generally not a local ring map: the coefficient uniformizer becomes a unit. These two uses of
“point” must retain their own categories.

What the quotient removes
As an R-module, T has annihilator (x), hence

The other generic point (y) is missing because it does not contain x. The quotient has not created a

Why no idempotent selects just this branch
In a commutative local ring, e2 = e implies e = 0 or e = 1. Indeed, e + (1 −e) = 1 forces at least one
of e, 1 −e to be a unit; canceling in e(1 −e) = 0 gives the conclusion. Thus this node has no nontrivial
idempotent even though it has two irreducible components.
By contrast, a product of two Artin local rings has two disjoint maximal points, with canonical idempotents
(1, 0) and (0, 1). Those idempotents act on any supplied module over the product. Their two ranges are
the packets studied on V – 12.
Read this as: Intersecting local components are not product factors. Use quotient ideals and
support for the node; use canonical idempotents only where an actual product decomposition exists.
