PATCHING SUPPORT, HECKE PACKETS, AND CONDITIONAL
AUTOMORPHIC COMPLETION
KOMOREBI P.
Abstract. Let ¯ρ be a residual Galois representation and let R be a global deformation ring with
declared local conditions. We separate the global automorphy problem into residual occupancy,
deformation support, Hecke occupancy, and arithmetic realization. Local conditions are encoded
by quotient rings rather than by idempotents, since irreducible components of a local deformation
ring need not be clopen. For an ordinary or derived patching datum, full support descends
through the augmentation quotient. Near faithfulness is shown to be equivalent to full support for
finite modules over Noetherian rings. An isomorphism R ∼= T transports support, Fitting ideals,
and point fibers, but a quotient map can erase an entire component. Finite Hecke algebras are
resolved into canonical Artin packets, so simultaneous generalized eigenspaces are available without
assuming semisimplicity. Nested packets in one finite carrier stabilize; for changing finite carriers,
nonzero stable cores give compatible inverse limit vectors. These results give a pointwise criterion:
a characteristic zero deformation point in automorphic support produces a Hecke eigensystem.
With attached Galois representations, local global compatibility, and classicality, the point is
automorphic. A fixed compatible system then propagates the conclusion to every coefficient
place. For finite coefficient quotients, compatible conjugacy classes admit compatible continuous
representatives. Declared polynomial quotient conditions survive their adic reconstruction. These
completion statements require explicit finite occupancy, continuity, and integral Frobenius data.
Universal companion existence and unrestricted reciprocity are not proved.

None of these steps is merely a change of notation. A deeper lift above one residual representation
cannot create a missing residual companion. Freeness over an auxiliary group algebra does not
imply that every component of a deformation ring is occupied. An isomorphism of rings transports
support that already exists, but it does not create a nonzero automorphic vector. A nonzero class
in completed or torsion cohomology need not be classical without a theorem that says so.
The Taylor Wiles method and its later forms solve these problems in many important settings
[27, 24, 9, 14, 12]. Higher dimensional and derived versions use a broader range of local deformation
rings, residual image conditions, torsion classes, and cohomological carriers [8, 3, 6, 25, 17, 22, 7, 19].
The present article does not replace those theorems. It isolates the algebraic support statements
that explain exactly what a patching package supplies once its arithmetic hypotheses have been
verified.
The first correction concerns local packets. A local deformation ring is usually complete and
local. Its irreducible components are rarely open and closed. They cannot generally be represented
by central idempotents. The correct object is a quotient ring defining a local condition, together
with the support of a module or complex on the resulting spectrum. Central idempotents reappear
later, where they belong, inside finite Artin Hecke algebras and finite dimensional characteristic
zero carriers.
The second correction concerns patching. The useful global conclusion is full support, often
expressed as near faithfulness. For a finite module over a Noetherian ring, near faithfulness is
equivalent to full support. Full support descends through the quotient that removes the auxiliary
variables. This statement is elementary, but it is the exact bridge from a patched module to
componentwise occupancy at finite level. The same conclusion holds for perfect complexes through
derived support.
The third correction concerns Hecke packets. Semisimplicity is not required to isolate a maximal
Hecke packet. Every finite commutative algebra over a field is a product of Artin local algebras.
Its canonical central idempotents project onto simultaneous generalized eigenspaces. Trace is only
a secondary criterion in characteristic zero. Support and localization are the primary notions.
The last part assembles these statements into a conditional theorem. A characteristic zero
point of R is automorphic when it lies in the support of the automorphic carrier, the comparison
with the Hecke algebra is available, and the corresponding Hecke fiber satisfies the declared
realization theorem.
If the point belongs to a fixed strictly compatible system, equality of
Frobenius polynomials propagates the automorphic identification to every coefficient place by
Chebotarev density and Brauer Nesbitt [5, 18, 12].

and residual occupancy appear in section 4. Auxiliary group algebras are treated in section 5.
Ordinary and derived patching support appear in section 6. The effect of R ∼= T is isolated in
section 7. Finite Hecke packets and infinite occupancy are developed in section 8. Pointwise

Corollary 3.2 (Simultaneous local conditions). A continuous point of Rloc with Hausdorff target
factors continuously through Rloc/J if and only if its restriction to every Ri factors continuously
through Ri/Ii. Each factor is unique. If the original point is local, its quotient factor is local.
Proof. Let f : Rloc →A be the point. Since A is Hausdorff and f is continuous, ker f is closed.

images of the Ii. This is equivalent to every local restriction killing Ii. The universal property
of the actual ideal quotient gives the factors, and the quotient topology gives their continuity.

to the original ring to see that its factor also reflects units.

reserved for finite Artin algebras or other settings with an actual product decomposition.
