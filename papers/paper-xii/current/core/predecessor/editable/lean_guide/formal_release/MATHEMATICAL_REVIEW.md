# Mathematical review of the completed tensor block

This is a self-review of the new mathematical mapping and construction, not an
independent external referee report. The Lean kernel and axiom audit are separate evidence.

## Claim 3.1: actual objects and actual topology

The raw object is Mathlib's finite `PiTensorProduct`, with its actual algebra structure.
The ideal of definition is the sum of the extended scalar ideal and all extended factor
ideals. The scalar term is retained for the empty family. `FiniteTensorQuotient.lean`
constructs the ordinary quotient equivalence; that alone is not counted as completion.

`CompletionQuotient.lean` constructs both directions between the completion of a
quotient and the quotient of the completion by the closed extended ideal. Completeness
of the latter follows from the countably based additive uniformity, not from a supplied
answer. Closedness yields separation. The inverse laws are proved on dense representatives
and extended by continuity. `AdicQuotientTopology.lean` identifies the quotient topology
with the image-ideal adic topology, preventing a silent replacement of topologies.

`CompletedFiniteTensor.lean` combines these constructions as an actual coefficient-algebra
equivalence. The images of each factor agree, and `closedConditions_eq` identifies the
ideal with the literal closure of the ideal generated by the completed factor images.
No final inverse, surjectivity, or completed-tensor equivalence is postulated.

An independent comparison with Mathlib's algebraic inverse-limit `AdicCompletion`
constructs a dense, uniformly inducing map, identifies the ideal powers through finite-level
evaluation, and constructs the completion equivalence. This supplies the maximal-adic
topology and the local-ring instance from finite generation and common residue data.
The Noetherian assumptions provide finite generation internally. A proper quotient is
local; an inconsistent unit-ideal condition still satisfies the ring comparison with
the zero ring, but no false local-ring-with-residue-field instance is asserted for it.

## Claim 3.3: no assumed pushout or attachment

The target coefficient factor is the independently constructed completed tensor of local
quotients. Its identification with A/J is the theorem of 3.1, not an input datum. The
transport module proves equality of the defining ideals under that identification.
The actual global object is the completion of B tensor_A C for this actual factor C.

The quotient and tensor comparison produces the factor of each continuous point. All
local restrictions are identified with the original canonical factor maps. The closed
kernel criterion discharges precisely the local quotient conditions. The Noetherian/local
capstone derives finite generation and the structural ideal bound from the manuscript
conventions. It does not introduce a new arithmetic existence assumption.

For complete B the global ring is bicontinuously isomorphic to B modulo the closed
extension of J. Its canonical map is therefore surjective. Consequently a factor of a
local point is local. The coefficient action is the actual composite through B; the
factor is an O-algebra map, and uniqueness gives naturality. Representability of the
original arithmetic deformation problem remains the separately declared input 2.2.

## Scope and retained boundaries

The displayed completion/quotient comparison is proved in a general adic setting and
specialized to the paper's Noetherian local context. It does not assume that the raw tensor
ring is local or Noetherian. No claim of a new Noetherianity classification theorem is made.
Ordinary tensor right exactness, finite testing, and a supplied universal-property structure
are never used as stand-ins for the completed object construction.

The old 71 modules and the ten Fitting modules are unchanged. All 96 modules receive a
fresh cumulative compiler check and all 1,229 named declarations receive transitive axiom
audits. The 49-claim coverage map has 291 distinct targets, all present among the audited
names. Original-wording and external arithmetic boundaries are retained.

The new proof texts expand the arguments of 3.1 and 3.3 without changing their statements.
The manuscript bibliography and pre-existing arithmetic citations are retained, not newly
certified. This release does not assert historical novelty of the standard completion
and quotient mechanisms. External arithmetic realizations and universal Langlands claims
are not promoted.
