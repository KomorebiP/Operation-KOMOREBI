1 How to read the formal companion
Paper V places several kinds of mathematics in one signal processing language: coordinate partitions,
bounded Hilbert space operators, finite filter banks, finite tree optimization, formal series, and pro-
jective actions. The formal development keeps their types and hypotheses distinct. Following those
distinctions is the most useful way to read its proofs.
A pointwise partition identity needs little structure. An energy theorem needs a normed space with
the intended norm. A transform theorem needs convergence. An optimal finite pruning needs a finite
family and an objective. A fractional linear expression needs a chart, while its projective action survives
at the chart’s poles. Each transition has a named construction or theorem in the source.
Follow the model before the theorem name
Start with the ambient type, then locate the bridge to the intended signal model. A coordinate
identity, a bounded operator theorem, and an analytic transform law have different inputs. The
exact excerpt shows where those inputs enter the declaration.
1.1 What the completed contract contains
The selected release, 1.1.0-expanded-lean-checked, records twenty scoped theorem-like manuscript
results with explicit proofs and 225 supporting theorem targets. The inherited namespace audit re-
ports 827 declarations. These are different denominators: the 827 includes definitions and compiler-
generated declarations, and the 225 includes supporting results rather than 225 independent new math-
ematical claims.
The formal core is the expanded manuscript preserved as Part I of the final traditional edition. Part II
preserves the broader Annals development. Its additional proof records are prose mathematics. They
do not enter the twenty-result formalization contract. Section 10 gives a separate crosswalk.
The two edge recovery conjectures remain open. Known-edge cancellation is established, measurable-
mask perturbation is established, and finite adaptive selection is established within its hypotheses.
Those results do not provide an estimator with the full statistical and approximation guarantees re-
quested by the conjectures.

1.3 Read a theorem as a typed contract
The name after theorem is a reusable declaration. Parenthesized arguments are explicit inputs. Braced
arguments are generally inferred. A hypothesis such as (hN : 0 < N) is an actual input to the theorem.
A typeclass argument such as [AddCommMonoid E] specifies the algebraic laws available to its proof.
The expression after the colon is the proposition. The proof after := by constructs evidence of that
proposition. Common commands here have small, specific jobs: ext reduces equality to coordinates,
rw rewrites using an equality, simp uses declared simplification rules, ring normalizes polynomial
identities, omega handles integer arithmetic, and linarith combines linear inequalities. Their successful
use belongs to the recorded compilation, not to a textual count of tactic names.
A definition marked noncomputable may use classical choice or real-number constructions that do not
give executable numerical code. Its theorems can still be checked by the kernel. In particular, the prun-
ing evaluator over exact real costs specifies a mathematical algorithm and a count of unit operations.
Lean evaluation speed is outside that count.

2 Partitions, signed addresses, and the Hilbert bridge
2.1 A packet first means a coordinate mask
Take an index set 𝐼, a label set 𝑅, and a phase map 𝜙∶𝐼→𝑅. The packet with label 𝑟keeps coordinate
𝑖exactly when 𝜙(𝑖) = 𝑟:
(𝑃𝑟𝑥)(𝑖) = {𝑥(𝑖),
𝜙(𝑖) = 𝑟,
0,
𝜙(𝑖) ≠𝑟.
The definition requires a zero in the value type and a decidable label equality. It does not require a
Fourier transform, a measure, or an inner product.
Source: KomorebiPaperV/Packets.lean.
def packet (phase : I →R) (r : R) (x : I →V) : I →V :=
fun i => if phase i = r then x i else 0
Applying the same mask twice changes nothing. Distinct labels cannot both be retained at the same
coordinate. If the label type is finite and values can be added commutatively, the finite sum of packets
returns the original value. At any one coordinate exactly one summand survives.
Source: KomorebiPaperV/Packets.lean.
theorem packet_complete (phase : I →R) (x : I →V) (i : I) :
(∑r : R, packet phase r x i) = x i := by
classical
calc
(∑r : R, packet phase r x i) = packet phase (phase i) x i := by
apply Finset.sum_eq_single (phase i)
· intro b _ hb
simp [packet, Ne.symm hb]
· simp
_ = x i := by simp [packet]
The proof uses Finset.sum_eq_single to isolate the surviving label. This simple mechanism is reused
after stronger ambient structures are introduced.

2.6 Impulse expansion means norm convergence
The impulse at 𝑖with amplitude 𝑥𝑖is represented by lp.single 2 i (x i). The full theorem is a HasSum
statement in the ℓ2 space.
Source: KomorebiPaperV/HilbertPackets.lean.
theorem impulse_expansion (x : Signal ℤ) :
HasSum (fun i : ℤ=> lp.single 2 i (x i)) x :=
lp.hasSum_single (by norm_num) x
Thus the series of impulses converges to 𝑥in the ambient norm. The finite support version is an exact
finite sum. The shift is an isometric equivalence with formula 𝑆𝑑𝑥[𝑛] = 𝑥[𝑛−𝑑], and shifting the
impulse at zero gives the impulse at 𝑑. Density of the impulse basis yields the dense shift-generated
span.

3 FIR transfer and faithful perfect reconstruction
3.1 Finite support makes time domain transfer unconditional
An FIR filter is a finitely supported function ℎ∶ℤ→𝐾. Negative tap addresses are allowed, so the
formal theorem includes advances as well as delays. For an arbitrary bilateral sequence,
(ℎ∗𝑥)[𝑛] =
∑
𝑡∈supp ℎ
ℎ[𝑡]𝑥[𝑛−𝑡].
No convergence hypothesis is needed because this sum is finite.
Source: KomorebiPaperV/FIRTransfer.lean.
def fir (h : ℤ→₀ K) (x : ℤ→K) (n : ℤ) : K :=
∑t ∈h.support, h t * x (n - t)
Fix output phase 𝑘and input phase 𝑠. The tap carrying an input channel delay 𝑚is
𝑡= 𝑁𝑚+ 𝑘−𝑠,
ℎ𝑘,𝑠[𝑚] = ℎ[𝑁𝑚+ 𝑘−𝑠].
The address identity is
(𝑁𝑛+ 𝑘) −(𝑁𝑚+ 𝑘−𝑠) = 𝑁(𝑛−𝑚) + 𝑠.
It says exactly which original tap contributes to which channel and delay. The source’s tapEquiv proves
the relevant reindexing is bijective, then partitions the finite support by input phase. It does not enlarge
the finite sum to an arbitrary infinite series.
