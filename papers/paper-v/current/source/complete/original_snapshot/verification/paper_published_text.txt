                                                Abstract
      This paper develops the signal realization of the projector-resolved seed calculus. The impulse δ0
  is taken as a primitive signal seed and its shift orbit supplies the address system. Residue projectors
  split a sequence into exact polyphase channels, nested divisibility gives exact refinement, and
  every finite impulse-response operator becomes a matrix of packet transfers. The standard perfect-
  reconstruction condition for a filter bank is therefore an analysis–synthesis identity in the common
  operator language of the series. Haar analysis and every finite-depth wavelet-packet transform are
  treated on complex ℓ2 , together with concrete finite-signal models and a predict–update lifting step.
  The edge problem is separated into two statements. When a discontinuity packet is known and
  retained exactly, approximation error is exactly the approximation error of the remaining smooth
  packet; no spectral approximation of the jump is performed. When the packet is estimated, a
  symmetric-difference bound isolates mask error, amplitude error, and smooth-background error.
  Numerical studies compare global Fourier truncation, exact known-packet reconstruction, and
  a deliberately narrow positive-contrast estimator in one and two dimensions, including curved
  boundaries, corners, and multiple amplitudes. The same cyclic phase action is then placed on the
  Smith reflection disk. Its pullback through the Cayley transform is a Möbius action on impedance,
  and the two-phase case is precisely impedance–admittance inversion. Nonlinear coefficient products
  obey cyclic packet convolution. The revised edition integrates a Lean formalization of all twenty
  theorem-like statements under the hypotheses stated below: 225 supporting theorem targets in 23
  modules. Eighteen regenerated figures, machine-readable numerical results, the 33-test original
  companion suite, and 36 additional exact-model and verifier tests accompany the paper. Exact
  signal theorems, limited numerical estimators, classical constructions, and open recovery problems
  are kept distinct throughout.

Scope and claim boundary. The paper proves exact polyphase reconstruction, exact finite
filter-transfer identities, perfect reconstruction for the stated filter banks, the conditional known-
edge theorem, mask and amplitude perturbation bounds, nonlinear residue-convolution laws,
and the Smith-coordinate phase action. It does not claim that every discontinuity can be
detected automatically, that every form of Gibbs ringing has been eliminated, or that the simple
two-class estimator supplied with the release is a universal segmentation method. The estimator
is included as one reproducible finite example. For every finite packet refinement tree, the paper
now proves existence of a globally optimal complete pruning, an exact dynamic-programming
selector for additive costs, and a deterministic margin-stability certificate. Wavelets, filter banks,
the Cayley transform, and the classical Smith grid are treated as classical inputs organized in
the common seed–packet–refinement language.

1    The signal realization of the seed calculus
Let ℓ2 (Z; C), abbreviated ℓ2 (Z), be the Hilbert space of square-summable complex discrete signals.
The inner product is conjugate-linear in its first argument. A superscript ∗ on a bounded operator
denotes its Hilbert adjoint. Write δn for the unit impulse at address n and let the bilateral shift be

                                           (Sx)[n] = x[n − 1].

Then

completion is the ℓ2 norm.

Euclidean norms; theorem statements specify the ambient norm when it matters.

2.1   Nested refinement
Suppose N, M ≥ 1 and N | M . Every coarse phase has a disjoint family of fine descendants:

                           ChildM (N, r) = {s : 0 ≤ s < M, s ≡ r              (mod N )}.

Theorem 2.3 (Exact nested refinement). For N, M ≥ 1 and N | M ,
                                                          X
                                            PN,r =                   PM,s .
                                                       0≤s<M
                                                     s≡r (mod N )


Proof. An integer n satisfies n ≡ r (mod N ) exactly when its unique residue s = n mod M lies among
the listed descendants. The fine indicators are disjoint, so their sum is the coarse indicator.

    This identity is the signal form of the refinement law introduced in Paper II. It is not merely a
denser sampling rule. The coarse masked packet, represented on the original integer lattice, is exactly
the sum of its fine masked descendants. Decimated channels at different moduli must first be placed in
these common coordinates before being added.

this gives
                                       X(z)

Theorem 4.1 (Perfect-reconstruction criterion). For d ∈ Z, such a bank reconstructs every input up
to a delay of d channel indices exactly when the following Laurent-polynomial identity holds:

                                             R(z)E(z) = z −d IN .

If the right side is cz −d IN with c ̸= 0, reconstruction holds up to gain c and channel delay d. After
interleaving, this is a delay of N d original samples. A negative delay denotes an advance.

Proof. Finite convolution represents Laurent-polynomial multiplication: composing shifts adds their
delays, and distributivity handles finite sums. Thus the matrix identity implies the asserted action.
Conversely, let B = RE − cz −d IN and suppose its action vanishes on every channel sequence. Insert
an impulse at channel s, address zero, with every other channel zero. Output channel k reads the
coefficients of the Laurent polynomial Bks at all addresses. All of these coefficients vanish, so Bks = 0
for every k, s. The representation is faithful, proving necessity as a polynomial identity rather than
merely equality at one evaluation point. The displayed unit-gain case is c = 1.

A      Algorithm statements
A.1     Exact polyphase filtering
Given x, h, and N :

    1. Form xs [n] = x[N n + s] for 0 ≤ s < N .

    2. Form every transfer kernel hks [m] = h[N m + k − s].

    3. Compute                                     XX
                                        yk [n] =            hks [m]xs [n − m].
                                                   s   m

    4. Interleave the yk channels.

   The algorithm is algebraically identical to direct convolution; its purpose is to expose independent
and interacting packets for block computation and refinement.

A.2     Conditional known-edge reconstruction
Given a decomposition f = s + j:

    1. Apply the selected smooth approximation Qm to s only.

    2. Retain j exactly.

    3. Return Qm s + j.

    The error certificate is ∥s − Qm s∥.

A.3     Limited positive-contrast estimator
The included finite estimator does the following:

    1. Split the observed values into two classes by an Otsu threshold.

    2. Keep coherent connected components and fill holes.

    3. Harmonically fill the selected packet to estimate the missing background.

    4. Smooth the filled background spectrally.

    5. Estimate one packet amplitude by a robust median residual.

    6. Recombine the estimated smooth and edge packets.

   This procedure is useful for the supplied separated synthetic case. It assumes a positive-contrast
two-class packet and is not promoted to a general edge theorem.

D.3      Three bridges that matter for the statement
Finite filters and numerical transforms. The filter-bank proof is an algebraic theorem about
finite Laurent-polynomial kernels acting faithfully on bilateral sequences. Equality at a single complex
value of z would be insufficient to prove a polynomial identity; the impulse argument avoids that
error. The separate transform theorem proves the finite-filter action agrees with evaluation wherever
the weighted input series converge absolutely. An arbitrary bilateral ℓ2 sequence need not have an
absolutely convergent numerical z-transform. The all-sequence time-domain theorem makes no such
assumption.

Recursive trees and set-valued prunings. The formal tree permits any positive finite arity at an
internal node, including arity one. A node has a position in the tree, so equal data labels on distinct
nodes do not identify the nodes. A recursive cut is either a stop or a collection of child cuts. Its
equivalence with a covering antichain is proved in both directions. The cost, envelope, and operator
sums are then shown to equal actual finite sums over the selected node set. This connects the recursive
optimizer to the manuscript’s set-valued definition instead of assuming that connection.

packet operators are constructed on complex ℓ2 . Square summability and boundedness are established

Lean kernel. Likewise, the Python Smith routine evaluates only finite charts; the Lean projective
