# Paper I completion, Block 03: primary generators over arbitrary fields

## The result proved in this block

Let k be any field, V a finite-dimensional k-vector space, and T an endomorphism of V. Equip V with its actual polynomial action p·v = p(T)v. This is `Module.AEval' T`, not a surrogate module with an assumed relationship to T.

The development constructs a finite positive primary presentation

    V ≃ ∏ᵢ k[t]/(pᵢ^eᵢ),   pᵢ irreducible,   eᵢ > 0,

as a k[t]-linear equivalence. For a primary label a, represented by the principal ideal (p), let c(a) be the number of factors with (pᵢ) = a. Then it proves

    seedRank_{k[T]}(V) = maxₐ c(a).

The maximum is zero for an empty presentation. Irreducibles that differ by a unit have the same principal-ideal label and are counted together. No assumption that the irreducibles are linear is used.

`exists_operator_primary_seed_formula` produces both the presentation and the least-generator proof from T and finite-dimensionality. The public `primaryGeneratorCount` uses a mathematically chosen presentation, and the API proves that its value is the canonical seed rank. It is not defined to be seed rank and is not accompanied by an assumed equality.

The proof establishes a classical formula within Paper I's actual definitions. This is an integration and completion result, not a claim that the underlying classical formula was newly discovered.

## 1. Constructing a genuine positive primary presentation

`operatorModule_isTorsion` applies the proved finite-dimensional polynomial-module torsion theorem. `exists_primaryProduct_equiv` instantiates Mathlib's proved PID torsion-module structure theorem on that actual module. The finite direct sum returned by the theorem is converted to the equivalent finite product.

The upstream theorem permits an exponent-zero factor. Such a factor is k[t]/(1), the zero module. Counting it as a cyclic primary summand would give an incorrect minimum. `primePower_quotient_subsingleton_of_exponent_zero` proves that this factor is trivial. `dropZeroExponentEquiv` constructs the restriction equivalence to the nonzero-exponent subtype, with an explicit inverse that fills discarded coordinates by zero. The surviving exponents are proved positive.

`PrimaryPresentation` records the finite index type, irreducible polynomials, positive exponents and actual linear equivalence. It contains no seed-number equality, generating family, quotient lower bound or cyclicity conclusion. `primaryPresentation_nonempty` proves its existence. `chosenPrimaryPresentation` uses classical choice on that proved existence statement.

## 2. Residue fields give the lower bound

Suppose a finite generating family has n vectors. Any surjective linear image of the module is generated by their images. If the target also carries a compatible field action through R → K, those images span the target over K. `residue_finrank_le_card_of_generates` therefore proves that its K-dimension is at most n.

For a selected maximal ideal a, `residueFiberMap` is the literal coordinatewise quotient map

    ∏ᵢ R/Iᵢ  →  ∏_{i : Pᵢ=a} R/a,

where Iᵢ ⊆ Pᵢ. The map is constructed from the quotient factor homomorphisms and coordinate projections. Its surjectivity is proved by choosing a lift for each selected coordinate and setting all others to zero. The target is a vector space over the field R/a of dimension exactly the number of selected factors.

It follows that every generating family has at least c(a) vectors, for every primary label a. Taking the maximum supplies the lower bound.

The field in this argument is R/a, not k. For p=t²+1 over the rationals, the residue field has rational dimension two, but one primary summand contributes one dimension over that residue field. Multiplying the seed count by deg(p) would be wrong.

The code proves actual surjective quotient maps and a dimension lower bound. It does not yet identify their kernels with the printed subspace p(T)X_p. That additional identification is explicitly left open in the publication map.

## 3. CRT gives the matching shared-generator upper bound

Let r be the maximum number of factors with the same primary label. Within each label fibre, rank the factors by 0, …, c(a)−1. `fiberColor` assigns that rank as a color in Fin r. `fiberColor_injective_on_fiber` proves that factors with the same label cannot have the same color unless they are the same factor.

For each color j, define a vector whose i-th coordinate is one when factor i has color j and zero otherwise. These are `coloredSeeds`.

Distinct factors with the same color must have different primary labels. Their irreducible-power ideals are coprime. For a desired target vector x, the Chinese remainder theorem constructs, separately for every color j, a coefficient a_j that realizes all target coordinates assigned that color. The finite sum

    x = ∑ⱼ a_j · coloredSeeds(j)

is proved as an equality in the actual product module. This gives a generating family of size r.

`primaryProduct_seedNumber_isLeast` combines that constructed upper bound with the residue-field lower bounds. `primePowerProduct_seedNumber_isLeast` discharges the maximality and coprimality hypotheses for positive powers of irreducibles over a PID.

The proof uses one shared polynomial action, not independent actions that could select coordinates without justification. CRT supplies precisely the independent coefficient control available between coprime primary factors. Repeated factors cannot share that control, which is why their multiplicity determines the minimum.

## 4. Transport back to the paper's seed rank

A primary product theorem is not yet a theorem about the paper unless the generating predicates agree.

`familyGenerates_linearEquiv_iff` transports actual submodule generation along a linear equivalence. `familyGenerates_iff_polynomialModule` proves both directions between the paper's k-linear span of polynomial orbits and the k[t]-submodule span in `Module.AEval' T`. Its proof is by span induction and the actual evaluation action.

`operator_seedNumber_isLeast_of_polynomialModule` then transports the exact finite minimum, not only an upper bound. The existing proved bridge to the algebra generated by T gives both the natural-valued `seedFinrank` and cardinal-valued `seedRank` formulas.

`PrimaryPresentation.maxCount_eq` proves that the maximum is independent of all choices in the presentation. This is invariance of the maximum. The block does not separately establish presentation invariance of every individual primary multiplicity.

## 5. Cyclicity without split-polynomial assumptions

For every actual positive primary presentation D, the API proves

    T has a cyclic vector  ↔  D.label is injective.

Forward: a cyclic vector gives a one-element generating family. The residue-field lower bound forces each primary fibre to contain at most one factor.

Reverse: when the labels are injective, all distinct factors are coprime. Assign every factor the same color, apply CRT, and transport the single constructed generator back to V.

This criterion includes the zero space, where the at-most-one-generator predicate holds. It is then composed with the genuinely proved Block 02 polynomial bridge to obtain

    D.label is injective  ↔  minpoly(T) = charpoly(T).

This closes a primary-count characterization of cyclicity. It is not yet the complete printed six-way criterion involving intrinsic primary submodules, rational canonical form and geometric multiplicities after extension to an algebraic closure.

## 6. The invariant-factor corollary at its printed scope

The printed invariant-factor corollary assumes an actual decomposition

    V ≃ ∏_{j=1}^r k[t]/(f_j),   f₁ | f₂ | … | fᵣ,

with nonconstant f_j. The new theorem proves that the seed rank is r from exactly that data. It does not assume the desired seed count.

The r coordinate unit vectors give the upper bound. For r>0, choose an irreducible divisor p of f₁. The divisibility chain implies that p divides every f_j. The product therefore maps onto r copies of k[t]/(p), whose dimension over the residue field is r. This gives the lower bound. The empty-product case is handled separately and has minimum zero.

`invariantFactor_operator_seedRanks` transports the result to the actual operator. Existence or computation of an ordered invariant-factor presentation is not asserted here. That is not needed to certify the conditional corollary whose statement already supplies such a presentation.

This moves exactly one proof-level publication record from partial to covered: `PSC-I-C-INVARIANT-FACTOR-COUNT`.

## 7. Boundary checks and remaining distinction

The regression suite proves the irreducibility of t²+1 over the rationals, checks two repeated nonlinear primary chains require exactly two seeds and cannot be cyclic, checks a single squared nonlinear primary block needs one seed despite rational dimension four, checks the residue-field base dimension two, handles zero exponents and the zero operator on the zero space, groups associated irreducibles, and proves two distinct coprime factors share a single seed.

These are exact Lean theorems, not numerical experiments. They are regression checks, not additional monograph records.

The remaining printed single-operator obligation is precise:

    c(p) = dim_{k[t]/(p)}(X_p / p(T)X_p)

for the intrinsic primary component X_p of the given operator. The present code proves the left-hand count formula and constructs the relevant surjective residue maps. It still needs the intrinsic-submodule identification and the exact kernel equality that induce the displayed quotient equivalence.

After that, the arbitrary algebraically closed, non-diagonalizable eigenspace formula and the scalar-extension clause of the six-way cyclic criterion remain to be assembled. The earlier self-adjoint eigenspace result stays unchanged.

All constructions here are noncomputable mathematical objects. No executable eigensolver, symbolic factorization routine or verified normal-form algorithm is produced. The final verification receipt distinguishes the actual fresh module builds, ownership audit and same-kernel replays from reused upstream caches.
