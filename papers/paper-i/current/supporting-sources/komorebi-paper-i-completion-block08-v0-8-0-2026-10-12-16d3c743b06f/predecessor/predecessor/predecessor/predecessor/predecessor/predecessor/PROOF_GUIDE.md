## 1. The cyclic-vector bridge is now a theorem, not an input

Let k be any field, V a finite-dimensional k-vector space, and T an endomorphism. The new result is

    HasCyclicVector T ↔ minpoly k T = T.charpoly.

No algebraic closure, diagonalizability, Jordan basis, invariant-factor list, or supplied polynomial equivalence appears in the hypotheses.

### The polynomial module is the actual action

`Module.AEval' T` is V with the polynomial action p·v = p(T)v. For a vector x, `orbitMap T x` is the k[t]-linear map p ↦ p(T)x. The proof first shows that the paper's singleton polynomial-orbit span equals the range of this map. Therefore one-seed generation is equivalent to its surjectivity.

The map is not an abstract witness whose relationship to T is assumed. `orbitMap_apply` is a definitional identity, and the surjectivity equivalence is proved against the existing `FamilyGenerates` definition.

### Forward direction

If x is cyclic, every vector is q(T)x. A polynomial killing x consequently kills every vector, by commutativity of the polynomial action. Thus the kernel of the orbit map is the annihilator of the entire polynomial module.

Mathlib identifies that annihilator with the principal ideal generated by the minimal polynomial. The first isomorphism theorem now identifies the orbit with k[t]/(minpoly T). Its k-dimension is the degree of the minimal polynomial. Because the orbit is all of V, this degree is dim V.

The minimal polynomial is monic and divides the characteristic polynomial. The characteristic polynomial is also monic and has degree dim V. Equal degree and divisibility give equality.

### Reverse direction

The key classical input is the proved PID-module theorem `Module.exists_ker_toSpanSingleton_eq_annihilator`. Applied to the actual polynomial module of T, it constructs a vector whose annihilator is the annihilator of the whole module.

This is not the assertion that this vector is cyclic. Its orbit can still be proper. The new proof identifies the dimension of its orbit with the minimal-polynomial degree. When that degree equals dim V, the orbit subspace has full dimension, so it equals V. Surjectivity and cyclicity follow.

The classical PID theorem is an imported proved dependency, not a new axiom. The source-owned axiom audit follows the transitive dependencies of the new result.

### Connection to the canonical seed rank

The block also proves that the polynomial one-seed predicate equals `Cyclic` for the actual image algebra `operatorAlgebra T = Algebra.adjoin k {T}`. On a nonzero finite-dimensional space this gives

    seedRank (operatorAlgebra T) V = 1 ↔ minpoly k T = T.charpoly.

The nonzero hypothesis is necessary only for this exact-rank-one formulation. The at-most-one-generator predicate remains true on the zero space, whose rank is zero. The current traditional monograph already contains the nonzero guard in its six-way cyclic criterion. The older archived machine source does not; the new coverage map follows the current traditional source instead.

`cyclic_four_way_without_polynomial_certificate` then removes the old `hpoly` input from the four-way assembly. The exact seed-number certificate and geometric/block-multiplicity identification are still inputs. They have not been renamed or hidden inside a structure.

## 2. Spectral certificates are constructed from self-adjointness

For a finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product space and a symmetric/self-adjoint endomorphism T, Mathlib supplies a proved orthonormal eigenbasis. That basis is not required from the caller of the new public theorem.

An eigenbasis is initially indexed by a finite set of coordinate positions. Repeated eigenvalues occur in that list. The new code defines the distinct label set as the actual range of the eigenvalue list and defines each multiplicity as the cardinality of its fibre.

`groupedIndexEquiv` constructs the equivalence between the original index set and the dependent sum of the finite eigenvalue fibres. Reindexing gives the precise grouped basis required by the existing Paper I argument. The code proves that the vector in each grouped slot has the declared eigenvalue.

The existing distinct-eigenvalue theorem then constructs the polynomial selectors by interpolation. These recover individual eigenspace coordinates from padded sums of basis vectors. The upper bound on seed number is the maximum multiplicity. The matching lower bound uses the actual scalar quotient onto a largest eigenspace. The maximum, a maximizing label, polynomial extraction, and quotient data are all internal constructions.

`spectralList_multiplicity_eq_finrank` identifies each fibre count with the intrinsic dimension of the corresponding eigenspace. The result therefore concerns actual geometric multiplicities, not a supplied list of abstract natural numbers.

The public consequences give the exact least polynomial seed number and the canonical natural and cardinal seed ranks over k[T]. The zero-dimensional case is included, with maximum zero.

For matrices, Hermitian symmetry is sufficient over the complex numbers. Over the reals, ordinary matrix symmetry suffices. Ordinary complex symmetry is deliberately not substituted for Hermitian symmetry.

### Computational boundary

These constructions use classical finite choices, bases and the mathematical spectral theorem. They are noncomputable Lean definitions. This block does not produce an executable eigensolver, a symbolic factorization algorithm, or a certified floating-point procedure. It closes the mathematical spectral-certificate input for the stated self-adjoint class, not the executable-algorithm obligation.

## 3. Conditioned coordinates

For a continuous linear equivalence e : X ≃L Y, the block defines

    conditionNumber e = ‖e‖ · ‖e⁻¹‖.

It defines the actual coordinate conjugate as e ∘ T ∘ e⁻¹ and proves that inverse coordinate transport recovers T. Two applications of the operator-norm composition inequality give

    ‖e T e⁻¹‖ ≤ conditionNumber e · ‖T‖.

Applying the same bound to e⁻¹ proves the reverse inequality. On a nonzero space, the identity operator has norm one, so composition of the two inverse maps shows that the condition number is at least one.

The general theorem is independent of a particular direct-sum norm: any declared bounded coordinate equivalence satisfies it. The packet specialization identifies e and e⁻¹ with the existing literal packet analysis and synthesis maps. It closes the condition-number definition and the conditioned-coordinate proposition at their current printed scope.

The zero-space condition number is zero. A positive regression and a deliberately rejected misuse of the nonzero theorem preserve this boundary.
