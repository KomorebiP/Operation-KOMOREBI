Exact current corrected corollary
=====================================
\begin{corollary}[Algebraically closed multiplicity formula]
\label{cor:algebraically-closed-formula}
Let $\bbk$ be algebraically closed, let $A$ be a unital algebra of finite dimension over $\bbk$, put $J=\Jac(A)$, and let $M$ be a finitely generated left module over $A$.  Assume compatible decompositions indexed by an integer $q\geq0$:
\[
A/J\cong\prod_{i=1}^{q}M_{d_i}(\bbk),
\qquad
M/JM\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{q}(\bbk^{d_i})^{\oplus m_i},
\]
where the $d_i$ are positive integers and the $m_i$ are nonnegative integers.  The first isomorphism is an isomorphism of algebras over $\bbk$.  The second is an isomorphism of left modules over $A/J$, using the natural column module for each matrix factor and zero action from the other factors.  Then
\[
\srank_A(M)=\max_{1\leq i\leq q}\left\lceil\frac{m_i}{d_i}\right\rceil,
\]
with $\max\varnothing=0$ when $q=0$.
\end{corollary}
\RecordID{PSC-I-C-ALGEBRAICALLY-CLOSED-FORMULA}
\Depends{PSC-I-T-MODULE-TOP and PSC-I-T-SEMISIMPLE-SEED-RANK.}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:module-top}, $\srank_A(M)=\srank_{A/J}(M/JM)$.  Apply \cref{thm:semisimple-seed-rank} to the displayed decompositions of $A/J$ and $M/JM$, with every $D_i=\bbk$.  This gives the stated maximum.  If $q=0$, the unital $A/J$-module $M/JM$ is zero, and \cref{thm:module-top} gives seed rank zero.
\end{proof}

Types and actions
=================
The A/J-action is (a+J)(m+JM)=am+JM. Under the chosen algebra isomorphism,
the i-th matrix factor acts by usual LEFT multiplication on column vectors,
diagonally on the m_i copies; every other factor acts by zero there. No
opposite-ring convention, right-module convention, or dualization is used.

For the summary, cA acts on cA^{oplus r} by componentwise LEFT multiplication.
The evaluation map sends (a_1,...,a_r) to sum a_j s_j. Its kernel N_S is a
LEFT submodule. Its quotient is a LEFT module, not necessarily an algebra.
This quotient is the algebraic hull, including in examples with a topology.
Topological closure/completion requires the already separate completion theorem.

Split decomposition: assume it for this bounded repair
====================================================
The printed Wedderburn--Artin input produces matrix rings over division rings
D_i. It does not itself state or prove that D_i=k when the original algebra is
finite dimensional over an algebraically closed field. The elementary proof
would use linear dependence of powers of an element and factorization of its
annihilating polynomial, but that additional argument is absent from the
current internal statements. Therefore the narrowest repair is to ASSUME the
displayed compatible split decomposition, exactly as the current text does.
This keeps the proof entirely within the two cited internal theorems. Once the
split decomposition is explicit, algebraic closure and finite dimension are
stronger than logically necessary; retaining them preserves the intended local
corollary and does not invalidate it. Do not claim that the corrected proof has
also established the existence of splitting from algebraic closure.
