1. EXACT SOURCE CARDS AND BASELINE VERDICTS

A. prop:cyclic-characterization, lines 708–722
Record: PSC-I-P-CYCLIC-CHARACTERIZATION
Objects: unital ring A and nonzero unital left A-module M.
Conclusion: srank_A(M)=1 iff some m has Am=M.
Verdict: proved as written.
A one-element generating set gives rank at most 1. Rank cannot be 0 for
nonzero M, because the empty hull is 0. Conversely a minimum set of cardinality
1 is a singleton and is a cyclic vector. No finite-generation assumption must
be added: a rank-one module is already finitely generated. If M=0 were included,
0 would generate but srank(0)=0; the printed M != 0 assumption correctly
handles this boundary.

B. thm:exact-sequence-bounds, lines 799–827
Record: PSC-I-T-EXACT-SEQUENCE-BOUNDS
The requested alias thm:exact-sequence-rank does not occur; this is the actual
label of the exact-sequence seed bounds.
Objects: any algebraic short exact sequence 0 -> L -> M -> N -> 0 of left
A-modules, over a unital ring, with no finiteness or topology assumption.
Conclusion: srank_A(N) <= srank_A(M) <= srank_A(L)+srank_A(N).
Verdict on raw argument: incomplete, with the smallest missing implication
being the treatment of arbitrary cardinal-sized generating families.
The formula is true for the full stated domain. The printed upper proof
chooses lists l_1,...,l_r and n_1,...,n_s, so literally proves the finite-rank
case. Safe completion: choose minimum generating sets indexed by I and J;
lift each quotient generator; their union generates M. Every particular
pi(m) uses only a finite subset F of J. Subtract the same finite combination
of chosen lifts; the remainder lies in iota(L) and uses finitely many
l_i. The generating set has cardinality at most |I|+|J|. The quotient lower
bound follows from the already proved quotient monotonicity.
Boundary checks: L=0 gives M isomorphic to N; N=0 gives M isomorphic to L;
both zero give 0 <= 0 <= 0. Infinite scalar modules k^(I) and k^(J) give
rank |I|+|J| for their scalar direct sum, consistently with cardinal addition.
No topological exact-sequence assertion is made or obtained by this proof.

The nearby hypothesis guide incorrectly mentions a lower bound involving K
and restriction/lifting hypotheses supposedly printed in this theorem. There
is no such lower bound. Safe wording: the quotient gives a lower bound;
L needs its own generators for the upper bound. Minimal warning example:
A=k[x,y], M=A, L=(x,y), N=A/L. M and N have rank 1 while L has rank 2. Indeed
L/(x,y)L has basis x,y, so one generator cannot generate L. No general
srank(L) <= srank(M) should be introduced.

C. prop:shared-direct-sum, lines 830–846
Record: PSC-I-P-SHARED-DIRECT-SUM
Objects: a single A acting diagonally on M direct-sum N.
Conclusion: max(r_M,r_N) <= rank_A(M direct-sum N) <= r_M+r_N.
Verdict: proved as written, once 'generators' is read as arbitrary indexed
families, as permitted by the definition. Coordinate projections give the
lower bound, and embedded minimum generating sets give the upper bound.
The zero cases pass. For scalar A=k and M=N=k, rank of the sum is 2 although
the component ranks are 1; hence the maximum lower bound can be strict.

D. thm:independent-direct-sum, lines 848–878
Record: PSC-I-T-INDEPENDENT-DIRECT-SUM
The requested alias thm:direct-sum-rank does not occur; this is the actual
label for the independent direct-sum formula.
Objects: left A-module M, left B-module N; product-ring action
(a,b)(m,n)=(am,bn) on the finite direct sum.
Conclusion: rank_(A x B)(M direct-sum N)=max(rank_A(M),rank_B(N)).
Verdict on raw argument: incomplete, with the missing implication being
cardinal indexing rather than a finite list of length r.
The formula is true as stated. Choose an index set I of cardinality the
maximum, inject both minimal generating index sets into I, and extend each
family by zeros. For a pair (m,n), take the finite union F of the supports
used in its two expressions. Then
(m,n)=sum_(i in F) (a_i,b_i)(m_i,n_i).
Thus the paired family generates and has cardinality at most |I|. The lower
bound is the two semilinear coordinate projections. Cardinal comparisons and
choices here are the ordinary classical set-theoretic convention also used
in the project's existing Lean definitions. If both ranks are 0, I is empty
and the module is 0. If just one is 0, pair its zero family with the other
family. Infinite cardinal ranks use finite supports for each vector and do
not require any infinite sum or completion.

The section recap at baseline line 981 says 'exactly additive for independent
product actions'. This is false and contradicts the displayed theorem.
Smallest nonzero example: A=B=k and M=N=k have independent-product rank 1,
not 1+1=2, because (1,1) generates using coefficients in k x k. Replace
'additive' with 'given by the maximum'. The single-operator proof correctly
uses the maximum; it must retain that formula.

E. prop:tensor-bound, lines 881–901
Record: PSC-I-P-TENSOR-BOUND
Objects: unital k-algebras A,B over a field, and left modules M,N; algebraic
tensor product with (a tensor b)(m tensor n)=am tensor bn.
Conclusion: rank_(A tensor B)(M tensor N) <= rank_A(M) rank_B(N).
Verdict on raw argument: incomplete, with the missing implication being
arbitrary cardinal-indexed generating sets.
The formula is true. Choose generating families indexed by I,J of minimum
cardinality. Bilinearity and the finite expressions for m,n express each
simple tensor as a finite sum of (a tensor b)(m_i tensor n_j). Simple tensors
span the algebraic tensor product, so the I x J family generates. Its image
cardinality is at most |I||J|. If either module is 0, its rank is 0, the
product family is empty, the tensor product is 0, and cardinal multiplication
0 times any cardinal is 0. This does not prove a completed tensor bound.

The stated warning against universal equality is correct. A concrete strict
example follows from the project's semisimple formula: take A=B=M_2(k),
M=k^2, N=(k^2)^3. The factor ranks are 1 and 2. A tensor B is M_4(k), and
M tensor N is three copies of its natural four-dimensional simple module,
so its rank is ceil(3/4)=1 < 2. This example is not a new dependency of the
bound and need not be added to the manuscript.

F. cor:cyclic-matrix, lines 1310–1328
Record: PSC-I-C-CYCLIC-MATRIX
Objects in raw statement: finite-dimensional X over arbitrary k, with T in
End_k(X) as established in the section; no X != 0 assumption.
Conclusion: equivalence of cyclic vector, rank 1, cyclic primary components,
one invariant factor, minpoly=charpoly, and geometric multiplicity 1 at every
eigenvalue after scalar extension to an algebraic closure.
Verdict: refuted, with the smallest valid counterexample X=0 and T=0.
The empty family generates X and its seed rank is 0, so item (ii) is false.
The zero vector generates X, so (i) is true under the actual cyclic definition.
There are no nonzero primary components and no eigenvalues, making (iii)
and (vi) vacuous. Rational canonical form has zero nonconstant invariant
factors, so (iv) is false. With the standard zero-space convention, both
polynomials are 1, making (v) true. The equivalence already fails from (i)
and (ii), independently of any convention on minpoly.

Least correction: require X != 0. Specify item (iii) as 'Every nonzero
p-primary component is cyclic.' Correct the proof sentence 'every mu_p(T)
equals one' to 'every mu_p(T) belonging to a nonzero primary component equals
one'. An absent component has mu_p=0. This is a proof quantifier repair even
when X != 0. Example: T=0 on k has mu_t=1 and mu_(t-1)=0, yet is cyclic.
The corrected criterion passes one-dimensional cases, repeated same-primary
blocks (rank at least 2), and distinct primary cyclic summands (rank 1 by
Chinese remainder combination). No algebraic-closure, separability, or
algebraic-closed base field restriction should be added. The existing theorem
correctly concerns arbitrary fields and then a scalar extension.

G. prop:conditioned-coordinates, lines 1630–1659
Record: PSC-I-P-CONDITIONED-COORDINATES
Objects: Banach X and a finite bounded packet system. Analysis A_P and
synthesis S_P are inverse bounded maps for a finite product norm equivalent
to the maximum norm; this is the exact cited thm:banach-packets contract.
Conclusion: kappa=||A_P||||S_P|| >= 1; both coordinate-operator norm estimates
have factor kappa.
Verdict: refuted, with the smallest valid counterexample X=0.
Take one packet P_1=0=I_X (or the permitted empty packet family). Both maps
are the zero maps between zero spaces, so both norms and kappa are 0. Thus
kappa >= 1 is false. The proof's equality ||I||=1 requires X != 0.
Both norm estimates themselves remain valid at zero: all terms are 0.
For nonzero X the inverse identities and submultiplicativity give precisely
the stated estimates and kappa >= 1; zero packet summands cause no problem.
Least correction: add X != 0 to the lower-bound claim, or to the whole
proposition if uniform local scope is preferred. Clarify the condition-number
definition's 'Banach direct-sum norm' as the equivalent finite product norm
of thm:banach-packets, so boundedness uses the actual named norm contract.
An orthogonal Hilbert sum in its usual l2 norm gives kappa=1, consistent with
the interpretation. No orthogonality hypothesis is needed for the theorem.

H. Unnumbered 'Packet descent to a quotient' microexample, lines 2709–2712,
immediately before thm:quotient-packet-descent
Objects: X=X_1 direct-sum X_2, K subset X_1, and T preserving K.
Conclusion in raw explanation: quotient packets are X_1/K,X_2, and T descends
with 'exactly the same block pattern', using induced quotient blocks.
Verdict: refuted, if 'block pattern' denotes the pattern of nonzero blocks,
as it does in the surrounding support discussion.
Smallest counterexample that retains both quotient packets nonzero:
X_1=k e_1 direct-sum k e_2, X_2=k e_3, K=k e_1,
T(e_3)=e_1, T(e_1)=T(e_2)=0.
Then T preserves K; the original source-2 target-1 block is nonzero; but all
induced quotient blocks are zero. The quotient still has the two nonzero
one-dimensional packets represented by e_2 and e_3. Thus the failure is not
just removal of a zero packet. In dimension 2 the analogous example with
K=X_1 already contradicts literal preservation, but removes a packet.
Safe corrected statement: source/target organization and every prescribed
zero block descend; a nonzero block may vanish, so support can shrink.
For retained quotient indices, Supp(overline T) is contained in Supp(T).
K=0 gives unchanged blocks; K=X gives a zero quotient; K=X_1 removes the
first packet. The quotient-packet theorem and block-descent corollary
immediately after the example are already correct: packet invariance and
T(K) subset K make all induced maps well defined, and
bar T_(beta alpha) pi = pi T_(beta alpha)
is exactly the correct statement. No reverse nonvanishing implication
follows from this identity.

2. DEFINITION AND CARDINALITY CONTRACT

Baseline def:generated-hull, lines 484–495: Hull_A(S)=sum_(s in S) A s for
S subset X. This is the submodule sum, so each element is a FINITE linear
combination even when S is infinite.
Baseline def:seed-rank, lines 694–705: min{|S|:AS=M}, with no finite-set
restriction; the text explicitly says 'When the value is finite'. The
analogous topological definition minimizes sets whose hull closure is X.
These definitions are cardinal-valued. A minimum exists under ordinary
classical set theory because S=M is a generating set and cardinalities are
well ordered. The zero module is generated by the empty set.

Contradictory baseline exposition occurs at the notation table (line 275),
seed-rank section roadmap (line 690), and compact glossary (line 3671), each
calling rank the minimum size of a FINITE family. For k^(N) with scalar
A=k, rank is aleph_0; no finite generating family exists. A fortiori no
finite minimum is defined there. Change this prose to cardinality and
state that a finite value is the usual minimal number of generators.
The finite distinguished family in def:seed-system is a separate valid
choice of object; it does not restrict the general module rank definition.
Do not silently replace the rank by N union {infinity} or add finite-
generation hypotheses to general formulas: that changes the actual source
contract and loses the distinctions between infinite cardinal ranks.
