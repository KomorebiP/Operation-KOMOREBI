# Roots of unity, projectors, and falling-factorial coordinates

**An extended hand-worked example · Komorebi P.**

One polynomial will pass through a Fourier-style residue projector, a Newton/falling-factorial coordinate change, finite differences, exact shift-transfer blocks, and residue refinement. We will keep the objects and operations separate. In particular, we will see why projecting *after* a change of coordinates requires transporting the projector, not reusing its old formula.

The mathematical identities are classical: the roots-of-unity filter, Stirling inversion, Newton interpolation, umbral polynomial coordinates, and the forward difference. The purpose of this example is to exhibit their precise composition and recovery conditions in the realization-and-transfer language of the collection. No new theorem, Lean compilation, general analytic continuation, or novelty claim is made here.

## 1. The destination: recover one ordinary power from three packets

Our fixed polynomial is

\[
f(x)=x^5.
\]

For a nonnegative integer \(k\), write the **falling factorial**

\[
x^{\underline{k}}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1),
\qquad x^{\underline0}=1.
\]

The classical Stirling expansion reads

\[
\boxed{x^5
=x^{\underline1}+15x^{\underline2}
+25x^{\underline3}+10x^{\underline4}+x^{\underline5}.}
\]

We will separate those *falling-factorial degrees* into three congruence classes, then evaluate at \(x=4\).

The exact falling factorials at four are

| \(k\) | \(4^{\underline{k}}\) | \(S(5,k)\) | Weighted contribution |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 4 |
| 2 | 12 | 15 | 180 |
| 3 | 24 | 25 | 600 |
| 4 | 24 | 10 | 240 |
| 5 | 0 | 1 | 0 |

Group the weighted contributions by \(k\bmod3\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
r=0:&\quad 25(24)=600,\\
r=1:&\quad 1(4)+10(24)=244,\\
r=2:&\quad 15(12)+1(0)=180.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{600+244+180=1024=4^5.}
\]

![Three exact residue packets after falling-factorial transport, with their values 600, 244 and 180 summing to 1024.](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/projection-diagram.svg)

[Download the complete coefficient and contribution table](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/packet-contributions.csv).

The remaining sections explain exactly which projector and coordinate map produce this calculation. They also exhibit a deliberately wrong operation.

### The packets count actual functions

There is a completely different way to calculate the six contributions above. Let \(X\) have five elements and let \(Y\) have four labelled elements. Every function \(f:X\to Y\) uses some number \(k\) of **distinct values** in its image.

The number of functions with image size \(k\) is

\[
\boxed{\#\{f:X\to Y:\ |\operatorname{im}f|=k\}
=S(5,k)\,4^{\underline k}.}
\]

To see why, first partition the five input elements into \(k\) nonempty fibers, in \(S(5,k)\) ways. Then assign **distinct** labels from \(Y\) to those fibers, in \(4^{\underline k}\) ways. This assignment is bijective: every function specifies exactly its inverse-image partition and its fiber labels.

The exact image-size histogram is

\[
(k,\#f)=(0,0),(1,4),(2,180),(3,600),(4,240),(5,0).
\]

Grouping by \(k\bmod 3\) reproduces the three projected values \(600,244,180\). In the executable checker, this table is independently generated by enumerating all \(4^5=1024\) functions as actual five-tuples and counting distinct entries. That independent algorithm does **not** call the Stirling recurrence, Newton transform, or roots-of-unity projector.

![Exact Python-generated histogram of all 1,024 functions from five inputs to four labels, by number of distinct image values.](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/function-image-histogram.svg)

[Download independently enumerated image-size histograms](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/image-histograms.csv).

This answers a question the original three-packet calculation left implicit: **the projector records the number of used output labels modulo three**. It is not simply sorting powers of \(x\).

## 2. Roots of unity: project onto residue degrees

Work over the polynomial ring \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)[z]\), where \(m\ge2\) and \(\zeta_m\) is a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity. For each \(0\le r<m\), define

\[
\boxed{
P_r^{(m)}g(z)=\frac1m\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
\zeta_m^{-r\ell}g(\zeta_m^\ell z).
}
\]

This expression is a finite sum of polynomials. There is no analytic convergence issue.

Apply the operator to a monomial:

\[
P_r^{(m)}z^k
=\left(\frac1m\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}
\zeta_m^{(k-r)\ell}\right)z^k
=\begin{cases}
z^k,&k\equiv r\pmod m,\\
0,&k\not\equiv r\pmod m.
\end{cases}
\]

The inner sum is either \(m\), or the geometric series with nonunit ratio and total zero. Consequently, on rational polynomials the result has rational coefficients, despite the cyclotomic expression:

\[
P_r^{(m)}g(z)=
\sum_{k\equiv r\ (m)}[z^k]g(z)\,z^k.
\]

It follows immediately that

\[
P_rP_s=\delta_{rs}P_r,
\qquad
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}P_r=I.
\]

These are complete, pairwise orthogonal *algebraic* projectors. Orthogonality here means \(P_rP_s=0\), not a claim about a particular inner product.

**Exact check without floating-point roots.** Let \(C\) be the cyclic permutation matrix of size \(m\). Its eigenvalues are precisely the \(m\)-th roots of unity and

\[
\frac1m\operatorname{tr}(C^{k-r})
=\begin{cases}1,&k\equiv r\pmod m,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

The standalone program verifies this equality using only integer counts of fixed coordinates of the cyclic permutation. This is an independent exact representation of the character filter, not a numerical evaluation of \(\zeta_m\).

## 3. The coordinate change is a linear map, not an algebra homomorphism

Define a \(\mathbb Q\)-linear map on polynomials by specifying its action on the monomial basis:

\[
\boxed{\Phi(z^k)=x^{\underline{k}}.}
\]

Because each falling factorial is monic of degree \(k\), this is a degree-preserving triangular linear isomorphism

\[
\Phi:\mathbb Q[z]\longrightarrow\mathbb Q[x].
\]

Do not read \(\Phi\) as evaluation at a particular \(z\). Its input and output variables name **different coordinate descriptions**.

The coefficients of the two triangular changes are the Stirling numbers:

\[
x^n=\sum_{k=0}^n S(n,k)x^{\underline{k}},
\]

and

\[
x^{\underline{k}}=\sum_{j=0}^k s(k,j)x^j,
\]

where \(s(k,j)\) are the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Phi^{-1}(x^n)=
\sum_{k=0}^nS(n,k)z^k.}
\]

For our fifth power,

\[
\Phi^{-1}(x^5)=
T_5(z)=z+15z^2+25z^3+10z^4+z^5,
\]

the fifth Touchard polynomial encountered in the [Stirling, Bell and Fubini demonstration](STIRLING_BELL_FUBINI.md).

Why is the word *linear* essential? Consider

\[
\Phi(z)^2=x^2
\qquad\text{but}\qquad
\Phi(z^2)=x(x-1).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\Phi(z^2)\ne\Phi(z)^2.}
\]

We cannot transport multiplication through \(\Phi\) while pretending the product in the target is unchanged. We will construct the correct transported product in Section 9.

## 4. Transport the projector into falling-factorial coordinates

The projector acting on the ordinary polynomial \(f(x)\) is

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}=\Phi P_r^{(m)}\Phi^{-1}.
}
\]

For a polynomial expanded as

\[
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^d a_kx^{\underline{k}},
\]

this becomes the explicit finite operation

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}f(x)=
\sum_{\substack{0\le k\le d\\k\equiv r\pmod m}}
a_kx^{\underline{k}}.
}
\]

Because \(\Phi\) is invertible, the transferred operators obey

\[
Q_rQ_s=\delta_{rs}Q_r,
\qquad \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}Q_r=I.
\]

This is the original projector system transported to a new basis, not a new numerical approximation.

For \(f(x)=x^5\) and \(m=3\), the three packets are

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0f&=25x^{\underline3},\\
Q_1f&=x^{\underline1}+10x^{\underline4},\\
Q_2f&=15x^{\underline2}+x^{\underline5}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Expanding them in ordinary powers makes the change of basis visible:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0f&=25x^3-75x^2+50x,\\
Q_1f&=10x^4-60x^3+110x^2-59x,\\
Q_2f&=x^5-10x^4+35x^3-35x^2+9x.
\end{aligned}
\]

All unwanted powers cancel in the sum, giving exactly \(x^5\).

There is also a concrete matrix formula. Let

\[
M_{i,k}=s(k,i),\qquad
(M^{-1})_{k,j}=S(j,k)
\]

be the linear coordinate matrices in ascending ordinary-power order. Then

\[
\boxed{
[Q_r]_{\text{power}}
=M\,D_r\,M^{-1},
\qquad
(D_r)_{k,k}=\mathbf1_{k\equiv r\pmod m},
}
\]

so that, for \(i\le j\),

\[
\boxed{
[Q_r]_{i,j}=
\sum_{\substack{i\le k\le j\\k\equiv r\pmod m}}
s(k,i)S(j,k).
}
\]

The source-bound [matrix CSV](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/projector-matrix.csv) gives all three \(6\times6\) rational matrices for degrees zero through five. The checker also verifies these entries directly against independently computed finite differences.

### A wrong projector, with an exact witness

If we mistakenly apply the original degree-filter rule directly to \(x^5\) in the *ordinary-power basis*, we obtain

\[
P_0^{(3)}x^5=0,\qquad
P_1^{(3)}x^5=0,\qquad
P_2^{(3)}x^5=x^5.
\]

At \(x=4\), that allocates the entire value \(1024\) to residue two.

But the **correct transported packets** give \((600,244,180)\). In particular,

\[
\boxed{(P_2x^5)(4)=1024
\quad\ne\quad
(Q_2x^5)(4)=180.}
\]

Both projector systems are mathematically legitimate. They project different coordinate labels. The failure comes from changing the representation without changing the operation.

## 5. Newton differences give a second, independently computable inverse

Write the forward difference as

\[
(\Delta f)(x)=f(x+1)-f(x).
\]

The falling-factorial basis satisfies

\[
\boxed{\Delta x^{\underline{k}}
=kx^{\underline{k-1}}.}
\]

Iterating, evaluating at zero, and using \(0^{\underline{k}}=0\) for \(k>0\) gives the exact Newton coefficient formula:

\[
\boxed{
a_k=\frac{(\Delta^kf)(0)}{k!},
\qquad
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\frac{(\Delta^kf)(0)}{k!}
x^{\underline{k}}
}
\]

for any polynomial \(f\in\mathbb Q[x]\) of degree at most \(d\).

This can be proved without Stirling numbers. It also reconstructs the same Stirling coefficients when \(f(x)=x^n\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\Delta^k x^n)(0)
&=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom kj j^n\\
&=k!S(n,k).
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equality counts surjections onto \(k\) labelled targets by inclusion and exclusion; it uses \(0^0=1\) in the usual polynomial-evaluation convention.

For the fifth power, the Newton coefficients are

\[
\left(\frac{\Delta^k x^5(0)}{k!}\right)_{k=0}^5
=(0,1,15,25,10,1).
\]

Thus the same coefficients have **three descriptions**: the Stirling triangle, the inverse linear map \(\Phi^{-1}\), and differences at zero.

The general transported projector can now be written without introducing an auxiliary variable:

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}f(x)=
\sum_{\substack{k\le\deg f\\k\equiv r\pmod m}}
\frac{(\Delta^kf)(0)}{k!}\,x^{\underline{k}}.
}
\]

This is a finite, executable formula for every rational polynomial.

### An all-degree generating-function bridge

The same partition counts form a single exponential generating function. Let

\[
G_r^{(m)}(t,x)=\sum_{n\ge0}Q_r^{(m)}(x^n)\frac{t^n}{n!}.
\]

The classical fixed-\(k\) Stirling identity

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}S(n,k)\frac{t^n}{n!}
=\frac{(e^t-1)^k}{k!}
\]

immediately yields

\[
\boxed{
G_r^{(m)}(t,x)=
\sum_{\substack{k\ge0\\k\equiv r\pmod m}}
\binom{x}{k}(e^t-1)^k.
}
\]

Here \(\binom{x}{k}=x^{\underline k}/k!\) is a polynomial in \(x\). The series is understood formally in \(t\), with every coefficient a polynomial in \(x\). Thus no convergence at a numerical \(t\) is required.

Summing over all residue addresses gives

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}G_r^{(m)}(t,x)
=(1+(e^t-1))^x
=\boxed{e^{xt}},
\]

the EGF of \(x^n\). This identity is also the finite-function counting formula \(\sum_k S(n,k)x^{\underline k}=x^n\) simultaneously for every \(n\).

For the concrete integer codomain \(x=4\) and \(m=3\), put \(u=e^t-1\). Because \(\binom4k=0\) for \(k>4\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_0^{(3)}(t,4)&=1+4u^3,\\
G_1^{(3)}(t,4)&=4u+u^4,\\
G_2^{(3)}(t,4)&=6u^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Extract the coefficient of \(t^5/5!\) and we recover, independently,

\[
\boxed{
5![t^5](G_0^{(3)},G_1^{(3)},G_2^{(3)})
=(600,244,180).
}
\]

The exact program checks this formal-series identity coefficientwise through degree eight for integer **and rational** parameters \(x\) and moduli two, three, and four. The integer values count functions. Noninteger values remain valid polynomial identities, but are not assigned a literal finite-set counting interpretation.

## 6. Transport differentiation: a cyclic finite-difference law

Let \(D_z=d/dz\). Since both operators lower their respective basis degrees in the same way,

\[
\Delta\Phi(z^k)
=kx^{\underline{k-1}}
=\Phi D_z(z^k).
\]

By linearity,

\[
\boxed{\Delta=\Phi D_z\Phi^{-1}.}
\]

Differentiation moves degree \(k\) to \(k-1\). Hence it moves residue \(r\) to \(r-1\), and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta Q_r^{(m)}
=Q_{r-1\pmod m}^{(m)}\Delta.
}
\]

In particular, \(\Delta^m\) commutes with every \(Q_r^{(m)}\).

For the fifth-power example, evaluate the differences of the *three correct packets* at \(x=4\):

| Original packet | Difference polynomial | Value at 4 | Target residue |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \(Q_0f=25x^{\underline3}\) | \(75x^{\underline2}\) | 900 | 2 |
| \(Q_1f=x^{\underline1}+10x^{\underline4}\) | \(1+40x^{\underline3}\) | 961 | 0 |
| \(Q_2f=15x^{\underline2}+x^{\underline5}\) | \(30x^{\underline1}+5x^{\underline4}\) | 240 | 1 |

These add to

\[
900+961+240=2101=5^5-4^5
=(\Delta x^5)(4).
\]

The shift in packet address is part of the operator transfer. Comparing residues without that shift would conflate two different operations.

[Download the finite-difference transfer table](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/difference-transfer.csv).

### Spectral projectors from one backward-difference operator

The cyclic projectors admit another realization that needs neither complex roots nor an explicit Stirling-matrix inverse for each input.

Introduce the **backward difference** \(\nabla f(x)=f(x)-f(x-1)\). Define the transported degree operator

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N=\Phi\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)\Phi^{-1}
=x\nabla.
}
\]

The second equality follows directly from

\[
x\nabla x^{\underline k}
=x\left(x^{\underline k}-(x-1)^{\underline k}\right)
=kx^{\underline k}.
\]

Thus the falling-factorial basis consists of \(\mathcal N\)-eigenvectors with eigenvalues \(k=0,1,2,\ldots\). This operator is **not** the ordinary Euler differential operator \(x\,d/dx\). For example,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N(x^2)=x(x^2-(x-1)^2)=2x^2-x
\ne 2x^2=x\frac{d}{dx}(x^2).
}
\]

On the finite-dimensional polynomial space \(V_d=\mathbb Q[x]_{\le d}\), define the usual rational Lagrange spectral factor

\[
L_{k,d}(\mathcal N)
=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le d\\j\ne k}}
\frac{\mathcal N-jI}{k-j}.
\]

Its action on \(x^{\underline h}\) is \(\delta_{kh}x^{\underline h}\) for \(0\le h\le d\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}\big|_{V_d}
=\sum_{\substack{0\le k\le d\\k\equiv r\pmod m}}
L_{k,d}(\mathcal N).
}
\]

This is a finite polynomial in the **actual difference operator**, with rational coefficients. The expression depends on the chosen degree bound \(d\); it is not claimed to be one finite-degree polynomial in \(\mathcal N\) valid on all of \(\mathbb Q[x]\).

We now have three exact descriptions of the *same* projector: a cyclotomic character average, conjugation by Stirling/Newton coordinates, and a rational spectral polynomial in \(x\nabla\). The checker constructs the third independently and compares its polynomial coefficients with the other descriptions.

A further compatibility identity is

\[
\boxed{
[\mathcal N,\Delta]=\mathcal N\Delta-\Delta\mathcal N=-\Delta,
}
\]

because on each basis vector \(x^{\underline k}\), the forward difference lowers the \(\mathcal N\)-eigenvalue by one. This supplies another algebraic explanation for the residue shift \(\Delta Q_r=Q_{r-1}\Delta\).

[Download the rational spectral/projector comparison](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/spectral-projectors.csv).

## 7. Translate the input and follow every cross-packet term

The earlier differentiation rule describes how a packet's degree decreases. We can now transport the **whole shift**, including its diagonal part and the terms crossing between residue classes.

Write

\[
(T_hf)(x)=f(x+h),\qquad h\in\mathbb Q.
\]

Falling factorials satisfy a binomial-type addition formula:

\[
\boxed{
(x+h)^{\underline k}=
\sum_{j=0}^k\binom kj
x^{\underline j}h^{\underline{k-j}}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the formal identity

\[
(1+u)^{x+h}=(1+u)^x(1+u)^h
\]

using \((1+u)^x=\sum_{k\ge0}x^{\underline k}u^k/k!\) in \(\mathbb Q[x][[u]]\), and compare the coefficient of \(u^k/k!\). No convergence or evaluation at a complex argument is involved.

If \(f=\sum_k a_kx^{\underline k}\), this supplies every cross-packet transfer block:

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}T_hQ_s^{(m)}f=
\sum_{\substack{k\equiv s\ (m)}}a_k
\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le k\\j\equiv r\ (m)}}
\binom kj h^{\underline{k-j}}x^{\underline j}.
}
\]

This expression works for rational \(h\), including negative and fractional steps. The corresponding source-to-target block matrix is not assumed to be diagonal.

### An especially simple two-tap shift

At \(h=1\), the only nonzero falling factorials \(1^{\underline\ell}\) occur at \(\ell=0,1\). Hence

\[
T_1=I+\Delta
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}T_1Q_s^{(m)}
=\delta_{rs}Q_s^{(m)}
+\delta_{r,s-1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)}\Delta Q_s^{(m)}.
}
\]

For our three residue packets:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_0T_1f\\Q_1T_1f\\Q_2T_1f
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
I&\Delta&0\\
0&I&\Delta\\
\Delta&0&I
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}Q_0f\\Q_1f\\Q_2f\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]

The off-diagonal difference operators are genuine transfers from one falling-degree residue into another.

At \(x=4\), compute each output packet independently:

| Target residue | Original \(Q_rf(4)\) | Carry from next residue | \(Q_r(T_1f)(4)\) | \((T_1Q_rf)(4)\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 600 | \(\Delta Q_1f(4)=961\) | **1561** | 1500 |
| 1 | 244 | \(\Delta Q_2f(4)=240\) | **484** | 1205 |
| 2 | 180 | \(\Delta Q_0f(4)=900\) | **1080** | 420 |
| Total | 1024 | 2101 | **3125** | 3125 |

Both right-hand columns reconstruct \(5^5=3125\), but they label the components differently:

\[
(Q_0T_1f,Q_1T_1f,Q_2T_1f)(4)
=(1561,484,1080),
\]

whereas

\[
(T_1Q_0f,T_1Q_1f,T_1Q_2f)(4)
=(1500,1205,420).
\]

![Python-generated exact transfer diagram displaying the original packet values, the incoming difference carries, and the shifted output values.](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/downloads/roots-unity-falling-factorial/translation-carry.svg)

The discrepancy is a **noncommutation witness**, not numerical error. In fact,

\[
\boxed{
[Q_r^{(m)},T_1]f
=\Delta Q_{r+1}^{(m)}f-\Delta Q_r^{(m)}f
}
\]

and for the three packets its evaluation at four is

\[
\boxed{(61,-721,660),\qquad61-721+660=0.}
\]

The sum of the commutator packets vanishes because \(\sum_rQ_r=I\), even though individual commutators need not vanish.

### Arbitrary integer steps and finite transfer bandwidth

When \(h\) is a nonnegative integer, \(h^{\underline\ell}=0\) for \(\ell>h\). Thus an input falling degree \(k\) can reach only degrees \(k,k-1,\ldots,k-h\). The \(m\times m\) residue transfer therefore has at most \(h+1\) allowed cyclic diagonals, possibly fewer when residues coincide. For instance, \(h=2\) requires three possible shifts rather than two.

Fractional or negative \(h\) need not have this fixed finite bandwidth: the connection formula is still a finite sum on each polynomial of fixed degree, but its number of potentially nonzero terms can grow with the degree.

The checker computes \(Q_rT_hQ_s\) from the falling-factorial binomial identity and, independently, by ordinary-power translation followed by the projector. It compares both through multiple moduli, polynomial degrees and rational shifts. A complete source-to-target table for \(h=0,1,2\) is downloadable.

### The corresponding carry in the all-degree generating functions

The filtered generating functions from Section 5 satisfy a compatible identity. The formal binomial recurrence

\[
\binom{x+1}{k}=\binom{x}{k}+\binom{x}{k-1}
\]

implies, with \(u=e^t-1\),

\[
\boxed{
G_r^{(m)}(t,x+1)
=G_r^{(m)}(t,x)
+(e^t-1)G_{r-1}^{(m)}(t,x).
}
\]

This concerns **evaluation of the original \(r\)-packet at the shifted argument**. It corresponds to \(T_1Q_rf\), not to \(Q_rT_1f\). Those two operations must not be silently identified. For \(m=3,x=4,n=5\), its coefficient changes are \(900,961,240\), precisely the differences in the last numerical column above.

The checker verifies this formal-series carry equation coefficientwise for several integer and rational \(x\), not solely at \(x=4\).

## 8. Refine the same packets without changing their sum

The same congruence class modulo three splits into two disjoint classes modulo six:

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(3)}=Q_r^{(6)}+Q_{r+3}^{(6)},
\qquad r=0,1,2.
}
\]

On \(x^5\), the six child values at \(x=4\) are:

| Parent residue | Child \(r\) | Child \(r+3\) | Parent total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 600 | 600 |
| 1 | 4 | 240 | 244 |
| 2 | 180 | 0 | 180 |

The zeros are meaningful. The degree-zero coefficient of \(x^5\) is zero, and \(4^{\underline5}=0\).

More generally, if \(M\) is a multiple of \(m\),

\[
Q_r^{(m)}=
\sum_{q=0}^{M/m-1}Q_{r+mq}^{(M)}.
\]

This is exact address refinement, not an error-producing truncation. Discarding a child instead leaves the explicitly omitted polynomial, which can be evaluated or reconstructed exactly.

## 9. Multiplication must also be transported

The linear change \(\Phi\) does not preserve ordinary polynomial multiplication. If we want multiplication in the Newton coordinate algebra to survive, define a *transported* product on \(\mathbb Q[x]\):

\[
\boxed{
f\star g=\Phi\bigl(\Phi^{-1}f\cdot\Phi^{-1}g\bigr).
}
\]

In particular,

\[
x^{\underline a}\star x^{\underline b}
=x^{\underline{a+b}},
\]

so

\[
x\star x=x(x-1)\ne x^2.
\]

This product is associative, commutative and unital because ordinary multiplication in \(\mathbb Q[z]\) has those properties. The transformed difference is a derivation **for \(\star\)**:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(f\star g)
=(\Delta f)\star g+f\star(\Delta g).
}
\]

Residue packets now multiply by cyclic convolution:

\[
\boxed{
Q_r^{(m)}(f\star g)
=\sum_{\substack{0\le a,b<m\\a+b\equiv r\pmod m}}
(Q_a^{(m)}f)\star(Q_b^{(m)}g).
}
\]

For comparison, ordinary pointwise multiplication satisfies the **shifted** finite-difference product rule

\[
\Delta(fg)=(\Delta f)g+f(x+1)(\Delta g).
\]

The naive differential Leibniz rule fails even for \(f=g=x\):

\[
\Delta(x^2)=2x+1\ne2x.
\]

This is a useful boundary example. We can transport an operation exactly, but the transported operation need not be identical to the familiar operation on the target coordinates.

## 10. General scope and conditions

### A sharp characteristic-three obstruction

The distinction between coordinate transport and a particular spectral formula is even clearer over a finite field.

The linear transformation \(\Phi(z^k)=x^{\underline k}\) is unitriangular with **integer** Stirling coefficients and inverse. It remains an invertible change of polynomial basis over **any commutative coefficient ring**, including \(\mathbb F_3\). What fails in characteristic three is the *difference quotient* \(a_k=(\Delta^kf)(0)/k!\) for \(k\ge3\), not the existence of the falling-factorial basis itself.

Likewise, our rational Lagrange formula for spectral projectors divides by \(k-j\). In characteristic three, the operator \(\mathcal N=x\nabla\) has colliding eigenvalues:

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=x^{\underline0}=1,&\quad\mathcal Nv_0&=0,\\
v_3&=x^{\underline3}=x^3-x\ne0,&\quad\mathcal Nv_3&=3v_3=0
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3[x].
\end{aligned}
\]

The *coefficient projector* by parity is still perfectly well defined: the order \(m=2\) is invertible modulo three and the root \(-1\) exists in \(\mathbb F_3\). Its action is

\[
\boxed{
Q_0^{(2)}v_0=v_0,\qquad Q_0^{(2)}v_3=0.
}
\]

Suppose, contrary to fact, that one polynomial \(H(\mathcal N)\) represented \(Q_0^{(2)}\) on all polynomials of degree at most three. Both \(v_0\) and \(v_3\) are nonzero eigenvectors with the same eigenvalue zero, so \(H(\mathcal N)\) acts on each as multiplication by \(H(0)\). The first required value forces \(H(0)=1\), while the second forces \(H(0)=0\). Contradiction.

Thus **the projector survives, but its proposed realization as a polynomial in this single degree operator fails** in that coefficient field. On degrees strictly below the characteristic, the distinct eigenvalues permit the usual Lagrange construction; the collision becomes unavoidable at degree three for this example.

There is a parallel finite-difference obstruction: over \(\mathbb F_3[x]\),

\[
\boxed{\Delta^3=(T_1-I)^3=T_3-I=0.}
\]

The equality uses characteristic three and \(T_3f(x)=f(x+3)=f(x)\). Consequently, high-order differences cannot recover all falling-factorial coefficients by division. The integral Stirling matrix inverse still does.

This is a deliberate negative control against extending a rational spectral argument across a characteristic boundary without checking its denominators and eigenvalue separation.
