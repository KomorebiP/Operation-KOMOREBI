# Future Developments

My future research will focus on Hilbert's 12th and 24th problems, the Strong
Artin conjecture, and the Langlands program. Alongside these questions, I plan
to refine my work on machine learning and reasoning, improve the existing
mathematics and software, and make the website and its GitHub repositories
easier to access and use.

Updated 4 November 2026. The linked records describe progress, remaining
requirements, and the evidence for each work.

These plans and the existing work will likely evolve as I review research and
tools released by OpenAI and others.

## Main research directions

### Hilbert's 12th problem

The next steps are to develop the explicit arithmetic constructions further and
investigate the remaining questions about analytic comparison and realization.
The existing Hilbert 12 studies provide starting points. Their finite results
and open analytic obligations are recorded separately.

[Ambient dual slice](works/hilbert12-ambient-dual-slice.md) ·
[Analytic Fourier branch](works/hilbert12-analytic-fourier.md) ·
[Cubic divisor development](works/hilbert12-cubic-divisor.md)

### Hilbert's 24th problem

The work on proof paths, validity, interpretation, and review provides a basis
for studying proof simplicity and comparison. The aim is to find ways to make
proofs easier to understand, compare, and check.

[Proof Path Theory](works/proof-path-theory.md)

### Strong Artin

Research on the Strong Artin conjecture continues, with particular attention to
the remaining global automorphic attachment and reproducible checks of the
existing formal developments. The general conjecture remains open.

[Strong Artin: current work and evidence](works/strong-artin-reader.md)

### Langlands program

Papers XI and XII provide starting points for investigating connections between
arithmetic realizations, Hecke data, and automorphic constructions. The
outstanding arithmetic inputs and conditional comparisons remain explicit as
this work develops.

[Paper XI: arithmetic realization](works/paper-xi.md) ·
[Paper XII: conditional automorphic completion](works/paper-xii.md)

### Machine learning and reasoning

The current work on machine learning and reasoning includes the Large Signal
Model, Machine Native Reasoning, and Machine Understanding. Further development
focuses on sharper mathematical models, clearer links between theory and
implementation, and reproducible evaluation of practical claims.

[Large Signal Model](works/monographs-large-signal-model.md) ·
[Machine Native Reasoning](works/monographs-machine-native-reasoning.md) ·
[Machine Understanding](works/research-machine-understanding.md)

## Reading editions and visual guides

The twelve core papers already have online reading editions and visual guides.
The [Large Signal Model](works/monographs-large-signal-model.md) also has an
online edition with its statements, written proofs, and formal correspondence
linked together.

Plans for the reading library include more guided paths, additional books, and
closer connections between explanations and worked examples. The expanded
Machine Native Reasoning book is now available as a reconstructed editorial
edition, with 97 chapters, an editable TeX package, and recorded source and
build checks. Reconciling its statements with the accepted core and reviewing
the wider claims remain separate tasks.

## Lean and reproducible checks

Browser Lean development focuses on library loading, compiler compatibility,
and reliable checks of larger dependency chains. Wider access to retained
formal sources, particularly the newer Special Functions developments, is
another priority. Reproduction instructions should also stay aligned with
published editions.

Further formalization will follow explicit maps between manuscript claims and
Lean declarations. Editions that rely on inherited compiler evidence still need
fresh checks against their pinned sources and dependencies.

[Try browser Lean](https://KomorebiP.github.io/Operation-KOMOREBI/lean/browser/index.html) ·

## Interactive mathematics

Further development of the Signal, Recovery, and J-Space laboratories will
connect each experiment more closely to the mathematics it illustrates.
Guided experiments, useful comparisons, and results that readers can inspect
and reproduce remain priorities.

New examples will state their assumptions and the scope of their exact or
numerical results. They will also link to the relevant sources. The evidence
for the Large Signal Model's mathematical edition is recorded separately from
the evidence for its software and empirical development.

Easier navigation and access are priorities for the website and GitHub
repositories.

cross-links, simpler setup instructions, useful downloads, and straightforward

mathematical reading order, diagram descriptions, and the original PDFs.
