# 20. Separate support from multiplicity

[All examples](../HAND_WORKED_EXAMPLES.md) · [Glossary: module support](../GLOSSARY.md#module-support) · [Papers, proofs, Lean and code](#papers-proofs-lean-and-code)

Let $R=\mathbb Q[t]$. Compare the modules $M_1=R/(t)$ and $M_2=R/(t^2)$.
This is an elementary model for the support distinctions used in Paper XII.

## Write the presentations

Each module has one generator and one relation:

```math
R\xrightarrow{\ t\ }R\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow0,\qquad
R\xrightarrow{\ t^2\ }R\longrightarrow M_2\longrightarrow0.
```

The presentation matrices are the one-entry matrices $[t]$ and $[t^2]$.
Their zeroth Fitting ideals are generated by those entries: $(t)$ and $(t^2)$.
These are also the annihilator ideals in these two cyclic examples.

## Compare the fibers

Specializing the parameter to a rational value $t=\lambda$ replaces the
presentation matrices by $[\lambda]$ and $[\lambda^2]$ over $\mathbb Q$.
When $\lambda\ne0$, both matrices are invertible and both cokernels are zero.
At $\lambda=0$, both are zero maps and both cokernels have dimension one.

Thus both modules have support at the point $t=0$. More precisely, the only
prime ideal in their support is $(t)$. Their support sets agree even though
the ideals $(t)$ and $(t^2)$ retain different multiplicity information.

## Inspect the action inside each module

As a rational vector space, $M_1$ has basis $(1)$ and dimension one.
The space $M_2$ has basis $(1,t)$ and dimension two. Multiplication by $t$
on $M_2$ is

```math
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J\ne0,\qquad J^2=0.
```

The modules cannot be isomorphic because their rational dimensions differ.
Even the one-dimensional special fiber of $M_2$ does not retain its full
two-dimensional rational structure.

## Check whether a vector-space projector respects the module

The matrix $P=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ is an idempotent on the underlying
two-dimensional space. However,

```math
PJ=0,\qquad JP=J.
```

It fails to commute with multiplication by $t$, so it is not an $R$-linear
projector. A decomposition of the vector space need not split the module.

In fact, an element of the quotient algebra has the form $a+bt$ with $t^2=0$.
The idempotent equation gives

```math
(a+bt)^2=a+bt\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a^2=a,\qquad 2ab=b.
```

Over $\mathbb Q$, either $a=0$ or $a=1$, and either choice forces $b=0$.
Thus this algebra has only the idempotents zero and one. Its single support
point does not provide an extra packet splitting.

## Try it

Replace the relation by $t^3=0$. Find the rational dimension, Fitting ideal,
special-fiber dimension and nilpotency index of multiplication by $t$.

**Answer:** For $M_3=R/(t^3)$ they are $3$, $(t^3)$, $1$ and $3$.
In the basis $(1,t,t^2)$, multiplication shifts $1$ to $t$ and $t$ to $t^2$,
then sends $t^2$ to zero. The support is still the point $t=0$.

These calculations concern the supplied modules. Paper XII’s patching and
automorphic conclusions also require its stated arithmetic inputs.
