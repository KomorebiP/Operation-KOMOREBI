Reading the pictures
The diagrams in this guide describe mathematical objects with declared rules. A node is not a metaphor
for unspecified information. An arrow has a domain, a codomain, and a mathematical interpretation. A
loop can impose a new equation. A projection may preserve one quantity while changing another.
The forty plates follow four questions and their boundary cases.

 1. Which collections of local data form a coherent realization?
 2. What prevents prescribed data from extending, and what remains free after extension?
 3. How do symmetries and overlap identifications survive gluing?
 4. When does a small residual guarantee a nearby exact answer?

The accompanying monograph supplies the definitions and proofs. The plates can be read in sequence
or approached by topic through the contents. A diagram does not replace the hypotheses of the result it
illustrates.

  The organizing distinction
  A local appearance is a coordinate. A realization is a compatible family of coordinates. A symmetry
  compares realizations. An obstruction records a failure that the permitted corrections cannot
  remove.


The meaning of color and line
In the schematic diagrams, green marks retained structure and ochre marks a discrepancy or correction.
The numerical charts have their own labelled legends. Their meaning is encoded by labels, markers, and
line patterns, not by color alone. Open and filled squares in Plate 16 distinguish recovered coordinates
from possible support. Every chart evaluates the formula printed beside it. The three-dimensional
projection plate uses genuine spatial coordinates, not a perspective sketch.

Mathematical scope
The general theory of protected repair, bounded realization, correction budgets, regularization, vanishing-
noise limits, solver certificates, and changes of basis is supported by Lean proofs. The Lean companion
identifies the precise formal statements and explains how to reproduce their verification.
The explicit infinite diagonal examples have printed proofs, including their tail estimates, asymptotic
constants, and rounded noise schedule. Those instances are not themselves formalized in Lean. The
plotted enclosures illustrate the stated estimates. They do not replace the infinite arguments.

03 / The smallest nontrivial extension problem
The coarse value is prescribed. The finer value must be an integer whose double is that coarse value.
Filled points extend. Crosses do not.

For an even seed, the extension is unique. For an odd seed, it is absent. The seed 2 extends as (2, 1), so
the construction is not limited to the zero realization.
Adding an independent unprescribed integer coordinate changes the number of extensions of every even
seed from one to infinitely many. It leaves the parity obstruction unchanged. This separates feasibility
from ambiguity in a model that can be checked completely.

  The ambient realization space is nonempty, but not every prescribed partial datum belongs to the
  image of restriction.

06 / Retain the detail that coarsening removes
A right inverse of the coarsener supplies a chosen lift. Subtracting the lifted coarse part leaves the detail.
Repeating this construction gives an exact finite telescoping decomposition.

  A coarse observation alone can lose information. The pair consisting of coarse data and retained
  detail is lossless under the stated splitting identity.

forgets the comparison witnesses and the automorphisms.

For a single C2 loop with coefficients in S3 , cocycles correspond to involutions. There are four involu-

19 / A later choice can create an obstruction
An initial prescribed value admits infinitely many completions. Prescribing a middle coordinate removes
that freedom, but it may also select an impossible branch.




                           Initial seed (7, 0): every middle value is locally coherent

The first view is fixed at (7, 0). A proposed middle value (7, m) extends exactly when m = 2n. Even
values have a unique completion. Odd values have none. The initial problem itself remains solvable.
For nested prescribed domains U ⊆ W, the ambiguity group shrinks and the quotient obstruction
has a forgetful map QW → QU . A nonzero obstruction at the earlier stage persists, but a zero earlier
obstruction need not make every later choice feasible.

The complete picture
The set-valued layer asks whether the views agree. The additive layer measures their failure to agree
and restricts which corrections are permitted. The groupoid layer retains symmetries and comparison
witnesses. The stack layer glues coherent local objects on a specified site. The analytic layer asks
how large a correction is required and how that correction changes when the model changes. The
approximation layer then asks whether the candidates themselves converge. The bounded-budget
extension asks whether exactness is possible without unbounded change, and connects a regularized
optimizer to a directly checkable budget witness. The phase model and the escaping Hilbert model show
why coherent composition, approximate correction, and intended realization remain different questions.
These layers share comparison maps, not an unrestricted identity. Passing from one to another requires
the hypotheses printed in the monograph. A coherent family does not automatically realize every
intended semantic object, and a theorem about one declared norm is not automatically a theorem about
another.

  A practical reading rule
  Before applying a Komorebi Space result, identify the views, the restrictions, the protected data,
  the permitted corrections, and the kind of conclusion required. Existence, uniqueness, symmetry,
  convergence, and stable recovery have different mathematical tests.
