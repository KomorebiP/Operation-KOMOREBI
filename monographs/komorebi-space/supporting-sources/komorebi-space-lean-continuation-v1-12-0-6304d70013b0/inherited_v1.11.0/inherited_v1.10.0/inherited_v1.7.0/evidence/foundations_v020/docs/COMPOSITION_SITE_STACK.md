# Komorebi Space Theory: a stack on the composition site
## v0.14.0 · Komorebi P.

This continuation constructs a Grothendieck topology on full patches of the view category and proves that the existing cocycle gauge system is a stack for that topology. The conclusion is stated with mathlib's standard `Pseudofunctor.IsStack`, not a project-defined predicate that merely repeats the desired result. The coefficient system is still strict and group-valued. No infinity-category, universal view doctrine, or arithmetic correspondence is inferred.

## 1. The precise space and its base

Fix a category of views \(\mathcal V\), groups \(G_v\), and homomorphisms
\[
 f^*:G_w\longrightarrow G_v\qquad(f:v\to w),
 \quad (1_v)^*=1,\quad(gf)^*=f^*g^*.
\]
Groups need not commute, and the restriction homomorphisms need not be invertible. Universe and size bounds are exactly those in the Lean declarations.

The base category is the inclusion poset
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal P(\mathcal V)=\bigl(\operatorname{Set}(\operatorname{Ob}\mathcal V),\subseteq\bigr).}
\]
A subset \(W\) means the **full** subcategory \(\mathcal V|_W\): it retains every original arrow between its objects. The new category of patches is not confused with the original category of views.

At a patch \(W\), define
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak K_G(W)=Z^1(\mathcal V|_W;G|_W)\mathbin{/\mkern-6mu/}\prod_{v\in W}G_v.}
\]
Objects are the existing coherent labels
\[
 c_{1_v}=1,\qquad c_{gf}=c_f f^*(c_g),
\]
and arrows are actual gauges, with
\[
 (a\cdot c)_f=a_vc_f[f^*(a_w)]^{-1}.
\]
This is the unframed gauge groupoid. The double slash retains arrows and automorphisms rather than only orbit classes. Restriction to a subpatch retains the original coefficient homomorphisms and restricts the same cocycle and gauge values.

**Lean:** `PatchViews`, `GroupViewSystem.onPatch`, `patchRestrict`, `patchDiagram`, `patchPseudofunctor`.

## 2. The composition topology

A composable pair
\[
 q=(v\xrightarrow f w\xrightarrow g z)
\]
has support \(|q|=\{v,w,z\}\). Identity arrows are included, so the support can be a singleton or a two-element set.

A sieve \(\mathcal R\) on \(W\) is a downward-closed collection of subpatch inclusions into \(W\). Define
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal R\in J_{\mathrm{comp}}(W)
 \iff \forall q,\ |q|\subseteq W\Longrightarrow (|q|\hookrightarrow W)\in\mathcal R.}
 \tag{1}
\]
The code constructs an actual `GrothendieckTopology (Set V)` and proves all its fields.

**Maximality.** The maximal sieve contains every composition support.

**Base change.** For \(Z\subseteq W\), a support contained in \(Z\) is also contained in \(W\). It therefore belongs to the pullback of every covering sieve on \(W\).

**Transitivity.** Let \(\mathcal R\) cover \(W\), and suppose another sieve \(\mathcal T\) covers locally over every member of \(\mathcal R\). For a support \(|q|\subseteq W\), its inclusion belongs to \(\mathcal R\). Local covering on \(|q|\), evaluated on the same composable pair, puts the identity of \(|q|\) in the pullback of \(\mathcal T\). Thus \(|q|\hookrightarrow W\) belongs to \(\mathcal T\), proving (1).

For any family \(U_i\subseteq W\), the sieve generated by its inclusions is covering precisely when
\[
 \boxed{\forall(v\xrightarrow f w\xrightarrow g z)\text{ in }W,
 \quad\exists i:\ v,w,z\in U_i.}
 \tag{2}
\]
This is proved for the standard `Sieve.ofArrows`, not only for a separate generated-family definition. The family laws are also explicit:
\[
 \{W\}\in J_{\mathrm{comp}}(W),\qquad
 \{U_i\}\in J_{\mathrm{comp}}(W)\Longrightarrow
 \{U_i\cap Z\}\in J_{\mathrm{comp}}(Z),
\]
\[
 \{U_i\}\text{ covers }W,\quad
 \{T_{ij}\}_j\text{ covers }U_i
 \Longrightarrow \{T_{ij}\}_{i,j}\text{ covers }W.
\]
The empty patch has the empty cover. At the support of a composable pair, a covering sieve must be maximal. This last fact is a proved description of this chosen topology, not a general assertion about sites.

**Lean:** `CompositionSite.topology`, `ofArrows_covering_iff`, `identity_cover`, `intersection_cover`, `composite_cover`, `support_covering_iff_top`, `empty_cover`.

## 3. An actual restriction pseudofunctor

The patch assignment is first constructed as the strict category-valued functor
\[
 \mathfrak K_G:\mathcal P(\mathcal V)^{op}\to\mathbf{Cat}.
\]
Each value is a groupoid. Identity and composite restrictions are proved equal as functors. Its standard pseudofunctor interface uses identity comparison isomorphisms with all coherence laws proved.

This provides a fixed object to which the library's descent and stack definitions apply. The object is not replaced by an unspecified equivalent stack or by stackification.

## 4. Main theorem: standard stackhood

For the data above,
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak K_G\text{ is a stack on }(\mathcal P(\mathcal V),J_{\mathrm{comp}}).}
 \tag{3}
\]
The actual Lean statement is
```lean
theorem GroupViewSystem.patch_isStack (F : GroupViewSystem V G) :
    F.patchPseudofunctor.IsStack (CompositionSite.topology V)
```
with the category and group instances explicitly quantified in the module.

The target `IsStack` is imported from `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Descent.IsStack`. It includes the standard prestack condition and effective descent on every covering sieve. Neither field is assumed for the Komorebi system.

### The proof connects two descent interfaces

The v0.13 theorem used local cocycle labels and gauge values on overlaps of full patches. Mathlib's standard `DescentData` instead includes comparison morphisms on arbitrary common subpatches and naturality under further restriction. The new code connects those actual data.

1. **Extract local overlap values.** For a view \(v\in U_i\cap U_j\), evaluate the standard comparison on the singleton patch \(\{v\}\). Its naturality proves that this value agrees with evaluation of the comparison on every larger common subpatch. The standard composition law becomes the previous noncommutative triple-overlap equation.

2. **Construct a realization.** Apply the existing concrete effectuation theorem to the extracted labels. The resulting global cocycle and local gauges satisfy all standard overlap equations, not only the singleton equations. This gives an isomorphism from the standard restriction of the globalization to the original standard descent datum.

3. **Retain all morphisms.** A standard descent morphism between restrictions of two global cocycles yields local gauges that agree on overlaps. The previous morphism-gluing theorem supplies a global gauge. Restricting after gluing and gluing after restriction are proved inverse. Therefore standard restriction is fully faithful.

4. **Pass to every covering sieve.** Index a covering sieve by its actual arrows. For each composable pair, its support inclusion is an arrow of the sieve by (1). Thus this indexed family satisfies (2). Standard restriction is an equivalence for this family, supplying `IsStackFor` for every covering sieve. The standard `IsStack.of_isStackFor` constructor proves (3).

The topology does not mention \(G\). A single composition site supports this theorem for every strict group-valued coefficient system on the chosen \(\mathcal V\).

**Lean:** `StandardDescent.pullHom_value`, `hom_value`, `label_comp`, `label_edge`, `toConcrete`, `realizationIso`, `essentially_surjective`, `fullyFaithful`, `restriction_isEquivalence`, `GroupViewSystem.patch_isStack`.

## 5. What gluing means on this site

For a covering family \(U_i\subseteq W\), local objects \(c_i\) are accompanied by gauges \(a_{ij}\) satisfying
\[
 c_j(f)=a_{ij}(v)c_i(f)[f^*a_{ij}(w)]^{-1},
 \qquad a_{ik}(v)=a_{jk}(v)a_{ij}(v).
\]
Then there is a global object with compatible local identifications. Those identifications implement the supplied overlap gauges. Compatible morphisms also glue uniquely. Consequently the ordinary restriction functor is an equivalence of descent categories. Since the fibers are groupoids, it preserves their symmetry information.

Uniqueness is up to the unique comparison **respecting the chosen descent identifications**. It does not remove automorphisms of the underlying global object, designate a canonical global representative, or make arbitrary incompatible local data extendable.

Base changes and iterated refinements of covers are covered by the same statement, because the cover laws were proved rather than asserted.

## 6. Two finite witnesses prevent a vacuous interpretation

On the four-view chain \(0\le1\le2\le3\), take
\[
 U_i=\{0,1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}.
\]
Lean proves that all patches are proper but the generated sieve is covering. Every composable pair has at most three endpoints and is contained in a patch. The theorem therefore genuinely combines local pieces; it does not require a patch equal to the whole object.

On the three-view chain \(0\le1\le2\), take the analogous proper patches. Every object and individual arrow is covered, but the composition \(0\to1\to2\) lies in no patch. Lean proves that the generated sieve is **not** covering in \(J_{\mathrm{comp}}\).

Thus object coverage, arrow coverage, and composition coverage remain distinct. The new theorem does not silently extend the previous result to an insufficient covering notion.

**Lean:** `CompositionSiteExamples.four_proper_cover`, `four_patches_proper`, `four_sieve_covering`, `three_cover_arrows`, `three_not_composition_cover`, `three_sieve_not_covering`.

## 7. Scope of the stronger claim

The model is now an ordinary stack of nonabelian cocycle realizations on a specified combinatorial site. It is no longer necessary to call this model merely stack-like. This is not a proof that every earlier proposed Komorebi construction is the same stack.

Still outside this theorem: arbitrary category-valued coefficients, higher homotopies or infinity-stacks, unrestricted changes of view doctrine, equivalence with all intended mathematical objects, numerical or norm-preserving descent, or an arithmetic/automorphic existence theorem. The analytic tower and residual-reconstruction results retain their separate hypotheses.

The topology is an explicit choice determined by the original category and the decision to cover composition supports. It is not claimed to be the unique possible topology, a maximal topology of effective descent, or an unprecedented mathematical construction. The proof does not call a stackification procedure or assume effectivity as part of the definition of a cover.
