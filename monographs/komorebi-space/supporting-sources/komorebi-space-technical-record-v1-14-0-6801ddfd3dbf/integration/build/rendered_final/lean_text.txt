About this guide
This guide connects the mathematical statements of Komorebi Space to their Lean definitions and proofs.
It is intended for readers who want to inspect a theorem’s hypotheses, use the library in another formal
development, or reproduce its verification. The monograph supplies the mathematical narrative. The
present volume supplies the route from that narrative to exact declarations.
The library constructs coherent realizations from compatible observations, describes the obstruction to a
permitted correction, and studies quantitative repair in normed and Hilbert spaces. Its later analytic
results include attained budget profiles, sharp dual certificates, regularization limits, and coordinate
invariant stopping certificates. These results are organized below by mathematical subject.
The accompanying edition contains 160 production modules and 4 import entrypoints. Its source
contains 1,846 explicitly written theorem and lemma declarations. The accepted declaration inventories
contain 5,321 project declarations, a larger count that includes definitions, structure operations, instances,
and compiler generated helpers. These figures describe different objects and should not be interchanged.
The formal boundary. The coherent realization, obstruction, descent, repair, and general Hilbert
space results described here have checked Lean proofs. The explicit infinite diagonal family, its
certified tails, the sharp limit over all real budgets, and the rounded noisy parameter rate also have
checked counterparts. The source map identifies the exact declarations and their hypotheses. This
coverage concerns the stated results, rather than every explanatory sentence or illustration in the
monograph.
The ordinary build and the fuller verification audit serve different purposes. A build elaborates the
library. The accepted audit additionally checks declaration provenance, permitted axioms, source
identity, reproducibility, and diagnostic controls. A separate technical record preserves that evidence
without making the reader follow the development history.

1 Reading a formal statement
1.1 Objects, hypotheses, and conclusions
A Lean theorem is a term of its declared proposition. Definitions determine what the proposition means.
Reading only a theorem’s English name can therefore hide an essential distinction: a correction in the
whole permitted kernel differs from a correction in a chosen subspace, and an attainable residual differs
from a residual in the closure of the attainable range.
The library places these distinctions in types. A continuous linear operator has Lean type H →L[R] Y,
conventionally written with the continuous-linear-map arrow. A permitted correction for a protected
observation p : E →Z belongs to the subtype p.ker. Its ambient value is accessed by a.val. A full
coordinate system is a Module.Basis; the basis property supplies independence and spanning. A genuine
inverse coordinate change is guarded by IsUnit C.
The mathematical equations in this guide are readable forms of the stated propositions. For exact binders,
universe levels, and typeclass arguments, inspect the source or ask Lean to print the declaration. The
following commands can be placed in a file after the relevant import.
import KomorebiSpaceCombined
#check KomorebiSpace.CorrectionProblem.flatten_iff
#print KomorebiSpace.CorrectionProblem.flatten_iff
#print axioms KomorebiSpace.CorrectionProblem.flatten_iff
The first command displays the type, the second also displays the proof term, and the third displays
its axiom dependencies. An identifier appearing in a module need not share the module’s name. In
particular, both coordinate modules contribute to the namespace KomorebiSpace.CoordinateCovector.
1.2 Conventions that matter in applications
For a residual map D and a protected observation p, the repaired object is f −a, where a ∈ker p. Thus
its residual is D f −Da. The regularization objective uses ∥Ta −r∥2, whose sign is immaterial inside a
norm, but the dual witness is naturally r −Ta.
The library’s gradient is the normal equation residual
gα(a) = T∗(Ta −r) + αa.
It is one half of the conventional derivative gradient of ∥Ta −r∥2 + α∥a∥2. Every error and stopping
estimate below uses this normalization. Substituting the full derivative gradient without adjusting
constants changes the statement.
Statements involving minimization assume α > 0. Several algebraic identities involving coordinate
residuals hold for every real α. The positivity hypotheses must be read from the theorem in use rather
than transferred from a neighboring result.
1.3 Existence and computation
Many constructions are marked noncomputable. They use classical existence or choice, such as selecting
the minimum norm preimage or an inverse limit presentation. Their equations and estimates are kernel
checked mathematical results. They do not, by themselves, provide a floating point solver, a complexity
bound, or an executable algorithm for constructing the selected object.

2 Using and rebuilding the library
2.1 The four import entrypoints
The source distribution has a lean/ directory with the package configuration, toolchain specification,
dependency pins, and the KomorebiSpace/ module directory. The following imports are available.
KomorebiSpace
The coherent realization, obstruction, descent, transport, and quantitative repair foundation.
KomorebiSpaceRegularization
Regularized Hilbert repair, primal and dual certificates, and protected regularized repair.
KomorebiSpaceCombined
The general library, including bounded realization, budget duality and robustness, regularization
limits, and arbitrary basis coordinate certificates.
KomorebiSpaceDiagonal
The actual infinite diagonal model, its quantitative bounds, its sharp budget asymptotic, and its
rounded noisy parameter rate.
For a new application, import the narrowest module that contains the required theorem. To make the
whole edition available in one file, use both imports:
import KomorebiSpaceCombined
import KomorebiSpaceDiagonal
The two entrypoints retain the separately verified general and diagonal source interfaces.

The source targets Lean 4.34.0-rc2, compiler revision 6a10ac8c22beadecabdbb0919c2b50214762f91d. Its
mathlib revision is aeb72f1e9981b6d35b426b16dca7759be3e7ba06. The distributed dependency manifest

3 Coherent coordinates and realization
Module: KomorebiSpace.Realization
A resolution gives types Xn and restrictions rij : Xj →Xi for i ≤j, with identity and composition laws.
Its point space is constructed explicitly:
X.Point = {(xn)n∈N : rij(xj) = xi whenever i ≤j}.
The Lean type Resolution.Point is a subtype of the product of all coordinate types. No axiom asserting
the existence of a global realization is added. The type may be empty when the compatibility conditions
cannot be met.
An exact finite level map consists of maps fn : Xn →Yn that commute with every restriction. The
construction Resolution.Map.onLimit applies the maps coordinatewise. Its output is a point because
naturality converts coherence in X into coherence in Y.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.Resolution.Map.onLimit_unique
The uniqueness theorem says that a function g : X.Point →Y.Point with the specified coordinate values
equals the constructed function. Coordinate equality is the entire uniqueness condition. This does not
assert that every function between point spaces is induced by a map at the same resolution levels.
A cone from a type S is a compatible family S →Xn. The universal property is expressed as a unique
function into the explicitly constructed point type:
Lean: KomorebiSpace.Resolution.Cone.exists_unique_lift
∃! F : S →X.Point,
(F(s))n = legn(s)
for every s, n.

4 Finite observation and change of resolution
Module: KomorebiSpace.FiniteObservation

5 Protected correction and obstruction
Module: KomorebiSpace.Correction
Let E, R, S be additive commutative groups. A correction problem consists of a residual homomorphism
D : E →R and a protected semantics homomorphism p : E →S. An admissible correction lies in ker p.
Its possible residual changes form the subgroup D(ker p).
The obstruction of f ∈E is the quotient class
[D f ] ∈R/D(ker p).
The quotient is part of the Lean definition CorrectionProblem.Obstruction. Its vanishing is equivalent to
the existence of an admissible correction with residual D f.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.CorrectionProblem.obstruction_zero_iff
Subtracting that correction gives the central equivalence:

[D f ] = 0
⇐⇒
∃y ∈E,
Dy = 0 and py = p f.
Only additive group structure is needed. The formal statement therefore separates the algebraic obstruc-
tion from later metric or Hilbert space refinements.
5.1 Invariance and witnesses
The obstruction is unchanged by admissible correction. If a homomorphism L : R →A annihilates every
D(a) with pa = 0, but L(D f ) ̸= 0, it gives a witness that the obstruction is nonzero.

semantics condition is consequently substantive mathematical input.

6 View systems, cocycles, and descent
Module: KomorebiSpace.ViewSections
A view system associates an object type to each object of a category and restriction maps to its arrows. Its
ambient type consists of sections compatible with all the restrictions. This generalizes a linearly indexed
resolution to a category of observations.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.ViewSystem.evaluations_jointly_injective
Joint injectivity of evaluation means that two ambient sections are equal when all their view values agree.
The universal cone property supplies the corresponding construction of a compatible section from any
compatible family of maps.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.ViewSystem.Cone.exists_unique_lift
Restriction to a set of views produces a partial section. Its extension type retains the specified partial
values. Nonemptiness of this type is an extension problem, not an automatic property of arbitrary local
data.
6.1 The cover must support the intended compatibility
Module: KomorebiSpace.CocycleDescent
Group-valued transition data carry cocycle equations. To glue them, the cover must detect the equations
involving composable arrows. The definition GroupViewSystem.CompositionCover states the needed
coverage. It implies the corresponding transition coverage and supports a two-sided gluing equivalence
for cocycles.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.GroupViewSystem.CocycleMatching.descentEquiv
The result retains transition and composition information. Object coverage alone does not supply all the
data required to check a cocycle law.

8 Overlap transport and holonomy
Module: KomorebiSpace.OverlapPathRepair
Overlap transport describes compatibility along the edges of a graph. A routing chooses a path from a
root to each vertex. Integrating a residual along those paths constructs a correction. In the finite graph
setting, the routing radius bounds the number of accumulated edge errors.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.OverlapTransport.Routing.correction_bound
For actual residuals of vertex data, the path integral telescopes. This yields an exactness characterization
and a uniqueness theorem for the repaired data with the specified root value.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.OverlapTransport.Routing.unique_exact_repair
Different routings can agree on an actual differential even when their actions on arbitrary edge assign-
ments differ.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.OverlapTransport.Routing.routes_agree_on_actual_residual
The qualification about actual residuals is essential. It prevents an identity on the differential image from
being used as an identity on all edge data.

9 Minimum corrections and bounded realization
Module: KomorebiSpace.ProtectedHilbertRepair
Let E and R be real Hilbert spaces, let Z be a real normed space, and let D : E →R and p : E →Z be
continuous linear maps. The permitted correction space is the closed kernel of p. Define
T = D|ker p : ker p −→R.
The predicate Feasible asks whether D f belongs to the range of this restricted operator. Under feasibility,
the minimum norm correction is selected in the orthogonal complement of ker T.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.ProtectedHilbertRepair.exists_unique_nearest
The repaired point f −amin is the unique nearest point satisfying both Dy = 0 and py = p f. Its
Pythagorean identity compares it with every exact protected answer.
Lean: KomorebiSpace.ProtectedHilbertRepair.repair_pythagoras
Lean: KomorebiSpace.ProtectedHilbertRepair.closed_range_iff_protected_bound
Closed range is equivalent to a uniform protected repair bound. Existence of the nearest answer for one
feasible datum and existence of a uniform bound over all feasible data are separate results.

10 Nested constraints and energy
Module: KomorebiSpace.NestedMinimumCorrections

correction. In particular, the norms are nondecreasing.

15.2 Boundary cases
The finite index type is allowed to be empty. In a zero-dimensional permitted kernel the normal residual
and its certificate are zero. A proper subspace of the kernel is a different correction problem and does not
certify the full projected gradient. The raw coordinate sum qTq is basis dependent. The inverse Gram
factor is necessary to recover the invariant Hilbert norm.
16 Verification and formal scope
16.1 What the accepted audit checks
The accepted evidence consists of the preserved general-library audit and the additional diagonal audit.
The former compiles its 156 production and entrypoint targets with warnings treated as errors and
exports 5,048 project declarations. The diagonal replay compiles seven new modules and their import
entrypoint under the same warning policy, and checks all 273 additional exported declarations. Both
audits restrict axiom dependencies to
{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}.
These are standard logical principles used by Lean and mathlib. No project-specific axiom or admitted
proof is accepted. The audits also check that production declarations are not unsafe and bind their
declaration inventories to the verified source files.
The preserved general audit contains 44 diagnostic controls: 14 expected successes, 28 expected elabo-
ration rejections, and two axiom-policy rejections. They exercise boundary conditions such as positive
parameters, unit dual witnesses, permitted correction spaces, nonorthogonal coordinates, and empty
index types. The diagonal audit adds eight controls: two expected successes, five expected elaboration
rejections, and one axiom-policy rejection. These cover the positive integer parameter, positive noise
level, exact hard-prefix norm, asymptotic constant, and prohibition of admitted proofs and project
axioms. The two sets retain their separate verification records; the diagonal replay did not repeat the
historical 44 controls.
An expected rejection succeeds as a diagnostic only when it fails for its intended reason. A missing
import or another unrelated compiler error is not accepted as the intended mathematical rejection.
These controls complement proofs. They do not turn a finite collection of numerical examples into a
universal theorem. Universal claims in the library are supported by the corresponding Lean proof terms.
