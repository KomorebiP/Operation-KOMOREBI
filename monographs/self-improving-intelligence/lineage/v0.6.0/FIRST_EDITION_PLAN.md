# First-edition scope and release plan

## Scope to freeze

The first edition will cover one finite, explicit chain: repair synthesis; modification of the repair compiler; acquisition of an internal compiler rule; acquisition of a proof-checked search representation; repair-relative scope extension; and preservation of costs, evidence, and continuation obligations across those changes.

Version 0.6 supplies executable constructive witnesses for each of these finite roles. A first edition does not require a new neural model, autonomous invention of arithmetic, proof that AGI follows, or indefinite acceleration. Those would be different research claims.

## Remaining consolidation

1. Perform a statement-by-statement final assumption review across the whole retained manuscript, rather than relying only on textual preservation and regression tests for old proofs.
2. Consolidate the cost tables into a single reading ledger: model acquisition, common input validation, proposal work, proof search, checking, installed information, online execution, and stored controller. Keep unlike units separate.
3. Review all certificate lifecycles and semantic bindings: immutable experiments, repair-success sets, expression meaning, grammar order, and evaluator commitments.
4. Complete an itemized reference review at the actual accessible depth and keep its limits visible. External review remains desirable but is not something a version number can supply.
5. Freeze the source, regenerate figures, rerun exact and negative-control suites, inspect final page renders, rebuild from a fresh extraction, and verify the snapshot.

## Research beyond the first edition

Learned experiment semantics, generated proof search, grammar extension across substantially different domains, statistical benefit under a declared task law, and repeated improvement of the equation learner itself remain research extensions. They should not cause indefinite scope creep or be implied by a completed editorial release.
