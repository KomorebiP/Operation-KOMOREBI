\chapter{Fixed charge and exact-number circuits}
\label{ch:fixed-charge}
\index{Hamming weight}
\index{particle number}

\section{Excitation-number packets}
For $n$ qubits, define
\[
\widehat N
=
\sum_{q=1}^n\frac{I-Z_q}{2}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $k=0,1,\ldots,n$. The projector onto Hamming weight $k$ is
\[
P_{n,k}
=
\sum_{|x|=k}\ket x\bra x,
\]
and
\[
\dim P_{n,k}\Hh
=
\binom nk.
\]
The decomposition
\[
(\C^2)^{\otimes n}
=
\bigoplus_{k=0}^n\Hh_{n,k}
\]
recovers the full dimension through
\[
\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk=2^n.
\]

\section{Number-preserving gates}
Single-qubit $R_Z$ gates commute with $\widehat N$. A two-qubit exchange rotation acts in the ordered basis $\ket{00},\ket{01},\ket{10},\ket{11}$ as
\[
U_{XY}(\theta,\phi)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
0&\cos\theta&-ie^{-i\phi}\sin\theta&0\\
0&-ie^{i\phi}\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It rotates $\ket{01}$ and $\ket{10}$ while fixing $\ket{00}$ and $\ket{11}$, so it preserves exact Hamming weight.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-charge polynomial simulation]
\label{cor:fixed-charge-poly}
For every fixed $k$, a polynomial-time uniform family of number-preserving circuits initialized in $\Hh_{n,k}$ and measured by an efficiently computable observable is classically simulable in polynomial time, provided each restricted local gate is efficiently applied in the weight-$k$ basis.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $k$,
\[
\dim\Hh_{n,k}=\binom nk=O(n^k).
\]
The weight-$k$ basis is efficiently indexed by $k$-element subsets. A local number-preserving gate changes only basis states containing the affected occupation pattern. Apply \cref{thm:poly-packet}.
\end{proof}

\section{Sparse update structure}
A one-qubit diagonal phase changes one amplitude at a time. An exchange gate on modes $p,q$ couples a basis state only to the state obtained by moving one excitation between those modes. Thus every column of the restricted gate has at most two nonzero entries. For fixed $k$, the total update cost is polynomial in $n$.

\section{Uniformity and input encoding}
The fixed-charge corollary assumes $k$ is a fixed constant independent of $n$. It also applies when $k=O(1)$ is supplied by the circuit family. If $k$ is written in binary and allowed to scale as $n/2$, the sector can become exponential. The theorem is about the dimension and update cost of the actual active sector, not the name of the symmetry.

\section{Observables}
A diagonal observable depending on occupations is immediate. Few-body number-preserving observables are sparse in the subset basis. More global observables may require additional structure. The measurement hypothesis in \cref{thm:poly-packet} cannot be omitted: a compactly evolved state does not help if the requested output itself hides exponential work.
