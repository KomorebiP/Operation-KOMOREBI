1.1 Boolean and algebraic conventions

A Boolean function is a map 𝑓 : {0, 1} 𝑛 → {0, 1}. Unless a different field is explicitly stated, polynomials are
the unique multilinear representatives in

                                   R 𝑛 = F2 [𝑥 1 , . . . , 𝑥 𝑛 ]/(𝑥 𝑖2 − 𝑥 𝑖 : 1 ≤ 𝑖 ≤ 𝑛).

Addition in this ring is XOR. Multiplication is AND. Boolean OR is written ∨ and satisfies 𝑎 ∨ 𝑏 = 𝑎 + 𝑏 + 𝑎𝑏.
A statement ℎ ≤ 𝑓 is pointwise and means that ℎ has no positive value at a negative input of 𝑓 .
The degree of a nonzero function is its multilinear algebraic degree. A nonzero constant has degree zero.
For mathematical statements involving a constant-zero function we use deg 0 = −∞; stored profile tables and
Python helpers use their documented sentinels instead. Those implementation conventions do not change the
inequalities.

means an inclusion-minimal positive support, not an arbitrary positive input.

1.2 The restored classical oracle

A legal NOT changes one target bit. A CNOT adds a control bit to a distinct target. A Toffoli adds the product of
two distinct controls to a third distinct wire. Gates have positive controls and are self-inverse. Their composition
is a permutation of all register states.
A restored oracle is a circuit satisfying

                                            (𝑥, 0𝑎 , 𝑦) ↦−→ (𝑥, 0𝑎 , 𝑦 + 𝑓 (𝑥))                                (1.1)

for every logical input 𝑥 and both initial target values 𝑦 ∈ {0, 1}. The zero-workspace condition is imposed
at the boundary, not at intermediate times. The circuit may disturb logical inputs, use output feedback, reuse
intermediate values, and make arbitrary reversible excursions before restoring the required registers.
NOT and CNOT are free only when counting 𝑇. They are charged in 𝐿. This distinction is essential in the
projection example. There are no measurements, quantum phases, discarded work registers, or unverified ancilla
initializations in this model.


1.3 Comparison with ordinary circuits

Theorem 1.1 (Classical/restored simulation comparison). For every Boolean function 𝑓 ,

                                           𝐶( 𝑓 )
                                                  ≤ 𝐿( 𝑓 ) ≤ 6𝐶 ( 𝑓 ) + 1.
                                            5

Proof. Compute every Boolean gate onto a fresh zero wire. An AND costs one Toffoli. A NOT of an existing
signal is obtained with one NOT and one CNOT. An OR is computed as 𝑎 + 𝑏 + 𝑎𝑏, using two CNOTs and one
Toffoli. Repeated operands are simplified to a copy, not encoded as an illegal repeated-control gate. Compute
the output, copy it to 𝑦, and reverse the first computation. This uses at most 6𝐶 ( 𝑓 ) + 1 gates and restores every
work register.
Conversely, express each reversible gate as an ordinary Boolean update. XOR has a four-gate AND/OR/NOT
realization. A Toffoli needs that XOR plus one AND, hence at most five gates. Substitute zero for initial ancillas
and 𝑦 = 0, then read the final target. The resulting Boolean circuit has at most 5𝐿( 𝑓 ) gates.                 □


1.4 The degree bound used throughout

Lemma 1.2 (Degree per multiplication). An XOR–AND computation with 𝑡 AND gates has output degree at
most 𝑡 + 1, allowing constants and arbitrary sharing. The same bound holds for each coordinate of a reversible
computation containing 𝑡 Toffoli gates.

Proof. Introduce a formal Boolean variable for each AND output. Its defining expression is a product of two
affine forms in the logical inputs and earlier gate variables. The final output is affine in the inputs and all gate
variables.
Substitute the gate variables in reverse topological order. A multilinear monomial contains the variable being
replaced at most once. Replacing it by a quadratic expression raises degree by at most one; multilinear reduction
cannot raise it. After 𝑡 substitutions the degree is at most 𝑡 + 1.
A Toffoli contributes one multiplication to such a straight-line computation, while NOT and CNOT are affine.
The argument also applies to a suffix executed backwards because the gates are self-inverse.              □

The source identifies this standard degree bound with the XOR–AND background in [S1].

2.1 What the comparison does and does not require

The suffix need not reverse the prefix. Output feedback and a target direction distributed among many registers
are permitted. Only boundary correctness and reversibility are used. Equivalently,

                                   𝑓 (𝑥) = 1    ⇐⇒      𝑃(𝑥, 0) ≠ 𝑄 −1 (𝑥, 0).

This is a necessary representation, not a circuit lower bound from packet count. Expanded monomial counts,
individual degrees, and algebra dimensions remain distinct from the shared cost of constructing the complete
family. The declared finite cut checks are reported in Appendix B.

represented characteristic-two fields of polynomial size. Then choosing |𝐾 | ≥ 4𝑚 gives a one-sided randomized
polynomial-time satisfiability test. It never accepts an unsatisfiable verifier and accepts a satisfiable one with
probability at least 3/4 per evaluation.

Proof. Choose a uniformly random 𝑧 ∈ 𝐾 𝑚 , evaluate 𝑃 𝑅 (𝑧) under the assumed algorithm, and accept when
it is nonzero. If no witness accepts, Theorem 7.1 makes the polynomial zero identically, so there is no false
acceptance. Otherwise Lemma 7.4 bounds the rejection probability by 𝑚/|𝐾 | ≤ 1/4. A field of polynomial size
uses 𝑂 (log 𝑚) bits per element, so the random input has 𝑂 (𝑚 log 𝑚) bits. Under the explicit efficient-field and
evaluator hypotheses, the procedure has polynomial running time.                                                □

   Conditional means the evaluator is still missing

   The proposition assumes a uniform efficient evaluator that has not been constructed here. The implemented
   evaluator is exponential. Even with the assumed evaluator, the stated conclusion is randomized with
   one-sided error, not a deterministic proof of 𝑃 = 𝑁 𝑃.

The exact tests use GF(16) = F2 [𝑡]/(𝑡 4 + 𝑡 + 1). They evaluate every specified verifier through three witness
bits at every field point, while the independent top-coefficient check uses all field points through two witness
bits and the declared smaller coordinate grid at three bits. These domains must not be conflated.

APPENDIX C


Boundaries, corrections, and source provenance

C.1 Excluded inferences

The following boundaries are retained from the release’s failure register. They are part of the closed scope, not a
promise to finish every exploratory route.
Semantic elimination curvature. Exact existential eliminations commute. A nonzero semantic commutator
cannot be asserted for those exact operators.
Expanded-state counting. Exponentially many residuals or obstruction descriptions can arise from a small
circuit. The number of expanded objects is not their shared construction cost.
Small-case equality. Agreement of affine-optimized one-packet and complete-cover degrees through four
inputs does not extend to all sizes. Chapter 4 gives an exact separating family.
Free homogeneous extraction. A projected grade may have greater nonlinear cost than the whole and can fail
to remain safe. Chapter 5 keeps the input length and total affine cost explicit.
Unrestricted gluing. The exponential dimension theorem applies to a fixed faithful linear multiplicative map.
It does not exclude succinct, nonlinear, or context-dependent algorithms.
Context-free trace multiplication. The disjoint-variable product rule fails at shared witness variables. A short
expression containing an extraction operator is not an efficient implementation of that extraction.


C.2 Historical execution corrections

Earlier sources corrected illegal repeated-control Toffoli emissions to legal copies, replaced a timed-out large
clique search by explicit matching certificates, restored a missing module in isolated replay, and repaired a
log-sealing race. These are preserved historical events, not failures newly observed in preparing this PDF.
The release-assembly record also distinguishes a rejected streaming launch, inaccessible GitHub operations, and
a repaired draft metadata-summary check from scientific assertions. None is reinterpreted as mathematical
UNSAT, a counterexample, or hosted-publication success. The complete release record remains authoritative
for those events [R5].


C.3 What this reference does not claim

There is no resolution of 𝑃 versus 𝑁 𝑃, no superpolynomial unrestricted Boolean-circuit lower bound, no
polynomial-time weighted-trace evaluator, no quantum-circuit lower bound, no Lean kernel check, no independent
review, and no comprehensive novelty or best-known-bound audit.
The conditional randomized implication remains conditional and randomized. An exact finite pass does not
turn a written argument into a kernel-checked theorem. Historical archives are not automatically additional
maintained claims.
