28.1 The discrepancy is a vector, not an unspecified search

Suppose a symplectic embedding f  : (V, BV) →(W, BW) has already been supplied. Put

                               q′(v) = qW( f (v)),     ℓ(v) = q′(v) + qV(v).

The two quadratic refinements have the same polar form, so ℓis linear. Nondegeneracy gives a unique

                                      Arf(q′) + Arf(qV) = qV(a).                                    (28.2)

Proof. The refinement identity gives

Translation by a permutes V. Summing the signs yields G(q′) = (−1)qV(a)G(qV). Equation (27.1) identi-

28.2 Equal Arf does not validate an unchanged map

Take V = F22 with qV(x, y) = xy and q′(x, y) = xy + y. Both refinements have Arf invariant zero. The

29 Full extension actions and the remaining phase boundary

29.1 The discrepancy after a quadratic embedding has been chosen

Even an equivariant quadratic embedding need not give the required equivariant isomorphism of the
full finite extensions with every prescribed marking. This section isolates a conditional repair of the
group-action part.

For clarity, take nondegenerate µ2-central extensions of the same quotient dimension, or restrict a larger
target extension to a supplied invariant quadratic subspace. Suppose C acts genuinely on the source
and target extensions, fixes the center, and induces the same quotient action after identification. Let
F : Es →Et be a center-fixing extension isomorphism inducing that quotient identification. Such an F is
supplied by the quadratic extension criterion, but is not initially assumed equivariant.

An automorphism of Et that fixes its center and quotient is uniquely of the form

                           uχ(x) = (−1)χ( ¯x)x,    χ ∈HomF2(W, F2).                           (29.1)

Indeed, the ratio between the image of x and x is a central sign depending only on ¯x, and preservation
of multiplication makes it additive. Conversely every such character gives an automorphism. These
vertical automorphisms form the additive F2-space A = Hom(W, F2).
Write αg for the target action and βg = FαsgF−1 for the transported source action. Their discrepancy is

                                              δg = βgα−1g ∈A.

The action of C on A is conjugation by αg. Using additive notation in A, the genuine group-action
equations give
                                                δgh = δg + gδh.                                          (29.2)

Thus the discrepancy is an actual 1-cocycle, rather than an arbitrary list of mismatched operators.

29.3 What has not been repaired by this finite sum

The result starts after the arithmetic host, actual finite actions, invariant quotient subspace and an ex-
tension isomorphism have been supplied. It does not construct them from the original source diagram.
A native-normalizer obstruction can prevent the initial action from existing even when the later finite
cocycle would be removable.

There is a further representation-level boundary. An action by automorphisms of a Heisenberg group
preserves the isomorphism class of its representation with fixed nontrivial central character. Intertwin-
ing operators can therefore be chosen, but their products can carry scalar discrepancies. Passing from
those projective operators to the specified honest phase operators is a separate problem. Proposition 29.1
removes a discrepancy in the action on the finite extension group.  It does not prove vanishing of every
scalar projective obstruction in a representation, nor does it choose the prescribed Gauss or transport
phases.

The original full character domain, its central quasicharacter, the noncompact root, and all specified
section products remain part of the arithmetic contract. A finite central sign correction is acceptable
only when its interaction with that contract is explicitly checked.


29.4 A useful order of operations

The two finite corrections should be used in the following order. First supply an actual invariant host
and a quotient-equivariant symplectic embedding. Compare its pulled-back quadratic refinement with
the source. When the defect vector is isotropic, use the equivariant transvection to correct the quadratic
embedding. Next lift to the finite extensions and compute the resulting action cocycle. When the stipu-
lated hypotheses permit it, use the odd-order sum to correct that action. Finally check every prescribed
lift, section, and honest representation phase that these corrections were not authorized to change.

Each stage has a certificate and a failure mode. Combining them into one phrase such as “equivariant
attachment” would hide which input is missing. In the equal-capacity case, even the first quadratic
stage needs the native Arf match. In a source problem with a fixed embedding, the transvection may be
disallowed. In even-order action problems, the 1-cocycle may survive. In all cases, the same arithmetic
candidate must remain the subject of the construction.

30.2 A certificate for the next native-marking step

For a fixed arithmetic candidate at rank eight, a useful next certificate must identify the actual finite
central extension or specified finite subgroup in its scalar pushout. It must give the chosen lift of each
of the six symplectic basis vectors and compute its square under the full central character. These six bits
determine the refinement once the commutator basis is fixed. Their three pairwise products determine
the Arf sign through (27.1).

The certificate must also say whether the target marking is independently fixed or is allowed to be trans-
ported from the source. If it is fixed, compute the defect of the required quotient map. A vanishing defect
directly certifies that map. An isotropic nonzero defect supplies the transvection only when changing the
map is allowed. Every additional section element and transport lift then needs its actual multiplication
check.

This task cannot be replaced by evaluating a canonical-looking quadratic form on an abstract six-
dimensional space.  That would choose new data rather than calculate the data of the specified
arithmetic object. It also cannot be replaced by choosing another candidate with the same conductors.
The candidate and full inducing character must remain fixed throughout.
