13.2 The exact refinement substitution

The refinement step is worth displaying as a substitution table. It is the arithmetic bridge between the
explicit matrix recursion and a maximal field.


                 Published requirement      Value in the recursive construction

                      Initial simple stratum         [A, n, R, γ]
                  Perturbation                b = LiftE(C) ∈P−R
                 Derived parameter           s(b) = C
                Pure maximal derived field  E[C] ⊆Mpd−1(E) by induction
                Outer critical exponent       k0(γ, A) = −n
                  Inner critical exponent       k0(C, B) = −q1
                      Strict inequality         −2R > −n −q1

For odd p, n > pR > 2R. For p = 2, n > 2R. Both imply the required strict inequality. Maximality
of the derived field is a genuine induction hypothesis.  It is not implied merely by counting its matrix
entries.

14 The monomial branch and its actual scope

14.1 Three statements that must remain separate

The recovered monomial materials contain three different layers. The oldest manuscript develops a
classical-input route from rank-one characters through automorphic induction. The finite formal core
describes packet and group-theoretic interfaces. The ActualMonomialLocalGlobal module [M45]
connects a degree-induced representation to actual residue and Frobenius data. None of these layers,
merely by existing, constructs every arithmetic witness demanded by Conjecture 66.

A useful way to read the branch is to keep its input on the left:

  rank-one character and admissible induction tower  =⇒   the corresponding induced candidate.

This is not the implication from an arbitrary packet diagram to an admissible tower. The tower and its
compatibility are substantial data. Nor does an equality between finite conductor profiles identify the
full local factors of a separately specified candidate.


14.2 Actual Weil pullback and inertia invariants

Source [M45] uses an actual residue-action pullback and a degree homomorphism

                                            δ : W −→Z.

The subgroup for an induction of degree n is δ−1(nZ). A finite cyclic inertia map is explicitly required
to have image ker δ. This equality is a hypothesis to verify for the actual pullback, not an arbitrary
identification of an abstract cyclic group with inertia.

Lemma 14.1 (Fixed vectors depend on the actual image). Let ρ  : G →GL(V) and let ι  : H →G be a
homomorphism with image I. Then
                                             Vρ◦ι(H) = Vρ(I).

Proof. A vector fixed by every ρ(ι(h)) is fixed by every element of ρ(I) because every element of I has a
preimage. The reverse inclusion follows immediately from ι(H) ⊆I.


The hypothesis that the first positive ramification group acts trivially places the representation in the
tame sector. In the degree-induced examples, triviality of the inertia character gives an n-dimensional
fixed space. A nontrivial inertia character, together with its Frobenius conjugates, gives no inertia-fixed
coordinate. Thus the Artin conductor is respectively 0 or n, while the Swan conductor is zero. The local
polynomial is formed on VI, not automatically on the entire induced vector space.

This is meaningful arithmetic comparison at a finite interface. It is not a formal definition of an automor-
phic representation and does not import every analytic identity attached to one. The finite Gauss phase
also does not become a fully normalized local epsilon factor without the relevant additive character and
measure conventions.

Source and status. [M45] supplies the production module and binder conditions. The fixed-image lemma is proved
here. The full module’s terminal compilation receipt was not recovered. Earlier checked finite-tame comparison
is not thereby invalidated.

14.3 Admissible towers rather than a blanket solvability claim

Let Ωbe a finite group and Λ ≤Ω. For the induction route in the older manuscript, the required
condition is a chain

           Λ = Λr ◁Λr−1 ◁· · · ◁Λ0 = Ω,    Λi−1/Λi cyclic of prime order.               (14.1)

For a finite Galois extension with group Ω, the reversed fixed-field chain has cyclic prime-degree steps.
The rank-one character can then be processed through the stated cyclic automorphic-induction inputs.
Induction in stages identifies the resulting Weil parameter with the original induced character. Cuspidal-
ity requires the appropriate regularity and stabilizer conditions. Without them, an isobaric conclusion
must not be relabeled as a cuspidal one.

Proposition 14.2 (Subnormality is the subgroup requirement). For a finite solvable group Ω, a subgroup Λ
admits a chain of the form (14.1) if and only if it is subnormal in Ω.


Proof. One direction is the definition of subnormality. Conversely, start with a subnormal chain. Each
quotient of two successive terms is finite and solvable. A composition series of that quotient has cyclic
prime-order factors. Pulling it back refines the original step into the required chain. Repeat for all steps.



The statement does not say that every subgroup of a solvable group is subnormal. In S3, a subgroup
generated by a transposition has order two. There is no proper intermediate subgroup between it and
S3, and it is not normal in S3. It cannot serve as the bottom of (14.1). Yet S3 is solvable through its normal
subgroup of order three. The exact finite suite includes this countercontrol.

The recovered manuscript has valid additional sectors, including nilpotent groups and suitable abelian-
by-cyclic constructions. Their usefulness comes from producing the needed subgroup chain, not from
deleting that requirement. Similarly, a virtual character identity with negative coefficients is not itself
a construction of an actual automorphic representation. Dividing products of L-functions does not, by
that operation alone, prove the required holomorphy.


14.4 What cyclic Clifford extension really supplies

Proposition 14.3 (Extension across a cyclic quotient). Let H ◁G be finite, with G/H cyclic of order d. Let τ
be an irreducible complex representation of H whose isomorphism class is G-invariant. Then τ extends to G. Any
two extensions differ by a character of G/H.

Proof. Choose t ∈G lifting a generator. Invariance supplies an invertible operator A with

                                Aτ(h)A−1 = τ(tht−1).

The operator Adτ(td)−1 commutes with τ(H) and is scalar by irreducibility. The same conjugation iden-
tity shows that A commutes with τ(td). Rescale A by a complex dth root so that Ad = τ(td). Sending
t to this rescaled operator and retaining τ on H respects the defining conjugation and power relations,
hence defines an extension.

After identifying the two restrictions with τ, the ratio between two possible operators at t commutes
with τ(H) and is scalar.  Its dth power is one. These scalars are precisely the characters of the cyclic
quotient.
