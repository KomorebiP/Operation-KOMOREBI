A reading route through the argument

This atlas accompanies Toward Arithmetic Packet Realization, the 77-page cumulative research paper v0.4.0.
Each plate combines one spacious mathematical graphic with an explicit claim boundary and a route
back to that paper. The figure data are exact finite calculations or labeled dependency diagrams, not
invented measurements.

The central distinction. A finite packet, an actual arithmetic host, and the same candidate’s full local
factors are different objects. The first two plates fix the quantifiers. The rest explain what can be
constructed, what a particular obstruction rules out, and which stronger inputs are still required.



   Route          Mathematical purpose


    XI.1–XI.5        The witness contract, balanced products, compact binders, and a complete finite
                    corner obstruction.

    XI.6–XI.10       Whole characters, trace depth, defining sequences, native quotient capacity, and
                     enlarged-carrier recovery.

    XI.11–XI.19       Conductor conventions, candidate filters, information loss, descent, Clifford
                   dimensions, and occurrence/action obstructions.

    XI.20–XI.26         Full finite extensions, section products, quadratic markings, the equal-capacity
                   boundary, and transvection repair.

    XI.27–XI.32       Genuine action correction, its even-order limit, honest phases, the missing
                     native certificate, and the remaining endpoints.


Evidence labels. An exact finite model verifies the displayed example. A conditional profile comparison
retains the actual arithmetic construction hypotheses. A contract diagram records dependencies but does
not supply their inputs. Ordinary general proofs remain in the companion paper. None of these labels
denotes a new Lean run or external peer review.

The source line on every plate links to the cited physical PDF page when the atlas and research paper are
kept in the same folder. The final concordance gives exact section and page routes. The original core

PLATE XI.1

The three obligations

Finite comparison, arithmetic existence, and characterization have different inputs.





        Supplied source              Actual arithmetic             Characterization
              ρ, dρ                   witness R(dρ, π)               π ≃LLC(ρ)





          H1–H4                   Conjecture 66          H5 for this same π
        finite supplied data             existence open        + characterization





The source is a wild Weil atom ρ with the supplied datum dρ. H1–H4 include the declared finite
break decomposition, symplectic bridges, central extensions and phases, and coherent transports. They
describe an admitted input. They do not assert that every possible source arrives with canonical data.

The middle box is an actual arithmetic witness R(dρ, π). It carries the prescribed packet and recovers the
same supercuspidal representation, including the central character and noncompact completion. The
final arrow additionally needs H5 for that particular π and the applicable characterization theorem.

                 ∀ρ ∈A  ∃π R(dρ, π)      is the general realization endpoint.

An arrow is a dependency, not a declaration that its input has been constructed. Restricted witnesses
and finite comparison theorems remain useful even while this quantified endpoint is open. Historical
obligation 66 is not a measure of the amount of mathematics still required.





Evidence: Contract diagram.

PLATE XI.2

One candidate must satisfy both conditions

Separate existential statements cannot be joined by changing the witness.





             Realizes the packet                        Passes H5


           π1                   π2




            Two separately successful candidates do not supply one witness.

               {π1} ∩{π2} = ∅





Let A = {π  : R(d, π)} and B = {π  : H5(π)}. The desired comparison uses a member of A ∩B. The
diagram is an exact two-element countermodel: A = {π1} and B = {π2}, with π1 ̸= π2. Each condition
has a witness. Their conjunction has none.

                 (∃π R(d, π)) ∧(∃π H5(π))  ̸⇒  ∃π (R(d, π) ∧H5(π)).

There is a second pitfall. An implication about every realizer can be true when there are no realizers. A
comparison interface whose structure already contains an existence field does not construct that field
merely by using it.

These finite sets are logical illustrations, not a claim that the arithmetic realization and H5 classes are
actually disjoint. Their purpose is to make a change of candidate visible before it enters a proof.





Evidence: Exact logical countermodel.

Companion source: Part I, Section 1.2. Book page 1522.

PLATE XI.3

A balanced central product

The shared center is identified once, while the orthogonal packet factors remain.





          H3 × H3                    Quotient by              Balanced product
       27 ⋅27 = 729                      {(z, z−1)}                  729/3 = 243





            One shared center                     Heisenberg degree
           not two unrelated centers                     3 ⋅3 = 9



                  Orthogonal factors commute; the actual extensions are inputs.




Take two Heisenberg groups of order 33 = 27. Their direct product has order 729. Quotient by the
central subgroup {(z, z−1)} of order three. The balanced central product has order 35 = 243, with a
four-dimensional quotient over F3 and one common center.

                    (H3 × H3)/{(z, z−1)}  has Heisenberg degree  32 = 9.

The figure’s order calculation is checked by enumerating every fibre of the multiplication model. Each
fibre has exactly three elements. The representation degree agrees with the tensor product of two
degree-three Heisenberg representations whose central characters match.

The general argument requires the actual factor extensions and commutation between distinct factors. A
list of break labels does not supply either condition. Once those conditions hold, the balanced product
removes the proposed extra mixed extension ambiguity. It does not construct an arithmetic host or
identify independently fixed lifts.





Evidence: Exact finite model.

PLATE XI.4

What a compact binder can do

Compatible solutions in one fixed compact space can survive every precision.





                2            8           26           80          242
     branch −1





                1            1            1            1            1
     branch +1



                    31              32              33              34              35
                            modulus (arrows are reduction maps)




The points are all roots of x2 −1 modulo 3n, for 1 ≤n ≤5. The labels are canonical residue representa-
tives. The arrows reduce a root modulo 3n to a root modulo 3n−1. Both branches persist, corresponding
to 1 and −1 in Z3.
For the general binder, fix a closed X ⊆Om and continuous constraints fj. The sets

                        Cn = {x ∈X : fj(x) ∈ϖnO for every j ≤n}

are closed and nested. If every Cn is nonempty, compactness supplies a point satisfying every exact
equation.

The example does not prove simultaneous finite solvability for the arithmetic problem. Even x = 0 and
x = 1 have separately solvable congruences but no joint solution modulo three. Allowing the number of
coefficients or pole bounds to grow also changes the compact model. Those missing hypotheses cannot
be replaced by more isolated successful tests.





Evidence: Exact finite illustration.

Do not truncate the full character

Deep values, the full compact domain, and a noncompact generator are distinct certificates.





     deep congruence units              θ|Um


                                                       One full
        whole compact                  θ|H1                    inducing            character                                               representation

     noncompact generator            Λ(ϖ)



           A deep equality cannot fill in the two larger-domain certificates.





A trace expression first describes a character on sufficiently deep congruence units. The full simple
character also has values on the larger native unit domain. A full inducing representation retains further
central and noncompact data. These are successive obligations, not three interchangeable notations for
the same calculation.

The direct character construction transfers the whole preceding character, applies the actual permitted
correction, and then checks the intersection needed for shallow extension. The correction exhibits why
noncompact normalization matters. In exponents of ninth roots of unity,

                     ν(ϖ) = ζ9,    ν(ϖF/ϖ3) = ζ23  =⇒  ν(ϖF) = 1.

Setting ν(ϖ) = 1 instead gives ζ23, changing the central character. The accompanying finite check verifies
this root-of-unity identity only. The full unit character, intertwining, and type-extension statements
retain the arithmetic hypotheses developed in the progress paper.
