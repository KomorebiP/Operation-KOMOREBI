12 What has been gained and what remains

12.1 The restricted positive result is substantial

The recovered ordinary constructions now address arbitrary odd-prime and dyadic lengths in their
stated families. They build actual parameter fields, control native traces or trace visibility, extend full
characters through the required shallow intersections, and retain central and noncompact completion
choices. For the one-vertex trivial-stabilizer scalar-pushout contract, the dyadic low-rank repair allows
an actual enlarged assembly carrier where the original maximal-type quotient is too small. These are
mathematical results with explicit hypotheses, not merely numerical suggestions.

At the same time, these families do not supply an actual witness for every dρ admitted by the general
conjecture. The same-candidate H5 comparison also remains a separate conditional step. The new paper
should make both facts easy to see without reducing all progress to either “finished” or “unfinished.”

12.2 The remaining obligations are different kinds of problems

Admission and canonical source data. Broader source classes need intrinsic envelopes, the declared
equivariant bridges, and the requisite odd or dyadic phase and marking data. Conjecture 66, as stated,
starts after H1–H4 have been supplied. Extending the supply of those inputs is related work, but it is
not the same quantified task.

A common arithmetic host. Separate breakwise targets do not yet give one actual inducing system of
the total rank. The direct families establish some hosts. Their existence does not prove that the host can
realize every admitted source action, transport graph or numerical layer placement.

Nontrivial stabilizer actions. The native inner-normalizer obstruction forces a change of host or a dif-
ferent arithmetic action mechanism for genuinely nontrivial prime-to-p symplectic actions. Abstract
cohomological lifting has already removed some finite scalar defects. Repeating that lifting does not
solve the missing arithmetic attachment.

Independent native markings and fixed embeddings. Transporting a marked source extension is not
the same as matching a separately specified native quadratic refinement. A proposed match must respect
the actual fixed embedding, rather than merely an abstract isometry class.

General transport diagrams. A one-vertex packet has no nontrivial global transport constraint. Finite
holonomy theorems compare supplied source and target systems. A realization theorem for arbitrary
admitted diagrams must construct an arithmetic system and show that its maps recover those particular
transports.

Same-candidate factors. H5 remains about the actual candidate that realizes the packet. Conductor,
torsion and self-pair identities are useful tests, but they do not supply the full characterizing family. A
program seeking an independent factor comparison must construct that proof rather than invoke the
reference correspondence through an implicit definition.
