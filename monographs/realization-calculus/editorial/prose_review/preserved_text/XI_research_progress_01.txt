Its central object is not a numerical completion score. It is the proposed passage from a supplied finite
packet, with its character values, phases and transport maps, to an actual supercuspidal representation
carrying that same packet.

1 The realization problem and its quantifiers

1.1 Three different mathematical tasks

Paper XI separates finite comparison, arithmetic existence and local-factor characterization. These tasks
interact, but they have different inputs. A finite Heisenberg representation can be constructed from
a nondegenerate alternating pairing. A compatible family of intertwiners can be constructed after its
source and target representations are supplied. Neither statement, by itself, constructs the required
representation of a general linear group over a local field.

Fix the reciprocity, additive-character, local-factor and phase conventions of Paper XI. Let A denote the
declared class of irreducible wild Weil atoms. For ρ ∈A, write dρ for its supplied assembled finite datum.
The historical names H1–H4 refer to the following data: an intrinsic finite break decomposition, equiv-
ariant symplectic bridges to the supplied breakwise type quotients, compatible central-extension and
phase data including the declared markings, and a coherent transport system whose matrix holonomy
obstruction vanishes. These are assumptions on a specified source, not a theorem that every wild Weil
representation already comes equipped with a canonical instance [1, 3].

Definition 1.1 (Arithmetic realization witness). A witness R(dρ, π) consists of actual arithmetic inducing
data for an admissible supercuspidal representation π of GLdim ρ(F), together with comparison and
recovery maps carrying the prescribed assembled finite packet. Those maps must preserve the declared
stabilizer action, supplied character values, central-extension and phase data, and compatible transports.
Recovery returns the same π, with the fixed central character and noncompact completion.


An enlarged actual inducing carrier is permitted by this contract. The entire assembled packet need not
be the original maximal-simple-type quotient. Conversely, an independently fixed native marking, an
original matrix subgroup embedding, or a prescribed numerical depth is not automatically preserved
merely because the scalar extension is preserved. Such requirements must be stated and proved sepa-
rately.


1.2 The conjecture and the surviving comparison theorem

The endpoint is the following statement, with its historical identifier retained:

     Conjecture 66. For every ρ ∈A with its supplied H1–H4 datum dρ, there exist an actual
     admissible supercuspidal representation π of GLdim ρ(F) and a witness R(dρ, π).

This is the realization requirement isolated from historical Theorem 16.2, original proof obligation 66,
and row 95 of the all-records register. It is not row 66 of that register. It asks for one witness per admit-
ted source.  It does not ask that every proposed host work, or that mutually inconsistent noncompact
completions be reconciled.

Proposition 1.2 (Comparison of the same realized candidate). Suppose R(dρ, π) is supplied for one actual
admissible supercuspidal π of the required rank. Suppose H5 holds for this π: its required pair local factors agree
with the factors prescribed for ρ on the stipulated characterizing family of twists, in the fixed normalizations. Assume
the applicable local-factor characterization for that family and class. Then π ≃LLC(ρ).

Proof. The characterization applies to the two actual representations π and the reference representation
LLC(ρ). H5 provides equality of the characterizing factors, so their isomorphism classes agree. The

1.3 What a host obstruction does and does not refute

Let H(d) be the class of admissible hosts for a datum d. A failed construction establishes that one chosen
H does not realize d, or perhaps that no H in a particular subclass does. To refute Conjecture 66 for that
datum would require excluding every allowed arithmetic witness. The capacity and inner-normalizer
obstructions below are therefore substantive negative results without being counterexamples to the con-
jecture. They identify constraints a successful construction must avoid.

             ∑ (−1)q+(v) = 2,  ∑ (−1)q−(v) = −2.

The numbers of zeros are three and one. Thus the alternating form alone cannot recover the quadratic

4 Abstract stabilizer lifting is not an arithmetic normalizer

Two elementary arguments remove finite scalar obstructions under a coprimality hypothesis. They must
not be misread as constructing matrices in the required arithmetic normalizer.

Proposition 4.1 (Coprime lifting of an extension action). Let A be a finite abelian p-group, and let P be a
central extension by C×. Let a finite group C of order m prime to p act on A, preserving the extension class. Then
the action lifts to an action on P fixing the center.

Proof. Choose a lift qc for each c ∈C. The kernel of the group of extension automorphisms over the
quotient and center is M = Hom(A, C×), a finite abelian p-group. Conjugation defines the C-module
structure on M. Write the defect of the chosen lifts additively as

                                    qcqd =  f (c, d)qcd,        f (c, d) ∈M.

                    m f (c, d) = S(c) + cS(d) −S(cd).

5 The compact binder theorem and the missing solvability input

The binder construction asks whether finite compatibility conditions can be joined into one global
coefficient choice. The compactness theorem [9] identifies the precise missing input: joint finite
solvability within one fixed compact model.

Theorem 5.1 (A fixed compact coefficient binder). Let F be a nonarchimedean local field, O its compact ring
of integers, and ϖ a uniformizer. Let X ⊆Om be closed and let fj : X →O be continuous for j ≥1. Define

                        Cn = {x ∈X : fj(x) ∈ϖnO for every j ≤n}.

There is an x ∈X with fj(x) = 0 for all j if and only if every Cn is nonempty.

Proof. An exact zero belongs to every Cn. Conversely, each Cn is a closed subset of the compact space
X, and Cn+1 ⊆Cn. Nonempty nested closed subsets of a compact space have nonempty intersection.
Choose x in that intersection. For any fixed j, one has fj(x) ∈ϖnO for all n ≥j. The intersection of
these ideals is zero, so fj(x) = 0.

The theorem does not require surjectivity of a chosen sequence of reduction maps.  It does require a
single compact coefficient space and simultaneous finite solvability within that space. Allowing the
rank, pole order or number of coefficients to grow with precision changes the space and invalidates this
argument unless a separate compact model is supplied.
