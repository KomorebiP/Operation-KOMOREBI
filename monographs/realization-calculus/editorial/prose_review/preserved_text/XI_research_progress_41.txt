19 Compact binders and exact fixed-template obstructions

19.1 The compactness theorem with all hypotheses visible

A binder is intended to turn compatible finite constraints into one actual object. Compactness can per-
form that passage only after the admissible space and simultaneous finite solvability have been supplied.
It does not supply those premises from unrelated finite successes.

Let O be the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean local field, with uniformizer π. Fix a closed subset
X ⊆Om and continuous functions fj : X →O, j ≥1. Put

                         Cn = {x ∈X : fj(x) ∈πnO for 1 ≤j ≤n}.                           (19.1)

Proposition 19.1 (A compact simultaneous binder).  If Cn ̸= ∅for every n, there is x ∈X with fj(x) = 0
for every j.

Proof. The ring O is compact, so X is compact. Each Cn is closed by continuity and the closedness of
πnO. Also Cn+1 ⊆Cn. The finite intersection property gives an element x in their intersection. For fixed
j, the value fj(x) lies in every πnO with n ≥j, so it is zero.

The proof requires no surjectivity of the reduction maps between the sets of finite roots. For instance,
x2 = 17 has roots modulo every power of two, while the roots 3 and 5 modulo eight do not lift to
roots modulo sixteen. An arbitrarily chosen partial root can fail even when the compactness conclusion
applies to the full nested solution sets.
Separate solvability is also insufficient. The equations x ≡0 and x ≡1 (mod 3n) each have solutions,
but never have a common solution. Every finite test in the binder premise concerns one tuple satisfying
all current constraints together.


19.2 What makes the result an arithmetic certificate

The variables must remain in one bounded, closed model with fixed rank, lattice data and normalizations.
A nonzero leading coefficient should be encoded by a fixed nonzero residue class, a closed and open
condition. Merely deleting the zero point from a compact model need not leave a compact space. In
O \ {0}, all congruences x ≡0 (mod πn) are solvable, but the only exact zero has been removed.

For polynomial constraints, or rational constraints with denominators uniformly units, congruence eval-
uation is compatible with a fixed finite quotient. An exhaustive empty solution set at some precision
then certifies failure of that template. Conversely, if the exact common zero set in the compact template

=== XI_research_progress physical 50 ===
is empty, Proposition 19.1 implies that some finite stage is empty. This is a finite obstruction certificate
for the specified model, not automatically for every possible arithmetic host.

Full characters require particular care. The domain on which their finite quotients are modeled must
be fixed, and the evaluation constraints must be jointly continuous or uniformly descend to the claimed
finite quotient. Values on noncompact generators require their own normalization constraints. A binder
that only remembers the deep commutator form does not recover a full inducing representation for free.
