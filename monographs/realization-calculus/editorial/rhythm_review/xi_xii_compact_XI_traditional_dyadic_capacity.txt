[439–441; s0, d0] \hypertarget{dyadic_capacity-scalar-twists-shallow-or-deep}{% \subsubsection{Scalar twists, shallow or deep}[reference]}
[448–455; s0, d0] For an actual parameter, replacing beta by beta+aI with a∈F leaves its field, centralizer and commutator map unchanged. A shallow scalar addition does not change the leading level. A dominating scalar addition can change that level, but it contributes no new noncentral Heisenberg directions: in the defining sequence the added scalar stage has equal H and J orders. This agrees with the exact representation-level twist transport. Enlarging a displayed numerical level is therefore not a capacity repair.
[462–464; s0, d0] \hypertarget{dyadic_capacity-adding-residue-multiplicity-or-increasing-ambient-rank}{% \subsubsection{Adding residue multiplicity or increasing ambient rank}[reference]}
[466–470; s0, d0] The torsion-one condition forces a full-degree totally ramified parameter field and residue multiplicity one. A host with an unramified or residual multiplicity factor does not preserve that invariant. Increasing the ambient rank changes the required source rank. Neither maneuver closes the current fixed-rank construction.
[472–474; s0, d0] \hypertarget{dyadic_capacity-using-a-larger-arithmetic-carrier-inside-the-same-representation}{% \subsubsection{Using a larger arithmetic carrier inside the same representation}[reference]}
[476–483; s0, d0] A larger compact subgroup representation, a genuinely different arithmetic quotient, or an induction-stage carrier might have enough dimension. Such an enlargement is not ruled out here. It would need an actual finite Heisenberg action carrying the prescribed full source data and a proved recovery in the unchanged candidate. Artificially adding vector-space copies or transporting abstract operators to an induced space does not provide that arithmetic attachment or prove its kernel compatibility. No such repair is constructed in this note.
