Part I Finite typed transfer and the formal contract
The finite algebraic core supplies the algebraic foundation and a contract for its individual declarations.
Here, “roadmap” and “outside the contract” describe the scope of the finite Lean formalization. Ordi-
nary mathematical results in Part II may lie beyond that scope. The finite contract covers thirty result
environments. The source contract has 41 mapping rows because it also includes definitions and other
interfaces.
The formal companion records the exact Lean and Mathlib dependencies [3, 17], compilation, kernel
replay, and axiom checks. References to declarations from Chapters I–II retain their explicitly inherited
status. The companion distinguishes the finite algebraic contract from the additional packet-coordinate
and analytic interfaces.
1 Purpose and formal status
Chapter I fixed typed seeds, operator grammars, exact projector packets, generated hulls, and recon-
struction [13]. Chapter II added coherent refinement coordinates, weighted transfer, arithmetic sepa-
ration, and linear defect identities [14]. The present chapter begins the next layer: explicit edge words,
optional connection data, and obstructions within individual packets.
Canonical coordinate transport and a connection play different mathematical roles. Canonical trans-
port compares two exact descriptions of the same ambient object and is forced by analysis and synthesis.
A connection chooses transports that may carry extra modeling, discretization, or gauge information.
Canonical transport obeys a cocycle theorem. A connection may fail to obey a cocycle, and that fail-
ure is meaningful data. Keeping separate notation lets us distinguish the automatic coherence of exact
coordinates from the possible holonomy of a chosen connection.
1.1 Statement labels
Every substantial theorem, lemma, corollary, proposition, definition, API, and roadmap item carries
one or more of the three labels below. When separate clauses use a Chapter III result and an inherited
bridge, the statement carries both applicable labels. Illustrative examples do not require a label.
S1. Lean-backed in Chapter III: the displayed scope is no stronger than the named compiled dec-
laration in the identified Chapter III module.
S2. Inherited Lean-backed: the displayed result is supplied by the frozen formal contract of Chap-
ter I or Chapter II.
S3. Roadmap only: the text states a future interface or theorem target and is excluded from the
completion denominator.
