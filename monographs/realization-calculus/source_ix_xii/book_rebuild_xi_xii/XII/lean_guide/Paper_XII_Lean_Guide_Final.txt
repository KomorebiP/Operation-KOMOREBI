1    What this guide certifies and how to use it

The reference production tree is the formal_release/lean/ tree from the complete v0.17.1 bundle.
Its revised manuscript is the 36-page Paper_XII_Revised_Formalization_Complete_v0.17.0.tex;
the v0.17.1 release reverified its unchanged production sources. This guide is dated 2026-10-06 and
describes those exact sources, alongside the final manuscript’s explicit editorial clarifications. It
does not silently replace any production theorem or historical verification record.
Read Sections 4–7 for the completed local and global constructions, Sections 8–12 for support,
packets, occupancy and conditional realization, and Section 14 for replay. Appendices list every
source module and every revised internal claim. The machine-readable catalog gives full SHA-256
hashes, source locations, imports, declaration names and line numbers. The full production source
is supplied; printing every line in this PDF is unnecessary for reproducibility.
Three distinct kinds of evidence must be kept separate. A Lean kernel check verifies the exact
formal proposition under its hypotheses and dependencies. An ordinary mathematical proof ex-
plains a proposition in mathematical prose, with its own statement and citation obligations. A
finite computation verifies the tested cases, numerical identities or model bounds stated by that
computation. Neither a finite numerical certificate nor a source-token scan is a substitute for kernel
elaboration of the production proof.

In Lean, square-bracket arguments such as [CommRingR] and [CompleteSpaceP] are typeclass
hypotheses. A proposition-valued ordinary argument such as (hB:IsAdicmB) is also a hypothesis.

control that contribution. Reading only the line beginning theorem can therefore hide essential
assumptions. The full exact excerpt modules preserve these commands, imports, namespace and
section boundaries.

ambient hypotheses. PDF excerpts select line ranges and retain original line numbers. To avoid

     4      Claim 3.1: construct the tensor, then complete it

     For a finite index type 𝜄, commutative coeﬀicient ring 𝑂, and commutative 𝑂-algebras 𝑅𝑖 , the raw
     tensor is Mathlib’s actual PiTensorProductOR. It is not a record accepting an already supplied
     tensor equivalence. The factor inclusion is PiTensorProduct.singleAlgHom; the family lift is
     constructed from a multilinear product and the tensor’s universal map.
     Given ideals 𝑚𝑂 ⊆ 𝑂, 𝑚𝑖 ⊆ 𝑅𝑖 , and condition ideals 𝐼𝑖 , define

                               𝑆 = ⨂ 𝑅𝑖 ,          𝔞 = 𝑚𝑂 𝑆 + ∑ 𝑚𝑖 𝑆,            𝐿 = ∑ 𝐼𝑖 𝑆.
                                     𝑖∈𝜄,𝑂                         𝑖                    𝑖

     In Lean the finite sums are indexed suprema of mapped ideals. The scalar term is present even if

      6      Locality, universal maps and the edge cases

      For the manuscript specialization, supply a field 𝑘, an 𝑂-algebra structure on 𝑘, residue algebra

C    A practical reading and inspection sequence

A reader examining a mathematical claim should first locate its revised number in the crosswalk,
then read the exact production declarations, their ambient variables and the relevant proof. Fol-
low internal imports to any structure or construction the theorem consumes. Determine which
fields are supplied data and which declarations construct the desired maps, objects or identities.
Match the point category, coeﬀicient maps, topologies, completeness, finite-generation and locality
assumptions with the intended application. Consult the original ledger when comparing against
earlier wording.
For Claims 3.1 and 3.3, the central source chain is the raw tensor quotient, completion/closed
quotient comparison, adic quotient topology, completed finite tensor, algebraic adic compari-
son/locality, global tensor transport, local condition factorization, Noetherian/local capstone
and coeﬀicient/local point category. Checking only ordinary tensor quotients or an assumed
universal-property interface would miss the constructed completed objects and their actual
topologies.
For the arithmetic conclusion, trace the nonzero fiber or cohomology vector through the actual
carrier and point. Verify classicality for that selected eigensystem, then check attachment, match-
ing Frobenius polynomials, density, semisimplification and original-field representation comparison.
Finally ensure any propagated family already exists and belongs to the same attached automorphic
object. This sequence explains exactly what the formalization establishes and what an arithmetic
application must still provide.
