## 2. Why the limit is one-sided

The exact shell and principal phase are even in signed spacing, while the principal frequency divides that nonnegative phase by the signed spacing. Block 18 proved that the punctured frequency is odd in spacing. Therefore an expansion with leading term `+E` is false as an unrestricted two-sided statement. Every new continuum theorem is stated on the physical positive-spacing filter `𝓝[>] 0`.

Define

\[
E=\sqrt{m^2+k^2},\qquad
a=-\frac{m^2k^2}{6E}.
\]

The nondegenerate hypotheses explicitly require `E ≠ 0`. The point `m=k=0` is retained as a separate degenerate case and is never handled by silently dividing by zero.

## 3. Normalized sine-square and radial reconstruction

Starting from the already checked sixth-order shell expansion, the new proof derives an actual fourth-order expansion for the squared shell and defines the positive-side normalized quantity

\[
q_\varepsilon=\frac{1-C(\varepsilon)^2}{\varepsilon^2}
\quad (\varepsilon>0),
\]

with its value at zero chosen as `E²`. Lean proves

\[
q_\varepsilon
=E^2-\frac{E^4+m^2k^2}{3}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4).

The radial factor

\[
r_\varepsilon=\sqrt{q_\varepsilon}

\[
r_\varepsilon
=E-\frac{E^4+m^2k^2}{6E}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4).
\]

## 4. Actual principal-phase inversion

For positive spacing and sufficiently small `ε`, the exact branch identities give

\[
\sin(\phi_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon r_\varepsilon,
\qquad
\phi_\varepsilon=\arcsin(\varepsilon r_\varepsilon),
\]

where `φ_ε` is the actual principal phase `arccos C(ε)`. The arcsine cubic remainder is proved from the actual derivative at zero and exact sine Taylor control:

\[
\arcsin x=x+\frac{x^3}{6}+O(x^5).
\]

Combining it with the radial expansion yields

\[
\phi_\varepsilon
=E\varepsilon-\frac{m^2k^2}{6E}\varepsilon^3+O(\varepsilon^5).
\]

Dividing by positive spacing is justified by dedicated Big-O division lemmas. Thus the actual total positive-branch frequency satisfies

\[
\boxed{

=E-\frac{m^2k^2}{6E}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4)
\qquad (\varepsilon	o0^+).
}
\]

This is equation (8.11) for the exact principal-frequency branch, not merely for the polynomial candidate from Block 18.
