\hypertarget{reading-the-proof-evidence}{%
\section{Reading the proof evidence}\label{reading-the-proof-evidence}}

This guide maps the mathematical contracts to the saved Sufficient
Resolution v0.16.0 sources. It explains what the formal objects contain,
which hypotheses the theorems require, and how to inspect or replay the
proof evidence. The authoritative theorem statement is the elaborated
Lean type; the source files and their section variables are the
available specification when no compiler is present.

The source inventory contains \textbf{93 production modules},
\textbf{998 theorem/lemma declarations}, \textbf{144 definitions},
\textbf{9 abbreviations}, and \textbf{8 structures}, giving
\textbf{1,159 explicitly named declarations}. These counts include
supporting lemmas, examples, classical specializations, and definitions.
They measure the formal source corpus, not independent mathematical
discoveries or publication completion.

The final archived v0.16 checks, dated September 14, 2026, record two
separate source-tree runs. Each compiled the \textbf{31-module Block 16
dependency closure plus its block root}, inspected the transitive axioms
of \textbf{52 new named declarations}, and replayed that closure with
\texttt{leanchecker}. The archived summary explicitly
says that a fresh full 93-module corpus replay was \textbf{not run}.
Earlier blocks have their own archived checks; the latest closure does
not replace a full-corpus run.

For this final edition, source inventories, named mappings, preservation
of production files, publication builds, and finite Python checks are
current review activities. \textbf{No fresh Lean compilation was
performed.} The portable proof runtime and imported
\texttt{\seqsplit{.lake}} cache are absent from the recovered
source archive. Recovery of the first portable-runtime chunk and a
bounded independent retry both returned HTTP 502, preventing restoration
of the five-piece runtime. The repaired full-corpus driver is included
for a future replay with the pinned runtime.

The root-level current verification register distinguishes these
categories. A plan-only driver result or a textual absence of
placeholders is not kernel checking. A passing finite Python test is not
a proof of an infinite-dimensional or all-time statement.

\hypertarget{how-to-read-a-formal-contract}{%
\section{How to read a formal
contract}\label{how-to-read-a-formal-contract}}

Lean's \texttt{LinearMap} type means a linear map over
a declared scalar type; \texttt{ContinuousLinearMap}
means a continuous linear map over the declared normed field. Continuity
is part of the latter object and yields an operator norm. A formula
involving that norm concerns the complete map, not just selected input
vectors.

Lean's section variables are implicit theorem hypotheses. A displayed
theorem may depend on \texttt{CompleteSpace},
\texttt{FiniteDimensional}, or projection instances
declared above it. Reading only the theorem's final line can erase these
conditions. Conversely, some statements intentionally use less structure
than a convenient Hilbert-space specialization.

The source sets \texttt{autoImplicit false}. In the
Lake configuration, warnings are errors.
\texttt{noncomputable} marks definitions using
classical existence or selection; it does not mean the proof is
admitted. A noncomputable parameter selector with a proved specification
is distinct from an executable numerical root finder.

Unless a paragraph says otherwise, declaration names below have the
common prefix

\texttt{\seqsplit{Komorebi.SufficientResolution.}}

Operator multiplication applies the right factor first. A chronological
word \(w=[i_1,\ldots,i_n]\) applies \(T_{i_1}\) first, so
\(T_w=T_{i_n}\cdots T_{i_1}\). The empty word acts as the identity. This
convention controls the recurrence and defect formulas.

commutative-ring modules. The annihilating-polynomial and
characteristic-polynomial layer uses a field; finite hidden dimension
enters only the dimension-controlled certificate.

\texttt{BlockDynamics} constructs the operator

\[
T(y,z)=(Ay+Bz,\Gamma y+Dz),\qquad M_k=BD^k\Gamma.
\]

Here \(\Gamma\) denotes the source's block \texttt{G},
avoiding confusion with the source space used in the inverse-problem
chapters. The recurrence comes from powers of the actual full operator.
For arbitrary hidden initial state,

\[
\begin{aligned}
z_n&=D^nz_0+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}D^{n-1-j}\Gamma y_j,\\
y_{n+1}&=Ay_n+BD^nz_0+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}M_{n-1-j}y_j.
\end{aligned}
\]

Prepared evolution uses \(z_0=0\) and \(E_n=CT^nR\). Exact agreement
\(E_n=A^n\) for all \(n\), vanishing of every \(M_k\), the prepared
semigroup law, and vanishing of all power defects are equivalent. The
first nonzero \(M_k\) appears at full depth \(k+2\):

\[
E_{k+2}-A^{k+2}=M_k\quad\text{if }M_j=0\text{ for }j<k.
\]

A supplied monic annihilating polynomial \(p(D)=0\) makes the first
\(\deg p\) moments an all-depth certificate. Over a field with hidden
dimension \(d<\infty\), Mathlib's Cayley--Hamilton theorem supplies the
characteristic polynomial, yielding

\[
[E_n=A^n\text{ for every }n]
\iff[M_k=0\text{ for }k<d]
\iff[E_n=A^n\text{ for }n\le d+1].
\]

The visible space may remain infinite-dimensional and \(d=0\) is
included. These are equalities of whole linear maps. The canonical split
decomposition constructs the actual hidden space \(\ker C\) and applies
the same certificate to arbitrary split compression. All-state closure
is stronger: independence from every initial hidden state holds exactly

\hypertarget{block-4-quantitative-approximate-sufficiency}{%
\section{Block 4: quantitative approximate

For continuous linear maps on normed spaces over a nontrivially normed
field, let

\[
\delta_i=qT_i-U_iq,\qquad E_w=qT_w-U_wq.
\]

The algebraic transport identity is

\[
E_{i::w}=E_wT_i+U_w\delta_i,
\qquad E_{u++v}=E_vT_u+U_vE_u.
\]

It yields whole-map error budgets. Under nonnegative letter-specific
bounds \(\|T_i\|\le a_i\), \(\|U_i\|\le b_i\),
\(\|\delta_i\|\le\varepsilon_i\), the source defines product bounds
\(A_w,B_w\) and a recursively transported allowance \(D_w\), proving
\(\|E_w\|\le D_w\).

For uniform nonnegative bounds \(a,b,\varepsilon\), the response is

\[
H_0=0,\qquad
H_n(a,b)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a^jb^{n-1-j}\quad(n>0),
\]

and \(\|E_w\|\le\varepsilon H_{|w|}(a,b)\). The distinct-rate formula is
\((a^n-b^n)/(a-b)\); the equal-rate formula is
\(H_{n+1}(a,a)=(n+1)a^n\). No division by zero is used for equal rates.

If \(\|O-Vq\|\le\eta\), both mismatch sources remain:

\[
\|OT_w-VU_wq\|\le\eta a^n+\|V\|\varepsilon H_n(a,b).
\]

With true operations nonexpansive and reduced operations bounded by
\(0\le b<1\), the all-word bound is \(\varepsilon/(1-b)\). Initial
reduced-state error retains \(b^n\|qx-z\|\). Common operator-norm bounds
are assumptions; individual spectral radii do not supply them.

\[
[Ce^{tL}R=e^{tA}\text{ for all real }t]
\iff[BD^k\Gamma=0\text{ for every }k].

\[
E^{(n)}(0)=CL^nR,\qquad M^{(n)}(0)=BD^n\Gamma.

\section{Block 7: orthogonal obstruction and compatibility

Let \(R\subseteq K\subseteq V\) be real inner-product subspaces
admitting orthogonal projections. The actual components are

\[
r=P_Rv,\qquad o=(P_K-P_R)v,\qquad i=(I-P_K)v.
\]

Their unique, pairwise orthogonal decomposition satisfies

\[
v=r+o+i,\qquad \operatorname{dist}(v,R)^2=\|o\|^2+\|i\|^2.
\]

For a presentation \(G\xrightarrow{A}V\xrightarrow{\delta}W\) with
\(\delta A=0\), use \(R=\operatorname{Ran}A\) and \(K=\ker\delta\). The
original algebraic obstruction quotient is linearly identified with
\(K\cap R^\perp\). This block establishes a linear equivalence; its
isometry is supplied later in Block 9.

If \(\sigma>0\) and \(\sigma\|x\|\le\|\delta x\|\) for every
\(x\in K^\perp\), then

\[
\operatorname{dist}(v,R)^2\le\|o\|^2+
\left(\frac{\|\delta v\|}{\sigma}\right)^2.
\]

Compatibility residual, obstruction, and conditioning remain distinct.
Finite-dimensional \(V\) supplies some positive coercivity constant, not
its optimal value or a uniform constant over presentations. Closed range
remains explicit in Hilbert-space existence statements.

\hypertarget{block-8-actual-minimum-norm-reconstruction}{%
\section{Block 8: actual minimum-norm

For bounded real \(A:G\to V\), the core assumes complete source and
complete actual range, with the required orthogonal projections. The
public Hilbert constructor takes an explicit proof that
\(\operatorname{Ran}A\) is closed. It constructs the inverse of the
restriction

\[
A:(\ker A)^\perp\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ran}A
\]

using the open mapping theorem, then composes it with
\(P_{\operatorname{Ran}A}\). The resulting \(B=A^\dagger\) is
constructed; it is not supplied as an inverse or defined by assuming the
desired optimum.

\[
AB=P_{\operatorname{Ran}A},\qquad BA=P_{(\ker A)^\perp}.
\]

The source proves the Penrose identities, actual least-squares
optimality, characterization of every minimizer as \(Bv+k\) with
\(k\in\ker A\), and uniqueness of the minimum-norm minimizer. The
symmetry conditions are stated in real inner-product form, rather than
as separate adjoint equalities.

For arbitrary noise \(e\) and source \(g\),

\[
\|B(Ag+e)-g\|^2=\|Be\|^2+\|P_{\ker A}g\|^2.
\]

Whole-source conditioning \(\alpha>0\) gives \(\|B\|\le1/\alpha\). This
\(\alpha\) belongs to \(A\) and is independent of compatibility
conditioning \(\sigma\) for \(\delta\). A computed candidate needs
control of both image-fit error and source-kernel leakage to certify
proximity to the minimum-norm source.

\hypertarget{block-9-the-intrinsic-obstruction-metric}{%
\section{Block 9: the intrinsic obstruction

The source uses Mathlib's native quotient seminorm on the same Block 1
obstruction quotient. Under the projection hypotheses, it upgrades the
previous equivalence to a genuine linear isometry

\[
K/R\simeq_{\mathrm{lin,isom}}K\cap R^\perp.
\]

For compatible \(v\), \(\|[v]\|=\operatorname{dist}(v,R)\). The harmonic
section is the unique minimum-norm compatible representative of the
class. This local representative is different from Block 8's
minimum-norm global source.

For arbitrary data, the bounded intrinsic class is \(c(v)=[P_Kv]\), with
norm at most one. Then

\[
\operatorname{dist}(v,R)^2=\|c(v)\|^2+\|(I-P_K)v\|^2.
\]

Forward bounded presentation maps induce bounded maps on the existing
quotients. A reverse quantitative estimate requires a reverse
presentation map and a proved left-inverse identity on quotients.
Forward boundedness alone does not reflect zero obstruction.

For arbitrary submodule \(R\), including nonclosed ones,

\[
\|[v]\|=0\iff v\in\overline R,\qquad [v]=0\iff v\in R.
\]

Thus a nonclosed quotient can contain a nonzero zero-seminorm class. The
source proves that such a quotient has no linear isometry equivalence to
a genuine normed space. It does not prohibit noninjective
norm-preserving maps out of a seminormed space.

\hypertarget{block-10-constructed-regularized-recovery}{%
\section{Block 10: constructed regularized
recovery}\label{block-10-constructed-regularized-recovery}}

On complete real inner-product spaces, with actual bounded \(A\) and
\(\lambda>0\), define

\[
N_\lambda=A^*A+\lambda I,\qquad
R_\lambda=N_\lambda^{-1},\qquad
B_\lambda=R_\lambda A^*.
\]

Mathlib's Lax--Milgram theorem supplies the inverse after coercivity of
the actual normal form is proved. Closed range and finite dimension are
unnecessary for this construction. Both inverse laws and the actual
normal equation are established.

The literal objective has the exact increment

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\lambda(v,x)&=\|Ax-v\|^2+\lambda\|x\|^2,\\
F_\lambda(v,x)-F_\lambda(v,B_\lambda v)
&=\|A(x-B_\lambda v)\|^2+\lambda\|x-B_\lambda v\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves unique global optimality. The fixed-parameter bounds include

\[
\|R_\lambda\|\le\lambda^{-1},\qquad
\|B_\lambda\|\le\frac1{2\sqrt\lambda},\qquad
\|AB_\lambda v\|\le\|v\|.
\]

An actual normal residual \(r=N_\lambda x-A^*v\) gives
\(x-B_\lambda v=R_\lambda r\) and source allowance \(\|r\|/\lambda\).
Bias and propagated noise are generally not orthogonal; their sum is
orthogonal only to the source-kernel component in the retained
squared-error identity.

Changing operators and parameters have explicit resolvent sensitivity
bounds. Those estimates need not preserve rank but are not uniform as
\(\lambda\to0\). Integration with harmonic obstruction and
Moore--Penrose recovery reinstates actual complete/closed range
precisely where those predecessor objects are used.

assume every identifiable source is exactly \(A^*Aw\) with a controlled

Noise satisfies strong source consistency when
\(\|e_i\|/\sqrt{\lambda_i}\to0\). An inexact normal solve adds the
actual residual condition \(\|r_i\|/\lambda_i\to0\). These are
sufficient conditions, not universal necessary ones.

Strong convergence and operator-norm convergence differ. For nonclosed
actual range, the source-error operator has norm one at \textbf{every}
positive parameter:

\[
\|B_\lambda A-P_{(\ker A)^\perp}\|=1.
\]

For a nontrivial parameter filter, uniform convergence to zero is
equivalent to closed range. Fitted data always converge to
\(P_{\overline{\operatorname{Ran}A}}v\). Data in closure but outside
actual range may be fitted asymptotically while the reconstructed source
family has no norm limit; norm divergence is not asserted.

The new candidate-dependent allowance is

\operatorname{candidateNoise}(\eta,\delta,x)=\eta+\delta\|x\|.

accepted positive-parameter source is an actual Tikhonov solution
satisfying

\[
\eta+\delta\|x\|\le\|Cx-v\|\le\tau(\eta+\delta\|x\|).

comparison \(p\), the lower band implies \(\|x\|\le\|p\|\). Thus
accepted sources obtain the source-norm control previously supplied by

converge to the fixed identifiable source.

Acceptance and feasibility are separate. One sufficient condition for
selection is

\[
\delta\|p\|<(\tau-1)\eta,\qquad \eta>0,\quad\tau>1.

is not an input to the generalized acceptance predicate. The raw
\texttt{\seqsplit{GeneralizedDiscrepancySelection.selectedSource}}
constructor itself takes the true operator, a comparison source, and
truth/feasibility proofs, and uses classical choice. It is a
theorem-level existence witness with a proved specification, rather than
an executable available-data API. The formally checked zero-model
example has valid true allowances but no accepted generalized source,
refuting unconditional feasibility. Near-accepted computed outputs
retain their explicit source-correction budget.

\hypertarget{exact-and-redundant-frames}{%

\texttt{CalibrationFrame.Frame K G} contains bounded
\(U:K\to G\), \(R:G\to K\), and \(UR=I_G\). The calibration synthesis
need not be injective, square, or nonredundant. For measured response
operator \(Y:K\to V\), true operator \(A\), and model \(C\), assume

\[
\|Y-AU\|\le\eta_{\mathrm{cal}},\qquad
\|Y-CU\|\le f.
\]

The exact factorization and allowance are

\[
A-C=(AU-CU)R,\qquad
\|C-A\|\le(\eta_{\mathrm{cal}}+f)\|R\|.
\]

This converts justified calibration-truth error and measured model fit
into a whole-source operator bound. It does not infer
\(\eta_{\mathrm{cal}}\) from observations. Exact calibration identifies
the operator because the frame spans the source through its bounded
right inverse.

\hypertarget{recovery-hierarchy-and-approximate-frames}{%
\subsection{Recovery, hierarchy, and approximate
frames}\label{recovery-hierarchy-and-approximate-frames}}

The generated allowance enters the existing generalized discrepancy and
feasibility-aware selector. For a fixed true bounded operator \(A\)
between real Hilbert spaces, a fixed bounded split frame, and fixed
\(\tau\ge0\), accepted outputs converge to the fixed identifiable source
when valid data, calibration-truth, and fit allowances vanish. Actual
selected-source existence additionally uses \(\tau>1\) and the
relative-accuracy margin involving that comparison source. Changing
reconstruction maps with unbounded norms are not covered merely by
vanishing calibration errors.

For an arbitrary true source \(g\), apply the identifiable theorem with
\(p=P_{(\ker A)^\perp}g\) and genuine data bounds
\(\|Ag-v_i\|\le\eta_i\). Then the source-error norm tends to
\(\|P_{\ker A}g\|\). The noise allowance concerns the original source's
data; the projection has the same forward image.

Split frames compose; their reconstruction norms multiply in the bound.
For an approximate frame with

\[
\|I-UR\|\le\rho<1,
\]

the source proves

\[
\|C-A\|\le\frac{(\eta_{\mathrm{cal}}+f)\|R\|}{1-\rho}.
\]

No useful continuation at \(\rho=1\) is asserted. The decomposition
\(T=(TU)R+T(I-UR)\) supplies the estimate, without assuming the desired
conclusion.

\hypertarget{coverage-boundaries-to-retain}{%
\section{Coverage boundaries to
retain}\label{coverage-boundaries-to-retain}}

The formal project establishes the stated linear contracts and numerous
actual sharpness or failure examples. It does not establish an
unrestricted sufficient realization for every mathematical object or
task.

The remaining connections include deriving a calibration-truth allowance
from individual noisy samples or a justified stochastic instrument
model; linking exact behavioral minimality to stable target-only
recovery under task-specific norms; formal verification or extraction of
Python algorithms and parameter searches; and extensions to constrained,
positive, stochastic, integral, nonlinear, geometric, and varying-metric
settings.

The bounded continuous-time layer does not establish unbounded-generator
or domain results. The classical strong-limit layer does not supply a
common rate over all identifiable sources. Nonclosed actual range does
not become closed by approximate fit. Forward obstruction transport does
not automatically reflect realizability. No arithmetic existence theorem
or Langlands correspondence follows from the generic linear presentation
contracts.

Each completed theorem remains valuable within its explicit hypotheses.
The paper's claim register groups those results for readers; the
declaration inventory supplies the implementation-level detail.
