    E(t)=integral_0^t exp((t-s)A) delta exp(sL) ds.

This is derived by differentiation and FTC for the actual flow, all real times.
For t>=0, explicit growth bounds ||exp(sL)||<=ML exp(a s),
||exp(sA)||<=MA exp(b s) imply

    ||E(t)|| <= ML MA epsilon R(a,b,t),
    R(a,b,t)=(exp(a t)-exp(b t))/(a-b) if a!=b,
    R(a,a,t)=t exp(a t).

The more general envelope theorem only assumes bounds on [0,t]. Output/readout
and arbitrary-initialization discrepancies are separated. Bounded true evolution
and exponentially stable reduced evolution give a uniform model allowance plus
an exp(-rate t) initialization term. Both stages of a model hierarchy contribute.
The actual scalar flow attains the two-rate bound. Arbitrarily small positive
generator mismatch permits unbounded error without growth control.
