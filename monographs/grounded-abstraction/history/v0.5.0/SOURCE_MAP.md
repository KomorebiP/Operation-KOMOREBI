# Source map and review scope

**Review date: 9 October 2026.** The expanded paper contains twelve references. Entries 1-10 retain their first-draft review scope unless explicitly updated below. The review was targeted, not a complete novelty audit or a reproof of the cited literatures. Every formal result used as a theorem in this draft has a self-contained argument in the draft. Contextual citations do not silently import stronger unexamined results.

## Project predecessors

1. **Machine Understanding v1.0.0**, Komorebi P., 8 October 2026. The first-edition abstract and source relationships concerning behavioral state, acquisition, correction, and explanatory recipients were inspected. Its supplied source package's formatting was consulted. Related results retain their own proof boundaries; this new paper does not inherit a Lean certificate.

Exact mounted predecessor PDF hashes are in `history/v0.1.0/validation/predecessor_bindings.json`. The Library-only Sufficient Resolution source was read as parsed text; it was not downloaded merely to invent a byte-binding claim. None of these predecessor files was edited.

## Public foundations

4. **Rothenberg (1971), Identification in Parametric Models.** Primary bibliographic record checked, DOI `10.2307/1913267`. The attempted JSTOR page did not provide readable full text. Citation is contextual, to classical identification from observational laws; no unexamined local-rank or information-matrix theorem is used.

5. **Littman, Sutton, and Singh, Predictive Representations of State.** Conference 2001, proceedings volume 14 (2002), pp. 1555–1561. The original proceedings PDF was obtained and its opening and definition context inspected. The web abstract's author list was incomplete; the original PDF supplied all three authors. An author-copy fetch failed and the BibTeX endpoint was not readable through the tool, so neither was treated as full-text verification.

   Primary PDF: `https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2001/file/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Paper.pdf`

6. **Angluin (1987), Learning Regular Sets from Queries and Counterexamples.** DOI `10.1016/0890-5401(87)90052-6`. Publisher metadata and an accessible copy of the author's paper were consulted, particularly its abstract and teacher/query assumptions. The current paper does not claim to implement L* or transfer its complexity guarantees to this setting.

   Readable paper: `https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~dawnsong/teaching/s10/papers/angluin87.pdf`

7. **van Waarde, Eising, Trentelman, and Camlibel (2020), Data Informativity: A New Perspective on Data-Driven Analysis and Control.** IEEE TAC 65(11):4753–4768. Author publication metadata and the full arXiv paper were available. Review focused on the abstract, compatible-system sets, property informativity, and the common-controller quantifier. This is the closest acknowledged foundation for identifying a useful property without identifying a whole system. The paper does not claim that organizing principle as new.

   `https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00468`

8. **Geiger et al. (2025), Causal Abstraction: A Theoretical Foundation for Mechanistic Interpretability.** JMLR 26(83):1–64. Official abstract and publication metadata, including the author list, were checked. No complete proof audit of the 64-page paper was performed. Used to distinguish mechanism-level compatibility from an observer's acquisition and initialization problem.

   `https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-0058.html`

9. **van den Bos and Vaandrager (2019), State Identification for Labeled Transition Systems with Inputs and Outputs.** Author arXiv record and readable article context were inspected for the testing framework and distinction between kinds of identification. It is not the source of the draft's explicit three-world destructive-test example.

   `https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11034`

10. **Blackwell (1953), Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments.** Annals of Mathematical Statistics 24(2):265–272, DOI `10.1214/aoms/1177729032`. Primary publication metadata was available. The full-text endpoint did not yield readable text. The finite binary-testing and postprocessing arguments are proved directly, not attributed to an unread theorem.

## Added primary sources for v0.5

11. **W.-G. Tzeng (1992), A Polynomial-Time Algorithm for the Equivalence of Probabilistic Automata.** SIAM Journal on Computing 21(2):216-227, DOI `10.1137/0221017`. The primary publisher's bibliographic record and abstract were checked. The full article was not reviewed. Used only to acknowledge the established finite linear-algebra equivalence-testing tradition. The paired-kernel proof in this manuscript is self-contained and does not silently import the paper's complete algorithm or complexity theorem.

    `https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0221017`

12. **M. Petreczky, L. Bako, and J. H. van Schuppen (2013), Realization Theory of Discrete-Time Linear Switched Systems.** Automatica 49(11):3337-3344, DOI `10.1016/j.automatica.2013.07.022`. The author preprint, its readable HTML, and CWI publication record were inspected. Selected passages included reachability, observability, Theorem 3 on minimality, and the finite realization procedures. This was not a complete independent reproof of the cited article. The paper supplies the appropriate context for common linear dynamics and reachable/observable quotients. Our fixed-unknown-model comparison is kept separate from unrestricted switching.

    `https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.1343`
    `https://arxiv.org/html/1103.1343v2`
    `https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/18901`

The author surname uses **Petreczky**, as in the article and publication metadata; one surfaced preprint record used an inconsistent spelling. Entry 7's data-informativity metadata and abstract were also rechecked for the compatible-model interpretation. Its broader first-draft reading scope is retained, not represented as a new complete reading.

A related author paper, *On the Complexity of the Equivalence Problem for Probabilistic Automata* (`https://arxiv.org/html/1112.4644v2`), was consulted at its finite-span argument as an additional contextual check. It is not an added bibliography entry or an assumed theorem in the manuscript. Unrelated sections were not used.

## What the review does not establish

No comprehensive priority claim, citation-completeness claim, empirical performance claim, or proof-assistant result follows from this source map. Some foundations were inspected only through metadata or selected passages. No third-party full papers or standalone fonts are included in the package.
