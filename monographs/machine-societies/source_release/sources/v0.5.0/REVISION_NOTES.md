# Revision notes — v0.4.0 to v0.5.0

## Editorial direction

The manuscript remains theoretical and anonymous under the credit **Komorebi P.** The additions are integrated into the existing argument rather than attached as a second prospectus. The four-office commons supplies connecting examples. The original v0.4.0 files remain unchanged; their hashes are recorded in `sources/PREDECESSOR_SHA256.txt`.

## Eight integrated developments

### 1. Endogenous institutional boundaries
Membership records, voting positions, actual controllers, weights, thresholds, and configuration epochs are represented separately. Weight-conserving refinements preserve controller-coalition approval under a fixed threshold. A quorum-witness result explains why overlapping rosters alone do not provide safe reconfiguration. It is explicitly not a complete consensus protocol.

### 2. Institutional language
A typed message interpreter supplies admissibility, state transitions, and public event records. A state-dependent translation contract preserves those effects, and a proposition proves finite-conversation preservation and composition. A counterexample separates reporting an approval from issuing one. Internal codes do not acquire authority merely by naming a role.

### 3. Public knowledge and notice
Common knowledge is characterized by reachability through the union of indistinguishability relations. A three-world counterexample separates everyone knowing from common knowledge. Public announcements use an explicit idealized update and a stable propositional fact. Bounded-morphism conditions preserve and reflect epistemic formulas, including common knowledge; they compose with the operational contract only when both sets of hypotheses hold.

### 4. Noncommutative institutional holonomy
The binary cycle model is generalized to group-valued edge translations, with an explicit right-to-left composition convention and gauge law. Exact frames require identity loop holonomy. Compatible state sections instead require a fixed point for the holonomy action. A permutation counterexample shows these are different. A bi-invariant-metric bound converts loop defect into unavoidable interface error.

### 5. Constitutional confluence
Amendments are analyzed as a terminating rewriting episode. Newman's lemma is stated, attributed, and proved in that setting. Authorized last-write proposals illustrate ordering power. Endpoint agreement is separated from agreement about irreversible allocations, time, or audit histories. A finite episode's termination does not require an entire society to cease changing.

### 6. The computability boundary
The paper explicitly leaves the finite-state setting for one unbounded-program theorem. Reduction from halting establishes that a total constitutional-safety decider cannot exist for the unrestricted program class. Finite-state search, bounded horizons, restricted languages, and sound but incomplete proof-carrying rules remain available. No finite check is presented as establishing undecidability.

### 7. Evolving problem agendas
Methods and recognized questions are coupled under institutional admission and allocation rules. Positive finite production rules have a least closure reached by fair scheduling, with an explicit finite growth bound. This is not an open-endedness theorem. Changing the question set can manufacture apparent progress without increasing capabilities. Selection for copying is separated from selection for accuracy.

### 8. Founding authority
The record-factorization criterion states when an externally stipulated founding authorization can be determined from internal records. Procedural validity, historical authorization, and moral legitimacy remain distinct. A factorization is an information statement; it is not automatically computable, affordable, or normatively adequate.
