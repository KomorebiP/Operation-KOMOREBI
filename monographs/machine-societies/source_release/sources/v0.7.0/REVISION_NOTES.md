# Revision notes — v0.6.0 to v0.7.0

**Theme: one institution needs one jointly executable policy.**

## Mathematical development

The new common-monitor theorem replaces separate deviation tests with a single feasibility problem. Finite convex duality supplies a mixture obstruction: every deviation may be individually detectable even though a mixture reproduces the compliance law and makes simultaneous deterrence impossible. A robust sufficient margin carries observation-law error into the same chosen monitor and false-positive cap.

The synthesis section fixes a finite catalogue of already justified local protocol profiles and a common observation available by assumption. A greatest fixed point identifies all public possibility sets with safe continuations. A separate attractor gives sure certified service and exact finite progress ranks. A smaller invariant family can be checked without computing the largest kernel. An exact quotient preserving observations, enabled profiles, protected labels, and successor images preserves both safety and progress strategy existence.

The commons variant couples auditing with protected work. Two audit slots and three due service units do not fit in a four-slot budget, although each component promise is feasible separately. At five slots a concrete policy and certificate are produced. The source gives the general threshold and exact monitor-margin curve.

## Integration and preservation

Added six theorem/proposition statements and their prose proofs. All 50 predecessor statements and proofs are retained verbatim. Three formal counterexamples were added to the inventory. One formalizes the two-deviation warning that was already present informally in v0.6, rather than introducing a wholly new phenomenon. Two analytical vector figures, two bibliography entries, and an executable reference implementation were added. The existing Chatterjee et al. reference was reused rather than duplicated.

The abstract, reading route, remaining-problems section, limitations, conclusion, and commons appendix now connect separate guarantees to joint continuation. The source retains the distinction between institutional desirability, executability, spontaneous formation, and mathematical feasibility.

## Conditions that must not be dropped

The common monitor uses fixed, authorized, collectible penalties and stipulated observation laws. It does not establish the auditor's incentives. The synthesis uses finite stored state, a known transition support, fixed local protocol choices, and an implemented common information boundary. It is not complete for arbitrary decentralized architectures or unbounded private memories. Incentive constraints do not remove physically possible deviations from the safety model. Certified completion is stronger than a hidden event having occurred. Sure progress is stronger than almost-sure eventual completion. Exact strategy transport is not implied by a small average prediction error.

## Presentation and implementation corrections

A stale introduction anchor caused the first integration script to stop before writing. The preliminary build that followed was the unchanged staged predecessor, not the integrated revision. The anchor was corrected and the expanded source then built. Later visual review found the new legends encroaching on the plotted data region. Both were moved outside the axes and the pages were rendered again. A grammatical article and nonempty-catalogue convention were corrected. The certificate checker was hardened to reject malformed ranks, out-of-range goals, and negative state masks. No new mathematical assertion failed during this revision.
