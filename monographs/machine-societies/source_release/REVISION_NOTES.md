# Revision notes — v0.7.0 to v1.0.0

**First edition · 8 October 2026**

## Editorial consolidation

The manuscript is organized into five parts rather than presented as an undifferentiated sequence of expansions. The new front matter supplies an edition statement, three reading routes and a notation guide. The title and author credit are retained. The abstract, introduction, conclusion and integrated commons appendix now include accountability as part of the operational theory. The appendices start on a new page rather than leaving the first table row beneath the conclusion.

The 1.0 label freezes the scope of this edition. Open research questions remain identified. It is not an acceptance claim or a formal-verification label. The original v0.7 PDF and ZIP are unchanged.

## Added accountability chapter

Affected outsiders, infrastructure owners, participating members and decision holders are represented separately. A missing external reservation gives an explicit counterexample to equating exact internal accounting with complete authority.

The inspection model makes the auditor a strategic participant. It derives the unique independent mixed equilibrium of a specified simultaneous game, then separates this from a different verified-service-fee contract. It does not silently assume a receipt verifier or payment budget is free.

A sharp likelihood-ratio privacy envelope bounds enforcement under a simultaneous false-positive constraint. Two-outcome laws attain the envelope. Conditional transcript accounting is proved, and a two-marginal counterexample shows why the complete disclosure history matters. The neighboring-input relation remains explicit rather than treating all violations as privacy-protected neighbors.

The finite appeal model gives equivalent matching and interval-capacity criteria under unit-job, evidence and reviewer assumptions. A bounded service promise applies to a specified workload, not an unlimited arrival stream.

The ending construction first identifies states from which an authorized ending can be completed, then retains running states that preserve that ability. It distinguishes a safe indefinite loop from a state that can still settle. Suspension, settlement and dissolution have different target predicates.

Guarded certificate use receives a sufficient safety and common-rank composition theorem. Stale premises, resetting local ranks and unsupported cycles of assurance remain explicit failure modes. Handover and checking states are counted when they consume transitions.

## Reference-code improvement

The knowledge-game expansion now enumerates nonempty subsets within observation fibres instead of enumerating every global subset and filtering. It preserves the mathematical state space. Exhaustive small comparisons and a fully observed 128-state control check the implementation change. This is not a general cure for exponential partial-observation state spaces.
