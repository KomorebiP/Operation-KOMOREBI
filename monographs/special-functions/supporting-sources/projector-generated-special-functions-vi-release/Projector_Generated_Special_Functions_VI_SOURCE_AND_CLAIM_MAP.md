# Source and claim map

## Proved in the paper

- normalization of the log-Gamma packet
- the cross-shape Gamma-sech ambiguity formula
- strict shape affinity and its local metric
- exact beta-logit and Meixner factorization
- the Meixner convolution semigroup
- the shape recurrence for the spectral densities
- the Levy-Khintchine representation and generator
- the Bernoulli cumulant formula
- normalized two-child beta-binomial packet refinement
- many-child Dirichlet-multinomial refinement
- coassociativity of refinement
- the Meixner polynomial martingale law
- exact transfer singular values and cocycle
- compactness and Schatten-class thresholds
- coherent-state overlap and tight-frame resolution
- the Gamma-sech reproducing kernel
- exact Berezin-kernel factorization
- strict positivity of the Berezin multiplier
- injectivity and unbounded inverse
- Gaussian marginal and heat-operator limits
- diagonal shape, translation, and modulation metrics

## Classical inputs

- Gamma and polygamma functions
- beta and beta-binomial distributions
- Meixner distributions
- Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials
- Levy-Khintchine theory
- coherent states and the Moyal identity
- anti-Wick and Berezin quantization
- Gaussian heat kernels

The generated contribution is the common packet normalization and the exact network of refinement and transfer identities linking these inputs.

## External quantum context

The 2026 paper by Wei, Zhai, Lu, and collaborators uses a logarithmic number-basis Hamiltonian to encode phases of the form `n^{-it}` and gives polynomial-resource quantum constructions. This paper does not reproduce or adapt figures from that article. It uses the logarithmic coordinate only as external context for the arithmetic packet series.

## Not claimed

- no proof of the Riemann hypothesis
- no proof of the principal signed Mobius coherence inequality
- no historical priority claim for classical special functions
- no complete gate count for a quantum implementation of the shape channel
- no claim that the Berezin inverse is numerically stable
