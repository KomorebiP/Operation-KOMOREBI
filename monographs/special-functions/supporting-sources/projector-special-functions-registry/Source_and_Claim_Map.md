# Source and Claim Map

## Scope

The registry contains the canonical special-function families currently explicit in the transfer-block/projector corpus. It does not claim that every family is historically new. Applying a residue projector to an arbitrary analytic seed produces an infinite closure class, so the finite registry records only families with an intrinsic definition and several structural laws.

## Status classes

- **Classical boundary:** exponential residue sections and the denominator functions are established multisection or generalized hypergeometric objects.
- **Explicit derived family:** the defining series, recurrence, differential law, or polynomial sequence is proved and implemented, while historical equivalence remains possible.
- **Candidate-new family:** the organized family appears structurally distinctive, but a formula-level priority audit is still required.
- **Operator/schema family:** a useful functional calculus rather than one scalar special function.

## Strongest candidate families

1. The full transfer-secant tower `D_N(z)^(-r)` with its block convolution, triangular recurrence, partition formula, and arithmetic filtration.
2. The logarithmic-derivative transfer tangents `-D_N'(z)/D_N(z)` as companions to the reciprocal tower.
3. The congruential inverse sections and their higher powers, treated as a unified packet family.
4. The block-Appell and order-parameter polynomial extensions.
5. The spectral and residue-weighted block zeta functions, conditional on completing convergence and regularization.

## Boundaries

- Root-of-unity filtering alone is classical.
- The spectral zeta families remain analytically incomplete beyond their convergent and explicitly factored cases.
- The p-adic avatars require bounded or weighted Amice–Mahler estimates.
- Resolvent and nonlinear jet packets are operator-valued constructions, not automatically new scalar special functions.
