# Sources consulted for the essay

Access/review date: 23 October 2026. This is an administrative record, not a claim that
all listed project editions are the latest artifacts in every research branch.

## Working baseline

`Komorebi_Methodology_Essay_Outline_Professional.pdf` and its corresponding source ZIP.
The approved layout and nine-chapter narrative architecture were retained. The outline
was replaced by a complete prose manuscript, not merely rebound or relabeled.

## Project primary sources

1. **Komorebi Realization Calculus**, companion monograph essay, as preserved in
   `Operation_Komorebi_Collected_Papers_2026-10-10.pdf`. Relevant material: packet and
   transfer identities, the exact returning-coordinate example, admissible correction,
   and typed defect composition. Source excerpts were consulted through the research
   file record. No new project-wide replay was inferred from this reading.
2. **Komorebi Space: Coherent Realizations, Obstructions, and Descent**, publication
   edition 1.15.0, `Komorebi_Space_Monograph_v1.15.0.pdf`. The preface and reading/formal
   scope guide distinguish strict coherent families, symmetries, protected repair,
   analytic models, and their separate hypotheses.
3. **Proof-Path Theory: Certificates, Compression, Discovery Fields, and Mathematical
   Memory**, edition 1.2.0, `Proof_Path_Theory_1.2.0.pdf`. Sections 2–4, 7–10, 15–17,
   and 24.6–24.7 support the proof-field architecture, compression and provenance
   discussion, native declaration export, and recorded adversarial verification example.
   The essay attributes the source's reported compiler and axiom-policy results. It
   does not claim to have independently rebuilt that project during this handoff.

The editions above are bibliographic/source identifications. The companion PDFs and
full formal archives are not included in this source package.

## Public primary and official technical sources

4. George Pólya, *How to Solve It: A New Aspect of Mathematical Method*. Princeton
   University Press, 1945; second edition, 1957. University-hosted scan consulted,
   including the copyright, preface, and introductory heuristic discussion:
   https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/math436/polya.pdf
5. Imre Lakatos, *Proofs and Refutations: The Logic of Mathematical Discovery*.
   Edited by John Worrall and Elie Zahar. Cambridge University Press, 1976.
   Publisher record and description:
   https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139171472
6. David H. Bailey and Jonathan M. Borwein, “Experimental Mathematics: Examples,
   Methods and Implications.” Author manuscript, 2005. Author-hosted text:
   https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/ams-expmath.pdf
7. Lean Project, *The Lean Language Reference*, “Validating a Lean Proof”:
   https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/
8. Lean Project, *The Lean Language Reference*, “Axioms”:
   https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Axioms/
9. Lean Community, “Did you prove it?”:
   https://leanprover-community.github.io/did_you_prove_it.html

The public PDFs were checked in rendered form where used. Historical references are
brief contextual summaries, not claims to have proved that Komorebi originated the
heuristic or experimental traditions. The official Lean references concern the meaning
and limits of formal checking. Their consultation is not a Lean execution receipt.

No source is used to assert that the methodology has already outperformed alternative
research workflows. That comparison is explicitly a proposed research design.
