# RRCT v0.49 Instrumented Transformer Dyad Protocol

## Scientific question

When two separately initialized transformer agents are trained to solve a recurrent cooperative task through learned continuous messages, do their actual residual streams contain a shared J-space-like representation?

The protocol distinguishes four claims:

1. A task-relevant state can be decoded from both agents.
2. The states can be aligned across independently parameterized residual spaces.
3. The aligned state is causally used for shared reports and flexible planning while private routines remain outside it.
4. The state forms a persistent distributed workspace rather than a transient late decision representation.

A positive result on Claims 1–3 does not imply Claim 4.

## Transformer dyad

Each agent has independent weights and contains:

- one transformer block;
- model dimension 24;
- four attention heads;
- feedforward dimension 48;
- an eight-dimensional learned continuous message channel;
- four residual-stream token positions;
- recurrent message exchange over four stages;
- five shared output heads and one private routine head.

Agent A receives one three-way factor and one private four-way role. Agent B receives another three-way factor and a separate private four-way role. The agents must predict:

- both three-way factors;
- the nine-way joint action;
- two distinct flexible functions of the factors;
- their own private routine.

All 144 combinations are used during training. This is an internal-representation pilot, not a generalization benchmark.

## Instrumentation

Residual states are inspected at three recurrent positions:

- `h1`: after the first message exchange;
- `h2`: after the second message exchange;
- `h3`: the final decision state.

For `h1` and `h2`, exact average gradients of future factor-report logits are computed with respect to the residual state. At `h3`, those gradients equal the report-head rows directly. Directions are centered within each factor family, normalized, and reduced by SVD.

A broader late workspace is formed from all centered shared-head Jacobian rows. Activations are restricted to that row space, then reduced to a frozen rank-eight activation basis chosen on development seeds as the smallest rank at which all five shared tasks decoded perfectly in both agents while private-role decoding remained near chance.

This broader object is called the **Jacobian-informed late shared-access space**. The protocol does not assume that it is uniquely J-space-like; same-rank PCA and random subspaces are explicit controls.

## Causal tests

### Ablation

The candidate workspace component is removed from both `h3` residual states. Shared-output accuracy and private-routine accuracy are measured immediately and after two additional recurrent communication rounds.

### Bilateral patch

A joint-state centroid difference changes factor A from 0 to 2 while preserving factor B. The aligned patch is written into both agents' late workspaces. Reports, joint planning, flexible outputs, and private routines are evaluated.

### One-sided transmission

The same patch is written only into Agent A. One additional communication round tests whether Agent B's report and joint plan change. A second round tests whether Agent A retains the injected state or reconstructs its original local evidence. The symmetric Agent B-to-Agent A intervention is also evaluated.

### Communication-only control

A linear decoder receives only the two first-round messages. It may solve the immediate joint task without establishing a shared residual workspace.

## Development boundary

Development used only seeds:

```text
49001
49007
```

Development established the following expected pattern:

- perfect trained task performance;
- rank-four report Jacobians at early and late stages;
- poor semantic access, poor alignment, and weak causal necessity at `h1`;
- perfect factor reports and strong cross-agent alignment at `h3`;
- a rank-eight late subspace supporting all flexible tasks while excluding private roles;
- selective direct ablation and successful bilateral patching;
- one-sided cross-agent transmission followed by correction from persistent local evidence;
- same-rank PCA performing comparably to the Jacobian-informed basis.

No holdout seed was trained or evaluated before this protocol, runner, test file, and source freeze were written.

## Frozen endpoints

1. All shared and private heads reach exact accuracy 1.0.
2. The `h1` report-Jacobian rank equals four in both agents.
3. Mean `h1` direct J-score accuracy is at most 0.40.
4. `h1` cross-agent alignment R² is at most 0.35.
5. `h1` report-space ablation leaves mean shared accuracy at least 0.90.
6. The `h3` report-Jacobian rank equals four in both agents.
7. All four `h3` factor-report J-scores equal 1.0.
8. `h3` report-space alignment R² is at least 0.95.
9. Each `h3` report space explains between 25% and 55% of residual variance.
10. `h3` report-space ablation lowers shared accuracy to at most 0.80 while private accuracy remains at least 0.95.
11. The rank-eight Jacobian-informed space decodes all five shared tasks at least 0.95 in both agents.
12. Private-role decoding from the Jacobian-informed space is at most 0.45 in both agents.
13. Rank-eight cross-agent alignment R² is at least 0.95.
14. Bilateral patching changes all target shared outputs while preserving private routines at least 0.95.
15. An Agent A-only patch changes Agent B's factor report and joint action at least 0.75 while preserving private routine at least 0.95.
16. The symmetric Agent B-only patch satisfies the same threshold for Agent A.
17. After the patch returns through another round, each originating agent restores its locally evidenced factor, so target persistence is at most 0.25.
18. Rank-eight direct ablation lowers mean shared accuracy to at most 0.45 while private accuracy remains at least 0.95.
19. Two recurrent rounds reconstruct mean shared accuracy to at least 0.85 and private accuracy to at least 0.95.
20. The first-message communication-only decoder reaches joint accuracy at least 0.95.
21. Same-rank PCA reaches shared probe accuracy at least 0.95 and differs from the Jacobian-informed minimum by at most 0.05.
22. A same-rank random subspace leaks private role at least 0.65 and fails at least one flexible task below 0.95.
23. No valence model, identity reward, or survival reward is present.

## Interpretation boundary

Passing all endpoints would establish a bounded trained-transformer result:

- a late aligned residual representation supports shared reports, flexible tasks, causal patching, and cross-agent transmission;
- the analogous early average-Jacobian state does not satisfy the same workspace criteria;
- the representation is transient and reconstructible rather than a persistent autonomous pair-level workspace;
- Jacobian specificity is not established because PCA performs comparably.

It would not establish a shared J-space in pretrained language models, spontaneous open-ended workspace formation, shared consciousness, subjectivity, sentience, valence, or moral status.
